# Verdict on C8 (refuter 0, angle: evidence on the warp-shell leg (Fuchs et al.'s own text plus a 2026 preprint), backed by a physics/scale calculation on the rocket leg)
verdict: weakened
basis: cited-evidence

## Strongest attack
C8 bundles two claims:
1. **Rocket leg.** A 1 g photon rocket to 4.37 ly needs no negative energy and is "more than 20 orders of magnitude closer to demonstrated supply than the cheapest superluminal warp".
2. **Warp-shell leg.** Subluminal positive-energy warp shells (Fuchs et al.: 4.49e27 kg at 0.04c) "need no negative energy".

The rocket leg survives my attack. The warp-shell leg holds only in a narrower form than C8 states, and one qualifier ("cheapest") needs restricting.

**A. The Fuchs shell is positive-energy only while it coasts at constant velocity. Its own authors say the obvious way to accelerate it needs negative energy, and their only alternative is a rocket (cited evidence).**
- Fuchs et al. 2024, §5.3 (p. 27), on accelerating the shell:
  > "One possible approach is to have the bubble accelerate by simply accelerating the coordinate center and increasing the magnitude of the shift vector accordingly. However, this approach gives the exact same issue as the Schwarzschild Drive [16], ... This approach changes the metric such that it now requires a negative energy density throughout space, asymptotically approaching zero at infinity. An obvious alternative is to imagine that some basic momentum transfer occurs, where mass is shed in the process of creating the momentum flux in the bubble. In this way, a kind of rocket-like solution could be possible"

  [Fuchs et al. 2024, CQG 41 095013, arXiv:2405.02709, p. 27, ACCESS: full-text via fetch_text]
- So "subluminal positive-energy warp shells need no negative energy" holds for **the constant-velocity phase only**. C8 lists "no acceleration phase solved" under evidence against, but its headline still presents the shell as a no-negative-energy route. Using the paper's own words, the positive-energy route to a moving shell is a rocket.
- **Consequence for the reframe (calculation).** Accelerating and then braking a 4.49e27 kg shell to 0.04c by any external or reaction means costs at least 2(γ − 1)Mc² = 6.5e41 J (SI). That is 10^21.0 world-years of primary energy, and 10^18.3 times the 3.5e23 J of the 1e5 kg photon rocket. The trip to 4.37 ly then takes 109 yr at 0.04c, against 3.58 yr ship time for the rocket. An onboard photon rocket would need mass ratio 1.083 and 3.4e43 J (10^22.75 world-years) [calc: runs/2026-09-29-alcubierre-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C8-0_reframe_gaps.py].
- The rocket strictly dominates the Fuchs shell as a transport option, so the shell adds nothing to C8's answer except as a proof of concept that a constant-velocity shift-vector region can satisfy the energy conditions.

**B. Even the constant-velocity claim is contested at the shell boundary (cited evidence, preprint).**
- An independent 2026 preprint checked the Fuchs shell with a frame-independent test:
  > "An independent frame-independent verification of the Fuchs constant-velocity shell confirms interior energy-condition compliance (0 of 13 interior probes violate) but reveals Hawking--Ellis Type~IV violations in the smoothing tail beyond the nominal shell. A frame-independent scan over shell compactness and thickness (600 configurations) yields no admissible configuration in either source-first class. The same boundary deficit appears in the shift-free S-shell and persists in the static $v_0=0$ limit, which ties it to the transition geometry rather than to the shift."

  [An T. Le, arXiv:2605.25417v1 (25 May 2026), "On the boundary cost of source-consistent warp shells", abstract, ACCESS: abstract via fetch_text; preprint]
- My own reading of the limits of this evidence:
  - The failure sits in the smoothing at the source–vacuum boundary. It persists with zero shift, so it is a defect of how the matter shell was built, not proof that warp shells need negative energy.
  - v3 of the same arXiv number (17 Sep 2026) is retitled "Relativistic elastic shells: material support and cavity geometry". Its abstract drops the Fuchs sentence and says tangential elastic stress admits DEC-satisfying static shells "for sufficiently weak gravity". The Fuchs shell has 2GM/(c²R₂) = 0.33, which is not weak gravity.
- Fuchs et al. themselves show "No negative values are found" (Figs. 7 and 10), for sampled observers in Warp Factory.
- Net: the "satisfies all energy conditions" claim is unrefuted in the interior and contested at the boundary by one single-author preprint. That is a caveat, not a refutation.

**C. "Cheapest superluminal warp" is true only when restricted to established physics (internal inconsistency with the run's own gap table).**
- The engineer's table that C8 cites [calc: runs/2026-09-29-alcubierre-negative-energy/calc/lens-engineer_gaps.py] lists White's oscillating variant at 1e3 kg = 9.0e19 J, or 10^−0.8 world-years. That is **3.6 orders cheaper** than the photon rocket.
- It is excluded only because it relies on a brane-world relation, making it [speculative] under the brief ([U-02]). C8's 10^25.6 world-years is the cheapest option *within established physics*: the Bobrick–Martire-flattened, optimal-profile wall with Δ = 1 m, which itself ignores the quantum-inequality wall limit.
- The claim should say "cheapest established-physics warp".

- **Like-for-like supply.** Compare antimatter needed against antimatter ever made, and warp negative energy against demonstrated Casimir energy:
  - the photon rocket's 1.97e6 kg of antimatter is 10^17.1 times the ~16 ng ever made;
  - the warp's negative energy is 10^60.6 times the Bressi-plate Casimir energy of 5.0e-15 J;
  - so the rocket is **43.6 orders** closer on this measure, and 40.3 orders closer even with the pion rocket at v_e = 0.33c.
- **Shrinking the bubble instead.** At v = 1c, the thick-wall floor v²R/12 (geometric units) matches the rocket's 3.5e23 J only for R = 3.5e-20 m. A 1 cm bubble is still 17.5 orders above the rocket.
- Verdict on the margin: ">20 orders" holds for the ideal photon rocket, which is what C8 names. It holds for annihilation exhaust with v_e ≳ 0.4c, and it holds with a large margin on a like-for-like supply basis. It dips just under 20 (19.5) only for a realistic v_e = 0.33c pion rocket measured against world energy. C8's "more than 20" is therefore correct as stated but not conservative for "sub-light relativistic rockets" in general.
- The "about 23 orders" correction of the engineer's "roughly 25" (C8's own discrepancy note) is confirmed: 22.8.

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle (GR + semiclassical QFT):**
  - The relativistic rocket needs no negative energy and violates no energy condition. It is viable, and uncontroversial.
  - The Fuchs shell satisfies the energy conditions in its interior at constant velocity, contested at the smoothing boundary [Le 2026, preprint]. No positive-energy acceleration phase exists.
  - Its authors say the naive acceleration "requires a negative energy density throughout space", and that the positive-energy alternative is "rocket-like". As a *drive*, then, it is unproven in principle. As a moving shell pushed by a rocket, it is allowed.
  - Neither route is superluminal. By Olum's theorem [D-50], any superluminal version needs WEC violation.
- **In practice (by 2100):**
  - The photon rocket needs about 2e6 kg of antimatter, 10^17 times all production to date, and about 600 years of world primary energy. It is not buildable by 2100 at a 1e5 kg payload, as the engineer notes, but it is ~23 orders closer than any established-physics warp.
  - The Fuchs shell needs a 4.49e27 kg shell at compactness 0.33 and mean density 1.4e40 J/m³ (10^3.8 above quark–gluon plasma), plus 10^21 world-years of kinetic energy. TRL 1, not buildable.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **Survives:**
  - Dropping "superluminal" makes the question answerable.
  - A sub-light relativistic rocket (ideal photon, or annihilation exhaust with v_e ≳ 0.4c) needs no negative energy.
  - The rocket's energy need is > 20 orders closer to demonstrated supply than the cheapest **established-physics** superluminal warp: 22.8 orders against world energy, 43.6 like-for-like.
  - Every number C8 quotes checks out: ship time 3.58 yr, mass ratio 40.4, 3.5e18 J/kg, Fuchs 4.49e27 kg at 0.04c.
- **Narrowed:**
  - The Fuchs-type shell "needs no negative energy" only in its constant-velocity phase.
  - Its authors' only positive-energy acceleration route is rocket-like. That makes it at least 18 orders costlier than simply flying the ship, and 30× slower, so it contributes a proof of concept, not a transport option.
  - "Cheapest superluminal warp" must exclude White's [speculative] brane-world claim, which is cheaper than the rocket.
  - For realistic pion-rocket exhaust (v_e = 0.33c) the energy margin against world supply falls to 19.5 orders.
- **Remaining doubt:**
  - Le's boundary-violation result is a single-author preprint whose later version no longer states it.
  - Fuchs's energy-condition check samples observers rather than proving positivity for all of them. The [D-69] matrix-inequality test would settle this.
  - The C8 decisive test (solve a positive-energy acceleration phase for a Fuchs-type shell) is well posed. Fuchs et al.'s own §5.3 suggests the answer will be "only by shedding mass", which is a rocket.
