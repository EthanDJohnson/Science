## Strongest attack
C2 makes two claims. The first is a logical one: the bubble is viable in principle only if its wall is sourced by matter that the free-field quantum inequality (QI) does not govern. The second is a number: the bubble needs **at least 1.12e30 kg (0.56 M_sun)** of Eulerian negative energy at R = 100 m and v = 10c, "optionally Bobrick–Martire-flattened". The logical claim survives. The number is not a floor, for two independent reasons.

1. **Internal inconsistency (flattening).** C2's own argument uses Bobrick–Martire's rule E → E/α_X with α_X = 1 + v² = 101 [U-04]. It then quotes flattened totals of 2.5e29 kg and 7.4e29 kg, and both are below the "at least 1.12e30 kg" in the claim. Applied to the D → ∞ optimum, the same rule gives 1.12e30/101 = **1.11e28 kg (5.6e-3 M_sun)**. For v ≫ 1 it tends to the v-independent limit R/12 = 1.12e28 kg (SI, R = 100 m). So the "optionally flattened" bubble in the claim has a budget about 100× below the stated floor, by C2's own premises.

2. **Calculation (not tied to spherical symmetry).** For any Alcubierre-form shift β = (−v F(x,y,z), 0, 0) with unit lapse and a flat slice, the Eulerian density is ρ = −(v²/32π)(F_y² + F_z²) (geometric units). It contains **no ∂_x F term**. I verified this symbolically with gr_tensors for a fully general F.
   - The Cauchy–Schwarz floor −(v²/12)R(R + D)/D holds only for spherical f(r_s).
   - A non-spherical "pancake" profile removes it. Take F = g(x)h(ρ⊥), with the ship ball (R = 100 m) inside F = 1. Along the direction of travel g falls sharply, which costs no Eulerian energy. Across it, h is a logarithmic profile out to ΛR. The integral is E = −(v²/32π)(2R)(2π/ln Λ) = −v²R/(8 ln Λ).
   - That is below −v²R/12 whenever ln Λ > 1.5.
   - At a transverse extent of only 1 km (Λ = 10), E = −7.3e29 kg (0.37 M_sun), already below C2's "floor". At 1e8 m, E = −1.2e29 kg (a 10× cut). As Λ → ∞, E → 0, so no positive floor exists in this class. The approach is only logarithmic, though: a 100× cut needs a transverse extent of 10^67 m.

So "at least 1.12e30 kg" is correct only for spherical, unflattened profiles. It is not a general-relativistic floor for Alcubierre bubbles.

**Evidence check on the source branch (does not refute).** C2 says "No QI theorem exists" for interacting QFTs [U-09]. That is imprecise. Fewster–Hollands prove state-independent QEIs for a class of interacting fields, and Bostelmann–Fewster prove QIs in general interacting QFT. But Bostelmann–Fewster also state that state-independent bounds cannot be expected in general interacting theories. So the loophole C2 needs does exist in the literature, and the logical claim stands.

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle.** The logical claim holds.
  - Every thick-wall or flattened member needs its wall to be (D/Δ_QI)² ≈ 4e67 times thicker than the free-field bound allows at D = 100 m. So a source outside the free-field QI is indeed necessary.
  - Such matter is not excluded in principle. Bostelmann–Fewster, quoted above, say state-independent bounds are not expected in general interacting theories, and the nonminimally coupled scalar is a free field with averaged energy densities unbounded below.
  - Two caveats:
    - Meeting the condition is not sufficient. Horizons, no self-acceleration, the need to pre-lay the source, and the semiclassical instability [D-58, D-61, D-62] remain.
    - My pancake and Bobrick–Martire flattening both lower the Eulerian ρ only by moving the cost into ∂_x F, which appears in the momentum density and in K_xx stresses. Non-Eulerian observers see sharp walls along the direction of travel, and these meet the QI and ANEC problems more severely. The pancake also has |β| > 1 across most of its transverse extent, which means extended horizons.
- **In practice.** Nothing changes. The best pancake at a still-compact 1 km transverse extent needs 7.3e29 kg. A 10× cut needs a 3e8 m extent, and the Bobrick–Martire flattened optimum 1.1e28 kg is still about 5.9 Jupiter masses of negative energy. No source demonstrated or proposed this century approaches that, so the ~10^60 gap to Casimir scales stays.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **Survives:** "A thick-walled Alcubierre bubble at R = 100 m, v = 10c is viable in principle only if its wall is sourced by matter the free-field QI does not govern." C2's decisive test is still the right one: a QEI for the actual interacting or plasma source, evaluated at τ₀ ~ D/v.
- **Must be corrected:** "at least 1.12e30 kg" should read something like: "≥ 1.12e30 kg (0.56 M_sun) for spherical, unflattened profiles. Flattening along the direction of travel (≈ 1.1e28 kg at α_X = 101) and non-spherical transverse profiles (logarithmically toward 0) go lower in Eulerian energy, only by shifting stress into non-Eulerian components." The v²R scaling is not a universal floor.
- **Doubt:**
  - I did not compute the full stress tensor or the QI-relevant densities for the pancake along the direction of travel. That calculation could show that the "saving" is an artefact of the Eulerian slicing, as C2 itself argues for flattening.
  - I also did not verify whether Bobrick–Martire's E/α_X rule holds down to α_X = 101 with a physical interior. That rule is C2's premise, not mine.
