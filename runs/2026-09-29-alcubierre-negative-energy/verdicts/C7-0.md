# Verdict on C7 (refuter 0, angle: physics)
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation

## Strongest attack
The reframe's headline quantitative evidence is that zero expansion makes things *worse*: "Natário's is ~8e3 times worse at the reference case", growing as 0.8(R/Δ)². It explains this with the mechanism "a compact divergence-free shift must return the interior flux through the wall", and it discounts Lobo–Visser's "same v²R²σ scaling" [D-04] as resting on an assumption. The attack is that this (R/Δ)² penalty is not a property of zero expansion. It comes from the **compact support** of Natário's published example. A zero-expansion (div β = 0), unit-lapse, flat-slice, asymptotically flat shift that returns the flux through the whole exterior as potential flow around a sphere (a dipole exterior falling off as r⁻³) has the Alcubierre scaling v²R²/Δ, and its energy is about 13 times Alcubierre's at any R/Δ, not 3e4 times. So the candidate's own falsification clause — "Natário's thin-wall energy scales as v²R²σ, like Alcubierre [D-04]" — holds for the zero-expansion class as a whole. It fails only for the specific compact profile Natário happened to publish.

This does not overturn the reframe's thesis. A non-compact zero-expansion drive is still ≥ Alcubierre, so the drive is still no reduction. And the other legs survived my checks (Loup lapse, irrotational shift, QI-versus-thickness). What is lost is the claim that zero expansion carries an *intrinsic* (R/Δ)² penalty, together with the critique of D-04 built on it.

## Evidence
**1. Natário with compact vs non-compact support** [calc: runs/2026-09-29-alcubierre-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C7-0_natario_noncompact.py]. Geometric units (G = c = 1), v = 1, energies in metres.
- **The candidate's numbers reproduce.** For the tanh profile at R = 1 m (compact Natário), E_Alc = −0.2233, −0.4450, −0.8892 m and E_Nat = −3.288, −23.64, −183.8 m at σ = 8, 16, 32 m⁻¹. The ratios are 14.72, 53.12 and 206.7, which pass the first part of the candidate's decisive test.
- **Independent 3D K_ij quadrature check.** At R = 1 m, D = 0.25 m the two methods agree: E_3D = −38.12079 m against E_1D = −38.12094 m (compact), and −6.52205 against −6.52210 m (dipole). The divergence of β is 0 at the test points.
- **Same wall** (quintic smoothstep of full width D). Ratio to Alcubierre:

| R/D | compact Natário | dipole-exterior Natário |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 63.2 | 10.8 |
| 8 | 219 | 12.0 |
| 16 | 819 | 12.7 |
| 32 | 3171 | 13.1 |
| 100 | 30303 | 13.37 |

  The grid-doubling change is below 1.2e-8.
  - The compact ratio grows as ≈ 3(R/D)². This is the candidate's (R/Δ)² law; the coefficient differs from its 0.8 only through the profile and Δ convention.
  - The dipole ratio is flat. Its energy is ≈ −1.6 v²R²/D, i.e. the Alcubierre/Lobo–Visser scaling.
- **Reference case** (R = 100 m, D = 1 m, v = 10), SI:
  - Alcubierre: −1.62e32 kg (81 M_sun);
  - compact Natário: −4.91e36 kg (2.5e6 M_sun);
  - dipole Natário: −2.16e33 kg (1.09e3 M_sun).
- **The blend is not optimal.** The blend h = (1−S) + S(R/r)³ puts an O(1) bump into the tangential flow inside the wall. A pure vortex-sheet estimate for the dipole, ∫|ω|² with jump (3/2)v sinθ, gives ≈ −0.27 v²R²/D, i.e. ≈ 2.25× Alcubierre. The factor 13 is therefore an upper figure; the scaling is the robust result.
- **Why this works.** For div β = 0 and fall-off faster than r⁻¹, E_Eul = −(1/32π)∫|∇×β|² d³x (the candidate's own slice identity). Potential flow is curl-free outside the wall. Vorticity is therefore confined to a sheet of strength ~v across the wall thickness Δ, not to a backflow of ~vR/Δ.
- **What Natário published.** The published example is step-type [Natário 2002, arXiv gr-qc/0110086, ACCESS: full-text via fetch_text]: "X ∼ −vs(t)d [ f (r)r2 sin2 θdϕ ] … with f (r) = 1 2 for large r and f (r) = 0 for small r, we are guaranteed to have a divergenceless field". The dipole is f = ½(1 − R³/r³) outside, which is exactly Stokes potential flow past a sphere. It reaches ½ only asymptotically, so it lies in Natário's framework but not in his literal example.
- **Lobo–Visser's actual wording** [Lobo & Visser 2004, arXiv gr-qc/0406083, ACCESS: full-text via fetch_text]: "gradients of β in the bubble walls are of order vσ … So we can again estimate (as for the Alcubierre warp bubble) Mwarp ≈ −v2 R2 σ." The candidate is right that this is an assumption, and that the compact profile violates it. But the assumption is achievable within zero expansion, so D-04's "redistributes … does not remove or reduce its scale" stands for the class.

**2. Loup lapse, second half of the decisive test** [calc: runs/2026-09-29-alcubierre-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C7-0_nec_integral.py]. Output is in `falsifier-C7-0_nec_integral.out`. Parameters are the same as the lens: R = 1 m, σ = 8 m⁻¹, R_A = 1.6 m, σ_A = 4 m⁻¹.
- **Analytic result for the lapse hill alone** (v = 0, static −A²dt² + dx²):
  - The worst NEC density is (sum of the two smallest Hessian eigenvalues of A)/(8πA).
  - The worst-null NEC integral is −2.98, −5.65 and −10.5 m at A₀ = 3, 10 and 100.
  - The worst density is −0.325 m⁻² at A₀ = 10. The lens's 64-direction sample gave −0.314 m⁻², which is consistent.
- **Full metric, numerical.** The integral uses gr_tensors `from_adm` with 64 sampled null directions per point, on an axisymmetric grid over |x| ≤ 3 m and s ≤ 3 m, at h = 0.05 m and 0.035 m. Grid changes are ≤ 0.1%.

| case | worst-null NEC integral (m) | E_Eul (m) | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alcubierre (A₀ = 1) | −4.103 | −0.22334 | 18.4 |
| Loup A₀ = 3 | −3.989 | −0.02516 | 158.5 |

  The lapse-alone figure agrees with the analytic −5.65 m to within 7%, the gap coming from direction sampling. The grid reproduces the lens's E_Eul to 5 digits.
- **Comparison.** From A₀ = 1 to 10 the Eulerian total falls by 98×, while the worst-null NEC integral *rises* by 1.37×, almost all of it from the v-independent lapse hill. The candidate's prediction "the NEC integral does not fall in proportion to E_Eul" is confirmed for the lapse.
- **Why it cannot be designed away.** Wherever A has a maximum, its Hessian is negative-definite, so the NEC fails there. The analytic integrated deficit grows with A₀ (−2.98 → −10.5 m from A₀ = 3 to 100).

**3. The irrotational case is trivially consistent.** E_Eul = 0 exactly for a compact curl-free shift, by the slice identity, while the energy conditions fail pointwise (lens calc). No NEC integral of 0 is possible there.

**3b. The flattening claim checks out analytically.** For the Alcubierre shift β = (v f, 0, 0) (my own algebra):
- K² = K_xx², while K_ijK^ij = K_xx² + 2(K_xy² + K_xz²).
- K_xx therefore cancels from 16πρ = K² − K_ijK^ij, leaving ρ = −(v²/32π)(f_y² + f_z²).
- Along-track compression raises f_x and K_xx, which enter T_ij but not ρ_Eul. This is consistent with the candidate's "moves stress into K_xx".

**4. QI supply versus thick walls.**
- Demand/allowed ∝ v²Δ²/L_P², so (Δ/Δ_max)² = (1 m / 1.6e-32 m)² = 3.9e63 = 10^63.6. This matches the candidate.
- Thickening the wall lowers the total but raises the free-field-QI mismatch quadratically, so the "mutually exclusive" leg holds under the free-field QI.

**5. Minor internal inconsistency.** The candidate lists Natário among "published reductions", but the dossier's own [U-10] says "No energy-reduction claim was found for it." Natário is therefore a strawman rather than a relabelled reduction.

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle.** Zero expansion neither helps nor intrinsically hurts. With a non-compact exterior it costs O(1–10)× Alcubierre with the same v²R²/Δ law. So the "relocate, don't remove" thesis holds, but the (R/Δ)² penalty is a design choice, not a law.
- **In practice.** Nothing changes. Every variant still needs ≥ 1e32 kg of exotic stress at the reference case, even with classical exotic matter, and ~1e63 kg or more under the free-field QI.

## What survives and remaining doubt
**Survives:**
- Totals are not invariant and can be zeroed (irrotational), relabelled (the lapse's A₀⁻²) or moved into K_xx (flattening).
- Local worst-null NEC and QI-sampled density are the binding quantities.
- Under the free-field QI, thick walls and QI-bound supply are mutually exclusive.
- The lapse NEC integral does not fall with E_Eul; it grows.

**Narrower form required.** Replace "Natário's is ~8e3 times worse" with: "Natário's *published compact* profile is (R/Δ)²-penalised (~8e3× at R/Δ = 100). A non-compact zero-expansion shift restores the Alcubierre v²R²/Δ scaling at ≥ ~2–13× Alcubierre's energy, so zero expansion is never a reduction." Also drop the challenge to D-04's scaling at the class level.

**Remaining doubt:**
- The dipole drive has K_ij ≠ 0 in the whole exterior (Eulerian ρ < 0 falling off as r⁻⁸, total ≈ −(3/8)v²R). It therefore sits outside the "Minkowski exterior" class of Santiago et al. [D-51], though it is asymptotically flat. Whether that exterior spoils the horizon and causality picture was not checked.
- Some cases were not computed:
  - The worst-null NEC integral was computed for Loup only at one parameter set (R = 1 m, σ = 8 m⁻¹, v = 1), and not for the irrotational metric. There E_Eul = 0, so the prediction holds trivially.
  - The dipole-exterior Natário drive's local NEC was not computed.
- **Local measures can improve.** By the candidate's own local figure of merit, Rodal 2026 reports a genuine local improvement [D-28]: a "peak proper-energy deficit about 38× below Alcubierre" (dossier summary, not reproduced). So "without removing" is accurate, but "relocate or relabel" understates it: at least one published variant claims a real reduction in a local measure.
