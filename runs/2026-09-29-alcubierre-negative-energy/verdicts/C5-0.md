# Verdict on C5 (refuter 0, angle: physics, with supporting literature)
verdict: refuted
basis: calculation

## Strongest attack
C5 says demonstrated lab negative energy can be **scaled, concentrated and separated from its positive-energy source** into a warp-wall supply. It names three phenomena: Casimir cavities, squeezed vacuum and "negative effective mass". Each fails for a different reason, and each failure rests on a calculation I ran or on a source's own words.

1. **Casimir cavities are net positive at every gap. C5's own decisive test comes out on the eliminating branch.**
   - **Setup.** C5 asks for "the per-cell energy balance (plate rest energy plus ideal Casimir deficit) for the thinnest physical plate (one atom, ~0.3 nm)". I computed it [calc: runs/2026-09-29-alcubierre-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C5-0_lab_sources.py].
   - **Why the ideal formula is an upper bound.** The ideal perfect-conductor energy is the largest the deficit can be for any passive plate material. In the Lifshitz formula each term ln(1 − r²e^(−2qa)) grows in magnitude with r², and r² ≤ 1 on the imaginary-frequency axis.
   - **Graphene, a = 0.335 nm (SI).**
     - Plate rest energy: 6.84e10 J/m². Ideal Casimir deficit: 11.5 J/m².
     - The plate outweighs the deficit **5.9e9 times**.
     - An infinite stack has a net density of **+1.0e20 J/m³**.
   - **Wider gaps are worse:**

     | Gap | Plate / deficit |
     |---|---|
     | 1 nm | 1.6e11 |
     | 20 nm | 1.3e15 |
     | 100 nm | 1.6e17 |
     | Gold film, 100 nm thick, at a 100 nm gap | 4.0e20 |

   - **A scale-free bound covers every gap, however small** [calc: runs/2026-09-29-alcubierre-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C5-0_mirror_cost.py].
     - **Most reflective medium.** By the f-sum rule, ε(iξ) − 1 ≤ ω_p²/ξ² for any passive medium, so a lossless plasma slab is the most reflective plate for a given ω_p.
     - **Energy cost of a given ω_p.** The charges that set ω_p are electrons, the lightest charge carriers. For a degenerate gas, ω_p ≤ 0.0557 c k_F in every regime. Its kinetic energy density is therefore at least ħc k_F⁴/(4π²).
     - **What I solved.** I computed the Lifshitz energy of two plasma slabs of any thickness, requiring at least one electron per a² of plate area (below that the plate is not a continuum mirror at scale a). I counted only the electrons' kinetic energy, not their rest mass or the ions' rest mass.
     - **Result.** Every plate still carries **at least 3.5e3 times** the Casimir deficit it produces.
     - **Numerical checks.** The ideal limit reproduces −π²/720 ħc/a³ exactly, and the result is converged to 7 digits.
   - **Consequence.** No choice of scale or gap makes a volume that includes its own mirrors net negative. "Scaling" and "concentrating" Casimir cells only add positive energy.
   - **The wall's density needs a sub-nuclear gap.**
     - The Δ = 1 m, 10c wall's peak |ρ| of 1.2e44 J/m³ needs an ideal gap a = 1.38e-18 m. That is about 600 times smaller than the proton charge radius (8.4e-16 m).
     - Holding the Δ = 1 m, 10c budget |E_−| = 7.5e31 kg [D-17] in even the best graphene cells needs 5.8e47 m² of plates. They weigh **4.5e41 kg (2.2e11 M_sun)**, with a Schwarzschild radius of 6.6e14 m.
     - So the "supply" is a galaxy-mass positive source. In Eulerian energy it outweighs the bubble's own demand by 6e9.

2. **Casimir negative energy cannot be separated from its plates (literature).**
   - Graham & Olum 2005 tested one route by which a null ray could sample Casimir negative energy without crossing a plate: a hole. They found that ANEC holds. This is one worked example (Dirichlet and Neumann scalar models), not a general theorem.
   - The negative density exists only between boundaries whose own stress-energy outweighs it (point 1).

3. **Squeezed vacuum is excluded by a theorem that applies in exactly this domain.**
   - **Why the QI applies here.** Squeezed light is a free electromagnetic field in essentially flat space. For such a field the Ford–Roman quantum inequality (QI) is a proven bound [D-54], not an extrapolation.
   - **The bound.** For Lorentzian sampling it reads ρ̂ ≥ −3ħ/(16π²c³τ⁴) (SI; the EM field counts as two scalars).
   - **Wall case, Δ = 1 m at 10c.** Observers at rest outside the bubble see the wall pass in τ = Δ/(v c) = 3.3e-10 s. The QI then allows at most |ρ| = 6.0e-24 J/m³, against a demand of 1.2e44 J/m³: **10^67.3 too small**.
   - **Is the flat-space bound valid there?** Yes: cτ = 0.1 m, which is less than the ~1 m curvature scale, so the flat-space QI's assumptions roughly hold. With τ₀ = 0.1 Δ/(v c) the shortfall is still 10^63.3. This matches the examiner's 10^63.6 [calc: runs/2026-09-29-alcubierre-negative-energy/calc/lens-examiner_premises.py].
   - **Duration.** The demanded density is allowed only for τ ≤ 5.0e-27 s. Over the 1.38e7 s cruise the shortfall is 10^133.8.
   - **The candidate's supporting evidence does not help.**
     - Riek et al. measured sub-vacuum *field noise*, not an absolute energy density.
     - Maclay–Davis [D-46] found tension with a *proposed* squeezed-light QI, not with the proven Ford–Roman bound. They explicitly do not claim large negative energy.

4. **"Negative effective mass" is not negative energy.**
   - Khamehchi et al.'s own abstract defines it as a property of the dispersion relation. The atoms have positive rest mass.
   - For ⁸⁷Rb at n ~ 1e20 m⁻³, T_00 ≈ n m c² = +1.3e12 J/m³. The spin-orbit band energy (~h × 1 kHz per atom) is 5e-23 of the rest energy, so it cannot change the sign.
   - So this medium supplies no GR-sense negative energy at all.

**Checking my own assumptions:**
- The Lifshitz bound assumes passive, causal media in equilibrium. Driven, non-equilibrium states are squeezed or excited field states, and point 3 covers those.
- The scale-free mirror bound ignores all rest mass. It is conservative by many orders of magnitude at any gap above ħ/(m_e c) = 3.9e-13 m, where rest mass dominates. That is why graphene gives 5.9e9 rather than 3.5e3.
- The "one electron per a²" condition is a necessary condition for a continuum mirror, not a theorem. Without it, the slab-model minimum drifts toward a 2D-sheet limit whose electrons are about 45a apart. That is outside the model's validity, not a physical escape.
- I considered only electromagnetic Casimir set-ups, because those are the ones demonstrated. C5 claims only demonstrated phenomena.

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle (GR + semiclassical QFT):**
  - **Casimir:** fails as a net source. Any region that contains its mirrors is net positive, by at least 3.5e3 for any electron-plasma mirror at any gap (electron kinetic energy alone) and by at least 5.9e9 for atomic matter. Averaged over any wall scale Δ ≫ a, a layered Casimir wall has positive T_00. The Graham–Olum ANEC result closes the one null-ray "separation" route that has been computed.
  - **Squeezed vacuum:** forbidden by the proven free-field EM QI. That theorem's assumptions actually hold here: a free field, and at Δ = 1 m, 10c a sampling length (0.1 m) below the curvature scale.
  - **Negative effective mass:** T_00 > 0, so it supplies nothing.
  - So C5 fails in principle as stated. It does not merely fail in practice.
- **In practice (by 2100):**
  - **TRL.** Lab negative energy sits at about TRL 1 as a propulsion supply: it has been observed only as local densities (Casimir forces; sub-vacuum field noise), never as net or extractable energy.
  - **Density shortfall.** The best graphene cell reaches a gap density of −3.4e10 J/m³, 33.5 orders short of the wall's 1.2e44 J/m³ peak.
  - **Mass cost.** Holding the Δ = 1 m, 10c budget would take 2.2e11 M_sun of plates.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives:**
  - Lab systems do produce *locally* negative energy density: Casimir gaps and sub-vacuum squeezed intervals.
  - Casimir negative energy is gravitationally relevant in principle [D-08].
  - That is a statement about local densities. It is not the claim that lab negative energy can be scaled, concentrated and separated into a wall supply.
- **Remaining doubts:**
  - **Scale-free bound.** It assumes electrons are the reflecting charges, and it uses the "at least one electron per a²" continuum condition. Exotic boundaries at sub-nuclear gaps, such as hadronic bag or gluon-field Casimir effects, were not treated. They are not demonstrated lab phenomena, so they are outside C5.
  - **Casimir energy is never sampled by an inertial observer over long times without boundaries.** The proven QIs do not bind Casimir set-ups directly. That is why the Casimir attack rests on the per-cell energy balance, not on the QI.
  - **The squeezed-light QI mismatch [D-46]** is unresolved. It involves a proposed bound on idealised OPA models. Even taken at face value, it does not approach the 10^63 shortfall against the proven Ford–Roman bound.
  - **Source quality.** The BEC density (n ~ 1e20 m⁻³) is a typical order of magnitude, not taken from the paper. The sign of T_00 does not depend on it.
