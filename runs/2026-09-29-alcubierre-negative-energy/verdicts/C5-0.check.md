| Q8 Sopova & Ford 2002 | PRD 66, 045026; quote "We find a positive energy density of the electromagnetic field which diverges at the interface despite the inclusion of dispersion in the calculation." | verified | Exact words in the lit_search (INSPIRE) abstract. |
| Q9 Sopova & Ford 2002 | Used to say that "next to real dielectric boundaries the field energy itself turns positive" | misattributed | The abstract's positive, divergent term is for a single half-space. For two half-spaces it says the energy density is a negative Casimir term plus a positive position-dependent term, and "in some cases, including physically realizable ones, the negative term can dominate in a given region ... so the overall energy density can be negative in this region." The paper therefore supports a positive boundary-layer term, not a net-positive gap. The verdict flags the item "supporting only", but the paraphrase omits the abstract's own caveat that the gap region can stay net negative. |
| Q10 Maclay & Davis 2019 | [D-46]: they "found tension with a *proposed* squeezed-light QI, not with the proven Ford-Roman bound" | verified | Abstract: "We test a proposed QI for squeezed light"; the QI tested is Marecki's (Appendix A: "Derivation of Marecki's Quantum Inequality"), not the Ford bound. The paper says the results "bring into question the basis for QI" (p. 1) and that the discrepancy awaits resolution. Wording is stronger than "tension", but the direction matches. Foundations of Physics 49, 797 (2019). |
| Q11 Maclay & Davis 2019 | "They explicitly do not claim large negative energy." | unverifiable | grep for "do not claim" found nothing. The paper's wording on this point was not located. The abstract and introduction frame the work as a QI test, not a claim of large negative energy, but I did not find a sentence saying so. |
| Q12 Maclay & Davis 2019 vs Ford | The free-field bound ρ̂ ≥ -3ħ/(16π²c³τ⁴) for Lorentzian sampling | verified | Maclay & Davis eq. 1 gives ρ̂ ≥ -(3/16π²) ħc/(cτ₀)⁴ for the EM field in flat spacetime with the Lorentzian sampling function, attributed to Ford ("Ford has shown [6]"). Same expression as the verdict's SI form. The "EM field counts as two scalars" remark is the verdict's own. |
| Q13 Riek et al. | "Riek et al. measured sub-vacuum *field noise*, not an absolute energy density." | unverifiable | The only Riek paper found was Science 2015 ("Direct sampling of electric-field vacuum fluctuations"), whose abstract reports direct detection of vacuum fluctuations and says nothing about sub-vacuum noise. Maclay & Davis [5] cite Riek et al. for probing "the spectrum of squeezed vacuum fluctuations of the electric field". The sub-vacuum claim most likely refers to a later Riek et al. paper (squeezed vacuum, 2017), which the verdict does not cite. Needs a full citation. The "not an absolute energy density" reading is the verdict's own. |
| Q14 Ford QI as "proven" | The Ford-Roman QI is a proven bound for free fields in flat space | unverifiable | Rests on dossier [D-54], not checked. Maclay & Davis say only "Ford has shown" it for the EM field in flat spacetime and that such QIs are "derived for the free vacuum quantum electromagnetic field, with no sources or boundaries". That agrees with the verdict's scope. |
| Q15 Maclay & Davis 2019 | Context for the Casimir argument (not cited by the verdict) | verified (extra) | Their p. 1 says "the sum of the mass energy of the plates plus the negative vacuum energy inside the cavity is positive [1][2]". This independently supports point 1 of the verdict. |

## Most serious problems
- Q9: the Sopova & Ford paraphrase leaves out that their abstract says the gap between two dielectric half-spaces can still be net negative. It is flagged "supporting only", so the verdict's argument does not depend on it.
- Q13: the Riek et al. citation is incomplete, and the one Riek paper found does not match the "sub-vacuum" description.
- Q11: the claim that Maclay & Davis "explicitly" disclaim large negative energy could not be located in the text.
