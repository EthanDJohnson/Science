- [RC-03] CLAIM: Gibbons (1975) proves no pair production for a quantized MASSIVE scalar field on a c-number plane SANDWICH wave of arbitrary profile and polarization, and that the renormalized vacuum <T_mu nu> vanishes, so that a GW "far from its source" is unhindered by quantum effects. Scope limits that matter for B&O: (i) massive scalar, not massless photons; (ii) fixed classical (c-number) background, so no back-reaction and no graviton-number statistics; (iii) exact plane wave, for which the second-order metric correction sourced by the wave's own stress is absent. It is a no-go for single plane waves, not for a finite-aperture or source-attached wave.
  SOURCE: Gibbons 1975, Quantized Fields Propagating in Plane Wave Space-Times, Commun. Math. Phys. 45, 191, doi:10.1007/BF01629249 (INSPIRE 105138)
  QUOTE: "It is shown that the time varying gravitational field of the wave produces no particles and the Feynman propagator for the problem is calculated exactly. This is used to show that any reasonable regularization of the vacuum expectation value of the energy momentum tensor of the field must vanish. This means that a gravitational wave far from its source will propagate without hindrance by quantum effects."
  ACCESS: abstract
  STATUS: peer-reviewed
  CONFIDENCE: high for the abstract's content; the scope items (ii)-(iii) are my reading of "c-number" and "plane sandwich wave", to be checked by the theory facet

- [RC-04] CLAIM: Conflicting results exist within the plane-wave literature itself. Garriga & Verdaguer (1991) get a Bogoliubov beta proportional to delta(k_v + l_v) (v = null coordinate along the wave), which vanishes for massive quanta but can be non-zero for MASSLESS quanta exactly collinear with the wave; the Green-function / DeWitt-Schwinger route gives no production. Khusnutdinov (Eur. Phys. J. Plus 141, 532, 2026) computes the Maxwell (vector) Green function by three methods, finds exact agreement with the DeWitt-Schwinger expansion ("no massless particles production"), and states the tension with the Bogoliubov route is unresolved (he suggests vacuum ambiguity, Fulling). This is the one place where a published result could support production by a plane wave, and it concerns exactly collinear (measure-zero) modes, i.e. the corner where a 1 -> 2 massless decay would live.
  SOURCE: Khusnutdinov 2026, Quest for particles production in the plane gravitational wave spacetime, Eur. Phys. J. Plus 141, 532, arXiv:2508.11558, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.11558 ; Garriga & Verdaguer 1991, Phys. Rev. D 43, 391 (INSPIRE 299894)
  QUOTE: "The results coincide exactly with the DeWitt–Schwinger expansion, indicating no massless particles production in this background. Contrasting interpretations, based on Bogolyubov coefficient calculations predicting massless particle creation, are also addressed." and "For massive particles, kv+lv>0, ensuring β=0; for massless particles, the sum can vanish, allowing β≠0." and "The standard method based on the Green-function formalism [8] predicts no particle creation, whereas the Bogolyubov approach suggests a flux of photons in the direction of the GW. The aim of this paper is to draw the attention of the physics community to this unresolved issue."
  ACCESS: full-text (Khusnutdinov); Garriga-Verdaguer via Khusnutdinov's account and its INSPIRE abstract: "The cross section for scattering of the quantum particles by the waves is shown to coincide with the classical cross section for scattering of geodesics."
  STATUS: peer-reviewed (Khusnutdinov: EPJ Plus; Garriga-Verdaguer: PRD)
  CONFIDENCE: medium-high

- [RC-05] CLAIM: Jones, Singleton and collaborators made the earliest published claim of the same physical effect, without coherent-state language and without the N^2 mechanism: "G+G -> gamma+gamma or G -> gamma+gamma+G" attenuation of gravitational waves (2015 essay), then (2017, PRD) a massless scalar in a plane-wave background acquires a non-zero vacuum expectation value and current, interpreted as production at the expense of the wave. Their own number is the OPPOSITE conclusion from B&O's for propagation: the attenuation length is astronomically long unless the strain is huge, so the effect is "significant only close to the source". Their Table I (omega ~ 300 Hz, k ~ 1e-6 1/m, SI) lists decay lengths of 1e89 m for h=1e-21, 1e65 m for 1e-15, 1e41 m for 1e-9, 1e25 m for 1e-5, 1e17 m for 1e-3 (the observable universe is ~1e27 m). I could not reconcile the table with the garbled printed formula Lambda = 15/(2k) (h0)^-4 (the formula gives ~7e26 m, not 1e25 m, at h=1e-5), so use the Lambda proportional to h^-4 scaling and the text, not the table digits. B&O do not cite these papers (grep for "Jones" in B&O v2 finds no match) while claiming their calculation "supersedes previous semiclassical calculations". The 2017 method (VEV of a field in a plane-wave background) is contradicted for plane waves by Gibbons and by Khusnutdinov's Green-function result (RC-03, RC-04).
  SOURCE: Jones, Singleton et al. 2015, Gravitons to photons - Attenuation of gravitational waves, Int. J. Mod. Phys.   QUOTE: "The take away message from Table I is that the conversion of the gravitational background wave into the massless field is significant only close to the source of the gravitational waves where h+ is large." and "Unless h+ > 10−5 our estimate for the decay length, Λ, given in Table I, was so large that one would not expect this process to weaken the gravitational" [wave; sentence cut at the page break]. - [RC-06] CLAIM: Dorca & Verdaguer (1993) show a case where an exact-GW background does create quanta: head-on collision of two plane waves that focus into a Killing-Cauchy horizon. The "in" vacuum contains "out" particles with number proportional to the inverse frequency, thermal in the long-wavelength limit at a temperature inversely proportional to the focusing time. This is a genuine non-collinear (two-wave, horizon-forming) production, but it is a scalar field, an exact spacetime with a horizon, and the particle count is infrared dominated; it does not map onto a single outgoing wave from a compact source and gives no rate of the form of B&O's eq. 8. B&O cite it only as an "other limit".
  SOURCE: Dorca & Verdaguer 1993, Quantum fields interacting with colliding plane waves: particle creation, Nucl. Phys. B 403, 770, doi:10.1016/0550-3213(93)90370-5 (INSPIRE 34044)
  QUOTE: "It is found that the “in” vacuum contains a number of “out” particles which is proportional to the inverse of the frequency. In the long-wavelength limit the spectrum is consistent with a thermal spectrum at a temperature which is inversely proportional to the focusing time of the gravitational plane waves."
  ACCESS: abstract
  STATUS: peer-reviewed
  CONFIDENCE: high for content; relevance to B&O medium

- [RC-07] CLAIM: A 2026 objection-by-structure comes from the very paper B&O cite as an "other limit" (Redi & Tesi, arXiv:2606.28297, a preprint, 3 citations on INSPIRE). They show that the particle yield from a gravitating source is controlled by the unequal-time two-point function of the stress-energy tensor on its TIME-LIKE support (q^2 > 0), whereas gravitational-wave emission is controlled by the same object on the LIGHT-LIKE support (q^2 = 0); and that on sub-horizon scales the production "practically reduces to gravitational scattering". Consequence for B&O: pair production needs off-shell (time-like) components of the second-order metric h^(2) ~ h h, which exist only where the waves are not collinear, i.e. near the source or in crossing beams; the on-shell (q^2 = 0) part of a purely outgoing wave carries none. This is a general-structure argument, not a refutation of eq. 8 within its model; the theory facet should check whether B&O's coherent amplitude, summed over a spherical packet, has any time-like support at r >> lambda.
  SOURCE: Redi & Tesi 2026, Particle Production from Inhomogeneities: the off-shell side of gravitational waves, arXiv:2606.28297, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.28297
  QUOTE: "While GW production depends on the two-point function in the light-like region of momenta, qμqμ = 0, particle production is determined by the time-like region, qμqμ > 0, corresponding for elementary particles to real production of particle pairs." and (abstract) "Our results show that, on sub-horizon scales, particle production from inhomogeneous metric backgrounds practically reduces to gravitational scattering."
  ACCESS: full-text
  STATUS: preprint
  CONFIDENCE: medium (setting is cosmological inhomogeneities, not a flat-space binary; the inference to B&O is mine)

- [RC-11] CLAIM: Analogous 'transparency' arguments show Bose-enhancement can cancel against absorption, a check on B&O's stimulated factor (1 + 2 f_gamma) and on 'N^2' growth. Flauger & Weinberg (2019): inverse-bremsstrahlung absorption of GWs is nearly cancelled by stimulated emission, so the ionized gas in galaxy clusters does not block nanohertz GWs. Ai, Ellis & Pradler (2025 preprint): for the converse problem (GW background acting on matter), leading-order real emission and absorption seem to enhance decay rates, but resummation shows "inclusive decay rates remain essentially unchanged": Bose-enhanced emission and absorption "compensate each other to all orders". Scope: these concern matter coupled to a GW background through soft-graviton emission, not g g -> gamma gamma, so they are an analogy and a warning, not a rebuttal. B&O cite both papers.
  SOURCE: Flauger & Weinberg 2019, Absorption of Gravitational Waves from Distant Sources, Phys. Rev. D 99, 123030, arXiv:1906.04853 ; Ai, Ellis & Pradler 2025, Soft Gravitons, Hard Truths: Infrared Safety of Particle Processes in a Gravitational-Wave Background, arXiv:2510.27690
  QUOTE: Flauger-Weinberg abstract: "Nevertheless, because of the near cancellation of absorption by stimulated emission, the ionized gas in galaxy clusters does not block gravitational waves at the nanohertz frequencies that may be detected by the use of pulsar timing observations." Ai et al. abstract: "we show that inclusive decay rates remain essentially unchanged. Bose-enhanced emission and absorption in graviton spectra persist but compensate each other to all orders; the mutual transparency between matter and gravitational radiation hence follows from infrared safety."
  ACCESS: abstract
  STATUS: peer-reviewed (Flauger-Weinberg); preprint (Ai et al.)
  CONFIDENCE: medium (analogy only)

- [RC-12] CLAIM: Gertsenshtein-type graviton-photon conversion is a different, background-dependent process and cannot be cited as support for vacuum decay. It needs an external magnetic (or plasma) field: e.g. Palessandro (CQG 41, 215011, 2024) frames it as "firing [a graviton] through a region filled with a constant magnetic field that enables its conversion to a photon", finds a single-graviton Gertsenshtein detector is generically inefficient (probability of detection << 1), and efficient only on astrophysical scales for futuristic Hz-kHz single-photon detectors. A parametric-resonance variant (arXiv:2205.08767) removes the need for a strong background magnetic field but requires a plasma, with condition m_plasma^2 < (1 - c_s^2) omega^2/4; this is a medium effect, consistent with B&O's own remark that decays in a medium differ. Any such flux, where relevant, scales linearly with the GW energy flux and with B^2 L^2, not as G^2 N^2 sigma^2.
  SOURCE: Palessandro 2024, Graviton-photon oscillations as a probe of quantum gravity, Class. Quantum Grav. 41, 215011, arXiv:2405.01407 ; arXiv:2205.08767 (Graviton to Photon Conversion via Parametric Resonance), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.08767
  QUOTE: Palessandro abstract: "The Gertsenshtein effect could in principle be used to detect a single graviton by firing it through a region filled with a constant magnetic field that enables its conversion to a photon, which can be efficiently detected via standard techniques." 2205.08767 (p. 5): "Our analysis fits nicely into this area providing a new conversion process with the same order of magnitude for the growth parameter as for the three wave interaction [24], without requiring a strong background magnetic field to exist, provided that the plasma mass is sufficiently small compared to the frequency of the gravitational wave, m2plasma < (1−c2s)ω2/4."
  ACCESS: abstract (Palessandro); full-text (2205.08767)
  STATUS: peer-reviewed (Palessandro); preprint (2205.08767; I did not check publication status)
  CONFIDENCE: high for the distinction; medium for the linear-scaling remark (standard Gertsenshtein result, not quoted here)

- [RC-15] CLAIM: Classical-limit objection (premise 10). B&O's gamma emission is linear in hbar only through the photon energy ("Being linear in hbar means that the quantumness of this number comes from the energy of photons, confirming the semiclassical origin for the gravitational field"), and they write that with a CMB background "all the hbar disappear, and our calculation corresponds to a classical process", yet they also say a classical decay is impossible in pure vacuum because photons couple quadratically. The two statements are consistent only if the 'classical process' means 'classical stimulated amplification of an existing photon field by the second-order GW metric', i.e. parametric amplification of a pre-existing (CMB) photon field; in pure vacuum the photon number is driven by vacuum fluctuations (zero point), which is the part that is quantum. This is a distinction B&O blur and the theory/quantitative facets should price separately: the stimulated part ~ 2 f_gamma times the spontaneous one.
  SOURCE: Blas & Oller 2026, arXiv:2607.24930v2, pp. 1 and 3, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24930
  QUOTE: "In the presence of a background, one can see that all the ℏ disappear, and our calculation corresponds to a classical process." and "Being linear in ℏ means that the quantumness of this number comes from the energy of photons, confirming the semiclassical origin for the gravitational field."
  ACCESS: full-text
  STATUS: preprint
  CONFIDENCE: medium (the reading of the two statements is mine)
