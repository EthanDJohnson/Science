## Strongest attack
On the scale angle, the claim fails if some allowed background, or some epoch, makes the eq. 14 rate matter. "Matter" means an O(1) fraction of the GW energy converted per Hubble time, or a signal within reach of a planned instrument. Two routes were tried:

1. **Raise rho_GW.** The rate is (8 pi/15) G^2 rho_GW^2 (natural units) and does not depend on the spectrum, so only rho_GW matters. The largest rho_GW allowed today is set by DeltaN_eff.
2. **Go to earlier epochs.** Hotter, denser epochs give larger rates, so the question is where the conversion fraction per Hubble time first reaches O(1).

I also looked for an internal inconsistency in "exactly", and checked the "only in multi-directional ensembles" clause against a beam with finite angular spread.

Neither route works. The only place the conversion fraction per Hubble time reaches O(1) is T of about 0.4–0.9 M_Pl. That is the standard graviton-decoupling regime, where tree-level EFT is not valid. Every allowed background below that scale falls short by tens of orders of magnitude or more.

- **Maximum allowed rate today (SI).** At DeltaN_eff = 0.3, rho_GW = 2.84e-15 J/m^3 and Gamma = 2.78e-109 pairs m^-3 s^-1 (log [1]). This reproduces the candidate's numbers and M-CONSTRAINTS-02.
- **Whole observable universe (SI).** The Hubble volume is 1.08e79 m^3 and the Hubble time 4.58e17 s. Over these, at the DeltaN_eff = 0.3 maximum, the yield is:
  - 1.4e-12 photon pairs in total, spontaneous (log [2]);
  - 6.3e-3 pairs with the 25 Hz CMB stimulation factor of 4.5e9. That is 9.4e-25 J against 3.1e64 J of GW energy in the volume.

  Fewer than one pair forms in the observable universe per Hubble time. This ignores redshift dilution and plasma, both of which lower it further.
- **Radiation era (natural units), conversion fraction per Hubble time.** The fraction is F = Gamma x 4T/(rho_GW H). The upper bound of 4T energy per pair assumes Rayleigh–Jeans stimulation. F reduces to (32 pi/15) x sqrt(pi g*/80) (T/M_Pl)^3, where x = rho_GW/rho_tot; the closed form matches the direct evaluation to 7 digits (log [3]).
  - With g* = 106.75 and x = 0.105 (DeltaN_eff = 0.3 propagated back through g_s), F is:
    - 2.2e-9 at the maximum reheating temperature allowed by inflation, 1.4e16 GeV;
    - 7.9e-4 at 1e18 GeV.
  - F reaches 1 only at T = 0.885 M_Pl (x = 0.105), or 0.418 M_Pl even for x = 1.
  - This T^5/M_Pl^4 rate is the familiar graviton-decoupling scaling, Gamma/H ~ (T/M_Pl)^3. It is O(1) only where the tree-level amplitude, with s ~ M_Pl^2, is no longer trustworthy.
- **Required vs achievable for the CMB (dimensionless).** Take the cosmic bound Drho/rho <= 4.3e-79 at r = rho_GW/rho_gamma = 0.0681 [M-CONSTRAINTS-03, verified], which scales as r^2. Reaching PIXIE's sigma(mu) ~ 3e-8 [D-36] would need r = 1.5e34. Reaching FIRAS would need r = 8.3e35. The allowed value is 0.0681 (log [4]). Those required values would put more energy in gravitational waves than in all other radiation by some 34 orders of magnitude, which is impossible by construction.
- **The collimated corner (dimensionless).** A beam with angular spread dth has its rate suppressed relative to isotropic by about (dth^4/4)/(4/3): 1.9e-5 at 0.1 rad and 1.9e-13 at 1e-3 rad (log [5]). This rests on the verified theta^4 law [M-DIALECTICIAN-10's corrected form]. A real beam is therefore weakly "multi-directional" and its rate is non-zero but smaller still. The clause "only in multi-directional ensembles" stands, with the understanding that any finite angular spread counts as an ensemble.

## Corrections to details
- **"Vanishes for a collimated background"** (predictions) holds only for perfect collimation. A beam of finite spread dth keeps a rate suppressed by about dth^4/5 relative to isotropic, as shown above.
- **"Negligibly small"** should carry its domain: negligible for every T well below M_Pl. At T ~ M_Pl the same formula gives F = O(1), which is graviton decoupling and lies outside the tree-level EFT. This does not affect any late-universe consequence.
- **"Exactly"** is true of the value, not the method. B&O's eq. 14 comes from an effective-volume substitution into the coherent formula (eq. 8), not from a kinetic derivation. The two agree identically.
- **The BBN entry in my own log** (log [3], 7.9e-67 at 1 MeV) reuses g* = 106.75 and x = 0.105 and is order-of-magnitude only. With g* = 10.75 it changes by an O(1) factor. No conclusion depends on it.

## What survives and remaining doubt
The core stands at every scale an allowed background can reach:
- Eq. 14 equals the kinetic rate exactly.
- The rate is fixed by rho_GW alone, and rho_GW is capped by DeltaN_eff.
- Under that cap the yield is below one photon pair per Hubble volume per Hubble time today.
- The conversion fraction reaches O(1) only at Planckian temperatures.

The plasma cutoff, w < w_p, only closes the channel further: about 4 Hz in today's IGM, and kHz present-day frequencies in the mu era [M-CONSTRAINTS-09].

Remaining doubts sit outside the scale angle:
- Whether a phase-locked, non-random-phase multi-mode state could change the coefficient by an O(1) factor. The coherent-to-kinetic ratios 0.982 and 0.931 are unverified [M-IDEALIZER-14]. A factor of order 2 would not alter "negligibly small" by any meaningful amount.
- The cross sections are toolkit inputs, checked only through the verified amplitudes [M-MECHANIST-01].
