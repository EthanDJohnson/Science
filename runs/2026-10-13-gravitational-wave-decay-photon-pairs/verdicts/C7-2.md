# Verdict on C7 (refuter 2, angle: scale)
claim: The 'N^2 coherent enhancement' is classical second-order field nonlinearity driving photon-vacuum pair creation, not a quantum decay of gravitons; the pair number is hbar-independent.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: B&O's N^2 yield is the hbar-independent, second-order background-field (semiclassical) creation of photon pairs from the quantised Maxwell vacuum by the classical wave. It needs no graviton quantisation, but it is not pair creation on the second-order metric h^(2) alone: the contact term, the t/u photon exchange (iterated scattering on h^(1)) and the s-channel h^(2) term are gauge invariant only as a sum, and the s-channel alone is wrong by factors from 0 to infinity.

## Strongest attack
C7 identifies the mechanism as "photon-vacuum pair creation on the second-order metric h^(2)". Its first prediction and its decisive test both rest on that identification: "a Redi–Tesi-type Maxwell yield (c_J = 16) on the classical O(h^2) metric reproduces P with no graviton quantisation". The Redi–Tesi functional [D-60] couples the pair only through h·T, so its amplitude is linear in the metric perturbation it is given. Feeding it h^(2) therefore computes only the s-channel of g g → γγ. The full tree amplitude also has a contact term (the O(h^2) piece of sqrt(-g) g g F F) and t/u-channel photon exchange (two O(h^(1)) insertions on the Maxwell field). Only the sum of the three is gauge invariant.

I ran the decisive test C7 proposes, using the toolkit's background-field amplitudes for two crossing plane waves in the lab TT gauge. Integrated over the final phase space, the s-channel alone does not reproduce the full yield, even roughly:

| beams | ∫\|M_s\|² / ∫\|M_full\|² |
|---|---|
| head-on R/L | ~1e-64 (s-channel zero, full yield nonzero) |
| 90°, R/R | ∞ (full yield zero by the helicity selection rule, s-channel nonzero) |
| 90°, R/L | 1.01 |
| 120°, R/L | 0.25 |
| plus/plus at 180°, 90°, 30° | 3.0, 10.75, 22.2 |

These results trigger C7's own "if false" clause: "a coherent-state in-out calculation differs from the semiclassical h^(2) yield at leading order". The clause is met at leading order, by any factor from 0 to infinity, depending on polarisation and angle.

C7 also misreads its source on this point. B&O v2 claim equivalence to "the creation of photons in a background h_c, once considered at second order (including ... the induced metric sourced by the energy-momentum tensor of the GWs)" [D-16]. In that sentence h^(2) is one ingredient ("including"), not the whole background.

This narrows the mechanism. It does not overturn C7's central point, because the full amplitude is itself a background-field calculation. The tool builds it by putting in the two gravitons as classical plane waves and expanding the matter action to second order. So the yield is still second order in the classical wave, hbar-free in pair number, and needs no graviton quantisation.

"Classical" also needs qualifying. Classical Einstein–Maxwell on F = 0 gives no field at all [D-17, D-54]. The pairs are vacuum fluctuations of the quantised Maxwell field, amplified by a classical background. That is semiclassical (QFT in a classical background), not a classical nonlinearity. C7's argument concedes this ("No classical emission from F = 0"), but its heading word "classical" overstates it.

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle.** The core reframe holds with the full O(h^2) background-field amplitude in place of h^(2). The pair number is a functional of the classical wave alone (it scales as h^4, G- and ħ-free at fixed strain), and no graviton quantisation is needed. "Decay of gravitons" adds nothing that this semiclassical calculation lacks. C7's "what follows" step (production needs time-like Fourier support, K = q1 + q2 with K² > 0) also survives the narrowing: every channel carries the same overall δ⁴(q1 + q2 − k1 − k2), so the support condition applies to the bilinear h^(1)⊗h^(1), not just to h^(2).
- **In practice.** The hbar-independent pair number is only the spontaneous part. With the CMB occupation extrapolated on Rayleigh–Jeans down to Hz frequencies, the log gives "f = 150 Hz: f_gamma = 3.786e+08; 1+2f = 7.572e+08" (and 1.1e10 at 10 Hz, 1.1e14 at 1 mHz). In any real environment the stimulated term would exceed the vacuum term by about 1e9 to 1e14. Its photon number scales as 1/ħ, as a classical field's photon count does, and its power is hbar-free. So the "hbar-independent pair number" describes a part that is negligible in practice. The dominant term is classical parametric scattering of the ambient field, as C7 says, and it is not pair creation from the vacuum. This supports C7's reframe, but it limits the last clause to vacuum.

## Corrections to details
- "The process is photon-vacuum pair creation on the second-order metric h^(2)" is wrong. It is pair creation at second order in the classical background: contact term + t/u photon exchange on h^(1) + the s-channel on h^(2). The s-channel alone is off by factors from 0 to ∞ (table above).
- Prediction 1 and the decisive test (a Redi–Tesi Maxwell yield on the classical O(h^2) metric reproduces P) would fail as stated. The semiclassical functional must be taken to second order in h^(1), not first order in h^(2). Read that way, the prediction is just the background-field result and holds.
- "which B&O v2 concede is 'equivalent' [D-16]" misreads D-16. B&O's "equivalent" refers to the full second-order background calculation, of which the induced metric is one part ("including").
- "Classical ... nonlinearity" in the heading should read "semiclassical, second order in the classical background". The photons come from the quantised Maxwell vacuum; classical Einstein–Maxwell keeps F = 0.
- "The pair number is hbar-independent" holds for the spontaneous (vacuum) term only. The CMB-stimulated term, which is larger by 2f_γ ≈ 7.6e8 at 150 Hz (Rayleigh–Jeans extrapolation), has a photon number ∝ 1/ħ.

## What survives and remaining doubt
What survives:
- With N = E/(ħω), the N² enhancement is h⁴ of a classical wave: ħ-free in pair number and needing no graviton quantisation.
- "Quantum decay of gravitons" is a misdescription of a semiclassical background-field effect. That part of C7 holds.

What does not:
- The specific mechanism C7 names (pair creation by h^(2)) and the decisive test built on it.

Remaining doubts:
- I did not test C7's second prediction, that an incoherent background with the same ρ gives the same rate as a coherent one. It is plausible at leading order, because same-mode (collinear) pairs contribute nothing, so the g^(2) difference between coherent and chaotic light does not enter. But packet-level cross-mode interference could change it by O(1) factors.
- The s-channel ratios depend on gauge. I evaluated them in the lab TT gauge of the waves, which is the natural reading of "the classical O(h^2) metric". In another gauge the numbers change, but the conclusion does not: a gauge-dependent piece cannot be the physical yield.
