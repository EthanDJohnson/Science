status: final

basis: calculation
narrowed: A real source's photon-pair production is a finite, distance-independent total made in and around the source and far too small to observe, but it is not of the order of B&O's per-packet total. The source's own off-shell (linear-in-h) channel exceeds that total by about 0.022 x L_P/L_GW: x70–110 for GW150914, ~1e3 for inspiral chirps, ~5e22 for a Galactic WD binary. B&O's total bounds only the N^2-fusion piece, and only to within C3's unresolved O(0.02–1) retarded share.

## Strongest attack
C4's size clause says the source's total pair yield is "of the order of B&O's per-packet total". C4 itself names the falsifier: "another channel dominates (C5)". On scale, the source's linear channel, in which one off-shell graviton goes to gamma gamma, dominates for every reference source, and by a margin that grows as the source gets weaker.

1. **Production rule.** I used the toolkit's first-order (Born, in-in) gravity-vertex yield, N = (1/4) Int d^4K/(2pi)^4 theta(K0) rho2(s) X2, with rho2(photon) = s^2/(40 pi). The toolkit checks it against Han–Lykken–Zhang and Redi–Tesi c1 = 16 (`qft_production.py` Part D).
   - I fed it the metric a conserved quadrupole source actually makes: h_bar~(K) = 16 pi G T~(K)/K^2.
   - T~ is that of a circular Newtonian binary at w = 2 Omega: T^ij = -(1/2) w^2 I^ij, T^0i = -(1/2) w k_j I^ij, T^00 = -(1/2) k_i k_j I^ij. The conservation residual is 1e-16.
2. **Normalisation check.** The same T~ on the light cone, put through Weinberg's formula, reproduces L_GW = (32/5) G mu^2 a^4 Omega^6 exactly (ratio 1.000000).
   - So the time-like support |k| < w is that of the very source that radiates the wave.
   - T~(w, k) is a polynomial in k, so it cannot vanish below the light cone while being nonzero on it.
3. **Result (natural units).** N_lin = k_lin G w E_GW, with k_lin = 0.03385. The value is identical on two quadrature grids.
   - In SI this is N_lin = k_lin G w E_GW / c^5, which is hbar-free.
   - Against B&O's own Parseval-form total, P_fus = (12 pi/25) G^2 w Int L^2 dt (natural units), the ratio for a steady source is N_lin/P_fus = 0.0224 x (L_P/L_GW), with L_P = c^5/G = 3.63e52 W (SI). Since L_GW << L_P for every real source, the linear channel always wins.
4. **Numbers (SI).** The B&O-model totals for comparison come from M-IDEALIZER-09/10.

So B&O's per-packet total is not "the order of" what a real source produces. At best it is the size of one sub-dominant piece: the |h^(2)|^2 fusion term. Where the K-supports of the two channels overlap (a broadband merger), the h^(1)–h^(2) interference term would itself exceed the pure fusion term by ~sqrt(N_lin/P_fus) ~ 10. That is my inference; I did not compute it.

The attack also disposes of the counter-argument C5 cites against itself (mechanist F10a, M-MECHANIST-10): that an inspiral's near-zone field is purely space-like. M-MECHANIST-10 verified only a local wavenumber estimate (q_phi = m/r) for one rotating harmonic. The global Fourier transform of a compact rotating source is nonzero at |k| < w, at quadrupole order, with the same T~ that gives the GW luminosity (check 2 above). If it vanished there, the binary could not radiate either.

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle.** The near-source yield is finite and does not grow with distance. This holds for both channels: the linear channel's support is set by T~(K) of the source, and fusion's outgoing-wave share falls as x^-3 beyond kr ~ 1 (M-IDEALIZER-08). It is not B&O-sized. It is set by the source's off-shell field, at ~0.03 G w E_GW / c^5 (SI).
- **In practice.** It is unobservable for any source.
  - N_lin = k_lin (G E/c^4)(w/c) < k_lin x O(1) per event, because a source cannot radiate E ~ Mc^2 at w >> c^3/(GM).
  - GW150914 gives 3e-4 pairs of 150 Hz photons. These cannot propagate where n_e > 2.8e-4 cm^-3 [D-62].
  - The headline "far too small to observe" therefore stands, and more firmly: the real-source yield is now bounded with its dominant channel included.

## Corrections to details
- "Of the order of B&O's per-packet total" applies at most to the N^2-fusion contribution. Even that rests on the uncomputed r < c/w share (C3: 0.02–0.1 outside kr ~ 1). It does not describe the source's total.
- The prediction "it scales as (r_m/l)^2 (w/sigma), and for chirps it tracks w Int L_GW^2 dt" holds only for the fusion piece. The leading real-source yield scales as w E_GW (∝ N, not N^2) and dominates by L_P/L_GW.
- For chirps, the fusion integrand grows as Omega^4, but the linear channel's integrand, w dE, grows as w^(2/3) per unit w. Both are top-weighted. The merger still dominates GW150914's total (3.1e-4 against the inspiral's 2.5e-5).
- The quadrupole calculation assumes v << 1 and k a << 1. At the GW150914 merger (v/c ~ 0.5, w r_g ~ 0.3) k_lin is indicative only, uncertain by an O(1) factor. That cannot close a factor 20–100 gap without a cancellation I have no reason to expect, but numerical relativity is needed to pin it down.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives.**
  - The finiteness and distance-independence of the near-source yield.
  - Its location in the source region.
  - Its unobservability.
- **What does not.** The identification of its size with B&O's per-packet total. For the source's total that is wrong by x20–100 at GW150914 and by ~1e3 or more for inspirals. This is also what C5 claims: my calculation computes C5's uncomputed coefficient, k = 0.034, well above C5's 3e-4 threshold.
- **Remaining doubts.**
  - k_lin is a quadrupole, Newtonian-orbit value; strong-field merger corrections are unknown.
  - Black holes are treated as point sources, valid for k r_s << 1.
  - Whether one counts "the photon-pair total" as all channels or only the fusion channel. If C4 is read as a claim about the fusion channel alone, its size clause still rests on C3's unresolved r < c/w share, and my attack does not reach it. Because of that reading I chose weakened rather than refuted.
