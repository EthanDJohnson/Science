## Strongest attack
I attacked each of the three clauses with my own calculations, independent of the toolkit's cross-section routine. The calculations are in [calc: runs/2026-10-13-gravitational-wave-decay-photon-pairs/calc/falsifier-C6-0_kinetic_eq14.py] (log beside it).

1. **"Exactly the kinetic rate."** I tried to break this in two ways: through the spectrum, and through the polarisation and direction content of the bath.
   - I integrated the tree amplitudes |M| = 8 pi G t^2/s and 8 pi G u^2/s for an opposite-helicity graviton pair (natural units) [D-01; M-MECHANIST-01]. Before integrating, I checked the photon-helicity labels against the exact rotational constraint at u -> 0. The finite |M| there carries a pure phase [32]/<23>, the usual collinear behaviour of gravity amplitudes, so there is no inconsistency.
   - A Gaussian quadrature of dsigma/dOmega, with the 1/2 for identical photons, gives sigma_RL/(G^2 s) = 2.5132741229 = 4 pi/5 (A1).
   - A Monte Carlo of (1/2) n^2 <sigma (1 - cos theta)> over isotropic directions and random helicities gives Gamma/(V G^2 rho^2), as a ratio to 8 pi/15:
     - 1.0003 ± 0.0006 for a monochromatic spectrum;
     - 1.0014 ± 0.0011 for a two-line spectrum (1 and 50);
     - 0.9991 ± 0.0065 for a log-uniform spectrum over six decades (A2).
   - So eq. 14 is reproduced with no shared code and does not depend on the spectrum.
   - B&O reach the same number by a different route [D-25]: their eq. 8 with V w sigma^2 -> 40 pi^2/9 gives (3/(25 pi))(40 pi^2/9) = 8 pi/15. That is an identity.
   - **The attack fails for an unpolarised isotropic bath.** It succeeds only on a qualifier the candidate does not state (see Corrections).

2. **"Negligibly small."** I looked for any frequency or epoch where the rate stops being negligible (SI).
   - At the DeltaN_eff = 0.3 ceiling, rho_GW = 2.84e-15 J/m^3 and the rate is 2.78e-109 pairs m^-3 s^-1 (B1).
   - The fraction of GW energy converted per Hubble time rises with frequency:
     - 1.5e-108 at 25 Hz (vacuum), or 6.7e-99 with CMB stimulation;
     - 5.9e-101 at 1 GHz;
     - 1.1e-66 even at the Planck frequency, 1.9e43 Hz (B2).
   - In the radiation era, the stimulated conversion per e-fold scales as 1.444 r^2 (T/M_Pl)^3 [M-CONSTRAINTS-03 scaling]:
     - 3.7e-12 at T = 1e16 GeV;
     - it reaches a FIRAS-like 1e-5 only at T = 0.114 M_Pl = 1.4e18 GeV (C1, C2).
   - That temperature lies above any reheating temperature compatible with the CMB tensor bound, and at the edge of the EFT. In that regime the thermal photon mass (omega_p ~ eT) also closes the channel for omega << T [M-CONSTRAINTS-09 logic].
   - **No admissible regime makes the rate non-negligible.**

3. **"Only in multi-directional ensembles."** The candidate supports "only" with theta-scaling premises, and those are unsourced [D-55]. I tested whether a single source could still give a sustained yield.
   - For any local rate density K rho^2 g(theta) on a shell of fixed thickness and energy E: rho ∝ r^-2, so the yield beyond R is ∝ Int_R^inf g(theta(r)) r^-2 dr.
   - The power law in R (D1):
     - R^-1.000 even if the angular spread stays fixed;
     - R^-3.000 for theta ∝ r^-1/2;
     - R^-5.000 for theta ∝ r^-1.
   - So a single source's local yield converges whatever its angular structure. Geometric dilution alone guarantees this.
   - A collimated bath is suppressed as the fourth power of its cone half-angle:
     - 1.7e-2 of the isotropic rate at a half-angle of 0.5 rad;
     - 3.1e-5 at 0.1 rad;
     - 3.1e-9 at 0.01 rad (A4).
   - Only a bath that is non-collinear and does not dilute (an ensemble) gives a production rate per volume that persists.
   - **The attack strengthens the clause rather than defeating it**, within the local, kinetic description. Whether B&O's non-local coherent formalism escapes that description belongs to C1/C2, not to this clause.

## Corrections to details
- **"Exactly" holds only for an unpolarised (non-chiral) bath.**
  - Only opposite-helicity pairs fuse, so a bath with net circular polarisation chi gives eq. 14 × (1 - chi^2). That is 0.75 at chi = 0.5 and zero for a fully chiral background (A3).
  - The candidate should say "isotropic, unpolarised". The factor is at most 1, so it only reinforces "negligibly small".
- **"Coherence adds nothing" rests on unverified toolkit ratios (0.982, 0.931)** [M-IDEALIZER-14]. A better support is structural:
  - For a random-phase ensemble, interference between distinct graviton-mode pairs averages to zero.
  - The one place coherent and chaotic statistics differ is same-mode pairs (<a†a†aa> = n^2 against 2n^2), and those are exactly collinear, where the amplitude vanishes.
  - So the kinetic rate is the expectation value for either statistics. This is an argument, not a computed ratio.
- **"Negligibly small" needs a stated domain.** It holds at every frequency today (≤ 1.1e-66 per Hubble time even at the Planck frequency) and at T ≲ 1e16 GeV (≤ 3.7e-12 per e-fold). It fails only at T ≳ 0.1 M_Pl, outside the admissible EFT regime.
- **"Multi-directional ensembles" should not imply that randomness is needed.** Two coherent waves crossing at a finite angle (from two sources) also fuse throughout their overlap. The requirement is non-collinear momentum content that does not dilute geometrically, not stochasticity.
- **Where the ~1e-110 m^-3 s^-1 figure comes from.** It corresponds to DeltaN_eff ≈ 0.1 (3.08e-110 pairs m^-3 s^-1, B1). The candidate's bandwidth caveat [D-44] stands: Omega_GW(25 Hz) is not the total rho_GW.

## What survives and remaining doubt
The core stands:
- Eq. 14 equals the incoherent, isotropic, unpolarised kinetic rate, (8 pi/15) G^2 rho^2/c^7 (SI), independent of the spectrum and with hbar cancelling. My code shares nothing with the toolkit and reproduces it to 1e-3 (Monte Carlo) and 1e-10 (cross section).
- It is negligible by more than 60 orders of magnitude in every observable regime.
- A sustained rate per volume needs an ensemble with non-collinear momenta that does not dilute. A single source's local yield converges at least as R^-1 even with no collinear suppression.

Remaining doubts:
- The amplitudes are a tree-level input [D-01], verified elsewhere [M-MECHANIST-01] but not re-derived here from Feynman rules.
- "Only" is shown within a local, kinetic description. A non-local coherent effect of a single wave is excluded here by geometric dilution, not by a spin-2 in-in calculation (that is C1/C2's decisive test).
- Whether an ensemble rate counts as "decay of a GW in vacuum" in the brief's sense is a question of definition, not physics.
