# Verdict on C8 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: Gravitational waves convert into ultralight-dark-matter scalar pairs at astrophysically relevant rates through Bose-enhanced resonant fusion, as B&O's eqs. 16-18 state for supermassive binaries.
status: final
verdict: refuted
basis: internal-inconsistency
narrowed: none

Units: natural units (hbar = c = 1) for eq. 17; SI (W, s, Msun, Hz) for astrophysical numbers; particle masses in eV. B&O = Blas & Oller, arXiv:2607.24930 v2.

## Strongest attack

B&O's eq. 18 contradicts their own description of the source, before any outside physics comes in. Eq. 17 holds only for a graviton packet whose fractional bandwidth is sigma_w/w_s ≈ v^2/2 = 5e-7 (v = 1e-3 c). Eq. 18 then feeds it "a binary merger of total mass M" at "ISCO frequencies", using the merger's whole graviton number N0 ∝ E_rel/m. Elsewhere in the same paper B&O set the merger and inspiral bandwidth at ε = 0.1 (Fig. 2) and quote ε ≈ 0.15 for the GW150914 ringdown.

So in B&O's own description the packet is 2e5 to 3e5 times too broad, and it lasts 5e-6 of the coherence time 1/sigma_res = 1.3e12 s that the resonance needs. Only gravitons inside the 5e-7 band can be resonant. Keeping only those cuts N0^2 by 1e-11 to 2.5e-11, so eq. 18's 3.8e13 W x_MW becomes 4e2 to 1e3 W x_MW (SI). The packet also lasts only 6.6e6 s, against the 1.3e12 s the narrow-band rate assumes.

The verified inspiral version of the same shortfall is worse: 12 s in band against 1.3e12 s needed (9e-12), with an in-band suppression of 5.1e-14 and W_phi ≈ 2 W x_MW [M-EMPIRICIST-03, M-CONSTRAINTS-12, both verified]. These shortfalls do not depend on eq. 17's unverified v^6 power or its 1/128 [M-EMPIRICIST-07].

The literature then removes the parameter point itself. Eq. 16 is normalised at 1e-22 eV and eq. 18 at 1e-21 eV. Both masses are strongly disfavoured by the Lyman-alpha forest, and excluded by ultrafaint-dwarf kinematics at 99%. At the masses still allowed, eq. 18 taken at face value falls as m^-2:
- 9.6e10 W x at 2e-20 eV;
- 4.3e8 W x at 3e-19 eV.

Reaching even one solar luminosity would then take x ~ 4e15 to 9e17 times the cosmic mean density, i.e. 1.3e8 to 3e10 Msun/pc^3.

B&O's escape route is "stellar" luminosities from galactic-centre solitons. Bar et al. (2019) looked for exactly such solitons around Sgr A* and M87* and found none. They also argue that the SMBH's dynamical effect can make the soliton-halo extrapolation over-predict the soliton mass "by orders of magnitude".

What is left is tiny even before these corrections: W_phi/L_GW ≈ 1e-31 to 1e-36 at x = 1e5. The products couple only gravitationally. Nothing about this rate is astrophysically relevant.

## In principle vs. in practice

**In principle.** The arithmetic is right within B&O's model: substituting eq. 16 into eq. 17 gives ratio 1, and 7/(450 pi) and tau_phi = (54/7) tau reproduce [M-EMPIRICIST-01, M-IDEALIZER-02]. Eq. 17 itself (v^6, 1/128) has not been re-derived. Even granting it, its stated validity window, a fractional bandwidth of 5e-7 held for about 1.3e12 s with near head-on gravitons, is one that B&O's own merger and inspiral descriptions do not satisfy (ε = 0.1 to 0.15). An outgoing wave at r >> hbar c/m lacks the head-on pairs altogether. The Bose-enhanced resonant rate therefore does not apply to the supermassive binaries eq. 18 names.

**In practice.** Take eq. 18 at face value anyway. At x = 1e5 it gives 1e-8 Lsun at a disfavoured mass, and 2.5e-11 to 1.1e-13 Lsun at allowed masses. That is 1e-31 to 1e-36 of the GW luminosity, carried by particles coupled only gravitationally. No instrument can see it within 20 years, and GW-amplitude depletion at the 1e-32 level is about 30 orders of magnitude below PTA amplitude errors of ~30% [D-35].

## Corrections to details

- The brief's "resonant coefficient 1/16" does not appear in the v2 text. The printed coefficient is 1/128 (eq. 17), unverified [M-EMPIRICIST-07].
- The 1e14 W in eq. 18 is per unit x_MW. With B&O's own x_MW ~ 1e5 for the Milky Way, it is about 1e19 W ≈ 1e-8 Lsun, not a stellar luminosity.
- Eq. 18 matches the reproduced 3.8e13 to 8.6e13 W only to within a factor of about 3, depending on whether E_rel = 2/65 or 3/65 M c^2 [M-EMPIRICIST-02].

## What survives and remaining doubt

What survives is the algebraic substitution of eq. 16 into eq. 17, and the general point that a large ULDM occupation number enhances a gg -> phi phi process whose final states are occupied. No astrophysical rate survives.

Remaining doubt:
- Eq. 17's own v^6 and 1/128 are unverified. A different coefficient cannot rescue the claim, because the bandwidth and duration shortfalls (1e-11 to 1e-14) and the 2v head-on restriction are independent of it.
- The "only in-band gravitons count" premise is an inference, not a theorem. It is, however, implied by B&O's own resonance condition, which restricts w_s to the interval m to m(1 + v^2/2).
- A long-lived, near-monochromatic wide SMBH binary held within 5e-7 for about 4e4 yr would satisfy the bandwidth condition. Such a binary radiates far less, at f ≈ m/h well below its merger, and no individual one has been detected; its rate has not been computed here.
- The soliton argument rests on the Bar et al. bounds and their own caveat (SMBH dynamics may shrink solitons). Those bounds are model-dependent, but the needed densities of 3e5 to 3e10 Msun/pc^3 (SI-derived) at x = 1e13 to 9e17 remain unsupported by any observation.
