# Verdict on C7 (refuter 0, angle: physics)
claim: The "N^2 coherent enhancement" is classical second-order field nonlinearity driving photon-vacuum pair creation, not a quantum decay of gravitons; the pair number is hbar-independent.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: The N^2 term is ordinary vacuum pair creation of quantised photons by the classical two-wave metric at second order in the background strain (semiclassical, the same calculation as tree-level coherent-state graviton fusion, and equal to the kinetic binary-collision rate for crossing beams), and its pair number is hbar-independent; but it is not "pair creation on the second-order metric h^(2)" or any other classical field nonlinearity: the gauge-invariant amplitude needs the contact and photon-exchange terms, and a linear-response (Redi–Tesi-type) yield fed with h^(2) gets it wrong by factors from 0 to infinity.

## Strongest attack

C7 has two parts: (a) the hbar counting, and (b) a mechanism. The mechanism says the N^2 effect is a "classical second-order field nonlinearity", spelled out in the argument as "photon-vacuum pair creation on the second-order metric h^(2)". The decisive test and first prediction make this concrete: "a Redi–Tesi-type Maxwell yield (c_J = 16) on the classical O(h^2) metric reproduces P with no graviton quantisation". C7 also names its own falsifier: "a coherent-state in-out calculation differs from the semiclassical h^(2) yield at leading order."

I ran that falsifier with the toolkit's tree-level Einstein–Maxwell code (qft_second_order, which sums all diagrams) and with the first-order pair-yield functional of qft_production Part D, fed with the second-order metric dg2. Part D is the same linear-response form as D-60: yield = sum over final states of |h~(K).<k1 k2|T|0>|^2.

1. **h^(2) alone gives the wrong answer, by factors from 0 to infinity.**
   - Same-helicity waves (R/R) at 90 degrees: the h^(2) yield is 8.26 times the R/L rate, but the full amplitude gives zero.
   - Head-on R/L waves: the h^(2) yield is zero to roundoff (1.3e-64), while the full amplitude is nonzero.
   - Linear polarisations: h^(2)-only/full = 22.2, 10.75 and 3.0 for plus/plus at 30, 90 and 180 degrees, and 9.0 and about 0 for plus/cross at 90 and 180 degrees.

   So the coherent in-out (= full background-field) result differs from the "semiclassical h^(2) yield" at leading order, by C7's own falsification criterion.

2. **The h^(2) piece is gauge-dependent, and in de Donder/TT gauge it carries none of the head-on amplitude.** For head-on R(+z) + L(-z) going to photons (+,-) at 0.7 rad:
   - Unshifted gauge: contact = 0, s-channel (h^(2)) = 0, and photon exchange (t/u) = -78.28. The whole amplitude is second-order Born scattering of the photon vacuum by the linear wave h^(1). Here neither the Einstein nonlinearity nor the quadratic Maxwell-metric contact term plays any part.
   - After the gauge shift eps1 -> eps1 + (q1 xi + xi q1)/2: the s-channel becomes 17.14 - 11.43i and the contact term -8.57 + 5.71i, while the total stays at -78.28 (imaginary part 5e-15).

   "Field nonlinearity" therefore names no gauge-invariant piece of the process. The gauge-invariant statement is "second order in the classical background".

3. **No linear-response functional can work, whatever metric you feed it.** The qft_second_order self-test (check 12) shows the s-channel lies in span{h~(K).T(k1,k2)} with residual below 1e-10, but the full amplitude does not (relative residual > 0.1). The reason is structural: the contact term contracts each wave's polarisation with each photon's field strength separately, and the t/u propagators depend on k1 and k2 separately, not on K alone. A D-60-type yield is by construction |h~_eff(K).T|^2, so it cannot reproduce P for any effective h~_eff. That includes the full metric h^(1) + h^(2): h^(1) has only null support, so it adds nothing. The candidate's decisive test, done as specified, would come out against its mechanism.

What the attack does not touch:
- **hbar counting.** The pair number is hbar-free, the unstimulated power is linear in hbar, and the Rayleigh–Jeans-stimulated power is hbar-free. All three hold (below).
- **Semiclassical equivalence.** The full background-field second-order calculation of photon production on the classical two-wave metric is the coherent-state tree amplitude. It reproduces the kinetic rate exactly, so the "not a quantum decay of gravitons, but semiclassical vacuum pair creation" reading stands. The two are the same calculation described two ways, not rival mechanisms.

## In principle vs. in practice

- **In principle:** the reframe's interpretive core holds. The effect is background-driven vacuum pair creation, hbar-free in number, and it is not an extra "coherent" enhancement over the kinetic n1 n2 sigma v rate (exact equality for crossing monochromatic beams). Its stated mechanism (h^(2), a field nonlinearity) does not hold.
- **In practice:** the narrowing changes no number. The C2/C3 conclusions about where pairs are made still have to be argued with the full O(h^2) amplitude, or with the kinetic proxy validated in idealizer F13. They cannot use "time-like Fourier support of h^(2)", because h^(2) alone mis-weights polarisation and angle by O(1) to infinite factors.

## Corrections to details

- "The process is photon-vacuum pair creation on the second-order metric h^(2)" is false as a mechanism. h^(2) is gauge-dependent, and in de Donder gauge it carries none of the head-on R/L amplitude. Only the sum of contact, t/u and s terms is physical. B&O v2's own wording (D-16) includes the induced metric "at second order" together with photon creation in h_c at second order, not h^(2) alone.
- "What follows ... whether h^(2) of an outgoing wave has time-like Fourier support far from the source" is the wrong criterion. The right object is the time-like Fourier support of the product of first-order fields, i.e. of non-collinear pairs of null components, through the full vertex set. The conclusion (no production far from the source, where the wave is locally plane and collinear) may still be right, because every channel vanishes as s -> 0, but not for the reason given.
- Prediction 1 (a Redi–Tesi c_J = 16 yield on the O(h^2) metric reproduces P) is false: no |h~_eff(K).T|^2 functional can reproduce the full amplitude (span residual > 0.1).
- Prediction 2 (an incoherent background with the same rho gives the same rate) holds for crossing monochromatic beams and for phase-averaged ensembles. For a specific deterministic broadband packet, the coherent yield |FT[h h]|^2 can differ from the phase-averaged one, as idealizer F13's 0.98 and 0.93 hint. It is an equality on average, not an identity.
- "Classical": the photon field equation on a fixed metric is linear and homogeneous in F, so nothing in the classical Einstein–Maxwell system "nonlinearly" makes the photons. The driver is classical, but the response is quantum vacuum fluctuation, and the unstimulated power (2 R hbar w) does vanish as hbar -> 0 at a fixed classical wave. C7 states this itself, so this is a wording point, not a contradiction.

## What survives and remaining doubt

Survives, with calculations:
- The pair number of eq. 8 and of the two-beam rate is hbar-free and depends only on the classical strain and frequencies. For crossing beams it is G-independent at fixed strain.
- The "N^2" is the n1 n2 of binary collisions, with no extra coherent enhancement for crossing beams.
- The coherent-state graviton-fusion picture and semiclassical background-field pair creation are the same tree-level calculation.
- The stimulated power is hbar-free.

Refuted: the identification of the driver as a classical field nonlinearity, in particular h^(2), and the decisive test built on it.

Remaining doubt:
- I did not check the equivalence of coherent-state in-out and background-field in-in beyond the tree level and the beam geometry that the tool covers.
- "Not a quantum decay of gravitons" is partly semantic, since the two descriptions are dual at this order.
