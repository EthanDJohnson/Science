# Verdict on C2 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: No vacuum decay: an outgoing gravitational wave's photon-pair production converges within a few wavelengths of its source, so there is no lifetime, no path depletion and nothing observable.
status: final
verdict: survives
basis: cited-evidence

Units: natural (hbar = c = 1) for the kinematic check; SI for rates, path lengths and depletion fractions, as marked.

## Strongest attack

From the evidence angle, the best case against the null is the peer-reviewed literature that says gravitational waves *as detected* make massless quanta, or are attenuated, far from their source. I examined three items.

1. **Chakraborty et al. 2024, Phys. Rev. D 110, L021701 (arXiv:2404.01840).** This is a PRD Letter claiming perturbative creation of massless quanta, linear in h, for any GW profile. It explicitly includes the signals LVK detects, so it implies production along the path. That would contradict C2 directly.
2. **The Jones–McDougall–Singleton line.** PRD 95, 065010 (2017) gives a non-zero Bogoliubov beta for massless fields in a plane GW. Jones & Singleton 2015 turn this into an attenuation length of about 1e11 m (SI) for a 1e2 s^-1 wave, with Gamma growing linearly in r [D-53].
3. **Khusnutdinov 2026.** He calls the collinear-sector tension "an unresolved issue" [D-51, RT-11].

None of the three defeats the core claim. The reasons are under Evidence.

The second-strongest attack is the gap in evidence. Nobody has published a spin-2 in-in calculation of P(>R) for a retarded quadrupole wave [D-66; dossier section 6]. The slope -3 rests on scalar toys. That makes C2's *exponent* soft. It does not make the *convergence* soft, as shown below.

## Evidence

**(1) The dissenting linear-in-h claim breaks four-momentum conservation for an on-shell pair.**
- Chakraborty et al.'s spectrum carries delta(k'^2) on the GW momentum k'. They require the created scalar momentum k to be non-collinear with it.
- Their words (fetch_text, arXiv:2404.01840, p. 4): "we see that k.k′ cannot vanish. But since k and k′ are both non- parallel null vectors, we have that k.k′ = ω⃗kω ⃗k′ (1−cos θ)".
- The partner then carries k' - k, with (k' - k)^2 = -2 k.k' < 0 (natural units). That is space-like, not an on-shell massless particle.
- The only on-shell option, a pair with null total momentum q, forces k ∥ p ∥ q. There a transverse-traceless eps(q) kills the minimal-scalar vertex. Calculation, 5 random TT tensors: max |eps_mn T^mn| = 1.9e-16, i.e. zero to rounding. A non-collinear control needs q^2 = 3.75 > 0, a time-like Fourier support that a free wave lacks [calc: runs/2026-10-13-gravitational-wave-decay-photon-pairs/calc/falsifier-C2-1_crossfusion.py].
- The same paper gives no number: "The details of our prediction for the spectrum of creation of scalar quanta will be treated in a subsequent paper" (p. 4, fetch_text).
- The toolkit's tested `qft_second_order.py` docstring states the same no-go: "a single gravitational plane wave, or any freely propagating packet, makes NO pairs at first order: every Fourier component of a free wave is null."

**(2) The published no-go results and universality results agree with C2.**
- Redi & Tesi (arXiv:2606.28297, p. 6, fetch_text): "Notice that null-like tensor and vector perturbations do not produce particles."
- Garani, Redi & Tesi (arXiv:2502.12249, JHEP 08 (2025) 037; abstract via fetch_text) fix massless production by "the two-point function of the energy-momentum tensor", with production only on time-like support [D-60, D-69]. Their examples of production are stochastic, multi-directional backgrounds (phase transitions, inflationary perturbations). That is C6's regime, not a single outgoing wave.
- Gibbons 1975 (massive scalar) and Khusnutdinov 2026 (massless scalar and vector) find no production by an exact plane wave [D-56, D-57]. B&O themselves concede that "no particle production occurs for an individual plane-wave GW" [D-17].
- The disputed collinear Bogoliubov sector [D-51] is where the g g -> gamma gamma amplitude vanishes [D-13]. Per Khusnutdinov, any photons made there "remain in the sandwich and leave with it" [RT-11]. That is not path depletion.

**(3) Observation contradicts the only dissenting claim that has a number.**
- Jones & Singleton's abstract (fetch_text, arXiv:1505.04843): "we propose that the Unruh-DeWitt detector response implies a "decay" or attenuation of gravitons ... Over large distances such a decay/attenuation may have consequences in regard to the detection of gravitational waves."
- Their decay length of about 1e11 m (SI) [D-53] would suppress GW150914's amplitude at 410 Mpc by exp(-6.3e13). GW150914 was seen with <= 11.29% amplitude calibration, which requires an e-folding length > 5.3e25 m [M-CONSTRAINTS-14; D-39].
- GW170817's d_GW/d_EM agrees within [-0.17, +0.06] (68%) [Lagos et al. 2019, via M-EMPIRICIST-05].
- So the observed record is consistent with "no path depletion" and excludes the only large-attenuation claim.

**(4) Convergence does not hinge on the unsourced angular-spread premise.**
- M-MECHANIST-04 (math check, confirmed): the shell yield integral of r^(-2-4a) over [R, infinity) converges iff a > -1/4, where theta(r) ~ (kr)^-a. Even a constant local spread (a = 0) converges, as 1/R.
- Convergence therefore follows from 1/r^2 dilution of the outgoing energy and the locality of the vertex alone. A spin-2 calculation could change the exponent, which is already window-dependent, -2 to -3 [M-EXAMINER-04]. It could change whether the yield converges only if the local non-collinearity grew with r, and nothing in the literature suggests that.

**(5) Persistent cross-fusion with other GWs is real but sub-observable.**
- A far-zone 150 Hz graviton crossing an isotropic SGWB with Omega_GW = 1e-8 is fused at Gamma = 5.2e-128 s^-1 (SI). This holds for a 25 Hz or a 3.2e-8 Hz background alike, since Gamma ∝ omega_1 rho_bg.
- The fraction fused over 410 Mpc is 2.2e-111 [calc: runs/2026-10-13-gravitational-wave-decay-photon-pairs/calc/falsifier-C2-1_crossfusion.py].
- This channel does grow linearly in R. But it needs a second, non-collinear GW field. It is the multi-directional case C2 scopes out (C6), not decay of the wave itself.

**(6) Literature search.** INSPIRE finds no comment, rebuttal or citation of B&O as of 2026-10-13 (lit_search, "gravitational waves decay in vacuum photon pairs coherent graviton", since 2026: one hit, B&O itself, 0 citations).

## In principle vs. in practice

- **In principle:** a single outgoing wave cannot fuse with itself far from its source. Every Fourier component of the free far field is null, and the second-order time-like support sits at kappa ~ 1/R and shrinks with R. The supporting evidence is the Redi–Tesi theorem, the plane-wave no-go results, and the kinematic check above. What is not established is the exponent of the fall-off for spin-2 and a real quadrupole source.
- **In practice:** nothing is observable.
  - Near-source totals are about 1e-6 pairs or fewer per GW150914 [C2/C3 rows of the slate].
  - Cross-fusion with an SGWB depletes 2.2e-111 of the wave over 410 Mpc.
  - Hz–kHz photons cannot cross the ISM, the solar wind or the ionosphere [D-62].

## Corrections to details

- **"No lifetime, no path depletion"** is exact only for an isolated wave in empty space. In the real universe, cross-fusion with the stochastic GW background gives a sustained, R-linear depletion. Its size is Gamma ≈ 5e-128 s^-1, a lifetime of about 2e127 s, and a fraction of about 2e-111 over 410 Mpc (SI). Like gamma-ray absorption on the CMB, this is scattering on other radiation, not vacuum decay. It is far below any observable.
- **The exponent "(lambda/2 pi R)^3"** depends on the window [M-EXAMINER-04: a sharp cut gives R^-2] and on the vertex weight [M-CONSTRAINTS-08: -(p+1)]. Only "falls faster than R^-1, and converges" is robust.
- **"Within a few wavelengths."** The toys put 0.4%–7% of the near-zone total beyond kr ≈ 1–2 pi [M-CONSTRAINTS-08, M-IDEALIZER-08]. Production is therefore concentrated at r ≲ lambda. That is the region where the free-graviton model fails for a real source [examiner F7]. So "converges near the source" is supported, but the near-source number itself is model-dependent (C3/C4).
- **Deser 1975 [D-58]** is a search summary only. It should not be counted as evidence for C2.

## What survives and remaining doubt

The core claim stands. The published dissent (Chakraborty et al. 2024, Jones–McDougall–Singleton 2017, Jones & Singleton 2015) either breaks on-shell four-momentum conservation, lies in the collinear sector where the amplitude vanishes and the photons never leave the wave, or is excluded by GW150914 and GW170817. The convergence of a single outgoing wave's yield follows from 1/r^2 dilution even with a constant angular spread.

Remaining doubt:
- No spin-2 in-in calculation of P(>R) for a retarded quadrupole source exists.
- Second-order memory and tail terms have not been computed explicitly. Memory is itself outgoing radiation with null support at large r, so I expect it to change the exponent at most.
- The Bogoliubov collinear dispute is formally open.

None of these points is published evidence for a sustained self-decay, and the observational bounds leave no room for an observable one.
