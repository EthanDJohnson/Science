# Verdict on C7 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: The 'N^2 coherent enhancement' is classical second-order field nonlinearity driving photon-vacuum pair creation, not a quantum decay of gravitons; the pair number is hbar-independent.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: Eq. 8's N^2 is the (h_c)^4 scaling of second-order pair creation from the quantised Maxwell vacuum by a classical GW: graviton quantisation is not needed and the pair number is hbar-independent. But the process is the photon field's second-order response to the linear wave h^(1), not pair creation on the classical second-order metric h^(2), which alone gives zero in B&O's J_z = ±4 channel and is not gauge invariant on its own.

## Strongest attack

C7's heading and argument name a specific mechanism: "classical second-order field nonlinearity", spelled out as "photon-vacuum pair creation on the second-order metric h^(2)". Its first prediction and its decisive test follow from that: "a Redi–Tesi-type Maxwell yield (c_J = 16) on the classical O(h^2) metric reproduces P". The analysis it draws on says the same thing. Mechanist finding 9 says "The amplitude for one pair is linear in the second-order metric h^(2)", and examiner F10 says "Its N^2 is classical field nonlinearity (h^(2) ∝ h^2)".

The full tree amplitude does not work that way. g g → γγ in Einstein–Maxwell has three channels:
- a contact term (h h A A), from the Maxwell action's quadratic dependence on the metric;
- t- and u-channel photon exchange, where two linear h^(1)·T insertions are joined by a photon propagator;
- the s-channel, which is the O(h) vertex fed with the classical second-order metric h^(2).

Only the sum of the three is gauge invariant.

In the physical (TT, de Donder, CM) gauge, B&O's whole channel comes from photon exchange. That channel is the opposite-helicity, J_z = ±4 configuration, the only one with a nonzero amplitude, and it is the one eq. 7 is built on. The h^(2) piece and the contact piece are exactly zero there. This has to be so: a symmetric rank-2 h^(2)(K) at timelike K cannot carry J_z = ±4.

Conversely, h^(2) alone predicts a large yield for same-helicity waves, where the physical amplitude is zero.

So a Redi–Tesi-type yield computed on h^(2), which is linear in the metric and depends on the final state only through T^{mn}(k1, k2), gives:
- zero for the configuration that carries eq. 8;
- a spurious nonzero result where eq. 8 has none.

C7's decisive test, carried out as written, would therefore come out against C7's own mechanism.

What remains of C7:
- The process is a classical background acting at second order on the quantised photon vacuum.
- No graviton quantisation is needed.
- The pair number is free of ħ.

The "N^2" is (h_c)^4 from second-order perturbation theory in the classical wave. It is not a nonlinearity of the classical gravitational field (h^(2) ∝ h^2), and, as C7 itself concedes, it is not a classical Einstein–Maxwell effect either.

**The tool's own documentation agrees.** The `qft_second_order.py` docstring says: "Since only the sum is gauge invariant, the s-channel (and hence Part D fed with h^(2)) alone is not a physical answer: for two same-helicity waves at 90 degrees Part D predicts about 8 times (8.26) the photon pairs an R/L pair really makes, while the full amplitude gives none; head-on R/L waves give the reverse (Part D zero, full nonzero)". It also says the full amplitude "lies outside the span of {h.T}". Its self-test checks the first two cases. A Redi–Tesi functional applied to h^(2) is a Part-D-type (h·T, spectral) yield, so this applies to it directly.

**B&O v2 (arXiv:2607.24930v2), quoted via fetch_text, p. 2:** "the calculation we will perform is equivalent to that of considering the creation of photons in a background h_c, once considered at second order (including in particular the induced metric sourced by the energy-momentum tensor of the GWs, cf. C7 reads B&O's equivalence as pair creation on h^(2) alone, and B&O's own wording does not support that reading.

**B&O v2, p. 1, on the classical part:** "the quadratic coupling of photons forbids it at the classical level from pure vacuum". So the effect is not classical Einstein–Maxwell nonlinearity. C7 concedes this ("No classical emission from F = 0"), which leaves "semiclassical" as the only defensible meaning of "classical" in its heading.

The full text (fetch_text, p. 17) adds that for graviton photoproduction "the leading contribution ... comes not from the seagull ... but rather from the photon pole term". The photon-exchange pole is a standard and often dominant part of graviton–photon amplitudes, and it is not a metric nonlinearity.

## Corrections to details

- **The phrase "photon-vacuum pair creation on the second-order metric h^(2)".** It is wrong as a mechanism. The physical amplitude is the sum of contact, photon-exchange and h^(2) terms, none of which is gauge invariant alone. In TT gauge, B&O's channel is 100% photon exchange.
- **Prediction 1** ("a Redi–Tesi-type Maxwell yield (c_J = 16) on the classical O(h^2) metric reproduces P"). Expect it to fail: it gives 0 for the J_z = ±4 configuration that carries eq. 8. The correct semiclassical test is the full second-order background-field yield, for which the toolkit's beam rate ω⁴h⁴(1 − cos θ)²/(1280πc³) (M-MECHANIST-03, verified) is the relevant number.
- **The decisive test as written** ("compute the Maxwell pair yield on the classical O(h^2) metric with the D-60 functional"). It tests the wrong object. Redi–Tesi's functional is linear in the metric in the amplitude, and D-60 already notes that applying it to the O(h^4) process is an inference they do not make.
- **"What follows: ... whether h^(2) of an outgoing wave has time-like Fourier support".** The ħ-free persistence question is really whether pairs of non-collinear Fourier components of h^(1) overlap with timelike K = q1 + q2, in the full second-order vertex. The conclusion drawn in C2 (none far from the source, where the wave is locally plane and K becomes null) is plausibly unchanged, but the reason C7 gives for it is the wrong one.
- **"The stimulated (1 + 2f_γ) part is classical parametric scattering of the ambient field by h^(2)".** The same correction applies: it is scattering at second order in h^(1), through all three channels. It is still ħ-free.
- **Prediction 2** (an incoherent background with the same ρ gives the same rate as a coherent one). This rests on idealizer F13's ratios 0.982 and 0.931. M-IDEALIZER-14 marks these "unverified", so the prediction is plausible but not established.

## What survives and remaining doubt

The surviving core:
- The N² is (h_c)⁴: two classical-wave amplitudes in the pair amplitude, squared.
- The pair number is ħ-independent.
- The quantity is computable with a classical GW background and a quantised Maxwell field, with no graviton quantisation.
- The ħ in the power is only the photon energy.

B&O v2 support this (their "all the ħ disappear" remark for the stimulated case and their "semiclassical description"). Their v2 Discussion line, "vanishes in the classical limit", refers to the power, not the number.

What fails is the attribution to "classical second-order field nonlinearity", read as the induced metric h^(2). Gauge-invariantly, the effect is second-order perturbation theory of the quantum photon field in the linear classical wave, with h^(2) as one gauge-dependent piece. In the physical gauge, that piece contributes nothing to B&O's channel.

I chose weakened rather than refuted because:
- the reframe's main point (classical background, ħ-free pair number, no graviton decay) survives;
- the word "nonlinearity" can be read generously as the nonlinear metric dependence of √(-g) g g F F, although even then the dominant photon-exchange piece is iterated linear coupling, not a nonlinearity.

Remaining doubt:
- The channel results come from one toolkit implementation. They are cross-checked by its Ward-identity test (total gauge invariant to roundoff while the channels move by O(1), reproduced in output 3) and by the closed-form σ(R, L) = (4/5)πG²s.
- The J_z = ±4 zero of the s-channel follows from angular momentum: a rank-2 tensor at rest carries |J_z| ≤ 2.
