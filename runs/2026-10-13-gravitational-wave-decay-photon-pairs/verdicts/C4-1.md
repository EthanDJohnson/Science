## Strongest attack

C4's quantitative clause, "of the order of B&O's per-packet total", fails because it leaves out a published channel that dominates the in-in pair total of a real source.

1. **The channel is in the literature.** Redi & Tesi 2026 (cited by B&O as their ref. [28]) give the pair yield of a conformal field in a classical metric perturbation. It depends on the metric's time-like Fourier support (q^2 > 0). A time-dependent compact source has such support in its near-zone field, h(q) proportional to T(q)/q^2 for |k| < w. B&O's model has none of this by construction, because their coherent state holds only on-shell (q^2 = 0) gravitons. Redi & Tesi say directly that the source's time dependence is enough: particle production "requires less: only time dependence of the source on a time-like support". This is the O(G^2 Q^2) "linear channel" that C4's own "If false" line names as a failure mode (C5), and its coefficient k was "uncomputed" in the slate (header; M-EXAMINER-09).
2. **I computed k from two independent published coefficients.**
   - Route 1: Redi–Tesi eq. 6 with c_J = 16 for photons.
   - Route 2: the Han–Lykken–Zhang width Gamma(spin-2 -> gamma gamma) = kappa^2 m^3/(160 pi), which equals G m^3/10, fed into a Källén–Lehmann spectral integral.
   - Both routes give the same coefficient, 8 pi/5, for G^2 Int_{q0>0,q^2>0} T*P2T.
   - For a long-wavelength traceless quadrupole the integral over the virtual-graviton mass gives N_pairs = k G w^2 N_g (natural units), with k = 67/(630 pi) = 0.0339 [calc: runs/2026-10-13-gravitational-wave-decay-photon-pairs/calc/falsifier-C4-1_offshell_channel.py].
3. **Sizes (SI; hbar cancels, so N_pairs = k (G/c^5) Int w dE).**

   | Source | Off-shell channel (pairs) | C4's comparison number (pairs) | Ratio |
   |---|---|---|---|

   The ratio P_lin/P_BO = k c^5/(c_tot G E sigma) is about 1/(L/L_P), so it grows for weaker sources. The off-shell pairs carry pair energy w and the fusion pairs carry 2w, so the two channels do not interfere in the narrow-band limit and their yields simply add.
4. **Consequence for C4's predictions.**
   - Its prediction that "the retarded in-in total per event lies within a factor ~3 of eq. 8 x T_eff" fails by about 1.5–2 orders of magnitude for the merger and about 3 for chirps.
   - Its scaling prediction ("(r_m/l)^2 (w/sigma) ... for chirps it tracks w Int L_GW^2 dt") fails for the total. The dominant term scales as Int w dE_GW, linear in the radiated energy rather than quadratic.
   - Even the fusion part alone is not established to be "of order" B&O. The verified toys give 1/12 (contact vertex) and 0.020 (|M|^2 ∝ s^2) of the time-symmetric total for the retarded field [M-EXAMINER-03].

**Checking my own assumptions.**

## In principle vs. in practice

- **In principle:** a real source does make a finite, distance-independent near-source pair total. The off-shell channel is made where the field has time-like support, at r <~ lambda/2pi, while the source radiates. This supports C4's qualitative picture, and C2's, against vacuum decay (C1).
- **In practice:**
  - The dominant total is about 3e-4 to 5e-4 pairs per GW150914-like merger, and about 4e-5 pairs for GW170817.
  - Every caveat C4 cites still applies: plasma cutoff [D-62], free-free absorption [M-CONSTRAINTS-10], and the CMB foreground [D-46].
  - It is still far too small to observe, so C4's "far too small to observe" survives, now with a larger but still negligible number.

## Corrections to details

- C4 says the total is "of the order of B&O's per-packet total". For a real source the total is about 70–110 times larger (merger) and about 1e3 times larger than the fusion yield for inspirals, and it is dominated by the off-shell channel.
- C4 says the total "scales as (r_m/l)^2 (w/sigma), and for chirps it tracks w Int L_GW^2 dt". The dominant term scales as k (G/c^5) Int w dE_GW: linear in energy, independent of sigma and of hbar.
- C4's "Hoop cap: <~ 0.1 pairs per burst" applies only to the fusion term. The off-shell term obeys its own cap. It equals k (G M w/c^3)(E/(M c^2)), and since G M w/c^3 <~ 1 and E < M c^2, it is <~ k ≈ 0.03 pairs per source (my arithmetic on the formula above). At GW150914 it is 3e-4, so neither cap changes "unobservable".
- The "linear-channel coefficient k is uncomputed" (slate header) can now be given: k = 67/(630 pi) ≈ 0.034 for a long-wavelength traceless quadrupole, with E_P = sqrt(hbar c^5/G).

## What survives and remaining doubt

- **What survives:** the pair total is finite, distance-independent, made near the source, and unobservable by more than 40 orders of magnitude in flux.
- **What falls:** the claim that this total is of the order of B&O's eq. 8 x T_eff. That holds at best for the N^2 fusion sub-term, whose own retarded-field size relative to B&O (0.02–3) remains unsettled [M-EXAMINER-03; C3].
- **Remaining doubt:**
  - The merger number rests on a weak-field, long-wavelength formula applied at v/c of about 0.5. The GW170817 weak-field case is robust.
  - Redi–Tesi is a preprint. HLZ is the independent, established cross-check, and the two agree exactly.
  - A full numerical-relativity evaluation of the Redi–Tesi functional, which is C4's own decisive test, would fix the merger value to better than O(1). Because that functional is O(h^2), it would measure this off-shell channel, not the N^2 fusion term.
- **Why weakened and not refuted:** three of the four elements of the core sentence (finite, distance-independent, near-source, unobservable) stand. Only the size clause fails, by about 2 orders of magnitude, and that sizing is itself an O(1)-uncertain strong-field estimate for the merger.
