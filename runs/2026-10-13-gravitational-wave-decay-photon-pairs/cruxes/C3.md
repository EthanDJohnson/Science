## Response to the verdicts

Narrowed core that C3 now defends:
(i) Eq. 8 x T_eff is the in-out yield of B&O's free, time-symmetric (Pauli-Jordan) coherent in-state, and it is generated by the incoming x outgoing overlap.
(ii) The outgoing wave of a retarded source, counted beyond kr ~ 2-3, yields 0.003-0.05 of that total.
(iii) For a quasi-circular (rigidly rotating) source, the whole-space total of the retarded field, near zone included, is also far below it: 2.9e-3 in the scalar toy. This protection is shown only for scalars.
(iv) Conceded: "a real source yields substantially less" is not a general statement. For sources with oscillating m = 0 quadrupole content, the scalar toy's near-zone yield exceeds eq. 8 x T_eff by ~(ka)^-6.

### C3-0 (physics: the l = 2 near zone reverses the inequality). Partly conceded, partly rebutted.
- **Conceded.** The monopole 1/12 and 0.020 [M-EXAMINER-03] do not transfer to a general l = 2 source. C3's prediction "0.02-0.1 before pattern factors" and its "<~ 3e-7 pairs" for GW150914 were stated for the total. As C3 wrote them, they do not hold for every quadrupole pattern. The "O(1) share at r < 1/w" in C3's evidence-against understated the problem for those patterns.
- **Rebuttal: the reversal depends on m.** C3-0 does not state which m its l = 2 toy uses. The (ka)^-6 law it reports is the signature of an L = 0 part in psi^2.
  - I expanded the sum-frequency (q0 = 2w) source psi_a psi_b in Y_LM using Gaunt coefficients, with near-zone R_l ~ (kr)^-(l+1) [calc: runs/2026-10-13-gravitational-wave-decay-photon-pairs/calc/crux-C3_multipole_selection.py].
  - The linear (head-on) quadrupole (2,0)x(2,0) contains L = 0, 2 and 4. Its L = 0 part has a small-r integrand ~ r^-4, which gives a yield ~ (ka)^-6. Its L = 2 part gives (ka)^-2. This reproduces C3-0's scaling and C3-2's (692 against 3^6 = 729).
  - The positive-frequency field of a rigidly rotating source is pure m = +2 (and m = +3, ... for higher harmonics). (2,+2)x(2,+2) is pure L = 4, M = 4. (2,+2)x(3,+3) is pure L = 5. (2,+2)x(2,+1) is pure L = 4, because L = 3 is excluded by parity. In every one of these the small-r integrand is ~ r^0: no near-zone divergence.
  - Verdict C3-2 confirms this in a direct coherent calculation. For the (2,2) circular pattern at p = 2, the whole-space ratio including the near zone down to r = 0 is 2.87e-3, and "the near zone adds nothing here" [verdict C3-2, Evidence].
  - So, for the source class the brief's anchors belong to (quasi-circular binaries), the scalar toy gives *less*, by a larger factor than C3 claimed. The reversal belongs to sources that oscillate in m = 0 (head-on, strongly eccentric, linear oscillators).
- **Not rebutted.** C3-0's own doubt applies equally to my rebuttal. Everything is a scalar toy. In spin-2, the photon pair can carry the J_z = +4 of the rotating source partly as spin, so an orbital L = 0 near-zone part is not obviously forbidden. The exact statement is that total J_z = +4 is conserved for a helically symmetric source. In a scalar toy that forces L >= 4; for spin-2 it does not. This is the open point the deciding calculation must settle.
- **Scale.** C3-0 puts GW170817 at ka = 0.32. That is the separation for a circular inspiral, so the (2,±2) protection applies there. At GW150914, ka = 1.07, which is strong field. I agree that no flat-space toy is valid there [M-EXAMINER-08; examiner F7].

## Deciding observation

No observation can decide any of these, because every yield is short of detectability by >= 1e30 [empiricist F10; D-45 to D-47]. Each crux below is a calculation. None is published as of 2026-10-13 [dossier section 6; D-66: 0 citations].

**One calculation underlies all of them: a spin-2 in-in (Schwinger-Keldysh) photon-pair yield at O(G^2), using the Redi-Tesi Weyl-tensor functional with c_J = 16 [D-60] extended to the O(h^2) fusion source, for two different field configurations with the same late-time outgoing wave:**
- (A) B&O's Pauli-Jordan state;
- (B) the retarded field of a rigidly rotating quadrupole (positive frequency, pure J_z = +2), near zone included down to a source radius a with ka = 0.3-1.

Output: the ratio B/A, plus B split into r < 2/k and r > 2/k. Who could do it: B&O themselves, or groups working with the coherent-state waveform formalism [D-02, D-03] or the Redi-Tesi functional [D-60]. It is tree-level, with flat-space propagators.

- **vs C4 (main rival: real-source total ~ eq. 8 x T_eff within ~3).**
  - C3 predicts B/A ~ 0.003-0.03 for the circular pattern, with no growth as ka -> 0.
  - C4 predicts 0.3-3 (times pattern factors 0.3-0.6).
  - The gap from C3's upper edge of 0.1 is a factor 3. From the circular toy value it is a factor 105 [crux-C3_numbers.py].
  - Required precision: order of magnitude (a factor ~3) suffices.
  - The single sub-result that decides it is whether the spin-2 sum-frequency source of a J_z = +2 rotator acquires an orbital L = 0 part that grows as (ka)^-6. If B/A grows as ka falls, C4 (or larger) is favoured. If B/A is flat and << 0.1, C3 is.
- **vs C5 (near-source pairs from the source's own field).**
  - Compute k in P_lin = k x 9.3e-3 pairs for a GW150914-like merger-ringdown [M-EXAMINER-09].
  - Once C3's reduction is applied, the bar for C5 to dominate falls from k > 3.1e-4 (against B&O's total) to k > 8.9e-7 (circular toy) or k > 8.8e-6 (monopole proxy) [crux-C3_numbers.py].
  - If k exceeds these, C5 dominates the per-event count, and C3 still holds as a statement about eq. 8. If k ~ 0 (mechanist F10a: inspiral support is space-like), fusion at C3's reduced level is the leading channel.
  - The two are compatible on eq. 8's meaning. The test decides only which channel dominates near the source.
  - Precision: order of magnitude in k.
- **vs C2 (null: the outgoing yield converges within a few wavelengths, nothing observable).**
  - C3 and C2 agree that the outgoing yield beyond R falls as ~R^-3 and that nothing is observable.
  - They separate only on whether calculation B gives a finite non-zero total of 0.003-0.03 x A (C3), or zero, as in C2's strong variant "identically zero for every vacuum configuration".
  - The same calculation decides it. Any non-zero B (even 1e-3 x A) favours C3 over the strong variant. C2's main claim (convergence) is shared, so it is not a discriminator.
  - The cheap analytic precursor is to evaluate B&O's eq. 5 field at t = -10/sigma, r = 10/sigma (natural units). A non-zero incoming shell confirms that A contains the in x out overlap that B lacks [examiner hardest question 1].
- **vs C6 (sustained fusion only in multi-directional ensembles; eq. 14 = kinetic rate).**
  - These are compatible: C3's time-symmetric shell is itself a two-direction (in x out) ensemble.
  - The calculation that would make them conflict: integrate C6's kinetic rate (1/2) n^2 <sigma(1 - cos theta)> over the in x out overlap 4-volume of the Pauli-Jordan shell and compare it with (4/25) G^2 N^2 w^3 sigma.
  - Agreement within O(1) means C3's mechanism is C6's kinetics. A large excess of the coherent value over the kinetic one would refute C6's "coherence adds nothing" while leaving C3 intact.
  - Precision: a factor ~2. The 0.982 and 0.931 coherent/kinetic ratios that C6 cites are unverified [M-IDEALIZER-14].
- **vs C7 (classical h^(2) nonlinearity, hbar-independent).**
  - These are compatible: C3's claim is about which classical field the functional is evaluated on.
  - The test: evaluate the D-60-type Maxwell yield on the classical O(h^2) metric of (A) and of (B).
  - C7 is favoured if A reproduces eq. 8 x T_eff with no graviton quantisation. C3 is favoured if B/A comes out at 0.003-0.03 in that semiclassical form too.
  - A coherent-state in-out result that differs from the semiclassical A at leading order would count against C7, not C3.
  - Precision: O(1).
