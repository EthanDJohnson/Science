## Response to the verdicts

All three verdicts return "weakened" and make the same main attack. They uphold the same core. I concede the mechanism and keep the core.

**C7 as narrowed (the version defended below).** B&O's N^2 pair yield is semiclassical vacuum pair creation of the quantised Maxwell field by a classical GW background, taken at second order in the linear wave h^(1). The amplitude needs all three gauge-dependent pieces, which are gauge invariant only as a sum:
- the contact term;
- t/u photon exchange;
- the s-channel on h^(2).

No graviton quantisation enters. The spontaneous pair number is hbar-independent. "N^2" is (h_c)^4, the n1 n2 of binary collisions. It is not pair creation on h^(2), and not a classical field nonlinearity.

**1. "Pair creation on h^(2)" is wrong (all three verdicts). Conceded in full.**
- Refuter 0 found that the h^(2)-only (Part-D-type) yield differs from the full tree amplitude by factors from 0 to infinity:
  - R/R at 90 deg: 8.26 against 0;
  - head-on R/L: 1.3e-64 against 1;
  - plus/plus at 30, 90 and 180 deg: 22.2, 10.75 and 3.
- Refuter 1 showed the h^(2) and contact channels are exactly zero in B&O's J_z = ±4 channel in TT gauge. There, photon exchange carries 100% of the amplitude (Y_s/Y_full = 0).
- Refuter 2 reproduced the ratio table independently.
- The gauge shifts move the channels by O(1) while the total stays fixed (-78.28 in refuter 0; -20.49 - 52.71i in refuter 1).
- Refuter 1's angular-momentum argument is clean: a rank-2 h^(2)(K) at rest has |J_z| <= 2, so it cannot carry J_z = ±4.
- The toolkit's own docstring warns of exactly this (references/tools.md: "An effective second-order metric fed to Part D captures only the s-channel").

I have no rebuttal. The examiner F10 and mechanist F9 wording that C7 relied on was wrong.

**2. Prediction 1 and the decisive test as written would fail. The test is replaced with the full second-order background-field yield, which is what refuter 2 says the prediction becomes "read that way". Two checks support the replacement:
- refuters 0 and 2 confirmed that this yield equals the kinetic closed form w^4 h^4 (1 - cos theta)^2/(1280 pi c^3) to 1e-12 (refuter 0: ratio 1.000000000000 at four angles; refuter 2: 2.912880e-101 m^-3 s^-1 both ways, SI);

My run reproduces the G-independence for R/L at theta = 0.5: 2.981375771339e-17 omega^4 at G = 1 and at G = 1e-3, natural units [calc: crux-C7].

**3. "Classical ... nonlinearity" in the heading overstates it. Conceded.** Classical Einstein–Maxwell keeps F = 0 [D-17, D-54]. C7's own argument already said this, as all three refuters note. The correct word is semiclassical: a classical driver acting on the quantised photon vacuum. Refuter 1 adds that the dominant photon-exchange piece is iterated linear coupling, not a nonlinearity even in the metric-dependence sense. I accept that too.

**4. C7 misread D-16. **5. "What follows": the time-like support criterion. The reason is conceded; the conclusion is kept, and I strengthen it with a calculation.** Refuters 0 and 1 say the right object is the time-like support of the bilinear h^(1) ⊗ h^(1) through the full vertex set, not of h^(2). Refuter 2 adds that every channel carries the same delta^4(q1 + q2 - k1 - k2), so the support condition holds for the full amplitude. I checked the collinear limit with the full (contact + t/u + s) background-field amplitude [calc: crux-C7]:

**6. The pair number is hbar-free only for the spontaneous part (refuter 2). Conceded, with the scaling made explicit.** Under Rayleigh–Jeans occupation f = kT/(hbar w), the stimulated extra pair number per packet is 8 G^2 E^2 kT sigma/(25 c^10 hbar), so d ln/d ln hbar = -1. So C7's last clause holds in vacuum only. The 1/hbar photon number of the stimulated term is the photon count of a classical field, which supports C7's statement that the stimulated part is classical parametric scattering. The size of the stimulated term rests on f_gamma = 3.8e8 at 150 Hz [D-43]. That value is a Rayleigh–Jeans extrapolation about 9 decades below ARCADE-2's 3 GHz [D-09].

## Deciding observation

C7 (narrowed) is a reframe. On the size and location of the yield it is compatible with every rival named, and it inherits C2's direction through the theta^4 law above. The test that could break C7 against all of them at once is this:

**Common test.** Compute the in-in mean photon-pair number of a coherent graviton state at O(G^2 N^2) and compare it with the semiclassical yield of the full second-order background-field functional evaluated on that state's classical field <h>:
- the functional is contact + t/u + s, with a quantised Maxwell field only;
- C7 predicts equality at leading order;
- quantum-graviton corrections may enter only at relative order 1/N, which is 1/(3.6e78) = 2.8e-79 for GW150914 [D-42: N0 = 3.6e78];
- any difference of relative order 1 refutes C7.

Tree level for crossing beams already passes (refuters 0 and 2; the toolkit's gw_coherent_fusion reproduces two-beam rate x overlap 4-volume to a ratio of 1.002, per references/tools.md). The open case is a localised packet with the retarded or Pauli–Jordan field.

Who could do it: B&O, or the coherent-state waveform groups [D-02, D-03]. Nothing is published [dossier section 6: no published classical second-order Einstein–Maxwell photon calculation for crossing GWs].

Rival by rival:

- **vs C2 (null beyond a few wavelengths).**
  - They do not conflict. C7's reframe makes the persistence question hbar-free.
  - Deciding calculation: the full second-order background-field in-in yield of a retarded spin-2 quadrupole field beyond radius R. The pairs come from non-collinear Fourier pairs of h^(1) with time-like K, not from h^(2).
  - C7 predicts that the coherent-state calculation gives the same slope as this semiclassical one. C2 predicts the slope is below -1 (toys: about -3).
  - If the coherent-state slope were +1 while the semiclassical slope were below -1, the result would favour C2's physics but refute C7's equivalence. If both are +1, C2 falls and C7 stands.
  - Precision: the sign of d ln P(>R)/d ln R, an O(1) slope.
- **vs C3 (time-symmetric state).**
  - Under C7, C3's question is purely classical: which classical field does B&O's state carry? The cheap analytic test separates them: is <h> of eq. 5 non-zero as an incoming shell at t = -10/sigma, r = 10 c/sigma? (C3's own test.)
  - C7's distinct prediction is that the full second-order functional applied to the Pauli–Jordan field reproduces B&O's per-packet total 4 G^2 E^2 w sigma/(25 c^10), times pattern factors, and that applied to the retarded field it gives C3's reduced total.
  - If B&O's coherent-state total differs from the semiclassical yield of its own <h>, C7 fails, whichever of C3 or C4 is right.
  - Precision: a factor ~2, enough to resolve C3's 0.02–0.1 against C4's 0.3–3.
- **vs C4 (near-source total, about eq. 8 x T_eff).**
  - Same structure as C3. One correction carries over from the verdicts on C7: C4's decisive test as written feeds NR h_mn "including h^(2)" into the Redi–Tesi functional, and that captures only the s-channel. On refuter 1's calculation it gives zero in B&O's J_z = ±4 channel.
  - The deciding calculation is the full second-order background-field yield on the NR h^(1) of a GW150914-like merger, compared with B&O's per-packet total.
  - C7 predicts that this classical-input calculation, with no graviton quantisation, gives the same number as any coherent-state calculation of it, whatever that number is. C4 predicts the number lies within ~3 of eq. 8 x T_eff.
  - Not done.
- **vs C5 (linear channel or Hawking emission dominates).**
  - C5's linear channel is also hbar-free: P_lin = k G E w/c^5 (SI) [calc: crux-C7]. The ratio (P_BO/P_lin) x k = (4/25) G E sigma/c^5 = 3.15e-4 for GW150914 [calc: crux-C7], which matches C5's 3.2e-4/k [M-EXAMINER-09].
  - So C5 is also semiclassical. It is the legitimate use of D-60: linear in the source's own h^(1) at time-like q, which is where C7 misapplied it.
  - Deciding calculation: k for an NR merger-ringdown, from the D-60 kernel with c_J = 16 on the source's h^(1) at time-like q.
  - If k >~ 3e-4, the N^2 term that C7 reframes is subdominant. That leaves C7's reframe true but about a minor channel. If k << 3e-4, C7's subject is the leading pair channel.
  - Neither outcome tests C7's hbar claim, because both channels are hbar-free.
- **vs C6 (isotropic background, kinetic rate).**
  - C6's eq. 14 = kinetic rate is C7's "N^2 = n1 n2 of binary collisions" applied to an ensemble. They agree.
  - Deciding calculation: the coherent yield of fixed-phase broadband multi-directional configurations, against the kinetic rate x overlap 4-volume (the unverified 0.982 and 0.931 of idealizer F13), averaged over random phases.
  - Both C6 and C7 predict that the phase average equals the kinetic rate to within quadrature error (gw_coherent_fusion's crossing-beam check: 1.002). A phase-averaged deviation of more than O(few %) would refute both. A per-realisation deviation of a few % refutes neither.
  - There is no observational test: the shortfall is >= 1e71 [M-CONSTRAINTS-03].
