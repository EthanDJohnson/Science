# Verdict on C6 (refuter 0, angle: strongest available: consistency, applied to GPP's own equations and backed by cited evidence)
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation

## Strongest attack
C6 makes four claims:
- (i) a **universal** clock-accuracy limit δT ~ t_P^(2/3) T^(1/3);
- (ii) a decoherence exponent of **(3/2)** t_P^(4/3) T^(2/3) ω12²;
- (iii) this makes quantum evolution **fundamentally** non-unitary;
- (iv) as a stated consequence, it removes the black-hole information problem [D-20].

I test each against the equations of the source, Gambini, Porto & Pullin 2004 (GPP; PRL 93, 240401, hep-th/0406260, full text fetched). All numbers are in SI units; GPP use ħ = c = 1. Script: [calc: runs/2026-09-29-problem-of-time-quantum-gravity/calc/falsifier-C6-0_gpp_bound.py].

**1. The black-hole consequence fails inside GPP's own model (calculation; internal inconsistency).**
- GPP Eq. (5) gives |ρ12(T)/ρ12(0)| = ((Tmax − T)/Tmax)^k with k = 1/(8π)² = 1.58e-3 (dimensionless). Coherence therefore vanishes only in the limit T → Tmax.
- One Planck time before evaporation (T = Tmax − t_P), the coherence left is:
  - 0.650 for a solar-mass hole (Tmax = 6.6e74 s);
  - 0.794 for a 1e12 kg hole (Tmax = 8.4e19 s).
- A single e-fold of decoherence is reached only at Tmax − T = 10^-199.5 s (solar mass) or 10^-254.4 s (1e12 kg). Both are far below t_P = 10^-43.3 s.
- Those intervals are shorter than the Planck-scale resolution the model itself sets. They also lie where the semiclassical evaporation model, and the divergent σ(T) ∝ (Tmax − T)^(-1/3), have no validity.
- Eq. (5)'s prefactor also does not follow from GPP's own Eq. (4) and Eq. (2). Using them, ω12²σ = 1/((8π)⁴(Tmax − T)), so k = 1/(8π)⁴ = 2.5e-6, not 1/(8π)². With the consistent k, the coherence left at Tmax − t_P is 0.9993.
- So "all information is lost through the fundamental decoherence before the black hole can evaporate" (GPP abstract) does not follow from the paper's own formulas at any resolvable time. This part of C6's "gives up" ledger should be struck.

**2. The rate is clock-relative, not universal (calculation).**
- GPP's stated spread rate is σ(T) = t_P (t_P/(Tmax − T))^(1/3), where Tmax is the interval the clock is optimised for.
- The decoherence built up by a fixed elapsed time of 1 s (Sr, ω12 = 2π × 429 THz) depends on that choice:
  - 2.22e-27 for Tmax = 1 s;
  - 3.35e-29 for Tmax = 1 day;
  - 4.68e-30 for Tmax = 1 yr;
  - 1.95e-33 for Tmax = 13.8 Gyr.
- These values span a factor of 1.1e6. The same physical system over the same elapsed time gets different "fundamental" decoherence depending on which clock one chooses.
- This is the clock-relativity of C2 (M-DIALECTICIAN-03/04), not a single universal law. GPP's own text concedes the point: "the argument is based on ordinary (unitary) quantum mechanics, we are just recasting the theory in terms of a realistic clocks and this is the root of the loss of unitarity". Claim (iii)'s word "fundamentally" is therefore stronger than the source supports.

**3. The (3/2) coefficient is not fixed by the paper (calculation).**
- GPP define σ(T) = ∂(δT)²/∂T. Applying that definition to their Eq. (1), (δT)² = t_P^(4/3) T^(2/3), gives the exponent 1 · t_P^(4/3) T^(2/3) ω².
- A direct Gaussian average of exp(−iωt) with spread δT gives 1/2.
- The quoted 3/2 arises only from GPP's separately stated σ ∝ (Tmax − T)^(-1/3), integrated all the way to T = Tmax. That σ is not the derivative of their Eq. (1): the derivative is (2/3) t_P^(4/3) T^(-1/3).
- The coefficient is therefore uncertain by a factor of 3 (1/2 to 3/2). At T = 1 s for Sr the exponent is 7.4e-28, 1.48e-27 or 2.22e-27. This does not change the order of magnitude or C6's "empirically idle" verdict.

**4. The T^(1/3) law, and with it any accumulating decoherence, rests on one contested premise (calculation plus cited evidence).**
- *Where the T^(1/3) comes from.* It enters only through the Salecker–Wigner wave-packet-spreading bound for a freely moving clock mass, (δT)² ≳ ħTmax/(mc²) (GPP p. 1). Combined with "clock size/c ≤ δT and no black hole", that bound gives δT_min = (2ħG T/c⁵)^(1/3).
- *Without that premise.* A clock-agnostic version keeps only energy–time resolution δT ≥ ħ/(2ΔE), mass-energy ≥ ΔE, and the no-black-hole light-crossing limit. It gives δT_min = √(ħG/c⁵) = t_P, which does not depend on T.
- *Consequence in GPP's own Eq. (2).* For a T-independent δT, σ = ∂(δT)²/∂T = 0. The decoherence rate vanishes, leaving at most a fixed, non-accumulating blur of t_P²ω²/2 = 1.1e-56 for Sr. With that, the T^(2/3) slope (C6's discriminating prediction) and the black-hole argument both disappear.
- *Published challenge to the premise.* The Ng–van Dam bound that GPP cite as their ref. [2] has a published counterexample. Baez & Olson (CQG 19, L121, 2002; abstract fetched): "Here we show that one can go below the Ng-van Dam bound by attaching the measuring device to a massive elastic rod. Relativistic limitations on the rod's rigidity, together with the constraint that its length exceeds its Schwarzschild radius, imply that zero-point fluctuations of the rod give an uncertainty greater than or equal to L_P." That is a T-independent Planck floor, which is exactly premise set 2 above.
- *The dispute is live.* Ng & van Dam replied (CQG 20, 393, 2003; gr-qc/0209021, abstract fetched): "Recently Baez and Olson have claimed that one can go below this bound by attaching the measuring device to a massive elastic rod. Here we refute their claim."
- *Result.* The universal δT ~ t_P^(2/3) T^(1/3) is a disputed heuristic, not a theorem. The candidate's own evidence against (K-08) concedes this. What C6 does not concede is that the T-scaling carries the whole effect.

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle:** C6 is internally coherent only as a conditional. If every physical clock obeys a free-mass-spreading (Salecker–Wigner) bound together with the black-hole limit, then relational evolution referred to such a clock dephases in the energy basis. The exponent is of order t_P^(4/3) T^(2/3) ω² (coefficient 1/2 to 3/2), and the underlying dynamics is unitary in the unobservable parameter t. The antecedent is disputed (Baez–Olson against Ng–van Dam). The black-hole consequence does not follow even when the antecedent is granted (point 1).
- **In practice:** unobservable. The exponent is about 1e-27 at 1 s for an optical transition, and it would be about 1e-56 and non-accumulating if the floor is t_P. No experiment within 20 years can test it. This agrees with C6's own "Observation: none" and with GPP ("too small to be observed in the lab").

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **Survives, in narrower form.** If one grants the disputed T^(1/3) clock bound, conditioning on the best allowed clock gives Gaussian energy-basis dephasing of order t_P^(4/3) T^(2/3) ω² (slope 2/3). This is a clock-relative, not fundamental, loss of unitarity in the sense of C2. Its size depends on the clock's design horizon Tmax and on an unfixed O(1) coefficient.
- **Does not survive as stated:**
  - "universal", since the rate depends on the clock choice by up to 1e6 at 1 s;
  - "fundamentally non-unitary", since GPP themselves ground it in unitary QM;
  - the exact "(3/2)";
  - the black-hole-information consequence, which is not reached before the last 1e-199 s of evaporation.
- **Remaining doubt:**
  - The Baez–Olson versus Ng–van Dam exchange is unresolved in the sources I could open (abstracts only). If Ng–van Dam's rebuttal holds for clocks, the T^(1/3) bound stands as a heuristic, and point 4 only weakens C6 rather than removing its accumulating effect.
  - My premise set 2 assumes a clock whose centre of mass does not freely spread, for example one that is bound or rigidly anchored. Whether such anchoring costs something that restores T-dependence is the content of that dispute.
  - I did not open GPP's longer NJP 6, 45 derivation. It may fix the coefficient or the σ(T) form differently from the PRL.
  - Points 1–3 are internal to the PRL's formulas and do not depend on this dispute.
