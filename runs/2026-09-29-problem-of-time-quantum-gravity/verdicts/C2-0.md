## Strongest attack

C2's headline makes three strong claims: (i) it dissolves the frozen formalism **exactly**; (ii) it **answers** Kuchař's two-time objection; (iii) it recovers GR time dilation as a **unitary** relational redshift. It then says its cost is only "a global time, a unique clock, and flow as a property of the global state". Each of (i) to (iii) is established only for a clock that does not interact with the system, or whose coupling to it commutes with everything else. That second case means the geometry is a c-number. Yet the regime where the problem of time actually exists is quantum, dynamical geometry, and there these results either fail or need a price the headline does not list.

1. **The source C2 relies on limits itself to non-interacting clocks, and says interactions bring non-unitarity (cited evidence).** The trinity theorem (Höhn–Smith–Lock 2021; D-06, K-09) assumes a constraint C_H = H_C + H_S with "no interaction". The authors call this "a strong restriction" in GR and "untenable in generic models". They add that interactions "will appear in generic models, particularly so in quantum gravity" and lead "to a non-unitarity of the relational dynamics in the turning regime of the clock", and that "these challenges must also appear in the Page-Wootters formalism". So C2's "trinity", its exact unitarity and its Kuchař reply all rest on a theorem whose own authors restrict it to the idealised non-interacting case. They also say that gravity makes that case generically false.

2. **When the geometry is quantum, the "unitary relational redshift" loses Hermiticity in the kinematical inner product (calculation).** I used C2's own Smith–Ahmadi coupling −λ(x̂)H_C H_S with an ideal clock (H_C = p_t, spectrum ℝ). The only change was to make the clock–system separation a dynamical quantum degree of freedom: two sites with a hopping term J. The conditional state then obeys iψ' = H_eff ψ exactly, with H_eff = B⁻¹C, B = 1 − λ(x̂)H_S and C = H_S + K. H_eff is Hermitian iff [λ(x̂), K] = 0.
   - Control, fixed (c-number) separation: the anti-Hermitian part is 0 and the kinematic norm stays at 1.000000. This reproduces C2's verified redshift.
   - Superposed, dynamical separation (λ = 0.05 s and 0.20 s, J = 0.5 rad/s, ω = 1 rad/s): the anti-Hermitian part is 4.93e-2 rad/s, and the kinematic norm of the conditional state wanders over [1.000000, 1.091054].
   - Scaling: the anti-Hermitian part ≈ 0.50·J·ω·Δλ. Checked at four parameter points, ratio 0.505–0.510.
   - What is conserved: the B-weighted norm ⟨ψ|B|ψ⟩, with drift ≤ 1.5e-14.
   - The constraint residual is at the finite-difference level (≤ 1.4e-4 at dt = 0.02 s), so these are genuine null states.

   So "unitarity" survives only after changing to a geometry- and coupling-dependent inner product B. That inner product also depends on how the constraint is rescaled: p_t B + C and p_t + B^(−1/2) C B^(−1/2) have the same solutions but different group-averaged norms. This is facet 3 (inner product) and facet 2 (choice) coming back inside C2's own gravitational coupling. C2's headline does not list this cost, and the trinity theorem does not cover it.

3. **C2's headline contradicts its own body (internal inconsistency).** The argument section's "Gives up" line also lists "exact unitarity once the clock couples generically" and "positivity of the inner product across frequency sectors". Its evidence-against section adds: "Gravity couples every clock to energy, so the realistic clock is the coupled one [K-09]". The headline's list of costs (global time, unique clock, flow) leaves both out. Taking the body at its word, the headline's "exactly" and "unitary" hold only for the idealised clock that the body itself calls unrealistic.

4. **The Kuchař answer is shown only for uncoupled clocks.** The verified record-conditioned Born values (0.146447, 0.500000, 0.853553, to about 1e-15) come from a GLM lattice history with an impulsive memory. By C2's own admission this is "not the null state of a coupled constraint". HSL's two-time resolution is proven inside the non-interacting framework (item 1). No independent assessment exists [D-19]. The dossier also records HSL's caveat that two-time gauge invariance is "not established" by Theorems 3–4, but the checker marked that sentence unverifiable, and I did not re-find it either. So I do not rely on it.

5. **"Recovers GR time dilation" means weak-field, Newtonian order, with c-number separation.** Smith–Ahmadi's own abstract describes the gravity example as corrections "on the order of G/c⁴ and inversely proportional to the distance". Its size is λω = 2.35e-60 (dimensionless) for an optical transition at 1 mm, which no experiment can test. This is a consistent embedding of first-order redshift, not a derivation of GR proper time. Facet 7 stays open, as C2 concedes.

## What survives and remaining doubt

**What survives, in narrower form.** For a clock that does not interact with the system (ideal, or a covariant POVM), and for couplings that are functions of commuting operators (Smith–Ahmadi with a c-number separation), relational dynamics does dissolve facet 1 exactly. The trinity equivalence holds, the conditional evolution is exactly unitary, and there are record-conditioned two-time Born probabilities. All of this is verified by the math checks to about 1e-15. In that regime, GR time dilation fits in as a first-order unitary redshift of size about 1e-60 in the lab. Relational *change* (the absence of freezing) also survives once coupling is present. What changes is that the evolution becomes time-nonlocal (Smith–Ahmadi), non-unitary near clock turning points (HSL), or unitary only in a coupling-dependent inner product (my calculation).

**What does not survive as stated.** "Exactly" and "unitary" as properties of relational time in quantum gravity. "Answers Kuchař" beyond non-interacting clocks. A cost list limited to global time, a unique clock and flow. The honest cost list must add, in the headline: exact unitarity, or a standard kinematical inner product, once clocks couple through quantum geometry. It must also add the facet-3 inner-product ambiguity from rescaling the constraint.

**Doubts about my own attack:**
- A defender can say that the B-inner product *is* the physical inner product, so unitarity is not lost but relocated. I accept that. This is why the verdict is "weakened" and not "refuted": it is a hidden cost and a limit on scope, not a contradiction.
- My model is finite-dimensional, with a two-site separation and toy-sized λ. The lab-scale effect is of order λω ~ 1e-60, so this is an in-principle point only.
- HSL's "may lead to serious challenges" is hedged. But their conclusion that the challenges "must also appear in the Page-Wootters formalism" is stated flatly.
