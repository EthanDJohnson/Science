# Verdict on C8 (refuter 0, angle: strongest available: cost and consistency, backed by cited evidence and one calculation)
verdict: weakened
basis: internal-inconsistency

## Strongest attack
C8 is a null with a disjunctive form. Every consistent position either (a) leaves facets 2 (multiple choice), 4 (global time) and 7 (spacetime reconstruction) open in strongly quantum, gravitationally coupled geometry, plus facet 5 (functional evolution) once constraints are local; or (b) removes them by modifying QM or adding background structure. A null of this kind is refuted only by a position that resolves those facets in that regime without paying cost (b). So I tried three things: to supply such a position; to show the disjunction is not exhaustive; and to show that C8's own statement or predictions contradict the slate's verified results.

**1. No counterexample survives (cited evidence), so the core null is not refuted.**
- *Facet 2 as a "feature" (Höhn & Vanrietvelde 2020, NJP 22 123048, arXiv:1810.04153, ACCESS abstract via fetch_text).* This is the strongest published dissolution of multiple choice. The abstract says the method "suggests a new perspective on the multiple choice problem, indicating that it is rather a multiple choice feature of a complete relational quantum theory". It is illustrated only "by means of the parametrized particle", and it concedes that "quantum uncertainties lead to discontinuity in the relational dynamics when switching clocks". Against this, in a gravitational (FLRW + scalar + fluid) model, demanding unitarity in different clocks gives physically different predictions: singularity resolution in one clock, "quantum recollapse" in another [D-03]. That is not a harmless change of frame. A new paper argues the ambiguity is worse than thought: "the clock ambiguity cannot be solved by a purely relational condition like the noninteraction condition, and it is even stronger, extending to evolution laws" [new: Stoica 2026, arXiv:2604.21805, preprint, ACCESS abstract via fetch_text]. Facet 2 therefore stays open in the gravitational case, as C8 says.
- *Local resolutions (Anderson).* Anderson's book claims that "a local resolution of the Problem of Time can be arrived at after various reconceptualizations of the facets" [new: Anderson 2017, *The Problem of Time*, Springer, ACCESS abstract via INSPIRE fetch_text]. His quantum-level work is "a local solution ... at the conceptual level and which is actually realizable for the relational triangle" [new: Anderson 2011, arXiv:1111.1472, preprint, ACCESS abstract via lit_search]. That is a local, toy-model (relational particle mechanics) result. It leaves the global facet 4 open by construction and does not reach gravitationally coupled field theory. It supports C8.
- *Framework-change proposals.* Klinger & Leigh argue the problem of time indicates "an issue within the framework itself" and propose that anomalous (projectively represented) null-surface constraint algebras "replace the standard interpretation of canonical time evolution with a fully quantum, diffeomorphism invariant notion of time" [new: Klinger & Leigh 2025, arXiv:2504.00152, GRF essay, preprint, ACCESS abstract via fetch_text]. This is a proposal, not a demonstrated resolution of facets 2, 4 and 7.

**2. The disjunction is not exhaustive as worded (cost; internal consistency).** Branch (b) names only "modifying QM or adding background structure". At least two routes fit neither branch:
- *Reinterpretation.* Höhn–Vanrietvelde recast facet 2 as a covariance feature. Rovelli-style "forget time" dissolutions do the same for facets 2 and 4. Neither leaves the facet "open" in its own terms, and neither modifies QM or adds background. C8 files them under "open" only on evidential grounds (point 1). This makes C8's standard asymmetric. It accepts dissolution as resolution for facet 1 (C2) and facet 5 (C1: "Several facets are already dissolved at no cost beyond a global flow", C8's own evidence-against), but refuses it for facet 2. Point 1 supplies the missing justification: the facet-2 dissolution is undemonstrated in gravity and contradicted in one gravitational model [D-03]. The null should say so explicitly.
- *Modifying the quantization of gravity* (anomalous or centrally extended constraint algebras; Klinger–Leigh) or adding physical matter clocks (Brown–Kuchař dust, scalar clocks in deparametrized LQG). Dust is dynamical matter, not "background structure". C8 can absorb it only through branch (a): the dust clock is one choice among many, so facet 2 stays open [D-10, F-02].
- The dichotomy therefore holds only as "no route has been *shown* to close facets 2 and 4 in gravity". It is not a proof that no route can.

**3. The facet-7 clause is partly true by definition (consistency).** "Spacetime reconstruction ... open in strongly quantum geometry" cannot fail. A strongly quantum state of geometry has no classical spacetime to reconstruct, just as a strongly quantum state of the electromagnetic field has no classical field configuration, and nobody counts that against QED. C8's own refutation criterion is the operational one ("such a construction exists and recovers GR proper times"), which is a *limit* requirement. On that reading, C3's Born–Oppenheimer/WKB expansion already recovers a classical background with local proper times at leading order [D-09; M-IDEALIZER-07 verified], formally for the full Wheeler–DeWitt functional equation. The non-vacuous claim is narrower: facet 7 is open *beyond leading semiclassical order* and *in non-perturbative frameworks* (LQG/spin foams), where the limit is unproven.

**4. C8's quantitative prediction contradicts the slate's verified math (calculation; internal inconsistency).** C8 predicts "No experiment within 20 years separates C2, C3, C4, C5 and C6. Their calculated differences are at most 1e-11 fractional". [calc: runs/2026-09-29-problem-of-time-quantum-gravity/calc/falsifier-C8-0_null_predictions.py]
- *C5 vs C2 differ at order one, not ≤ 1e-11.* For the non-Gibbs 3-level state used by C5 (E = 0, 1, 3 rad/s; weights 0.5, 0.2, 0.3; ħ = 1), the modular/energy ratios are 0.9163 and 0.1703 rad⁻¹·s [M-IDEALIZER-06 verified; reproduced]. If the modular parameter is matched to the clock on level 1 over t = 2π s, level 2's phase is off by 15.3 rad, a fractional mismatch of 0.81 (dimensionless). C5 itself states the empirical content: "If false: physical clocks in non-equilibrium states track Hamiltonian time and not the modular flow." So C5 and C2 differ at O(1) in what they say physical clocks track. Either C5 is already strained by ordinary non-equilibrium clocks, which is then an observation that separates it, or C5 must be read as making no clock prediction. In neither case is "≤ 1e-11" correct for C5.
- *The 1e-11 figure is itself an underestimate.* (E/m_P)² at E = 1e14 GeV is 1.42e-11 (Kiefer convention m_P = 2.650e19 GeV) and 6.71e-11 (standard 1.221e19 GeV) [M-CONSTRAINTS-10, M-IDEALIZER-13 verified; reproduced]. Both exceed "at most 1e-11". If the relevant scale is the inflationary energy (~1e16 GeV) rather than H, the values are 1.4e-7 to 6.7e-7 (natural units, GeV). The conclusion "no test within 20 years" still stands, because the CMB correction remains ~1e10 below cosmic variance [M-CONSTRAINTS-10], but the stated bound is wrong.
- The C6 vs C2 difference, 1.28e-15 (dimensionless) at 13.8 Gyr for the Sr transition, does fall inside the bound [M-CONSTRAINTS-12 verified; reproduced].

## In principle vs. in practice
- *In principle:* C8 is a claim about the state of knowledge, not a theorem. Nothing I found shows facets 2 and 4 *cannot* be closed. Höhn–Vanrietvelde's covariance programme and Klinger–Leigh's anomaly route are live proposals that could falsify C8 by meeting its own decisive test. C8 is refutable, and its refutation criterion (a clock-covariant, unitary, positive-norm, anomaly-free relational dynamics for local constraints that recovers GR proper times) is well posed.
- *In practice:* C8's claim that the conservative positions are empirically equivalent holds for C2, C3, C4 and C6, with differences ≤ ~1e-10 in cosmology and ~1e-60 in the lab. It fails for C5 as stated, because C5 carries an O(1) prediction about non-equilibrium clocks.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **Survives (narrower form):** "No position has been *shown* to close facets 2 and 4, or facet 5 for local gravitational constraints, in quantum, gravitationally coupled geometry without modifying QM, modifying the quantization framework, or adding structure or matter clocks. Facet 7 is closed only at leading semiclassical order."
- **Dropped or corrected:**
  - the implicit claim that the (a)/(b) dichotomy is exhaustive;
  - the definitional reading of facet 7 "in strongly quantum geometry";
  - the prediction "differences at most 1e-11" for C5, and the 1e-11 figure itself (1.4e-11 to 6.7e-11 at 1e14 GeV).
- **Doubt:**
  - Recent QRF and algebraic work (observer-dressed crossed-product algebras; De Vuyst et al. 2025) is moving fast. I checked abstracts only, not full texts.
  - A 2025–26 construction for local constraints could exist beyond what these searches found.
  - I chose weakened rather than survives because points 3 and 4 are concrete defects in C8's wording and predictions. The core null itself is not refuted.
