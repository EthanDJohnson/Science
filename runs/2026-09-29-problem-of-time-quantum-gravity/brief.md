# Brief: The problem of time — reconciling quantum-mechanical and general-relativistic time
slug: 2026-09-29-problem-of-time-quantum-gravity | depth: standard | type: foundations | date: 2026-09-29

## Question as asked
"Problem of time: In quantum mechanics, time is a classical background parameter, and the flow of time is universal and absolute. In general relativity, time is one component of four-dimensional spacetime, and the flow of time changes depending on the curvature of spacetime and the spacetime trajectory of the observer. How can these two concepts of time be reconciled?"

## Question made precise
The question is read as: **which positions on the problem of time are internally consistent, what does each give up, and could any observation tell them apart?** The answers are positions (resolve, dissolve, or modify), not a single derivation.

Definitions of loaded terms:
- **"Time in quantum mechanics."** Three regimes, to be kept apart:
  - *Non-relativistic QM (NRQM):* t is an external parameter, not an observable. The Schrödinger equation, unitarity (conservation of the inner product in t) and the Born rule on equal-t slices all presuppose it. Pauli's argument: no self-adjoint time operator is canonically conjugate to a Hamiltonian bounded below.
  - *Relativistic QFT on Minkowski space:* no absolute time; any inertial time (or any Cauchy foliation, Tomonaga–Schwinger) works, related by Poincaré symmetry.
  - *QFT on curved spacetime (QFTCS) and semiclassical gravity:* time is a fixed, non-dynamical background structure (a foliation, or a timelike Killing field where one exists). Evolution between arbitrary Cauchy surfaces need not be unitarily implementable (Torre–Varadarajan), and the vacuum is observer-dependent (Unruh, Hawking effects).
- **"Time in general relativity."** Coordinate time is gauge (diffeomorphism invariance); proper time along a worldline is observable. In the canonical (ADM) formulation of a spatially closed universe the Hamiltonian is a sum of constraints, H = ∫(N𝓗 + Nⁱ𝓗ᵢ) ≈ 0, so "evolution" is a gauge transformation. For asymptotically flat spacetimes a boundary (ADM) energy survives.
- **"The problem of time"** — the unresolved core. Quantizing the constraints gives the Wheeler–DeWitt equation ĤΨ = 0, with no external time. Following Kuchař (1992), Isham (1993) and Anderson (2017), treat it as distinct facets, and say which facets each position addresses:
  1. frozen formalism (no evolution in ĤΨ = 0);
  2. multiple choice (different internal clocks give inequivalent quantum theories);
  3. Hilbert space / inner product (Klein–Gordon-type inner products are not positive definite);
  4. global time (no internal clock is monotonic everywhere);
  5. functional evolution (consistency of evolution between arbitrary hypersurfaces);
  6. observables (constructing Dirac observables that commute with the constraints);
  7. spacetime reconstruction (recovering a classical spacetime and its local proper times).
- **"Flow of time."** Neither theory contains passage; both contain a parameter or a structure. The physics question — what plays the role of time in the dynamics, and how GR's proper times and QM's evolution parameter emerge from one framework — is in scope. Metaphysical passage (A- versus B-theories) is out of scope, except where a position ties it to physics (e.g., thermal time and the arrow of time).
- **"Reconcile."** Any of: (a) a single framework that recovers QM's parameter time and GR's proper times in the appropriate limits; (b) a demonstration that the conflict is only apparent; (c) a modification of QM or GR. Each position must say which it is.

Admissible physics:
- **Established:** NRQM, relativistic QFT, QFTCS, classical GR (covariant and ADM/canonical), semiclassical gravity as an effective theory where it is valid, and experiments such as clock redshift and gravitational time dilation (down to the millimetre scale), neutron and atom interferometry in gravity, and laboratory illustrations of Page–Wootters timelessness.
- **Speculative, allowed only when labelled:** every quantum-gravity framework (Wheeler–DeWitt and canonical QG, loop quantum gravity and cosmology, spin foams, string theory and holography, causal sets, shape dynamics, Hořava–Lifshitz and other preferred-foliation theories, unimodular gravity), collapse models, postquantum classical gravity, and interpretations (Everett, Bohm, consistent histories) where a position depends on one.

"Viable" has two tiers:
- **In principle:** internally consistent (math that checks), recovers NRQM/QFT time and GR proper time in the right limits, and contradicts no observation.
- **Distinguishable in practice:** an observation within roughly 20 years could separate it from rivals — for example gravitationally induced entanglement tests, collapse-model bounds, clocks in superpositions of heights or time-dilation decoherence, or cosmological signatures. Say which positions are empirically equivalent to one another.

Positions to cover (the slate may merge or add):
- **Relational time:** Page–Wootters conditional states; relational (partial/complete) Dirac observables (Rovelli, Dittrich); the "trinity" equivalence of these with quantum deparametrization (Höhn, Smith, Lock 2021); quantum reference frames for time; interacting and non-ideal clocks.
- **Semiclassical, emergent time:** a Born–Oppenheimer/WKB expansion of the Wheeler–DeWitt equation yields a Schrödinger equation for matter on a classical background (Banks; Brout–Venturi; Kiefer–Singh), with corrections at higher order and decoherence of the gravitational wavefunction.
- **Thermal time** (Connes–Rovelli): the flow of time is the modular flow of the statistical state. Include its modern relatives, such as observer/clock-dressed algebras in de Sitter and closed universes, if the research finds them.
- **Unimodular and other fundamental-time proposals:** unimodular gravity, where Λ is conjugate to a four-volume time (Unruh; Henneaux–Teitelboim); matter clocks that deparametrize (Brown–Kuchař dust, scalar-field clocks); preferred foliations (York/CMC time and shape dynamics, Hořava–Lifshitz).
- **Theories that modify QM:** gravity-related collapse (Diósi–Penrose); decoherence from realistic clocks (Gambini–Porto–Pullin); gravity kept classical (semiclassical Møller–Rosenfeld and Schrödinger–Newton, Oppenheim's postquantum classical gravity); histories formulations with no preferred time (Hartle's generalized QM).
- **Others the research turns up,** for example time from the boundary in holography, spin-foam or covariant path integrals, causal-set sequential growth, and "timeless" dissolutions (Barbour; Rovelli's "forget time").

How the research facets apply to this foundations question:
- *theory:* the formalisms above, and what is proven versus conjectured.
- *quantitative:* the numbers that bound or separate positions, e.g. collapse-model parameter bounds, clock-redshift precision, predicted decoherence rates and correction sizes.
- *critiques:* Kuchař's objections to the conditional-probability interpretation and the replies to them, the Unruh–Wald critique, no-go results for global time and unitarity, clock dependence of quantum cosmology, and failures of semiclassical gravity.
- *engineering:* read this as the **experimental state of the art**: what has actually been measured that bears on quantum time, such as Page–Wootters demonstrations, proper-time interferometry, clock redshift at small scales, and gravity-induced entanglement and collapse tests, each at its demonstrated scale and precision.

## Hidden premises to test
1. **"In QM time is a universal, absolute background."** True of NRQM. Relativistic QFT and QFTCS already accept relativity of simultaneity and gravitational time dilation on a fixed background. If so, the real conflict is narrower: a *dynamical or quantum* geometry, where the metric (and hence time) can be in superposition.
2. **QM needs an external time at all.** Page–Wootters, relational observables and sum-over-histories formulations claim to recover dynamics without one.
3. **GR's time is a physical "component of spacetime."** Coordinate time is gauge, and only relational quantities such as proper time along worldlines are observable. GR's time may already be relational.
4. **The Hamiltonian constraint necessarily freezes time.** This assumes a spatially closed universe (asymptotically flat spacetimes keep an ADM Hamiltonian) and Dirac quantization of the constraints. Reduced-phase-space and covariant path-integral quantizations may not agree with it.
5. **There is one reconciliation.** Positions may address different facets and be complementary (e.g., semiclassical time as a relational clock), rather than rivals.
6. **"Flow" is a physical notion either theory contains.**
7. **Gravity must be quantized.** Semiclassical gravity, postquantum classical gravity and emergent gravity deny this.
8. **The problem of time is independent of the measurement problem.** Collapse needs a time, Bohmian mechanics needs a preferred foliation, and Everettian conditional states fit relational time. The interpretation of QM may decide which positions are open.

## What counts as an answer
- A ranked comparison of positions. For each: what it claims; which facets of the problem (1–7 above) it resolves, dissolves or leaves open; what it gives up (unitarity, a global time, foliation independence, the standard Born rule, single outcomes, the quantum nature of gravity, …); its internal-consistency status, including known technical failures and whether published replies answer them; and whether any observation could tell it apart from rivals, with the best current bound where one exists.
- Which positions are mutually exclusive and which are compatible or complementary.
- **A worked toy model the math checks can verify:** a finite-dimensional Page–Wootters clock. The constraint (H_C ⊗ 1 + 1 ⊗ H_S)|Ψ⟩⟩ = 0; conditional states; recovery of Schrödinger evolution for an ideal clock; and what breaks for finite, degenerate or interacting clocks (non-unitary or time-nonlocal effective evolution, resolution limits). Kuchař's two-time propagator objection should be tested in the model. A minisuperspace Wheeler–DeWitt model with a Born–Oppenheimer/WKB derivation of the Schrödinger equation is welcome as a second model.
- A clear statement of whether the problem is solved, dissolved or open, facet by facet, and of what each surviving position would need to become established.
- Candidates that must be on the slate: the **null** ("no position resolves the problem of time within admissible physics; each leaves at least one facet open or pays an unacceptable cost") and a **reframe** (the question's premise misplaces the conflict; see hidden premises 1 and 3).
