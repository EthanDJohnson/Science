## Claims
- [RC-01] CLAIM: Kuchar's "reduction ad absurdum" of the Page-Wootters conditional probability interpretation (CPI): the naive two-clock-time conditional probability, built from a projector sandwich on the physical (constraint-satisfying) state, vanishes unless T'=T'' and Q'=Q'', so the interpretation "prohibits time to flow". Page's original reply was that only single-instant quantities are directly accessible. Dolby (2004) argues the formula used by Kuchar is the wrong two-time probability and gives a refined CPI in which multi-time questions are answered (for discrete E=0 spectrum, parametrized particle); Dolby states he does not defend Page's reply.
  SOURCE: C. E. Dolby 2004, "The Conditional probability interpretation of the Hamiltonian constraint", arXiv:gr-qc/0406034 (preprint, no journal ref), https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0406034
  QUOTE: "By your formula, that conditional probability diﬀers from zero only if T′ = T′′ and Q′ = Q′′. In short, your interpretation prohibits the time to flow and the system to move!” This is an important objection, which Kuchar refers to as a “reduction ad absurdum of the condition probability proposal”. Pages response (also recorded in [5]) was “In my viewpoint, only quantities at a single instant of time are directly accessible, and so one cannot directly test the two-time probability you discuss.” We do not intend to defend this response here."

- [RC-03] CLAIM: No-go for functional evolution (facet 5): on flat spacetime R x T^n with n>1 (toroidal Cauchy surfaces), the classical canonical transformation evolving a free Klein-Gordon field from a flat surface to a generic final Cauchy surface cannot be unitarily implemented on the Fock space. Evolution between surfaces related by an isometry (e.g. Poincare transformation) is unitary. In 1+1 dimensions evolution along arbitrary foliations is unitary (Torre-Varadarajan 1998). Failure is an ultraviolet effect; authors expect it on Minkowski space; consequence: the Tomonaga-Schwinger / Schrodinger picture for arbitrary foliations fails for free fields in d>2. Scope: free field, Poincare-invariant (standard Fock) representation; exotic CCR representations not excluded.
  SOURCE: C. Torre & M. Varadarajan 1999, "Functional evolution of free quantum fields", Class. Quantum Grav. 16, 2651, arXiv:hep-th/9811222; companion: "Quantum fields at any time", Phys. Rev. D 58, 064007 (arXiv:hep-th/9707221)
  QUOTE: "We show that this canonical transformation cannot, in gener al, be unitarily implemented on the Fock space for free quantum ﬁelds on ﬂat spacetimes of dimension greater than 2." and "It is not the curvature per se of the ﬁnal (and/or initial) surface which causes the proble m with unitarity, but rather that, in general, there is no isometry of the spacetime that w ill map the initial surface to the ﬁnal surface. Indeed, it is easy to see that evolution bet ween any two Cauchy surfaces related by an isometry is unitarily implemented."
  ACCESS: full-text
  STATUS: peer-reviewed
  CONFIDENCE: high

- [RC-05] CLAIM: Explicit clock dependence in a minisuperspace model (flat FLRW + massless scalar + perfect fluid, Wheeler-DeWitt quantization with clock-adapted inner product): demanding unitarity with respect to different clocks yields inequivalent quantum theories. With volume-like/fluid time t the clock is "slow" at the singularity and unitarity forces boundary conditions that resolve it; with the scalar field as clock the boundary condition sits at infinite volume, giving quantum recollapse at large volume (not singularity resolution). Scope: this one model; results are model- and clock-specific.
  SOURCE: S. Gielen & L. Menendez-Pidal 2022, "Unitarity, clock dependence and quantum recollapse in quantum cosmology", Class. Quantum Grav. 39, 075011, arXiv:2109.02660
  QUOTE: "When different notions of time exist, there will be corresponding different and inequivalent notions of unitarity." and "Rather than singularity resolution, this theory features a quantum recollapse of the universe at large volume" and "we have already seen in [8] that choosing diﬀerent variables as clocks leads to inequivalent quantum solutions, in particular regarding singularity resolution."
  ACCESS: full-text
  STATUS: peer-reviewed
  CONFIDENCE: high
- [RC-06] CLAIM: Unruh-Wald no-go (1989): in ordinary Schrodinger QM with Hamiltonian bounded below, no dynamical variable can correlate monotonically with the Schrodinger parameter t, so t cannot be replaced by a (sharp) dynamical clock variable in the conditional probability interpretation. They also argue that adding observers does not alleviate interpretive problems of quantum gravity. Strengthens Pauli's theorem (no self-adjoint T with [T,H]=i hbar if H bounded below) to any observable T monotonically correlated with t. Standard escape: unsharp clocks (POVMs) or clocks with unbounded spectrum (idealised).
  SOURCE: W. G. Unruh & R. M. Wald 1989, "Time and the interpretation of canonical quantum gravity", Phys. Rev. D 40, 2598 (abstract via INSPIRE API); description of theorem: arXiv:2607.01296 (preprint)
  QUOTE: "in ordinary Schrödinger quantum mechanics for a system with a Hamiltonian bounded from below, no dynamical variable can correlate monotonically with the Schrödinger time parameter t, and thus the role of t in the interpretation of Schrödinger quantum mechanics cannot be replaced by that of a dynamical variable. We also argue that the interpretive problems of quantum gravity are not alleviated by the incorporation of observers into the theo..." [truncated by source output]. Secondary (arXiv:2607.01296): "To avoid the problems raised by the Pauli and Unruh- Wald theorems, a usual strategy is to consider unsharp clock observables (positive operator-valued measure–POVM)".
  ACCESS: abstract (Unruh-Wald, INSPIRE); full-text (secondary)
  STATUS: peer-reviewed
  CONFIDENCE: high
- [RC-07] CLAIM: Published replies to Kuchar and Unruh-Wald: Hoehn-Smith-Lock (2021) claim that (i) covariant clock POVMs (non-ideal clocks) resolve the Unruh-Wald non-monotonicity issue, (ii) Kuchar's criticism that PW conditional probabilities violate the constraint is "incorrect" (they are quantum analogs of gauge-fixed gauge-invariant quantities), and (iii) Kuchar's wrong-propagator criticism is resolved exactly (no approximation, ideal clock, or ancilla) using a two-time conditional probability built from relational Dirac observables. Limits stated in the same paper: the time operator T and clock Hamiltonian form a Heisenberg pair (both self-adjoint) only for an ideal clock (Spec(H_C)=R); gauge-invariance was "not established" for the two-time conditioning directly (Theorems 3-4 apply to one-time conditioning), the two-time result is recovered via the algebra-homomorphism property; the equivalence of reduced-phase-space and Dirac quantization requires a condition (their Eq. 57) that fails in general; the formalism exhibits clock-dependent temporal nonlocality under clock change; scope is systems where the clock-system split, absence of interactions (or treated separately) and frequency superselection sectors are assumed.

- [RC-10] CLAIM: Semiclassical-time derivation (Born-Oppenheimer/WKB of Wheeler-DeWitt) has quantitative corrections that include a non-unitary piece: Kiefer-Singh find, to higher order in G, correction terms of two classes (breakdown of the classical background; quantum gravitational effects), with "a gravitationally induced violation of unitarity" in the general (non-adiabatic) case; the corrections are numerically extremely tiny except near the big bang and the final stages of a black hole, and are dependent on factor ordering. Scope: expansion in powers of G of the full functional WDW equation; not an experimental test.
  SOURCE: C. Kiefer & T. P. Singh 1991, "Quantum gravitational corrections to the functional Schrodinger equation", Phys. Rev. D 44, 1067 (abstract via INSPIRE API)
  QUOTE: "factor ordering which is chosen for the gravitational kinetic term. If the total state evolves adiabatically, the only correction term that survives contains the square of the matter Hamiltonian. In the general case there are also smaller terms which describe a gravitationally induced violation of unitarity. The corrections are numerically extremely tiny except near the big bang and the final stages of a black hole."
  ACCESS: abstract
  STATUS: peer-reviewed
  CONFIDENCE: high

- [RC-12] CLAIM: Gravity-related collapse (Diosi-Penrose) is empirically constrained: the Gran Sasso underground experiment (Ge detector; photon band 10-10^5 keV) sets a lower bound R0 >= 0.54e-10 m on the effective nuclear mass-density smearing radius, about one order of magnitude above the value (0.05e-10 m for Ge) implied by Penrose's argument, ruling out the natural parameter-free DP model; the bound is about three orders of magnitude stronger than previous bounds of that type. Scope: DP model with the specific spontaneous-radiation-emission channel; larger-R0 versions (a free parameter) remain allowed.
  SOURCE: S. Donadi et al. 2021, "Underground test of gravity-related wave function collapse", Nature Phys. 17, 74, arXiv:2111.13490
  QUOTE: "Our experiment sets a lower bound onR0 at 0.54× 10−10 m (green bar and arrow), which is one order of magnitude larger than what predicted following Penrose's argument. Therefore, this parameter-free version of the DP model is excluded."
  ACCESS: full-text
  STATUS: peer-reviewed
  CONFIDENCE: high
- [RC-13] CLAIM: Circularity objection to thermal time (Chua 2024): the thermal time hypothesis (time defined by the modular flow of a thermal state) arguably presupposes dynamics, hence time, to define equilibrium states, so it risks circularity as a solution to the problem of time. This is a philosophical-physics critique, not a theorem.
  SOURCE: E. Y. S. Chua 2024, "The Time in Thermal Time", arXiv:2407.18948 (preprint)
  QUOTE: "Here, I develop the worry that the thermal time hypothesis requires dynamics – and hence time – to get off the ground, thereby running into worries of circularity."

  CONFIDENCE: medium
