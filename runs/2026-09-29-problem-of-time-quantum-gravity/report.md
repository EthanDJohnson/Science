# The problem of time: report
*Run 2026-09-29-problem-of-time-quantum-gravity · 2026-09-29 · depth standard · question: "Problem of time: In quantum mechanics, time is a classical background parameter, and the flow of time is universal and absolute. In general relativity, time is one component of four-dimensional spacetime, and the flow of time changes depending on the curvature of spacetime and the spacetime trajectory of the observer. How can these two concepts of time be reconciled?"*

## Bottom line
On any fixed, globally hyperbolic classical spacetime the two concepts are already reconciled: relativistic quantum theory runs on GR's proper times, and a quantum clock resolving the gravitational redshift across 1 mm (measured −9.8(2.3)e-20 per mm against −1.09e-19 predicted, at 7.6e-21 fractional uncertainty, SI) confirms it (confidence high: peer-reviewed measurement plus verified calculation). The genuine "problem of time" is narrower than the question states: it arises when the whole system, clocks included, is quantum and subject to a Hamiltonian constraint with no external reference, as in a closed universe. There, relational time (C2) dissolves the frozen formalism and Kuchař's two-time objection exactly for clocks that do not interact with the system (verified to about 1e-15), and semiclassical WKB time (C3) is its heavy-clock limit explaining why QM's parameter t works everywhere tested; but the very source C2 rests on calls the non-interacting case "untenable in generic models" of gravity. No position has been shown to close the multiple-choice, global-time and spacetime-reconstruction facets for gravitationally coupled quantum geometry, so the weak null (C8) stands at credence about 0.85. The conservative positions are empirically equivalent (differences at most ~1e-10 in cosmology, ~1e-60 in the lab); the only near-term fork is whether geometry stays quantum (C7, credence ~0.15), tested by a gravity-mediated-entanglement witness.

## Why, candidate by candidate

**C1.** For: the 1 mm redshift measurement [D-14]; the tidal term is 1.57e-10 of the potential term over 1 mm and Rindler dilation has Riemann = 0 (M-EXAMINER-01); Torre–Varadarajan non-unitarity already occurs on flat R × Tⁿ [K-01], so facet 5 is not gravity-specific. Against: clause (iii) is a category error—the QGEM phase equals Gm²τ/(ħd) in an external time to 50 digits and separates only C7 from the rest; clause (i) holds only on globally hyperbolic backgrounds (Kay–Radzikowski–Wald); the facets appear in a geometry-free Page–Wootters model, so "superposed geometry" is neither necessary nor sufficient. Decisive test: internal-state optical clock in a superposition of heights, V = |cos(ωgΔhT/2c²)|, first zero at T·Δh = 10.7 m·s for Sr; any excess loss on a classical background refutes clause (i).

**C2.** For: five independent math checks reproduce exact conditional Schrödinger evolution for any uncoupled clock spectrum, the vanishing naive two-time probability, and record-conditioned Born values 0.146447/0.500000/0.853553 to ~1e-15; covariant POVM clocks resolve the identity (defect 3e-15) escaping Unruh–Wald; Smith–Ahmadi coupling gives a Hermitian redshift generator H_S(1−λH_S)⁻¹. Against: HSL themselves restrict the trinity to non-interacting clocks and say interactions "must also appear in the Page–Wootters formalism" with non-unitarity at turning points (full text); generic coupling leaves no exact null vector in 40 trials (M-CONSTRAINTS-07); with quantum separation, Hermiticity relocates to a B-weighted inner product (facet 3 returns); D-03 shows inequivalent unitarities across clocks in FLRW. Decisive test: relational dynamics for a constraint with local degrees of freedom and a gravitationally coupled, non-monotonic clock, checked for clock-covariance and positive inner product through the turning point.

**C3.** For: verified M2 series ω_n = E_n + E_n²/(4e₀M), corrected equation brings 1−F to 1.3e-9 at M = 10; [D-09] peer-reviewed; explains QM's t wherever tested. Against: correction is E_S/(4K_heavy), not universal (E/m_P)²; three routes give different O(1/M) equations [D-21]; anomaly condition [K-04]. Decisive test: a two-heavy-variable Born–Oppenheimer expansion to see whether the O(1/M) correction is clock-invariant.

**C4.** For: dust gives a local constraint linear in the clock momentum [D-10]; unimodular T4 monotonic in all three FRW models (M-CONSTRAINTS-02); uncertainty cost ΔΛ ≥ 9e-105 Λ_obs for 1 s over a Hubble volume (M-CONSTRAINTS-11). Against: three of four branches fail on the mechanism claimed (see table); Gödel shows energy conditions do not supply chronology (M-CONSTRAINTS-04); York turns at t* = 51–121 Gyr in closed ΛCDM. Decisive test: Dirac quantization of dust or unimodular-with-hypertime for foliation-independent functional evolution.

**C5.** For: Gibbs identity exact; Tolman–Ehrenfest gives −1.0927e-19 per mm (M-CONSTRAINTS-09). Against: the modern algebraic form supplies time through the observer's clock, not modular flow (peer-reviewed CLPW and Witten, full text); non-Gibbs O(1) mismatch; circularity. Decisive test: whether the modular parameter tracks the observer's clock in a non-equilibrium state of the dressed algebra.

**C6.** For: [D-20] full text; verified arithmetic. Against: everything in the table is internal to GPP's own equations. Decisive test: derive δT as a theorem binding every clock, including one conditioned on a better clock.

**C7.** For: hybrid-dynamics price proven [K-05]; arguments that gravity must be quantized are inconclusive [D-18]. Against: simplest variant eliminated; delta kernel excluded by 1e17; parameter-free Penrose excluded; DP predicts entanglement over part of its allowed window; foliation and chronology presupposed. Decisive test: QGEM-type witness with m ~ 1e-14 kg, Δx ~ 250 µm, ~1 s coherence.

**C8.** For: Kuchař's verdict [D-04] not overturned; D-03; Stoica 2026 (preprint) and HSL's caveats; no 2024–26 construction found for local constraints (search-summary). Against: facet 1 dissolved for ideal clocks; facet 5 not gravity-specific; Höhn–Vanrietvelde recast facet 2 as a feature (parametrized particle only). Decisive test: the construction named in the table.

## Assumptions every surviving answer shares
- Global hyperbolicity and chronology are assumed, not derived: Gödel satisfies NEC, WEC, SEC, DEC yet has CTCs for r > 0.8814 (G = c = 1).
- Dirac quantization of constraints defines the frozen formalism [D-01]; reduced-phase-space and covariant quantizations were not tested here.
- All toy models carry one global constraint; facet 5 for local, Lorentzian constraints is untested (only Euclidean LQG anomaly freedom is claimed, preprint).
- Gravitational clock coupling is treated at weak-field, Newtonian order (λ = 8.71e-76 s at 1 mm).
- Standard Hilbert-space QM and the Born rule; the dependence on interpretation (hidden premise 8) was not resolved.
If a positive-norm, clock-covariant relational dynamics exists for local constraints, C8 falls and C2 rises to a full resolution; if geometry is classical, C2–C6 are effective descriptions only.

## What would change this verdict
1. A QGEM-type entanglement witness at m ~ 1e-14 kg with ~1 s coherence: a positive result drops C7 below 0.05 for PQCG and small-R0 DP (not to zero, given Martín-Martínez–Perche and Trillo–Navascués); a clean null at full sensitivity doubles C7.
2. A construction meeting C8's decisive test (clock-covariant, unitary, positive-norm, anomaly-free relational dynamics for local gravitational constraints with a turning clock, recovering GR proper times) would halve C8 and make C2 the headline answer.
3. Excess visibility loss for a clock in a height superposition on a classical background (predicted V = 0.98921 at Δh = 1 m, T = 1 s) would refute C1 clause (i) and reopen the fixed-background conflict, moving C1 below 0.4.
