# Analysis: examiner (Socrates — examine the question before answering it)

## Method applied
Define each loaded term in the question ("time in QM", "time in GR", "flow", "universal/absolute", "reconcile"), test whether each definition survives, audit the hidden premises (support / falsifier / what the question becomes if false), look for a better-posed nearby question, and write the hardest checkable questions any answer must survive.

## Findings

1. **The "flow" of time is not what either theory's time does; both have a parameter or a structure.** The brief already puts passage out of scope; the dossier gives no physics claim that uses "flow" beyond parametrized evolution [D-12 is the only position that ties a flow to physics, and it is contested, D-24]. So "flow" must be read as "what generates evolution".
2. **The frozen formalism is established only for Dirac quantization of spatially closed GR** [D-01]; for asymptotically flat spacetimes the brief notes a surviving ADM energy, but the dossier has no source on it (gap, section 6); finding 11 supplies one (Regge–Teitelboim). The premise "the Hamiltonian constraint freezes time" is therefore conditional on topology and quantization scheme.
3. **The "universal, absolute" QM time is a feature of NRQM only.** Relativistic QFT already uses any Cauchy foliation, and even there, evolution between arbitrary Cauchy surfaces is not unitarily implementable on Fock space in more than 1+1 D [K-01]. So the QM side already has a (UV) functional-evolution problem on a fixed flat background; facet 5 is not a quantum-gravity novelty.
4. **Page–Wootters/relational dynamics removes the need for an external parameter only for an ideal, non-interacting clock** [D-05, D-06, K-09, U-01]: for any uncoupled clock the conditional state obeys Schrödinger's equation exactly; failures are coverage, readability and degeneracy, and genuine non-unitarity or time-nonlocality needs a clock–system coupling [U-01].
5. **"The flow of time changes depending on the curvature" is imprecise: laboratory time dilation is a potential (g_00) effect, not a curvature effect.** Over Δh = 1 mm at Earth's surface, the uniform-field term gΔh/c² = 1.09e-19 (dimensionless) matches the measured JILA gradient [D-14, Q-06]; the tidal (curvature) contribution is 1.7e-29, a ratio of 1.6e-10. Time dilation exists without curvature (uniform acceleration). The GR half of the question should read "proper time is path-dependent and set by the metric", not "set by curvature". [calc: runs/2026-09-29-problem-of-time-quantum-gravity/calc/lens-examiner_premises.py]
6. **Ordinary QM already accommodates GR time dilation on a fixed background, and it has been measured.** The Sr clock resolves a 1 mm redshift at 7.6e-21 fractional uncertainty [D-14, Q-05]; the clocks are quantum systems whose readings are observables, and their rates differ by the GR amount. What QM's parameter t cannot do is be a proper time; what QM's *clocks* do is read proper time. The conflict in the question conflates the parameter with clock readings (Pauli-type distinction, [K-02] and [K-09]: a time observable conjugate to H exists only for ideal clocks with unbounded spectrum).
7. **Where the real conflict starts: superposed sources of geometry. The QGEM entangling phase is literally a superposition of proper times of order 1e-38 s.** Reading the QGEM phase spread 0.23 rad over 1 s for m = 1e-14 kg [Q-10] as a rest-mass (Compton) clock gives Δτ = ħΔφ/(mc²) = 2.7e-38 s (fractional 2.7e-38); the fractional time dilation sourced by the superposed 1e-14 kg mass at 200 µm is G m/(c²d) = 3.7e-38, about 2e17 below the best clock precision (7.6e-21) [calc: lens-examiner_premises.py]. So no clock can yet see a superposed geometry; only interferometric phases of heavy masses can, and the proposal is unrealized [Q-09, Q-11, Q-12].
8. **A clock placed in a superposition of heights in Earth's (classical) field needs T·Δh ≈ 10.7 m·s for an Sr optical transition (429 THz) to lose visibility fully, and 5.0e5 m·s for a Cs microwave clock** (first zero of Zych's witness at Δτ = π/ω) [calc: lens-examiner_premises.py; witness from F-09]. This tests "proper time in superposition" on a fixed background: QM + GR in the regime the brief's premise 1 says is *not* the problem of time. It is an important test of the reframe, but it cannot discriminate among quantum-gravity positions [F-09, section 6 gap].
9. **Kuchař's two-time objection reproduced and its standard answer checked in the toy model.** Qubit H_S = 0.5σx (rad/s, ħ = 1), 8-level equally spaced clock, t1 = 0.785 s, t2 = 2.356 s: Kuchař's naive P(0 at t2 | 0 at t1) = 0.000000; the Giovannetti–Lloyd–Maccone memory construction gives 0.500000, equal to the textbook |<0|U(t2−t1)|0>|² = 0.500000 [calc: lens-examiner_premises.py using .claude/skills/conundrum/scripts/page_wootters.py]. Caveat from the tool's own docstring: the GLM state is built as a lattice history state with a memory written by an impulsive coupling, not as the null state of a coupled constraint. The objection is answered by turning a two-time question into a one-time question about a record; that is a reframe of what "probability at two times" means, not a derivation from the bare constraint. [K-03, D-19]
10. **Gravity's universality means clock and system always interact, but the Smith–Ahmadi coupling is negligible in labs.** λE = G E/(c⁴x) at x = 1 mm: 2.3e-60 for an optical-photon energy, 1.1e-49 for an Sr-87 rest energy, 7.4e-28 for a 1 g clock's rest energy [calc: lens-examiner_premises.py; tool `gravity` mode gives the same 2.349e-60]. The non-interaction assumption of the trinity theorem [K-09] is therefore an excellent approximation in every lab, and is violated only where gravity between clock and system is strong (early universe, black holes) — the same regime where semiclassical corrections become large [D-09]. The trinity's scope limit is not a lab issue; it is the cosmological issue.

11. **Premise 4 (the constraint freezes time) fails for asymptotically flat spacetimes.** [new: Regge & Teitelboim 1974, Annals Phys. 88, 286, abstract via lit_search, "for asymptotically flat spaces, the Hamiltonian does not vanish but its value is given rather by a nonzero surface integral"]. There, time translation at infinity is generated by a true (boundary) Hamiltonian, and the frozen formalism is specifically a closed-universe problem (quantum cosmology). A modern echo: subregion algebras that "encompass[] an asymptotic boundary" use the ADM Hamiltonian, while compact regions need "a model of an observer living in the region" [new: Jensen et al. 2023, JHEP 12, 020, abstract via lit_search, "When the subregion is spatially compact or encompasses an asymptotic boundary, we argue that the algebra is a type II von Neumann factor. To do so in the former case we introduce a model of an observer living in the region; in the latter, the ADM Hamil…" (abstract truncated by the tool)].
12. **Thermal-time's modern relatives converge on relational time.** The de Sitter static-patch algebra uses "operators gravitationally dressed to the worldline of an observer" [new: Chandrasekaran et al. 2023, JHEP 02, 082, abstract via lit_search, "We describe an algebra of observables for a static patch in de Sitter space, with operators gravitationally dressed to the worldline of an observer."]. Where no boundary Hamiltonian exists, a physical clock (observer) is put in by hand, which is the relational move of D-05/D-06. So thermal/modular time and relational time are not rivals on this point; the observer's clock is what makes the algebra and its modular flow well defined (my inference, not stated in the abstract).
13. **The genuine conflict is testable as a superposition of proper times.** [new: Christodoulou & Rovelli 2019, Phys. Lett. B 792, 64, fetch_text of arXiv:1808.05842 abstract, ACCESS full abstract, "We give a generally covariant description of this phenomenon, where the relevant effect turns out to be a quantum superposition of proper times. We point out that measurement of this effect would count as evidence for quantum superposition of spacetime geometries."]. This matches finding 7 (Δτ ≈ 2.7e-38 s). The reading is contested by the classical-mediator counter-argument [D-23] and by Kent 2024 (title/abstract only; not relied on).
14. **Operational framework for time with gravitating quantum clocks exists.** [new: Castro-Ruiz et al. 2020, Nat. Commun. 11, 2672, arXiv abstract via lit_search, "In general relativity, the metric is influenced by matter, and is expected to become indefinite when matter behaves quantum mechanically. Here, we develop a framework to operationally define events and their localisation with respect to a quantum clock reference…"]. This is the reframed question (time when the metric is indefinite) stated by practitioners; it is worked out in the weak-field (Newtonian-coupling) regime, not full quantum gravity.
15. **Premise 8 (interpretation-independence) fails at the first test case.** Page–Geilker's experiment "gave results inconsistent with the simplest alternative to quantum gravity, the semiclassical Einstein equations" and "supports (but does not prove)" quantized gravity [new: Page & Geilker 1981, PRL 47, 979, abstract via lit_search]. The standard reading (my inference; the abstract does not say it) is that this refutes only the no-collapse version of semiclassical gravity; with an objective collapse, semiclassical gravity survives that experiment. Collapse needs an external time [F-06]; PW conditioning is an Everett-compatible, collapse-free device [D-05]. So which "reconciliations" are open depends on the measurement problem.
16. **Premise 7 (gravity must be quantized) is not settled by argument** [D-18] but is squeezed by experiment for specific classical models [K-06, K-07]; the premise is empirically decidable in principle by QGEM-type tests [Q-09, D-23], with the 8.4-order mass gap to current interferometry [Q-12] and a 1e14 superposition-size gap for the heaviest cat state [D-16].

### C. Reframe

Better-posed nearby question: **"When the spacetime metric is itself in a quantum superposition (sourced by matter in superposition), what defines the time with respect to which quantum states evolve, and does it reduce to QM's parameter time and GR's proper times in the classical-metric limit?"**

- On a classical background the answer is known and tested: QFTCS/NRQM with proper-time coupling; clocks read proper time (7.6e-21 fractional precision [D-14]); the only formal gap is the UV non-unitarity of arbitrary-surface evolution [K-01], which is a QFT, not a gravity, issue.
- For weakly superposed geometry the answer is operationally worked out with quantum reference frames/quantum clocks (finding 14) and relational PW dynamics [D-06]; the gravitational clock–system coupling is ~1e-60 to 1e-28 in the lab (finding 10), so the ideal-clock idealization holds.
- The effect is a superposition of proper times of ~3e-38 s in QGEM (findings 7, 13); whether it exists is the first experimentally decidable fork (quantum vs classical geometry).
- Fully quantum geometry (cosmology, black holes) is where facets 2, 4 and 7 remain open [D-03, K-02, K-04].
The reframe has a clearer answer: "solved on classical backgrounds; operationally formulated for weak superpositions; open for strong quantum geometry; the fork between quantum and classical geometry is testable".

### D. Hardest questions any answer must survive

1. **Born-rule time.** In your position, with respect to what is the Born rule applied, and is evolution in it exactly unitary? Checkable: for an uncoupled clock the conditional Schrödinger equation holds exactly [U-01]; state what fails when the clock–system gravitational coupling λE is not negligible (λE ~ 1 near Planck densities; lab values 1e-60 to 1e-28, finding 10).
2. **Clock change.** Take the flat FLRW + scalar + fluid model [D-03]: do fluid-, volume- and scalar-time quantizations agree on whether the singularity is resolved? If not, which is physical and why, and what observation could tell?
3. **Two-time probabilities without ancillas.** Derive P(b at t2 | a at t1) from the physical state. The naive form gives 0.000000 where the textbook value is 0.500000 (finding 9). If your answer needs a memory, is the memory part of the physical (constraint-satisfying) state, and does gauge invariance survive (the HSL caveat in D-19)?
4. **Global time.** What happens when the chosen clock stops being monotonic (recollapse; Unruh–Wald for sharp clocks with H bounded below [K-02])? If you use POVMs, what time resolution do you lose and does unitarity survive the turning point?
5. **Arbitrary-hypersurface evolution.** How does your position evade or accept Torre–Varadarajan non-unitarity [K-01] and the Giulini–Kiefer anomaly condition [K-04]?
6. **Classical limit, quantitatively.** Recover the 1 mm redshift gradient 1.09e-19 per mm [Q-06] and the Zych witness V = |cos(ωΔτ/2)| (first zero at T·Δh ≈ 10.7 m·s for Sr, finding 8). Which step reconstructs a local proper time (facet 7)?
7. **Superposed source.** For the QGEM geometry (m = 1e-14 kg, Δx = 250 µm, phase 0.23 rad in 1 s [Q-10]), what does your position predict: entanglement (quantum geometry), no entanglement plus metric diffusion within a stated D2 window [Q-20, Q-21], or collapse set by R0 > 0.54e-10 m [Q-01]?
8. **Interpretation dependence.** Which interpretation does the position need (collapse needs an external time; PW conditioning is collapse-free; Hartle needs consistent histories), and does Page–Geilker [finding 15] bite on it?
9. **Topology.** Does the position treat closed and asymptotically flat universes the same? If the ADM surface Hamiltonian supplies time for the latter (finding 11), is the remaining problem only cosmological?
10. **A failing number.** Name a quantity measurable within about 20 years that the position would get wrong if false. If none, say it is empirically equivalent to which rivals [dossier section 6].

## What would change my mind

- An anomaly in a fixed-background proper-time experiment (clock in height superposition, MAGIS-type) would move me away from EXAMINER-A: it would mean QM and GR time conflict even without quantum geometry.
- A clock-independence theorem for a model with gravitationally interacting clocks and a turning point would move me away from the null (EXAMINER-B) toward C.
- An independent (non-HSL) confirmation that two-time conditioning is gauge-invariant without ancillas would upgrade C; a proof that the GLM memory cannot be embedded in a coupled-constraint null state would weaken it.
- Observed gravity-mediated entanglement would eliminate EXAMINER-D (unless the D-23 counter-argument holds up); a clean null at the required sensitivity would strain every quantum-geometry position.
