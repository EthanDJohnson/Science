# Analysis: decomposer (methodical doubt and decomposition)

## Method applied
Rewrite "how can QM time and GR time be reconciled?" as a tree of sub-questions that a calculation, a measurement or a cited result can settle; build a ledger of every premise (measured / derived / assumed / unknown); name the load-bearing assumption and its test; sort sub-questions into settled and open. Two small calculations test sub-questions the dossier left open (Kuchař's two-time objection in a finite Page–Wootters model; the size of the "background-time" conflict in regimes probed so far).

## Findings

1. **Hidden premise 1 is false as stated for relativistic QM.** QFTCS already runs on GR's time: any Cauchy foliation of a fixed Lorentzian background, with proper times along worldlines; its costs are observer-dependent vacua and non-unitary evolution between generic Cauchy surfaces, even on flat R × T^n [K-01]. Experiment agrees with QM plus GR proper time on a classical background down to a 1 mm height difference (fractional redshift 1.09e-19, dimensionless) [D-14, Q-05, Q-06; recomputed in calc: runs/2026-09-29-problem-of-time-quantum-gravity/calc/lens-decomposer_subquestions.py, Part 3a]. So the conflict the question names ("absolute QM time" vs "curvature-dependent GR time") is already reconciled on any *fixed* spacetime. The residual conflict appears only when the geometry itself is dynamical *and quantum* (superposed): that is where the frozen formalism, multiple choice and the other facets arise [D-01, D-02].
2. **Kuchař's two-time objection is reproduced exactly, and the memory/relational fix works exactly, in a finite model.** Qubit (H_S = 0.5 σ_x rad/s) + d = 8 equally spaced clock, lattice times t1 = 0.785 s, t2 = 3.142 s (hbar = 1). Kuchař's naive P(b at t2 | a at t1) = 0 for *all four* (a, b), so the "probabilities" do not even sum to 1; at t2 = t1 the naive rule returns 1. The GLM memory construction returns 0.1464 / 0.8536, matching |<b|U(t2−t1)|a>|² to 4e-16 [calc: runs/2026-09-29-problem-of-time-quantum-gravity/calc/lens-decomposer_subquestions.py, Part 1; tool page_wootters.py]. This settles the sub-question "does facet 1 survive two-time questions for an ideal uncoupled clock?" as *no*, consistent with HSL's claim [D-19] and Moreva 2017 [D-08]. It does not address coupled clocks or gravity.
3. **Multiple choice (facet 2) persists even in a finite, exactly solvable universe, and it is the interaction that makes it bite.** One constraint J on A ⊗ B ⊗ S, with A–S coupling g = 0.3 rad/s and B a resonant uncoupled 6-level clock; the physical subspace has dimension 6 either way. Relative to clock B, the effective generator for A⊗S equals H_AS to 3e-15 (Hermitian, time-independent; conditional norm constant at 1.0000). Relative to clock A, the effective generator for B⊗S has non-Hermiticity 0.19–0.30 rad/s and changes by up to 2.0 rad/s between clock readings; conditional norm oscillates 0.971–1.029 [calc: runs/2026-09-29-problem-of-time-quantum-gravity/calc/lens-decomposer_subquestions.py, Part 2]. Same physical state, same constraint: one clock choice gives unitary Schrödinger dynamics, another gives non-unitary, time-dependent dynamics. The trinity theorem [D-06] is explicitly restricted to clocks that do not couple [K-09]; gravity couples everything to energy, so the realistic case is the coupled one. Combined with the Gielen–Menéndez-Pidal cosmology [D-03], facet 2 is a live, demonstrated feature, not an artefact of ill-chosen clocks.
4. **The size of "quantum-time" effects in probed regimes is tiny, so no discriminating data exist yet.** For an optical clock (ω = 2π × 429 THz) the Smith–Ahmadi clock–system gravitational coupling λω is 2.3e-60 at 1 mm and 2.3e-63 at 1 m (dimensionless). A two-level internal clock split across heights Δh for T = 1 s loses visibility 1 − V = 6.8e-4 at Δh = 0.25 m and 1.1e-2 at Δh = 1 m (classical background, Zych-type [F-09]). A superposed source changes the local clock rate by 9.2e-39 (QGEM parameters, m = 1e-14 kg) or 7.4e-31 (1 g delocalised by 1 µm at 1 mm) (all dimensionless) [calc: runs/2026-09-29-problem-of-time-quantum-gravity/calc/lens-decomposer_subquestions.py, Part 3]. Proper time *in superposition on a fixed background* is within reach; time dilation *sourced by a superposed geometry* is 20–30 orders of magnitude below clock resolution (Q-05: 7.6e-21). The only near-term handle on superposed geometry is phase or entanglement (QGEM, Q-10: 0.23 rad), not clock rates.
5. **The closed-universe premise behind the strict frozen formalism is observationally open.** [new: Vagnozzi, Di Valentino, Gariazzo, Melchiorri, Mena, Silk 2021, ApJ 908, 84 (arXiv:2011.11645), ACCESS full-text (abstract page), "Combining Planck 2018 CMB temperature and polarization data with the latest CC measurements, we break the geometrical degeneracy and find $\Omega_K=-0.0054 \pm 0.0055$, consistent with a spatially flat Universe"]. Curvature cannot decide spatial closure (a flat or open universe can still have compact topology), and the local Hamiltonian constraint 𝓗 ≈ 0 holds pointwise whatever the global topology. So "open universe with ADM energy" rescues a boundary time for asymptotically flat *subsystems*, not for cosmology; hidden premise 4 is not load-bearing for the local facets (1, 2, 5, 6).
6. **Observer-dressed algebras are a modern relative of relational/thermal time.** [new: Chandrasekaran, Longo, Penington, Witten 2023, JHEP 02 082 (arXiv:2206.10780), ACCESS abstract, "We describe an algebra of observables for a static patch in de Sitter space, with operators gravitationally dressed to the worldline of an observer. The algebra is a von Neumann algebra of Type II$_1$."] The observer's worldline supplies the clock (relational), and the modular structure of the state supplies the flow (thermal-time machinery). [new: De Vuyst et al. 2025, JHEP 07 146 (arXiv:2405.00114), ACCESS abstract, "we show that this procedure depends on which observer is employed"] — the multiple-choice facet reappears in the algebraic setting. This supports reading relational and thermal time as complementary rather than rival (hidden premise 5), and shows facet 2 is not removed by the algebraic route.
7. **Hidden premise 4 (Hamiltonian ≈ 0) depends on boundary conditions, as the brief suspects.** [new: Regge & Teitelboim 1974, Annals Phys. 88, 286, ACCESS abstract, "for asymptotically flat spaces, the Hamiltonian does not vanish but its value is given rather by a nonzero surface integral"]. The bulk term is still a sum of constraints, so the frozen-formalism facet is avoided only for an isolated system seen from infinity, where the asymptotic observer is an external clock. This is the canonical form of the reframe: time exists when there is a classical, external reference (boundary, background, or heavy classical degrees of freedom) and is frozen only when the quantum system is the whole closed universe.
8. **Gravitationally interacting quantum clocks: relativity of time localisation is a feature, with a local unitary frame always available.** [new: Castro-Ruiz, Giacomini, Belenchia, Brukner 2020, Nat. Commun. 11, 2672 (arXiv:1908.10165), ACCESS abstract (arXiv page via fetch_text), "We find that, when clocks interact gravitationally, the time localisability of events becomes relative, depending on the reference frame. ... Even if the metric is indefinite, for any event we can find a reference frame where local quantum operations take their standard unitary dilation form."] This matches Finding 3 (clock-dependent unitarity under coupling) and gives the relational reply: multiple choice (facet 2) becomes covariance under quantum-reference-frame changes, with standard QM holding locally in a suitable frame. Scope: weak-field, Newtonian-order clock interactions; not full quantum gravity.

## Lens-specific outputs

### A. Sub-question tree
Root Q: *Can QM's parameter time and GR's proper/coordinate time be obtained from one consistent framework (or shown not to conflict), and can observation tell the ways of doing so apart?*

- **Q1. Is there a conflict at all on a fixed spacetime?**
  - Q1.1 Does relativistic QM/QFT need an absolute time? → No; any Cauchy foliation, Poincaré-related [brief; D-level textbook].
  - Q1.2 Does QFTCS reproduce gravitational time dilation for quantum systems? → Yes on a classical background; tested to 1e-19 per mm [D-14, Q-06; Finding 1].
  - Q1.3 Is QFTCS evolution between arbitrary Cauchy surfaces unitary? → No (free KG field, n > 1) [K-01]. So "unitary evolution in any time" is already lost *without* quantum gravity; this is a cost that every position inherits.
- **Q2. Where does a genuine conflict start?** When the metric is dynamical *and* quantum.
  - Q2.1 Is gravity quantum? → Unknown. Gedanken arguments inconclusive [D-18]; entanglement test proposed, interpretation contested [D-23]; classical-gravity models squeezed but not excluded [K-05, K-06].
  - Q2.2 Given quantum gravity via Dirac quantization of a closed universe, is the state frozen? → Yes, as a property of the formalism [D-01]. Asymptotically flat: boundary Hamiltonian survives [Finding 7].
  - Q2.3 Is our universe spatially closed? → Unknown; Ω_K = −0.0054 ± 0.0055 [Finding 5]. Not decisive, since the local constraint holds regardless.
- **Q3. Can dynamics be recovered from a frozen state? (facet 1)**
  - Q3.1 Single-time: Page–Wootters conditional states give exact Schrödinger evolution for any uncoupled clock spectrum [U-01, D-17].
  - Q3.2 Two-time (Kuchař): naive rule fails; memory/relational-observable rule succeeds exactly [Finding 2, D-19].
  - Q3.3 Coupled clocks: effective dynamics non-unitary/time-dependent [Finding 3]; relational reply via QRF covariance [Finding 8].
- **Q4. Is the recovered dynamics independent of the clock? (facet 2)** → No, demonstrated in cosmology [D-03] and in a finite model [Finding 3]; in algebraic form too [Finding 6]. Open whether QRF covariance [Finding 8] extends to full gravity.
- **Q5. Positive inner product and global time? (facets 3, 4)** → Per-sector for KG-type constraints [D-07]; Unruh–Wald no-go for sharp monotonic clocks, escaped by POVMs [K-02]. No gravitational construction with a global clock is known.
- **Q6. Functional evolution and anomaly freedom? (facet 5)** → Semiclassical Tomonaga–Schwinger time needs an anomaly-free full theory [K-04]; K-01 shows the obstruction exists even on flat space.
- **Q7. Dirac observables for gravity? (facet 6)** → Constructible with matter clocks (dust) [D-10], relationally in principle (Rovelli/Dittrich, not opened); explicit full-GR set not in dossier.
- **Q8. Spacetime and proper-time reconstruction? (facet 7)** → Semiclassical BO/WKB gives Schrödinger equation on a background plus (E/m_P)² corrections [D-09, Q-18]; but three semiclassical routes give different corrections [D-21]. No dedicated source [dossier gap].
- **Q9. Can any observation separate positions within ~20 years?**
  - Q9.1 Relational vs semiclassical vs thermal vs unimodular: no known discriminator [dossier §6]; corrections ≤ 3e-31 at LHC energies [Q-18].
  - Q9.2 Quantum vs classical gravity: QGEM-type entanglement (phase 0.23 rad at 1 s) [Q-10]; squeeze on postquantum kernels [K-06]; DP collapse bounds [K-07].
  - Q9.3 Proper time in superposition (fixed background): visibility loss 7e-4 at 0.25 m, 1e-2 at 1 m for T = 1 s [Finding 4] — tests Q1.2, not Q2.
  - Q9.4 Time dilation sourced by a superposed mass: 1e-39 to 1e-30 fractional [Finding 4] — not reachable by clock-rate measurements.

### C. Load-bearing assumption
**L4: the metric is a quantum degree of freedom that can be in superposition.** If L4 fails (gravity fundamentally classical, semiclassical or postquantum), there is one spacetime at all times, its proper time is the parameter of (modified) QM, and the problem of time as a quantum-gravity problem dissolves into the price paid by the modification: stochastic, non-unitary dynamics [K-05]. If L4 holds, the fixed-background reconciliation of Finding 1 no longer applies, and facets 1–7 are all in play. So L4 flips which *kind* of answer is available (modify QM vs resolve/dissolve within quantum theory).
*What would test it:* (i) observation of gravity-mediated entanglement between two delocalised masses (QGEM, Q-09/Q-10), with the D-23 caveat that the inference "entanglement ⇒ quantum mediator" is itself contested; (ii) closing the postquantum window from both sides (K-06): lower bound on D2 from coherence rising above the upper bound from gravimetry for all kernels; (iii) finding the predicted classical-gravity diffusion/metric noise. Secondary load-bearing premise: **L5/L8** (Dirac quantization with ideal uncoupled clocks). Its failure does not dissolve the question but determines whether "relational resolution" is a theorem [D-06] or a restricted special case. It is testable by calculation, not experiment: extend the trinity/QRF covariance [Finding 8] to a coupled gravitational constraint with a positive inner product.

## What would change my mind
- Observation of gravity-mediated entanglement with a closed classical-mediator loophole: moves D to eliminated and makes B/C the live options.
- A no-entanglement result at the QGEM sensitivity, or a detected diffusion term in gravimetry within the postquantum window: promotes D and strains B/C.
- A published construction that extends the trinity/QRF covariance to a coupled gravitational constraint with positive inner product, or an independent refutation of HSL's two-time reply: would move B to strong survival or to strained.
- A fixed-background anomaly in quantum proper-time experiments (visibility or redshift not matching g·Δh/c²): would refute A and every position.
- A computation showing the three semiclassical routes [D-21] agree after a field redefinition: would strengthen B's claim to predictivity.
