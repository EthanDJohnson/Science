# Brief: The Hubble tension: local (~73) versus early-universe (~67–68) H0
slug: 2026-10-30-hubble-tension-h0-discrepancy | depth: deep | type: anomaly | date: 2026-10-30

## Question as asked
"Hubble tension"

## Question made precise
The question is read as: **what is the dominant cause of the gap between the Hubble constant measured locally with the distance ladder (about 73 km/s/Mpc, SH0ES) and the value inferred from the cosmic microwave background (CMB) and baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) under ΛCDM (about 67–68 km/s/Mpc), with what credence, and which feasible measurement would decide it?** The answer is a ranked, mutually exclusive slate of causes. Each comes with the size of effect it needs, the evidence for and against it, and what it predicts for each class of measurement.

Definitions of loaded terms:
- **H0.** The present expansion rate of the homogeneous FRW background, ȧ/a at t0, in km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹. It is a global quantity. A **local** H0 is measured within roughly z ≲ 0.15 (a few hundred Mpc) and may differ from the global value by cosmic variance, coherent flows, or a local under- or overdensity.
- **"Early-universe" H0.** A value **inferred** within a model, not measured. The CMB pins the angular acoustic scale θ_* = r_s(z_*)/D_M(z_*) to about 0.03% and the physical densities ω_b and ω_c. H0 then follows only once the expansion history between recombination and today is assumed (flat ΛCDM by default). BAO+BBN gives a second early-universe value that does not use CMB anisotropies, with the BBN baryon density ω_b fixing the sound horizon r_d. The **inverse distance ladder** (BAO calibrated by r_d, plus uncalibrated SNe Ia) gives H0 without assuming ΛCDM at late times, but it does assume standard pre-recombination physics.
- **"Late-universe" or "local" H0.** A value that does not rely on r_s. It comes either from a **distance ladder** (geometric anchors → a primary distance indicator such as Cepheids, the tip of the red giant branch (TRGB), J-region AGB stars (JAGB) or Miras → SN Ia calibrators → the Hubble flow, or a secondary indicator such as surface-brightness fluctuations (SBF) or Tully–Fisher) or from a **one-step method** (strong-lensing time delays, megamaser disks, gravitational-wave standard sirens). Lensing time delays depend weakly on Ω_m and on mass-model assumptions.
- **The tension.** The difference between a chosen local H0 and a chosen early-universe H0, with its significance. Both the size and the significance depend on which results enter, and on correlations through shared anchors, shared SN samples and shared CMB data. They are to be established by the research and computed by the statistician, not assumed. "About 5σ" and "about 8%" are placeholders.
- **SN Ia absolute magnitude M_B.** The ladder gives H0 through 5 log10 H0 = M_B + 5 a_B + 25. The inverse ladder fixes M_B from the other end. The tension can therefore be stated equally as a ~0.2 mag disagreement in M_B. Which of the two statements is the right one is itself a premise to test (premise 8).
- **Unidentified systematic.** An effect within established physics and astrophysics that shifts a published value by more than its quoted uncertainty and is missing from, or underestimated in, its error budget. A systematic explanation must name:
  - the method and rung (anchor, primary indicator, SN calibration, Hubble-flow sample; or, for the early side, a CMB experiment, likelihood or foreground model, or BAO reconstruction);
  - the specific effect (for example Cepheid crowding or blending, a metallicity dependence of the period–luminosity relation, host dust or R_V, a difference in SN Ia standardisation between calibrator hosts and Hubble-flow hosts, calibrator selection, peculiar-velocity corrections, an anchor-distance error, the TRGB tip-detection method or the JAGB zero point; or, on the early side, a Planck foreground residual, the lensing anomaly A_L, or a BAO fiducial-cosmology bias);
  - the size it would need against the published budget, in magnitudes or percent of H0.
- **New physics.** Any departure from flat ΛCDM, with standard-model particle content, that moves the inferred early-universe H0 or the local measurement. Two classes, each one candidate with variants as sub-hypotheses:
  - **early-time / pre-recombination:** shrinks r_s (early dark energy (EDE), extra dark radiation ΔN_eff or self-interacting neutrinos, a varying electron mass or fine-structure constant, primordial magnetic fields, and others the research supports);
  - **late-time / post-recombination:** changes H(z) or the calibration after recombination (phantom or evolving dark energy including the DESI w0–wa preference, interacting dark energy, modified gravity changing G_eff or the Cepheid and SN physics, a transition in M_B or G at low z, and others).
- **Local structure.** H0 measured locally differs from the global value because we sit in an underdense region or in a coherent bulk flow, within standard ΛCDM structure formation or beyond it. It is its own candidate, distinct from both a systematic error and new physics in the expansion history.
- **"Settle."** A measurement, or a set of measurements, feasible within about 10 years (by 2036) that separates the leading surviving explanations at 3σ or more (5σ preferred). The answer gives its target precision, who is doing it, and when results are expected.

Admissible physics:
- **Established:** GR and flat (or curved) ΛCDM with three standard-model neutrino species; standard BBN; standard recombination physics; the stellar astrophysics of the distance indicators; linear and nonlinear structure formation as simulated in ΛCDM.
- **Speculative, allowed only when labelled:** every extension listed under new physics above, and inhomogeneous or backreaction cosmologies (timescape and similar).

"Viable" has two tiers:
- **Now:** consistent with every current measurement and bound, within its stated assumptions, including the collateral constraints (S8 and weak lensing, the CMB damping tail, BBN, BAO shape, SN Ia shape of the Hubble diagram).
- **Decidable:** a measurement expected by about 2036 would confirm or exclude it.

## Hidden premises to test
1. **The tension is real and about 5σ.** SH0ES alone against Planck gives about 5σ. CCHP's JWST TRGB and JAGB results sit near 70 and are consistent with both sides. Test: the proper combination of local results with their correlations (shared anchors and SNe), which local results to include, superseded values excluded, and how much the significance depends on choosing SH0ES.
2. **"Early versus late" is the right partition.** BAO+BBN and the inverse distance ladder give about 68 without CMB anisotropies. One-step late methods (time delays, masers, sirens) are less precise. The real partition may instead be "Cepheid-calibrated SNe against everything else", or "anything using r_s against anything not using it". Test each partition against the data.
3. **The CMB measures H0.** It measures θ_*, ω_b and ω_c; H0 needs the late-time model. Test how far the early-universe value moves under late-time freedom (w0–wa, curvature) and under pre-recombination freedom, and whether CMB experiments with independent systematics (Planck, ACT DR6, SPT-3G) agree among themselves.
4. **The local value measures the global H0.** Test the expected cosmic variance of local H0 in ΛCDM, and the size of the underdensity (depth and radius) needed to explain the gap, against the observed local density field and against the SN Ia Hubble diagram at 0.023 < z < 0.15.
5. **The rungs of the ladder are independent.** Every SN-based ladder shares the SN Ia standardisation, and calibrator hosts (star-forming spirals with Cepheids) differ from many Hubble-flow hosts. Test whether a single SN-side effect could move all SN ladders at once, and whether non-SN ladders (SBF, Tully–Fisher, masers) independently give about 73.
6. **JWST settled crowding.** SH0ES reports that JWST Cepheid photometry confirms HST to about 0.01–0.03 mag; CCHP reports a lower H0 with JWST TRGB and JAGB. Test whether the SH0ES–CCHP difference comes from methods (TRGB against Cepheids), from the choice of SN calibrator hosts, or from analysis choices, and what each side's re-analysis of the other's data finds.
7. **Late-time solutions are excluded.** The "no late-time solution" argument rests on the inverse distance ladder: BAO with the standard r_d, plus SNe whose standardisation does not evolve with redshift. Test how strong it is, and whether the DESI 2025 evolving-dark-energy preference changes it (and in which direction it moves H0).
8. **The tension is about H0 at all.** It may be better stated as a tension in M_B, or in r_d. Test whether that reframing changes which explanations are viable.
9. **Early-time solutions are viable.** EDE and related models must not worsen S8, must fit the ACT and SPT damping tail and polarisation, and may need fine-tuning (the coincidence of the injection redshift with matter–radiation equality). Test the current bounds from ACT DR6, SPT-3G and the combinations with DESI and SNe, and how much of the gap each model closes when fitted to all data rather than with a SH0ES prior.

## What counts as an answer
- **A ranked, mutually exclusive slate with credences.** It must include at least:
  - a **distance-ladder systematic**, with named rival effects as sub-hypotheses (Cepheid photometry or crowding, metallicity, dust, SN Ia standardisation or calibrator–host differences, calibrator selection, anchors, peculiar velocities);
  - an **early-universe analysis systematic** (CMB likelihood or foregrounds, BAO reconstruction), however weak;
  - **early-time new physics**, with its variants;
  - **late-time new physics**, with its variants;
  - **local structure** (an underdensity or bulk flow);
  - **the null:** a statistical fluctuation together with underestimated errors spread over several measurements, with no single dominant cause.

  It must also consider a **reframe** in which the premise is wrong, for example that the real anomaly is "SH0ES Cepheid-calibrated SNe against everything else", or that the gap is in M_B rather than H0.
- **For each candidate:**
  - the shift it produces in each class of measurement, with its sign, against the observed gap (to be recomputed from the combined values: roughly 5–6 km/s/Mpc, about 8%, about 0.17–0.19 mag in M_B or distance modulus);
  - its collateral predictions (S8, Ω_m, N_eff, the CMB damping tail, the shape of the SN Hubble diagram, BAO, the ages of the oldest stars);
  - its causal chain and weakest link;
  - the evidence for and against it, by dossier ID;
  - whether the published error budgets leave room for it.
- **A prediction matrix,** with one column per independent class of measurement:
  - Cepheid-calibrated SN ladder (SH0ES, HST and JWST);
  - TRGB-, JAGB- and Mira-calibrated SN ladders (CCHP and others);
  - non-SN ladders (SBF, Tully–Fisher, Type II SNe, and maser distances to hosts);
  - strong-lensing time delays;
  - megamasers;
  - gravitational-wave sirens (bright and dark);
  - CMB under ΛCDM (Planck, ACT, SPT, separately where they differ);
  - BAO+BBN without CMB anisotropies;
  - the inverse distance ladder (BAO + uncalibrated SNe);
  - CMB-independent shape measurements (the matter–radiation equality scale);
  - related tensions and parameters (S8 and weak lensing, N_eff from BBN, Ω_m from SNe, stellar ages).
- **The SH0ES–CCHP verdict:** what the JWST-era disagreement between the two ladder teams implies for the size and significance of the tension.
- **The early-versus-late verdict:** which of the two new-physics classes survives the inverse-distance-ladder argument and the ACT/SPT/DESI-era bounds, and how much of the gap the best surviving model closes when fitted to all data.
- **The deciding measurement:**
  - the single measurement that would settle the question, with its target precision, who is doing it, when it is expected, and the result each candidate predicts;
  - a runner-up.

## Research facets
- **theory:**
  - owns: the ΛCDM inference chain from CMB and BAO data to H0 (θ_*, r_s and r_d, D_M, the role of ω_b, ω_m and N_eff), and why r_s must shrink by roughly 7–9% for an early-time solution (the Knox–Millea "Hubble hunter's guide" type of argument);
  - owns: the formal models of the new-physics candidates (EDE in its variants, ΔN_eff and self-interacting dark radiation or neutrinos, varying m_e or α, primordial magnetic fields, phantom, evolving and interacting dark energy, modified gravity, low-z transitions in M_B or G), and the theoretical arguments that constrain whole classes (inverse distance ladder, sound-horizon argument);
  - owns: the theory of local structure: the cosmic variance of local H0 in linear and nonlinear ΛCDM, void models, and the meaning of a local H0 in an inhomogeneous universe (backreaction, labelled speculative where it is);
  - leaves numerical values to quantitative, observational bounds against models to critiques, and the local measurements to engineering.
- **quantitative:**
  - owns: the early-universe and inverse-ladder side, every result with its quoted uncertainties: CMB H0 under ΛCDM from Planck 2018 and later Planck reanalyses (PR4 / NPIPE), ACT DR6, SPT-3G and their combinations; θ_*, ω_b, ω_c, Ω_m, N_eff, Σm_ν, r_d and r_*; BAO (SDSS/eBOSS, DESI DR1 and DR2) and the BAO+BBN H0; the inverse distance ladder (BAO + Pantheon+, DES-SN5YR, Union3); CMB-independent equality-scale H0; BBN ω_b from deuterium; cosmic chronometers; ages of the oldest stars and globular clusters;
  - owns: the related parameters and tensions (S8 from KiDS, DES, HSC and CMB lensing; the DESI w0–wa preference and the H0 it implies);
  - owns: the planned or running early-universe and BAO experiments (Simons Observatory, the status of CMB-S4, SPT-3G and ACT next releases, DESI final and extension, Euclid, LiteBIRD), with target precision and timelines;
  - leaves the local measurements to engineering.
- **critiques:**
  - owns: published critiques of specific measurements proposing a specific systematic, with the size claimed and the replies: on the ladder (Cepheid crowding and colour or dust, metallicity, the NGC 4258–LMC–Milky Way anchor consistency, SN Ia host-mass and environment steps, calibrator selection, the SH0ES–CCHP disputes in both directions), and on the early side (Planck lensing anomaly A_L, foregrounds, PR3 against PR4 shifts, the DESI–CMB Ω_m discrepancy);
  - owns: the observational bounds against each class of explanation: EDE and related bounds from ACT, SPT, Planck and large-scale structure (S8); N_eff from BBN and CMB; varying m_e; primordial magnetic fields; late-time dark energy from BAO+SN; local-void bounds from SN data and galaxy surveys;
  - owns: the comparative reviews that rank solutions (for example the "H0 Olympics", Di Valentino et al. 2021, CosmoVerse 2025), and their scoring criteria;
  - leaves each experiment's own published error budget to engineering (local) or quantitative (early), and the formal models to theory.
- **engineering:**
  - owns: the local measurements of H0, method by method, each with its value, statistical and systematic uncertainties exactly as quoted, its largest systematic items, blinding, and which results supersede or share data with others:
    - geometric anchors (Milky Way parallaxes from Gaia, LMC detached eclipsing binaries, NGC 4258 maser, SMC);
    - primary indicators: Cepheids (SH0ES with HST and JWST), TRGB (CCHP, SH0ES and EDD analyses), JAGB, Miras;
    - SN Ia samples and standardisation (Pantheon+, CSP, ZTF), with the number of calibrator hosts;
    - non-SN ladders (SBF, Tully–Fisher, Type II SNe, fundamental plane);
    - one-step methods: lensing time delays (H0LiCOW, TDCOSMO), megamasers (MCP), gravitational-wave sirens (GW170817, dark sirens from GWTC-3 and GWTC-4);
    - any community combination of local results (for example the 2025 "Local Distance Network");
  - owns: the running and planned local programmes (JWST cycles for SH0ES and CCHP, Gaia DR4, Roman, Rubin LSST SNe and lensed quasars, LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA O4 and O5, ELT time-delay follow-up), with who runs each, its target precision and when results are expected;
  - leaves the early-universe results to quantitative.
- **frontier:**
  - owns: the newest material, newest first: preprints, conference talks and proceedings from 2025 to 2026 on either side of the tension (for example new SH0ES or CCHP JWST results and their exchanges, H0DN or other community combinations, TDCOSMO and lensed-SN updates, LVK O4 siren results, SPT-3G and ACT follow-ups, DESI DR2 extended-model analyses and any DR3 news, Planck PR4 updates, new EDE or other model fits to the 2025–26 data, Gaia DR4 timing), each labelled with its evidential status;
  - reports a result already in a journal only when something newer (an update, erratum, re-analysis or critique) exists, and otherwise leaves it to the facet that owns it.

Every facet: the field moves fast. Each facet still gives the current value for its own area, names any value it supersedes, and labels preprints and talks as such; frontier adds what is newer still.

## Worked calculations
- **statistician:**
  - the weighted combination of each class of local measurement (Cepheid-SN, TRGB/JAGB-SN, non-SN ladders, time delays, masers, sirens) and of each early-universe class (CMB per experiment and combined, BAO+BBN, inverse distance ladder), with the PDG scale factor and with the correlations through shared anchors, shared SNe and shared CMB data stated, superseded values excluded;
  - the tension between local and early-universe H0: SH0ES against Planck; SH0ES against the best CMB combination; CCHP against each; the full local combination against each; and grouped χ² within and between classes;
  - the Bayes-factor bound for "one common H0" against "two values", and how the significance depends on which local results are chosen;
  - the precision a future measurement needs to separate the leading candidates at 3σ and at 5σ.

  This is the reference for the observed gap.
- **constraints:**
  - the inverse-distance-ladder constraint: H0 × r_d from BAO + SNe, the r_d needed for H0 = 73, and the fractional reduction of r_s an early-time solution must deliver while keeping θ_* fixed;
  - the background-level effect of each early-time variant on r_s (EDE fraction and injection redshift, ΔN_eff, a shift in m_e), using the run's background-cosmology calculator if it is built;
  - whether any late-time H(z) can raise H0 to about 73 while fitting BAO+SN, and what it would need M_B to do;
  - the size of the local underdensity (contrast and radius) needed to explain the gap, against the ΛCDM cosmic variance of local H0.
- **mechanist:**
  - the size each ladder-systematic sub-hypothesis would need, in magnitudes and in percent of H0 (Cepheid crowding bias, a metallicity term, R_V, a calibrator–Hubble-flow SN offset, an anchor-distance error), against the published error budgets;
  - for each early-side systematic, the size of shift in the CMB or BAO inference it would need.
