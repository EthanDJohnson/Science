Units: astronomical, not SI. H0 is in km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ (written km/s/Mpc), magnitudes in mag, lookback times in Myr, fluxes in W/m² (SI) and temperatures in K. σ is a Gaussian-equivalent significance. The reference gap is H0DN 73.264 ± 0.806 [D-22] minus CMB-SPA 67.19 ± 0.38 [D-44], which is 6.07 km/s/Mpc or 0.188 mag. "Dominant" means carrying more than half of it: 0.094 mag, or a true H0 across ≈ 70.2. ## Response to the verdicts

All three verdicts return `weakened`. They agree on which variants die and which survive, so I answer variant by variant and give each attack.

### Variant (i): smooth H(z) (phantom w, w0wa, IDE). Conceded in full.
- **C4-0 §1, C4-1 §1, C4-2 §1 (inverse ladder).** Conceded. With a standard r_d, BAO plus uncalibrated SNe fix H0·r_d. The inverse ladder gives 67.19–67.67 [D-48], which is 5.3–5.9σ below H0DN (C4-2 §A) and 5.0–5.8σ below the ladder (C4-1). No smooth H(z) moves either number. The w0wa fit the data prefer lowers H0 to 63.6–67.5 [D-51]. A constant w needs w = −1.194 with Ω_m 3.6σ below DESI [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-10]. Any rescue would need a non-standard r_d, and that is C3, not C4. **(i) is withdrawn as a dominant cause.** At most it carries 0–8% of the gap (≤ 31% at 2σ; C4-0).

### Variant (iii): Cepheid-only screened fifth force. Conceded in full.
- **C4-0 §4, C4-1 §3, C4-2 §2.** Conceded. On matched hosts, JAGB − Cepheid = −0.03 ± 0.02 ± 0.05 mag [D-26], and Cepheid, TRGB and JAGB on the distance-limited JWST set give 73.4, 72.1 and 72.2 [D-28]. That limits a Cepheid-specific offset to 0.036–0.05 mag, 19–27% of the gap. The proposers themselves say it "can alleviate (though not resolve)" the tension (quoted in C4-1). If the force also shifted TRGB and JAGB by 0.19 mag, (iii) would lose the prediction that sets it apart (C4-0's own caveat). **(iii) is withdrawn.**

### Variant (ii-G): a universal step in G_eff in time, 9–12% at 145–333 Myr lookback. Conceded as dominant; the rebuttals are partial.
- **Red dwarfs (C4-1 §2c, C4-2 §3).** Sakstein & Desmond 2026 (preprint) exclude the 11% step at 130 Myr at 4.0σ, and at 3.3σ under their conservative radius shift (quoted in C4-1). My partial rebuttal: C4's window starts at 144.5 Myr in SPA-ΛCDM [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-11]. That is beyond the tested 130 Myr, and the memory weakens with lookback. Both refuters say this is unquantified. It narrows the bound; it does not remove it.
- **Insolation and paleoclimate (C4-0 §2, C4-2 §3).** Conceded. With either refuter's exponent, a 9–11% step changes the absorbed flux by −104 to −154 W/m² (cold sign) or by +209 to +374 W/m² (hot sign). Even before feedbacks that is tens of K. The reconstructed Phanerozoic range is only 11–36 °C (Judd et al. 2024, quoted in C4-0). The proposers' own estimate of "about 20% lower" Earth temperature (quoted in C4-0) contradicts that record. I have no calculation that rescues it.
- **Days per year (C4-2 §3).** I accept the refuter's γ = K/P argument as a calculation. A "fluid-like" R ∝ G⁻¹ needs γ = 5/3, and Earth's interior has γ ≈ 3.9–5.8. I note one limitation, as the refuter does: the fossil record's precision at 73–333 Myr was not quantified, so this is a third line of disfavour, not a stand-alone σ.
- **C4-2's correction: the live G-step model is the 4% step at 73 Myr (GSM).** Accepted. It does not rescue dominance: with L_SN ∝ G^1.46, a 4% step moves SNe by only 0.062 mag, 33% of the gap [calc: crux-C4_tests.py]. That is below the half-gap line unless a Cepheid contribution adds, and I did not compute that contribution. It is also disfavoured at 3.1σ by red dwarfs (quoted in C4-1 and C4-2).
- **C4-1's time-delay caveat** (under a G step, kinematic lens masses are biased by about 10%) is moot once (ii-G) is withdrawn.

### Variant (ii-SN): a non-gravitational SN Ia luminosity step of 0.146–0.188 mag between the calibrator volume (z ≲ 0.01) and the Hubble flow (z > 0.023). The only survivor; under real tension.
This is what all three `narrowed:` lines keep. It escapes the inverse-ladder no-go by changing M_B, not H(z) [D-70]. It keeps CMB, BAO+BBN and the inverse ladder at their values, so the true H0 is 67.19–68.50.

- **Non-SN probes read high (C4-0 §3, C4-1 §2b, C4-2 §4). Conceded as real tension, 1.4–3.6σ.**
  - Time delays, masers and sirens: 71.27 ± 1.93, which is 1.44–2.11σ above C4's 68.50–67.19.
  - Adding SBF: 72.26 ± 1.51 (2.33–3.27σ). Adding TF as well: 72.38 ± 1.41 (2.54–3.55σ) (C4-0; M-CONSTRAINTS-14).
  - I accept C4-0's correction to the matrix. Under (ii-SN), SBF should read 67–68.5, not "0". Observed 73.8 ± 2.4 is 2.14–2.72σ high [calc].
  - Two mitigations, both stated in the verdicts: the masers are statistical-only and preliminary [D-32], and the time delays and sirens are preprints [D-31, D-33].
  - **One real point in reply.** These probes do not separate C4(ii-SN) from C1. C1 predicts the same true H0 (67–68.5) and the same one-step values, and C1's own "evidence against" lists the same 72.39 ± 1.41 at 3.32σ. This attack lowers C4 and C1 together. It is evidence for C3, C6 and C8 over both, not a reason to prefer C1 over C4.
- **The near-volume two-rung H0 (C4-1 §2a). This is the most serious attack, and I concede more than the verdict claimed.**
  - Under (ii-SN), Cepheid distances are correct and the calibrator hosts lie inside the step. Their own redshifts must then give the true H0 of 67–68.5. Moving the step does not help: it has to fall between the calibrator SNe and the Hubble-flow SNe, so the calibrator hosts are always inside it.
  - Kenworthy et al. 2022 measure 73.1 +2.6/−2.3. Against C4 that is 2.54σ (CMB-SPA) or 1.94σ (BAO+BBN) [calc].
  - Huang et al. 2025 (peer-reviewed; abstract wording now confirmed by C4-1) measure 73.4 ± 1.0. Against C4 that is **5.81σ (CMB-SPA) or 4.24σ (BAO+BBN)** [calc]. C4-1 did not compute this; it follows from its own quote.
  - My only rebuttal is on precision. Huang's ±1.0 is 2.3–2.6 times tighter than SH0ES's own two-rung analysis of similar Cepheid-host data (Kenworthy, +2.6/−2.3). At cz < 3300 km/s the result is dominated by peculiar-velocity modelling. The dossier rated the Huang numbers unverifiable [D-82], and only the abstract has been checked.
  - So the robust figure against (ii-SN) is Kenworthy's ≈ 2–2.5σ. If Huang's error budget holds up, (ii-SN) is excluded at > 4σ, and C4 should then be scored refuted, not weakened.
- **No mechanism (C4-1, C4-2).** Conceded. No physical cause of a 0.15–0.19 mag SN Ia step localised to a 189 Myr lookback window has been proposed in the inputs once G is excluded. (ii-SN) is a phenomenological placeholder.
- **One piece of evidence that favours (ii-SN) over a rival.** An early-type-host SN ladder with TRGB distances gives 75.3 ± 2.9 (Newman et al. 2026, in candidates.md under C1). C4(ii-SN) predicts the ladder value (0.68σ from 73.26). C1's host-type sub-hypothesis (a) predicts 67–68.5 (2.30–2.77σ off) [calc]. This favours C4 over C1(a) only. It is one ≈ 4% measurement, and C1(b)–(e) are unaffected.

**Net position.** C4 survives only as (ii-SN), a phenomenological, non-gravitational SN Ia luminosity step. It sits in 1.4–3.6σ tension with SN-free probes and 1.9–2.5σ tension with the Kenworthy two-rung value, and possibly > 4σ tension with Huang's. I would not defend it as the most likely answer. Its defensible content is that it is the only post-recombination route not already closed by an independent bound.

## Deciding observation

The thresholds for 3σ and 5σ separation come from [calc: runs/2026-10-30-hubble-tension-h0-discrepancy/calc/crux-C4_tests.py]. Lens counts use the no-floor scaling from TDCOSMO's 3.3% with 11 lenses [D-31], which is optimistic.

- **Against all rivals at once: the near-volume two-rung H0.** Use the Cepheid, TRGB or JAGB host distances of the SN calibrators and their own redshifts at cz < 3300 km/s, with no SNe used.
  - C4(ii-SN) predicts 67.19–68.50.
  - C3, C6, C7, C8, and C1's anchor and Cepheid sub-hypotheses (e, f) predict ≈ 70–73.
  - Separating 73.02 from 68.50 needs σ ≤ 0.90 km/s/Mpc (1.3%) for 5σ, or ≤ 1.51 for 3σ.
  - Huang 2025 claims ±1.0 already. An independent re-analysis of the peculiar-velocity modelling at that precision would settle C4 in either direction. Natural candidates are Kenworthy's group, or the Stiskalek et al. Manticore-Local flow team, who have published TRGB two-rung work [D-34].
  - No date is given in the inputs. The precision is reachable with current data if the peculiar-velocity systematics can be controlled.

- **C4 vs C1 (same true H0 of 67–68.5; the two differ on where the M_B offset lives).** Measure the M_B of SNe Ia against distance, at fixed host colour, environment and type, across 20–100 Mpc, using SN-independent distances (JWST TRGB/JAGB, SBF, or masers) calibrated by one anchor.
  - C4 predicts a 0.146–0.188 mag brightening that switches on between z ≈ 0.01 and 0.023 (41–94 Mpc) whatever the host's properties.
  - C1(a)/(b) predicts an offset that tracks host or selection properties, not distance. C1(e)/(f) predicts no step.
  - Telling a 0.146 mag step from none needs σ(ΔM_B) ≤ 0.049 mag (3σ) or ≤ 0.029 mag (5σ). That is about 19 or 53 SNe on each side of the step, at an **assumed** total scatter of 0.15 mag per SN; that scatter is not in the inputs.
  - Who: C4's own decisive test names SH0ES or CCHP JWST programmes and ZTF DR2+ low-z SNe. Whether JWST TRGB reaches beyond 45 Mpc is not established in the inputs. No date is given.

- **C4 vs C3: the anchor-free, r_d-free one-step H0** (time delays, sirens, lensed SNe).
  - C4 predicts ≤ 68.50. C3 predicts ≥ 71.0, its best fit without a ladder prior (Poulin 2026 [D-53]).
  - Separation needs σ ≤ 0.83 km/s/Mpc (1.19%) for 3σ, or ≤ 0.50 (0.71%) for 5σ: about 85 or 235 lenses without a floor. Against the CMB-SPA end (67.19), it needs ≤ 1.27 (3σ) or ≤ 0.76 (5σ), about 36 or 101 lenses.
  - Who: TDCOSMO (now 3.3% with 11 lenses [D-31]) and LVK sirens. LVK IR1 is planned for January 2027 and O5 is "in discussion" (dossier §6).
  - A secondary check: C3 predicts damping-tail detection of f_EDE > 0 in SO/SPT-3G without a ladder prior, and C4 predicts none. No forecast or date is given in the inputs.

- **C4 vs C6: the size of the localised step.**
  - C6 says no single variable carries more than half the gap (≤ 0.094 mag). C4 needs ≥ 0.146 mag carried by redshift alone.
  - Same observation as against C1, but it needs σ(ΔM_B) ≤ 0.017 mag (3σ) or ≤ 0.010 mag (5σ), about 150 or 416 SNe a side at the assumed 0.15 mag.
  - The anchor-free H0 cannot do this job. C4's 67.19–68.50 and C6's 69–71 are as little as 0.5 km/s/Mpc apart, so a 3σ separation would need σ ≈ 0.17 km/s/Mpc (simple ratio 0.5/3, not in the calc log), which is far beyond any forecast in the inputs. The localised-step size is therefore the deciding observation against C6.

- **C4 vs C7: an SN-free, anchor-calibrated Hubble-flow H0** (SBF or TF galaxies at z > 0.023).
  - C7 says the whole anchor scale is offset, so these read 73.02 ± 0.78 [math: M-EXAMINER-06]. C4(ii-SN) says only SNe changed, so these read ≤ 68.50.
  - Separation needs σ ≤ 1.51 km/s/Mpc (3σ) or ≤ 0.90 (5σ). SBF is now 73.8 ± 2.4, which is already 2.14–2.72σ toward C7 [calc]. A threefold improvement in SBF's systematic error (now 2.3 km/s/Mpc [D-29], dominated by its few calibrators) would decide it.
  - Who: the Jensen/Blakeslee SBF team with more JWST TRGB-calibrated SBF zero points. No date is given.

- **C4 vs C8: the team-independent, SN-free local H0.**
  - C8 says the real local excess is ≈ 71.2–71.7. C4 says the non-SN truth is ≤ 68.50.
  - Separation needs σ ≤ 0.90 km/s/Mpc (3σ) or ≤ 0.54 (5σ): about 72 or 201 lenses on the no-floor scaling.
  - Who and when: the same as against C3.
  - C8's own test (a ≥ 35-host TRGB/JAGB-SN ladder on one SN system) separates the two only weakly. C4 predicts ≈ 73.26 and C8 predicts 70–72, so even at C8's ≤ 1.5% precision the separation is about 2σ.
