## Response to the verdicts

The verdicts are weakened (C7-0, magnitude), weakened (C7-1, evidence) and survives (C7-2, bounds). I accept most of what the two "weakened" verdicts take away. What is left is narrower than the candidate as written:

> **C7 as defended.** For the observable expansion history (0.02 ≲ z ≲ 2.3), and on the published SN Ia compilations, the local-versus-early H0 gap is a disagreement between two absolute calibrations: the anchor-to-SN Ia transfer (M_B; with M_B + 5a_B as the invariant) and the sound-horizon ruler r_d. No post-recombination H(z) change that fits BAO plus SN shape narrows the gap by more than ≈ 0.2σ_gap. The pin below z ≈ 0.1 holds only as well as the legacy low-z SN cross-calibration. On the r_d branch the physical cause may still be a higher pre-recombination expansion rate (that is C3). "Mis-posed" is downgraded to "mis-labelled": the reframe is an equivalent, more informative restatement of the split, not a correction that current data prefer.

### Verdict C7-0 (refuter 0, magnitude: weakened)

1. **The z ≲ 0.1 pin rests on the legacy low-z SNe (Nájera, Banik & Desmond 2026): mostly conceded, partly rebutted.**
   - *Conceded.* C7 does not state that its H(z) pin below z ≈ 0.1 depends on legacy low-z SNe sitting on the high-z photometric scale to about 0.04 mag. It should. With DES-only SNe the cosmographic fit gives 76.25 ± 2.87 under the standard r_d, so the shape freedom absorbs the gap at large error. With a free legacy offset the fit gives 71.03 ± 1.99, a +3.05 km/s/Mpc z ≲ 0.09 expansion-rate share (C7-0). That breaks C7's own "≤ 0.5σ" prediction as written.
   - *Rebuttal 1: the H(z) freedom appears only once a calibration is released.* In the free-offset case the gap is not moved out of the calibrations. Part of it goes into ΔM_legacy = 0.0646 ± 0.0391 mag, an inter-sample SN calibration offset equal to 0.34 of the 0.188 mag gap, and the H(z) enhancement then absorbs the rest. The refuter makes the same point in its self-doubt: "the legacy offset may itself be read as a third calibration". So the case widens C7's list of calibrations; it does not replace calibrations with expansion rates.
   - *Rebuttal 2: the case is weak on current data.* The offset is preferred at 1.65σ. The resulting 71.03 ± 1.99 is 1.04σ from H0DN and 1.90σ from CMB-SPA, so it decides nothing. With legacy SNe as published, the same paper finds 67.66 ± 1.01 with z1 unconstrained, meaning the pin holds. The paper is a two-week-old preprint.
   - *Physical cost.* A homogeneous z ≲ 0.09 H(z) excess of this size is, physically, local structure. That faces the C5 bound: δ ≈ −0.5 over ≳ 430 h⁻¹ Mpc, 84σ in ΛCDM [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-12]. Alternatively it is a hockey-stick dark-energy step, which Camarena & Marra 2021 bound through the M_B mismatch (cited in C7-2). I accept C7-0's caveat that this limits how plausible the third locus is, not whether the data pin it.
   - *Net.* I adopt C7-0's narrowing: "at least ≈ 85% of the gap maps onto the anchor-to-SN calibration versus r_d on published compilations; conditional on the legacy low-z cross-calibration."
2. **"Geometric-anchor distance scale" misplaces the calibration: conceded.** Leave-one-anchor-out moves H0DN by ≤ 0.53 km/s/Mpc [D-23]. NGC 4258 would need a 4.8–6.5σ error (C7-0). The calibration in conflict is the transfer from anchors to SN M_B (primary-indicator zero points and the calibrator SN sample), not the anchor geometry. C7's wording should say "anchor-to-SN calibration".
3. **M_B is not more basic than H0: conceded.** Once the cross-system intercept is restored, the measured M_B pair (−19.253 against −19.375/−19.394 mag [D-21]) reduces to the H0 gap. The residual is 5Δa_B = 0.059/0.025 mag (C7-0 §2; C7-2 point 2), matching the ≈ 0.04 mag DES5Y–Pantheon+ offset [D-85]. Only M_B + 5a_B is invariant across SN systems. C7's M_B language is an equivalent restatement, and the 0.146–0.188 mag figures are H0 ratios in magnitudes [math: M-DIALECTICIAN-12].
4. **"One anchor scale" has an internal spread comparable to the gap in magnitudes: partly conceded.**
   - Conceded: SH0ES against CCHP TRGB/JWST/JAGB differs by 0.094–0.169 mag at the shared NGC 4258 anchor (C7-0). With a common offset, the exhaustive partition moves CCHP to the early group at < 2 in χ² [math: M-DECOMPOSER-10].
   - Rebutted in significance: these splits are 1.4–2.3σ naively (C7-0). The anchor-group χ² is 2.20/3, p = 0.53 (C7-2). C7's own falsifier ("ladders split by more than their errors") is not triggered.
   - C7 already places the split inside the anchor family, as a TRGB/JAGB zero-point or sample problem at NGC 4258 [D-26, D-28, D-80]. That is still a calibration conflict. The §3 test for C8 is where this point can fail.
5. **The arbiter is untested, and a midpoint result would turn C7 into C6: conceded in full.** Now 68.8–71.1 ± 1.2–2.3 km/s/Mpc, 0.24–0.66 of the way between the poles (C7-0). This is the C6 crux below.

### Verdict C7-1 (refuter 1, evidence: weakened)

1. **r_d is an expansion-rate quantity, so "not between expansion rates" fails on the r_d branch: conceded as a scope correction.**
   - r_d = ∫ c_s/H dz over z > z_d. A uniform 6.06–9.05% rise in H(z > z_d) gives the needed 138.6–134.8 Mpc (C7-1 part b). Knox & Millea's "least unlikely" class is exactly such a pre-recombination expansion-rate change (C7-1).
   - C7's argument only ever claimed the pin "between z ≈ 0.02 and 2.3", but the heading overreaches. The corrected heading reads: "not between post-recombination expansion histories".
   - Two qualifications survive:
     - recombination-physics routes (varying m_e, PMF) shrink r_d without raising H (C7-1);
     - a pre-recombination H change is not observed as "an early H0". It is inferred only through r_d.
   - So the reframe still correctly says what the early side *measures*: θ_*, ω_b and ω_c turned into r_d, then into H0 [D-01]. The refuter grants that, with this reading, C7 is "close to survives".
2. **"Mis-posed" is stronger than the data allow: conceded; the partition-fit point is rebutted in part.**
   - Conceded: current partitions do not prefer C7's split over "early vs late". P1, P2 and P4 are statistically equivalent [math: M-EXAMINER-06], and the exhaustive search slightly favours all-local vs all-early [math: M-DECOMPOSER-02]. C7's evidence-for line citing P1/P2/P4 shows non-inferiority only.
   - Rebutted in part: this equivalence is expected under both framings. The two partitions differ only in where the arbiters go (time delays, sirens, r_d-free BAO), and those are at 1.2–9 km/s/Mpc precision [D-31, D-33, D-49]. Partition χ² cannot decide C7. It also cannot count against it.
   - The arbiters' lean toward the anchor side (likelihood ratio 2.3–4.2 : 1, C7-1 part c) fits C7's r_d branch as well as "early vs late". It does not favour one over the other.
   - Downgrade accepted: "mis-posed" becomes "mis-labelled". The content that survives is the inverse-ladder no-go [D-70; Efstathiou 2021 as quoted in C7-1], which the "early vs late" wording hides.
3. **The "≤ 0.5σ" prediction is ambiguous and partly false: conceded.** The source sentence (Schöneberg, Poulin et al. 2026) is about early-time models with evolving dark energy added, not "any modification of H(z)" (C7-1). DESI w0wa fits with SNe move the r_d-side H0 by −0.66 to −2.26 km/s/Mpc, 0.77–2.65 σ_gap [D-51; C7-1 part a; C7-2 point 1]. Corrected one-sided form (C7-2): no H(z) that fits BAO plus SN narrows the gap by more than ≈ 0.2σ_gap. Several widen it, all of them in the widening direction. C7's falsifier becomes "a smooth H(z) change is found that *narrows* the gap".
4. **The framing is not novel: conceded, and it helps.** Aylor et al. 2019, Camarena & Marra 2021 and Greene & Cyr-Racine 2022 already pose the tension as ladder-r_s against model r_s, or as a disagreement of distance measurements (quoted in C7-1, C7-2). C7 claims a correct framing, not a new one.
5. **C5 is removed by bounds, not "by construction": conceded.** A local underdensity is a local-versus-global expansion-rate difference. If C5 held, C7 would be false, not incomplete. It is excluded by the SN Hubble-diagram bound and cosmic variance [D-77; math: M-CONSTRAINTS-12]. C7's exclusion of C5 is an empirical bet the evidence supports.

### Verdict C7-2 (refuter 2, bounds: survives)

The verdict leaves the core standing. I accept each of its corrections:

- **"BAO + SN shape pin H(z)" is too strong.** The pinned quantity carries a 1–3% dependence on the late-time model and the SN sample (w0wa range 65.91–67.51 [D-51]). It also assumes flat FRW and SN standardisation that does not evolve with z [D-70 assumptions]. With Ω_k free, the chronometer route (Favale et al. 2026: r_d = 144.00 +5.38/−4.88 Mpc) does not yet separate the calibrations.
- **The equality-scale and r_d-free arbiters are clean only because of ACT's N_eff.**
  - Under ΔN_eff they move together with r_d (N_eff = 4.12–4.45 would carry the equality route to 73.26). ACT's N_eff = 2.86 ± 0.13 [D-74] excludes this at 9.7–12.2σ (C7-2 point 3).
  - The decisive test below names this assumption.
- **The r_d group is marginal.** CMB-SPA against BAO+BBN is a 1.89σ split (p = 0.059), and the DESI–CMB Ω_m discrepancy is 2.3–2.8σ [D-87]. If that discrepancy grows, a third locus opens inside the early side and C7's two-calibration picture is incomplete. This is a stated weakness, not yet a failure.
- **C7 is thin beyond D-70.** Conceded: its empirical content is the inverse-ladder no-go plus a prediction about where arbiters land.

**What C7 loses overall:**
- the word "mis-posed";
- the unqualified "not between expansion rates" (it now holds only after recombination);
- the two-sided "≤ 0.5σ" prediction;
- "geometric-anchor" as the locus;
- "by construction" for C5;
- an unstated assumption about legacy low-z SNe, now stated.

**What it keeps:** on published data, at least ≈ 85% of the gap sits in the anchor-to-SN calibration versus r_d. No SN-fitted late-time H(z) narrows it, and the question reduces to a calibrator-free arbiter.

## Deciding observation

C7 is a reframe. It wins against a rival only where the two predict different outcomes, and against C1 and C3 it is mostly *nested*: each of them names one of C7's two branches. In those cases I say plainly when C7 should give way to the more specific rival.

**Main observation (all rivals): an anchor-free, r_d-free global H0.** Candidate sources are time delays at ≈ 40–50 lenses, LVK O5 bright sirens, and DESI sound-horizon-free BAO. The equality-scale route counts only while ACT's N_eff bound holds. C7 predicts the value lands decisively near one pole: the anchor group 73.02 ± 0.78 or the r_d group 67.51 ± 0.29 [math: M-EXAMINER-06]. Required precision, using H0DN 73.264 ± 0.806 against CMB-SPA 67.19 ± 0.38:
- a result at the anchor pole excludes the r_d pole at 3σ with σ_arb ≤ 1.99 km/s/Mpc, or at 5σ with σ_arb ≤ 1.15;
- a result at the r_d pole excludes the anchor pole at 3σ with σ_arb ≤ 1.86, or at 5σ with σ_arb ≤ 0.91;
- a midpoint result (70.23) excludes both poles at 3σ only with σ_arb ≤ 0.61. With the group means instead: 1.81/1.66 at 3σ, 1.06/0.78 at 5σ, and 0.48 for the midpoint.

All of these are [calc: runs/2026-10-30-hubble-tension-h0-discrepancy/calc/crux-C7_deciding.py §1]. They agree with the run's σ ≤ 1.21 km/s/Mpc for 5σ separation [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-15].

Who and when:
- TDCOSMO is at 3.3% with 11 lenses [D-31]. Review forecasts are ≈ 40 lenses for 1% (dossier §6).
- The LVK O5 timeline is "in discussion", with IR1 planned for mid-January 2027 [dossier §6].
- No date for DESI DR3 was found [dossier §6].
- No collaboration-stated date for this precision was found.

**Against C6 (no single dominant cause).** The arbiter above at σ_arb ≤ 0.61 km/s/Mpc (0.87%) decides it:
- centred at 70–71 and excluding both 67.19 and 73.26 at ≥ 3σ: C6 wins and C7 is refuted, because its "decisive landing" prediction fails and "two calibrations" becomes "several partial loci";
- landing within 1σ_arb of either pole and excluding the other at ≥ 3σ (σ_arb ≤ 1.86–1.99): C7 is favoured and C6's hybrid fails.

This is the sharpest test C7 faces. It needs the sub-1% arbiter that no programme in the dossier has a date for.

**Against C1 (ladder systematic, true H0 ≈ 67–68.5).** The same arbiter decides it, but asymmetrically:
- ≥ 71.0 km/s/Mpc at σ_arb ≤ 1.2 (70.97 is 3σ above CMB-SPA 67.19 ± 0.38 [calc §1b]): C1 is refuted and C7 survives on its r_d branch;
- 67–68.5 at σ_arb ≤ 0.91 (5σ below 73.26): both survive. C1 is then the better answer because it names the side, and C7 shrinks to C1's accounting identity.

I concede that no single observation favours C7 *over* C1 when the arbiter lands low. A second test separates them inside the ladder: a same-SN-system comparison of ladder M_B against inverse-ladder M_B [D-21].
- C7 predicts the difference equals 5 log10 of the H0 ratio, 0.146–0.188 mag, with Hubble-flow intercept residuals 5Δa_B → 0.
- C1's sub-hypothesis (c), the peculiar-velocity treatment (Dong 2026: 74.30 → 71.37 [D-82]), puts up to 0.087 mag in a_B instead, which is not a calibration.

The comparison needs σ(5Δa_B) ≲ 0.02 mag to resolve the current 0.025–0.059 mag residual (C7-2 point 2). DES-SN5YR or SH0ES could make it on one photometric system. No date is known.

**Against C3 (pre-recombination new physics).** C3 is C7's r_d branch with a named cause, and an expansion-rate cause at that (conceded to C7-1). Two observations decide between them:
- *Arbiter landing.* A result ≤ 68.5 at σ_arb ≤ 0.91 favours C7 (anchor branch) and refutes C3. A result ≥ 71 favours both.
- *CMB signature.* If the arbiter lands high, SO or SPT-3G Ext-10k damping-tail and polarisation spectra decide. A detection of f_EDE > 0 at ≥ 3σ without a ladder prior, with the same sign in ACT, means C3 wins as the more specific and physical answer. C7 then keeps only its late-time statement. Non-detection with f_EDE bounded well below 0.06 [D-75], together with a high arbiter, leaves C7's agnostic "the r_d calibration is off" as the only standing description.

No forecast σ(f_EDE) and no release dates for SO or SPT-3G Ext were found [dossier §6].

**Against C4 (post-recombination new physics).** C4(i), a smooth late-time H(z) change, is the variant C7 directly denies. The deciding observation is a homogeneous low-z SN Ia sample tied to the DES-Dovekie photometric system: DEBASS, or ZTF DR2+ with a cross-calibration (C7-0; C4 decisive test). It tests whether the legacy low-z offset of Nájera et al. 2026 is real.
- ΔM_legacy consistent with 0 at σ ≤ 0.0215 mag, i.e. excluding 0.0646 mag at 3σ (0.0129 mag for 5σ), with no H(z) enhancement at z ≲ 0.09: the C7 pin holds and C4(i) and its low-z cousin are excluded.
- ΔM_legacy ≈ 0.065 mag confirmed, with an expansion-rate excess at z ≲ 0.09: a third, expansion-rate locus carries ≈ 50–60% of the gap (C7-0), C7's core claim fails, and C4(i) or C5-type physics gains.

For C4(ii), the M_B/G_eff step, C7 has no quarrel in principle: it is a calibration that fails to transfer. A 0.15–0.19 mag M_B step in hosts at 45–100 Mpc [C4 decisive test] would favour C4(ii) as the named cause on the anchor branch. No dates are in the inputs.

**Against C8 (single-team significance).** C7 says all anchor-calibrated ladders share one scale (73.02 ± 0.78). C8 says a team-independent ladder lands at 70–72. The deciding observation is a team-independent, full-sample (≥ 35-host) TRGB/JAGB ladder on one SN photometric system [D-80; C8 decisive test].
- A result ≤ 71.0 at σ_ind ≤ 0.8 km/s/Mpc is ≥ 2.0σ from SH0ES 73.49 ± 0.93. That triggers C7's own falsifier (anchor ladders split by more than their errors) and favours C8.
- A result ≥ 72.5 at the same precision favours C7 and, by C8's own statement, refutes C8.

At the C8 test's σ ≈ 1.1 km/s/Mpc, only a result ≤ 70.6 makes a 2σ split. Telling 71.0 from 73.02 at 3σ is impossible against the anchor group's own 0.78 error, and needs σ_ind ≤ 0.67 on measurement error alone [calc §3]. CCHP or a third team could make it. No collaboration date was found [dossier §6].
