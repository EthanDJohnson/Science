## Response to the verdicts

All three verdicts say `weakened`, and all three narrow C6 to the same object. I accept that narrowing. What survives is a two-part hybrid:
- a ladder-side offset L of about 2.3–3.0 km/s/Mpc, coming from SN calibration, selection or peculiar-velocity levers;
- a real pre-recombination r_d reduction E of about 2–3 km/s/Mpc;
- at most about a 1σ helpful fluctuation;
- a true H0 in the 1σ window 69.43–70.60 km/s/Mpc (2σ window 68.62–70.98) [C6-0 §3; C6-2].

Taking the attacks point by point:

**1. "Diffuse underestimated errors spread over several measurements" (sub-hypothesis (b)): conceded in full.** C6-1 and C6-2 show that the common ladder shift of 4.29 km/s/Mpc that (b) needs is 70.7% of the gap, so by the slate's own rule it is C1, not C6 [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-13]. The error-inflation reading is ×3.74 the H0DN error and 7.5× the H0DN variant spread of 0.40 [D-23]. All the excess χ² lies between classes, not within them [math: M-EXAMINER-05, M-EXAMINER-06]. The candidate text already conceded that "a diffuse null must in practice be a ladder null". The heading phrase "underestimated errors spread over several measurements" should be dropped.

**2. Pure fluctuation (sub-hypothesis (c)): conceded.** It needs 6.82σ against H0DN (6.27σ against SH0ES), and the heavy-tailed all-local excess is still 4.3–4.5σ [math: M-STATISTICIAN-14]. Fluctuation survives only as a minor term of ≤ 1σ (≤ 0.9 km/s/Mpc).

**3. "True H0 ≈ 69–71" and the decisive band "70–71": conceded.** At H0 = 69.0 with no fluctuation the ladder carries 70.2% of the gap (C1), and at 71.0 the early side carries 62.7% (C3) [C6-0 §2]. The correct statement is 69.4–70.6 at 1σ. The decisive band "centred at 70–71" was wrong at its upper end: a result at 71.0 favours C3 (P(early > ½) ≈ 0.98 [C6-0 §6b]). Landing inside the window at ±2σ needs σ_T ≤ 0.29 km/s/Mpc (0.42%), and ±3σ needs ≤ 0.19, not the 0.61 the candidate quoted [calc: crux-C6_balance.py §3; agrees with C6-0's 0.30].

**4. Poulin 2026 (71.0, 62.7%) and eROSITA (71.4, 69.3%) are C3 outcomes, not C6 partial contributions: conceded.** C6's early part must leave the early-side fit at H0 ≤ 70.2. The cited "30–67%" range should read "31–50%":
- P-ACT-LB EDE 69.9 (45%);
- modified recombination 69.6 (40%);
- PMF 69.1 (31%) [D-52; math: M-DECOMPOSER-05].

**5. The DESI–CMB Ω_m offset is not shown to be an early-universe bias: conceded.** It lies along Ω_m h³ = const [D-87], and the SPT authors attribute the DESI-driven EDE preference to it [D-53, C6-1 quote]. It also cannot carry the early share on its own. With E = 1.31 km/s/Mpc and the ladder at its ceiling, C6 needs a 1.73 km/s/Mpc fluctuation, 1.94σ (p = 0.026) [calc: crux-C6_balance.py §1, reproducing C6-1].

**6. The early part must be real r_d physics, which makes C6 "partial C1 + partial C3" (C6-2): conceded as a description, rebutted as a disqualification.**
- The r_d-calibrated routes cannot all be wrong by chance: at T = 70.2 they sit 7.9σ (CMB-SPA), 2.9σ (BAO+BBN) and 3.7–4.6σ (inverse ladder) low [C6-2].
- The r_d needed at T = 70.2 is 141.2 Mpc (−3.9%) with H0·r_d at the inverse-ladder value [C6-2], or 140.7 Mpc (−4.3%) with H0·r_d at CMB-SPA [calc: crux-C6_balance.py §3]. At the window edges the cut is 3.2–4.8%.
- Extra dark radiation is excluded for this cut (N_eff 3.36–3.70, 3.8–7.4σ above ACT [C6-2]), so the early part must be EDE-like or modified recombination.
- The slate itself, however, defines C6 by the half-gap rule ("contributions spread across several measurement classes with none above half are the null"), not by the absence of new physics. A 50/50 C1 + C3 split is therefore C6 by construction. That is not a contradiction; it is what the null means on this slate. C6-2 also notes that the narrowed C6 and "C1 + C3 at 50/50" are the same thing, and I accept that identification.

**7. Posterior weight of the balanced region (C6-0 §6: P(C6) = 0.06–0.10): conceded in substance, with one rebuttal that changes the comparison, not C6's standing.**
- My own grid with C6-0's prior (L and E flat on [0, 10] km/s/Mpc) reproduces it closely: P(C6) = 0.067 / 0.104 / 0.081 for the one-step, one-step + r_s-free and + CCHP/SBF/TF probe sets [calc: crux-C6_balance.py §2].
- That prior, however, lets the early shift E run to 10 km/s/Mpc, which ignores every CMB-only EDE bound. Under it, C3 takes 0.45–0.70.
- Capping E at what CMB-only fits allow removes C3 entirely. With E ≤ 2.71 (the P-ACT-LB EDE centre 69.9 [D-52]), P(C6) rises to 0.14–0.26 and P(C1) is 0.74–0.86. With E ≤ 2.31 (the Planck NPIPE 95% upper edge H0 < 69.5 [D-53]), P(C6) is 0.08–0.17 and P(C1) is 0.83–0.92 [calc: crux-C6_balance.py §2].
- So C6 is never the favourite under either prior. It is the runner-up in each case: to C3 when the early side is free, and to C1 when CMB-only bounds hold. Where C6 gains is that it is the only candidate that keeps meaningful weight under both priors.
- I concede that this is a fine-tuned position. Its probability is 0.07–0.26 in my calculation, not the 0.5 a symmetric null would imply.

**8. "Fine-tuning" is partly an artefact of a sharp 50% line.** C6-0 itself says so ("partly a feature of the slate's definition"), so I record it as agreed, not as a rebuttal that restores the broad null.

**What stands uncontradicted** (C6-1 lists the same):
- partial ladder levers of 1.9–2.9 km/s/Mpc each [D-35, D-82, D-88];
- the r_d-free values 69.2–70.3 [D-49] and the one-step mean 71.27 ± 1.93 [math: M-DIALECTICIAN-07], which sit at or near the window;
- an age at T = 70.2 of 13.22 Gyr, −1.7σ from the GC age [C6-2];
- no independent bound violated by the narrowed hybrid [C6-0, C6-1, C6-2].

**The single number C6 rests on** is an early-side shift E of 2.3–3.0 km/s/Mpc, i.e. an r_d cut of about 3–4%. With E at 2.31 (the Planck NPIPE edge) C6 needs a 0.82σ fluctuation; at 2.71 it needs 0.37σ [calc: crux-C6_balance.py §1]. That size sits at the upper edge of the CMB-only bounds and has not been detected. This is C6's real weak point, and it is also what decides C6 against its rivals.

## Deciding observation

The core crux, shared with C1 and C3, is how large the real early-side shift E is, measured without a ladder prior:
- C6 needs E = 2.3–3.0 km/s/Mpc, an r_d cut of ≈ 3–4%;
- C1 needs E ≈ 0 and a standard r_d of 147.00 Mpc;
- C3 needs E > 3.04.

Because E alone cannot resolve the 3.04 boundary (that would need σ(E) ≈ 0.1), each test below pairs it with an anchor-free, r_d-free measurement of the true H0, T. The precisions come from [calc: crux-C6_balance.py §3].

- **vs C1 (ladder systematic, true H0 ≈ 67–68.5).**
  - *Observation:* a CMB-only (no ladder prior) detection or exclusion of an r_d-shrinking component, i.e. EDE f_EDE or modified recombination, from Simons Observatory or SPT-3G Ext damping-tail and polarisation spectra, quoted as the H0 shift relative to ΛCDM.
  - *Which result favours which:* E ≥ 2.3 km/s/Mpc favours C6; f_EDE consistent with 0 and E < 1 favours C1.
  - *Precision:* σ(E) ≤ 0.77 km/s/Mpc separates 2.3 from 0 at 3σ, and ≤ 0.46 at 5σ.
  - *Cross-check:* an anchor-free, r_d-free H0 at σ_T ≤ 0.45 km/s/Mpc separates C1's centre 67.75 from C6's 70.02 at 5σ. This could come from TDCOSMO time delays (≈ 40–50 lenses), LVK sirens, or DESI sound-horizon-free BAO; today time delays reach 3.3% with 11 lenses [D-31].
  - *Schedules:* no SO or SPT-3G Ext release dates were found [dossier §6]; LVK IR1 is planned for mid-January 2027 and the O5 timeline is "in discussion" [dossier §6].

- **vs C3 (early-time physics dominant, true H0 > 70.2, ≈ 71–73).**
  - *Observation:* the same anchor-free, r_d-free T, together with an adjudicated ladder correction.
  - *Which result favours which:* T ≤ 70.6 favours C6; T ≥ 71 favours C3. Separating C3's 71.0 from C6's 70.02 at 3σ needs σ_T ≤ 0.33 km/s/Mpc (0.47%).
  - *Ladder leg:* C6 requires an identified ladder correction of ≥ 2.3 km/s/Mpc (≈ 0.07 mag), whereas C3 allows only a sub-half residual. A third-team re-standardisation of calibrator and Hubble-flow SNe, or CCHP's full ≥ 35-host TRGB set on one SN system, that lands at ≤ 71 favours C6; ≥ 72.5 favours C3 [D-80, D-82; C1's runner-up test in candidates.md].
  - *Schedule:* no collaboration dates were found [dossier §6].

- **vs C4 (post-recombination physics, mainly the M_B/G_eff step (ii)).**
  - *Observation:* the anchor-free one-step H0. C4(ii) puts the geometric one-step methods at ≈ 67–68; C6 puts them at 69.4–70.6. Separating 67.75 from 70.02 at 5σ needs σ_T ≤ 0.45 km/s/Mpc.
  - *Secondary test:* the calibrator-vs-Hubble-flow M_B step for SNe at 45–100 Mpc with JWST TRGB or geometric host distances. C4(ii) needs 0.146–0.188 mag; C6's whole ladder share is ≤ 0.092 mag. Separating those at 3σ needs σ ≤ 0.018 mag.
  - *Who:* ZTF DR2+ / JWST host programmes [candidates.md C4].
  - *Schedule:* no date is in the inputs.

- **vs C7 (two-calibrations reframe).**
  - *How they relate:* C6 is compatible with C7's statement that the conflict sits in M_B and r_d. The two differ on the outcome: C7 predicts the r_d-free, anchor-free H0 lands "decisively near one calibrator", while C6 says both calibrations are partly off.
  - *Observation:* r_d-free, anchor-free T at σ_T ≤ 0.29 km/s/Mpc (±2σ inside 69.4–70.6) or ≤ 0.19 (±3σ).
  - *Which result favours which:* a result inside the window favours C6; one at ≤ 68.5 or ≥ 72 favours C7's one-calibrator reading.
  - *What does not decide it:* a same-SN-system M_B comparison between ladder and inverse ladder [D-21]. Under both C6 and C7 that comparison shows the full gap. C6 says the true M_B lies between the two values, which only an anchor-free and r_d-free T can show. That comparison therefore decides between C7 and C4(i), not between C7 and C6.
  - *Schedule:* none found.

- **vs C8 (single-team significance).**
  - *How they relate:* C6 and C8 are largely compatible. C8 is a premise reframe that does not say which side errs, and the narrowed C6 puts the ladder bias mainly in the Cepheid-SN (SH0ES) ladder, with team-independent local probes reading roughly the true H0. C8 is still judged as a rival, so the separating observation is the one below.
  - *Observation:* a team-independent, non-Cepheid local combination (a full-host TRGB/JAGB ladder on one SN system, plus SBF and one-step methods).
  - *Which result favours which:* C8 predicts 70–72. C6 predicts at most 70.6, the upper edge of its window. A result at the current non-SH0ES mean of 71.56 would favour C8's ≈ 4 km/s/Mpc team-independent excess over C6, since it would push the early share above half (toward C3). Separating 71.56 from 70.6 at 3σ needs σ ≤ 0.32 km/s/Mpc, far below C8's own target of σ ≤ 1.1 [candidates.md C8].
  - *Schedule:* no collaboration date found [dossier §6].

**Single most decisive observation.** One anchor-free, r_d-free global H0 (time delays + sirens + sound-horizon-free BAO) at σ_T ≈ 0.3 km/s/Mpc (≈ 0.4%), together with a CMB-only r_d measurement at σ(E) ≤ 0.77 km/s/Mpc:
- T in 69.4–70.6 with E ≥ 2.3 favours C6;
- T ≤ 68.5 with E ≈ 0 favours C1 (and C4(ii) if an M_B step also appears);
- T ≥ 71 favours C3 (or C8's reading, if no r_d signal appears).

Current precision is about 3.3% (≈ 2.3 km/s/Mpc) for time delays [D-31], so the T leg is roughly a factor of 8 short. Only review-level forecasts exist: ≈ 40 lenses for 1% (Treu 2026, dossier §6), and even 1% (≈ 0.70 km/s/Mpc at H0 ≈ 70) is about 2.4× coarser than C6 needs. C6 is therefore the candidate whose confirmation lies furthest away.
