# Verdict on C4 (refuter 0, angle: magnitude)
claim: Post-recombination new physics, either a modified expansion history H(z) or a low-redshift change in G_eff or standard-candle luminosity, is the dominant cause of the gap.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: Only a 0.15–0.19 mag step in SN Ia luminosity between z ≈ 0.01 and 0.023 can still carry most of the gap, and only if its cause is either non-gravitational or a spatial rather than temporal G_eff boundary; that form is disfavoured at 2.1–3.6σ because non-SN local probes read 71–72, not the ≈ 67–68.5 it predicts.

Units: astronomical. H0 in km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹, magnitudes in mag, temperatures in K or °C, fluxes in W m⁻² (SI), times in Myr. Gap reference: H0DN-corrected 73.264 ± 0.806 [D-22] minus CMB-SPA 67.19 ± 0.38 [D-44] = 6.074 km/s/Mpc = 0.188 mag. Under the candidates file's own boundary rule, "dominant" means carrying more than half of it, i.e. moving the true global H0 across 70.23 [calc: runs/2026-10-30-hubble-tension-h0-discrepancy/calc/falsifier-C4-0_magnitude.py].

## Strongest attack

C4 names three variants. On magnitude, each one either carries less than half of the gap, or carries it only by implying something that is plainly false.

1. **(i) Smooth H(z) changes carry 0–8% of the gap, and at most 21–31% at 2σ.** The inverse distance ladder makes no assumption about the shape of H(z) once r_d is fixed. It gives 67.19 (cosmography) and 67.67 (flat ΛCDM) [D-48]. To reach the dominance line at 70.23, it would have to fluctuate upward by 3.7–4.7σ. The DESI DR2 w0wa fits move H0 the wrong way, by −59% to +5% of the gap. wCDM fits that include SNe carry 2–14%. Only DESI+CMB wCDM without SNe gets to 38%, which is still below half. The constant-w solution that reaches 73.26 needs Ω_m = 0.266. That is 3.6σ below DESI BAO and 4.6σ below the SN value of Ω_m.
2. **(ii) In its G_eff form, the M_B step carries the whole gap by construction, but it implies an Earth that never existed.** The step has to lie between z = 0.01 and 0.0233, which is a lookback of 144.5–333 Myr [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-11]. Marra & Perivolaropoulos put it at "roughly 100–150 million years ago".
   - The required G ratio is 0.891–0.914, or 1.094–1.122 under the opposite luminosity–G sign. With L_sun ∝ G⁷ and an adiabatic orbit r ∝ 1/G, insolation scales as G⁹ and the no-feedback temperature as G^2.25. The model's own authors use the same exponent.
   - Before the step, insolation would then have been 0.35–0.45 of today's: an absorbed-flux deficit of 132–154 W m⁻², which would need about 36–42 CO₂ doublings to offset. Surface temperatures would have been 219–253 K (−54 to −20 °C).
   - Under the opposite sign, insolation would have been 2.2–2.8 times today's, giving +75 to +128 °C.
   - The reconstructed global mean surface temperature over the last 485 Myr spans only 11 to 36 °C (Judd et al. 2024).
3. **(ii) With a non-gravitational, SN-only step, the leftover form predicts the wrong value for every non-SN class.** If only SN Ia luminosity changes, the true H0 is the early-universe value. Probes that do not use far-field SN luminosity should then read 67.2–68.5. They do not:
   - time delays, masers and sirens combined: 71.27 ± 1.93, 2.07σ above CMB-SPA;
   - adding SBF: 72.26 ± 1.51 (3.27σ);
   - adding SBF and Tully–Fisher: 72.38 ± 1.41 (3.55σ against CMB-SPA, 2.54σ against BAO+BBN).

   These classes put 67–86% of the gap on the local side of the sky, where an SN step cannot reach.
4. **(iii) A Cepheid-only screened fifth force carries 19–21% of the gap on matched hosts.** It needs Cepheids alone to be 0.188 mag too bright. On the same hosts, JAGB minus Cepheid is −0.03 ± 0.054 mag [D-26], so the prediction misses by 2.9σ. The JWST distance-limited sample gives Cepheid 73.4 against TRGB 72.1 and JAGB 72.2 [D-28]: a Cepheid-specific part of 0.036–0.039 mag, which is 19–21% of the gap. Even the CCHP TRGB headline, which uses different hosts and SNe, leaves only 0.094 mag (50%), right at the line and not above it.

## Corrections to details

- The prediction-matrix entry "(ii) low if the indicator is G-dependent, otherwise 0" for non-SN ladders is ambiguous. Under (ii) the true H0 is about 67, so a G-independent SBF or TF ladder should read ≈ 67–68.5. That is a 2.6σ shift for SBF (observed 73.8 ± 2.4), not "0" relative to the observed 73.
- "Time delays/masers/sirens read ≈ 67–68" is the prediction. The observed combination, 71.27 ± 1.93, is 2.07σ above it (1.37σ above BAO+BBN). This is a failure at the 1.4–2.1σ level, not a neutral entry.
- The step's timing: the candidate's 189 Myr window (144.5–333 Myr lookback) is correct. M&P's "100–150 million years ago" covers only the near edge of it.

## What survives and remaining doubt

- **Survives:**
  - A 0.15–0.19 mag SN Ia luminosity step at 41–94 Mpc is still the one post-recombination route that escapes the inverse-ladder no-go [D-70].
  - It survives only if its cause either does not touch the Sun (no change of G in time along our worldline), or is a spatial G_eff boundary around us. A spatial boundary is a non-Copernican coincidence like the void of C5, and I did not compute its likelihood.
  - Even then, non-SN local probes disfavour it at 2.1–3.6σ, depending on whether SBF and TF count as unaffected and which early value is taken as true.
- **Doubts about my own attack:**
  - L_sun ∝ G⁷ is a homology scaling at fixed composition. Detailed solar models give a somewhat smaller exponent, but even G⁵ for luminosity, with the orbital factor, gives an insolation change of order 40–60%.
  - The pre-step Sun has a Kelvin–Helmholtz time of about 30 Myr, much shorter than the more than 145 Myr pre-step epoch, so it would have sat in the low-G (or high-G) equilibrium.
  - Climate feedbacks (ice–albedo) amplify a cooling rather than cancel it. The CO₂-doubling count is illustrative only.
  - The time-delay value rests on a preprint [D-31], and the maser error is statistical only [D-32]. If their systematics are inflated, the SN-only form's tension falls toward 2σ, which is why the verdict is `weakened`, not `refuted`.
  - Variant (iii) escapes only if the fifth force shifts TRGB and JAGB by the same 0.19 mag as Cepheids. That would remove its distinguishing prediction, and it would then need an independent check that I did not run.
