# Verdict on C1 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: A distance-ladder systematic, most likely at the SN Ia calibration step shared by SN-based ladders, biases local H0 high by most of the gap; true global H0 is about 67–68.5 km/s/Mpc.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: A ladder-side systematic may still carry most of the gap, but only if it is not confined to the SN Ia step: it must also bias the SN-Ia-free ladders (SBF, Tully–Fisher, SNe II) high by about 0.17 mag, or those ladders must all be fluctuating high at about 3σ. On current anchor-free data (71.3 ± 1.9 km/s/Mpc), a true H0 of 67–68.5 km/s/Mpc is allowed but not favoured.

Units: astronomical. H0 is in km/s/Mpc, distance-modulus offsets in mag, and σ is a Gaussian-equivalent significance.

## Strongest attack

C1 names its own locus: the SN Ia calibration step that the SN-based ladders share. It says sub-hypothesis (a), calibrator-versus-Hubble-flow standardisation, is "the effect the lenses suspect most". An error at that step moves only the ladders that use SNe Ia. So the decisive evidence check is this: do the H0 determinations that skip the SN Ia step land at 67–68.5? They do not.

1. **Published SN-free ladders do not move.**
   - The Local Distance Network (H0DN) consensus paper states that "Replacing SNe Ia with galaxy-based indicators changes H0 by less than 0.1 km s−1 Mpc−1, while doubling its uncertainty". Its no-SNe variant is 73.434 ± 1.795 [D-23].
   - The SBF ladder is built "independent of Cepheid variables and Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia)". It gives 73.8 ± 0.7 (stat) ± 2.3 (sys).
   - The SNe II ladder replaces SNe Ia altogether and gives 75.4 +3.8/−3.7 (stat), with a 1.5 km/s/Mpc systematic.
   - None of these values contains the SN Ia calibrator-to-Hubble-flow step, so C1's SN-step sub-hypotheses (a), (b), (d) and (g) leave every one of them unchanged.

2. **My combination of probes that skip the SN Ia step excludes 67–68.5 at about 3–4σ** [calc: runs/2026-10-30-hubble-tension-h0-discrepancy/calc/falsifier-C1-1_snfree.py].
   - Set A is SBF, Tully–Fisher, SNe II, TDCOSMO-2026, masers and sirens. With a 1.5% shared anchor term among the ladder-based entries, its generalised-least-squares (GLS) mean is 72.55 ± 1.38 km/s/Mpc.
   - That is 2.94σ above 68.5, 3.48σ above 67.75 and 4.03σ above 67.0.
   - The set is internally consistent: χ² = 1.18 for 5 degrees of freedom, so no single outlier drives the mean.
   - The result is robust to the construction. Using the H0DN no-SNe variant in place of the individual ladders (set B) gives 72.71 ± 1.36, 3.10σ above 68.5. Bracketing the anchor term from 0% to 3% moves set A from 3.18σ to 2.55σ against 68.5, and from 4.32σ to 3.56σ against 67.0.
   - The SN-Ia-free ladders alone (SBF, Tully–Fisher, SNe II) average 74.01 ± 2.02 km/s/Mpc. To bring them to 68.5 they would need a non-SN bias of 0.168 mag (8.1% in distance). That is essentially the whole gap of 0.188 mag, and the SN Ia step cannot supply it.

3. **The lensing team's own SN-free and ladder-free combinations confirm this.** In TDCOSMO 2026 (Birrer et al., arXiv:2610.14881), the "no-SN" late-universe probe "is in 3.7σ tension with Planck". The authors add: "Even after completely excluding the LDL, the tension with Planck persists at the 2.7σ level". That ladder-free combination is TDCOSMO-2026 with megamasers and globular-cluster ages.

4. **The escape routes inside C1 are closed by its own text.** A ladder systematic large enough to also move SBF, Tully–Fisher and SNe II must sit at the anchor or primary-zero-point level.
   - C1's sub-hypothesis (e) needs a coherent 9.0% anchor distance error. C1 itself records NGC 4258 as known to 0.97%, which puts that error at 9.4σ.
   - Sub-hypothesis (f) caps Cepheid-only effects at about 0.8 km/s/Mpc.
   - Sub-hypothesis (c), a Hubble-flow peculiar-velocity error, needs a coherent outflow of 1355–1897 km/s [math: M-MECHANIST-03]. The Laniakea flow model moves SBF the wrong way (+1.4 km/s/Mpc) [D-36].
   - So C1's "most likely" mechanism is disfavoured at about 3σ by the probes that bypass it. The mechanism that would reach those probes is excluded at about 9σ by C1's own numbers.

5. **Why the verdict is weakened, not refuted.**
   - The attack is about 3σ against 68.5 (about 3.5σ against 67.75), not 5σ. If the true H0 is about 68.5, the SN-free ladders must each be fluctuating high by 1.3–2.3σ. Their small χ² suggests their quoted systematics are generous, which helps C1 slightly.
   - The cleanest anchor-free subset (time delays, masers and sirens) gives 71.27 ± 1.89 km/s/Mpc. That is only 1.46σ above 68.5 and 1.86σ above 67.75 [calc: same log, set C]. It disfavours 67–68.5 mildly, it does not exclude it, and it is also compatible with the 70.2 midpoint (0.57σ).
   - So C1 is not dead. Its stated locus, the SN Ia step, and its stated global value both sit against the current SN-free evidence at about the 3σ level.
   - Supporting evidence for C1 remains: CCHP gives 70.39 ± 1.94; Hoyt finds a Pantheon+ shift of +2 km/s/Mpc; and the Dong 2026 flow re-analysis gives −2.9 km/s/Mpc. These support a ladder-side contribution of part of the gap, about 1–3 km/s/Mpc. That is the C6 regime, not "most of the gap" placed at the SN step.

**Assumptions of the attack, checked:**
- The SNe II, SBF and Tully–Fisher ladders still use Cepheid or TRGB zero points tied to the anchors. They therefore test the SN Ia step, not the anchors. That is exactly the separation the attack needs.
- The maser error is statistical only, which overweights masers. Their weight in set A is small (σ = 3.7), and set B, which drops them, gives the same answer.
- Tully–Fisher is calibrated "through the same hosts" [D-30]. That shares the primary rung but not the SN Ia step.
- Huang et al. 2025, which claims to rule out third-rung SN systematics [D-82], is unverifiable in the dossier and is not used here.

## Size against the discrepancy

The reference gap is SH0ES 73.49 ± 0.93 minus CMB-SPA 67.19 ± 0.38, which is +6.30 km/s/Mpc or 0.195 mag. Against H0DN it is 0.188 mag.

| Measurement class | Shift C1 (SN-step version) produces | Observed now | Consistent with C1 at true 67–68.5? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cepheid–SN and TRGB/JAGB–SN ladders | ↓ 0.15–0.19 mag, to 67–68.5 | 73.3–73.5 (SH0ES, H0DN); 70.4 (CCHP) | requires the shift (C1's premise) |
| SN-Ia-free ladders (SBF, Tully–Fisher, SNe II) | 0 (SN step not used) | GLS 74.01 ± 2.02 (1.5% anchor) | no: 2.74σ above 68.5, 3.11σ above 67.75; needs an extra 0.168 mag non-SN bias |
| All SN-Ia-free probes (set A) | 0 | 72.55 ± 1.38 | no: 2.94σ above 68.5, 4.03σ above 67.0 |
| One-step, anchor-free (time delays, masers, sirens) | 0 (expected to "converge" to 67–68.5) | 71.27 ± 1.89 | marginal: 1.46σ above 68.5, 1.86σ above 67.75 |
| TDCOSMO no-SN and no-LDL combinations | 0 | 3.7σ and 2.7σ above Planck (source) | no at about 3σ |
| CMB, BAO+BBN and inverse ladder | 0 | 67.2–68.5 | yes |

**Net:** confined to the SN Ia step, C1's mechanism can remove the gap only from the SN-based ladders. The SN-free data then still sit about 4 km/s/Mpc (0.125 mag) above 68.5 [calc: set A shortfall]. That is about two-thirds of the gap, left for fluctuation or for another cause.

## Corrections to details

- The prediction-matrix cell for non-SN ladders says C1 "must call SBF a 2.7σ fluctuation". That treats one probe at a time. Taken jointly, SBF, Tully–Fisher and SNe II (74.01 ± 2.02) must be a 2.7–3.1σ joint upward fluctuation for a true H0 of 67.75–68.5 at a 1.5% anchor. Adding the one-step probes makes it 2.9–3.5σ.
- The candidate's evidence-against list does not cite the SNe II ladder (de Jaeger 2022: 75.4 +3.8/−3.7 stat ± 1.5 sys). The prediction matrix notes that "Type II SNe … not covered by any facet". This is the most direct SN-Ia-free replication of the ladder, and it points the wrong way for C1.
- The candidate cites globular-cluster (GC) ages as evidence for a low global H0. TDCOSMO's ladder-free combination, which includes those GC ages together with time delays and masers, still sits 2.7σ above Planck. GC ages therefore do not, by themselves, carry the late-universe data down to 67–68.5.
- "One-step values have drifted down" is accurate (time delays 74.5 → 71.1), but they now sit at about 71. That is closer to the gap midpoint of 70.2 (0.57σ) than to C1's 67–68.5 (1.5–1.9σ).
