# Verdict on C7 (refuter 0, angle: magnitude)
claim: The 'early versus late H0' premise is mis-posed: the conflict is between two absolute calibrations, the geometric-anchor distance scale (M_B) and the sound-horizon ruler r_d, not between expansion rates.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: cited-evidence
narrowed: On the published SN Ia compilations, which put the legacy low-z SNe on the same scale as the high-z samples, BAO plus SN shape pin H(z) and at least ≈85% of the local–early gap maps onto the anchor-to-SN calibration (M_B, located in the transfer rungs rather than the geometric anchors) versus r_d; that pin at z ≲ 0.1 rests on the legacy low-z SN cross-calibration, and without it a z ≲ 0.09 expansion-rate change can carry about half the gap.

## Strongest attack
C7's load-bearing step is "BAO + SN shape pin H(z) between z ≈ 0.02 and 2.3, so only the two absolute calibrations can differ". Below z ≈ 0.1 that pin is held only by the heterogeneous legacy low-z SN Ia samples. The newest model-independent inverse-ladder test (Nájera, Banik & Desmond 2026, arXiv:2610.23657, preprint, 16 Oct 2026) uses the standard r_d = 147.05 Mpc and puts numbers on this:
- With only the homogeneous DES-Dovekie SNe, a fourth-order cosmographic H(z) gives H0 = 76.25 ± 2.87 km/s/Mpc. That is 1.0σ from H0DN and 3.1σ above CMB-SPA. With a standard r_d the shape freedom absorbs the whole gap, and neither calibration needs to move.
- Adding the legacy SNe as published gives 67.98 ± 0.69. That drop of 8.27 km/s/Mpc has a 1.24% chance under ΛCDM mocks (about 2.5σ two-sided), and the authors read it as "a systematic tension between DES and legacy SNIa datasets".
- If the legacy sample is given a free offset, the fit prefers ΔM_legacy = 0.0646 ± 0.0391 mag (1.65σ) and H0 = 71.03 ± 1.99. That is +3.05 km/s/Mpc, or 58% of the (73.264 − 67.98) gap. It is carried by an enhanced H(z) at z ≲ 0.09, an expansion-rate change, while r_d stays standard and M_B stays at the ladder value [calc: calc/falsifier-C7-0_magnitude.py, section 7].

C7 sets its own threshold: "no modification of H(z) at 0 < z < 2 changes the gap by more than ≈ 0.5σ", which is 0.53 km/s/Mpc. The free-offset case moves the gap by 3.05 km/s/Mpc, or 2.87σ of the gap's error [calc: same, section 7]. So the claim "not between expansion rates" holds only on a stated assumption: that the legacy low-z SNe sit on the high-z photometric scale to better than about 0.04 mag. C7 does not state this assumption. It also "removes local structure by construction" using the same legacy SNe.

The attack does not refute C7. With the legacy SNe as published, the same paper finds the pin holds: 67.66 ± 1.01 with z1 unconstrained under an H(z) enhancement fitted alongside the local anchors. The offset is preferred at only 1.65σ, and the result is a two-week-old preprint by authors who favour a local-void reading. The core reframe survives on current data, but only conditionally, so the verdict is weakened.

## Corrections to details
- "Geometric-anchor distance scale" misplaces the calibration. The three geometric anchors agree: dropping any one moves H0DN by ≤ 0.53 km/s/Mpc. A 0.17–0.19 mag anchor error would be 4.8–6.5σ for NGC 4258 alone. The calibration in conflict is the transfer from anchors to SN M_B (primary-indicator zero points and the calibrator SN sample), not the anchor geometry [calc, section 4].
- The 0.146–0.188 mag "offsets" are H0 ratios restated in magnitudes, not measured M_B offsets. The only measured M_B pair (−19.253 against −19.375/−19.394 mag) reduces to the H0 gap once the cross-system intercept is restored: 5Δa_B = 0.059 mag (cosmography) or 0.025 mag (ΛCDM), which is 15–33% of the gap [calc, section 2]. C7 is right that the pair is invalid. But this also shows that M_B by itself is not invariant across SN systems; only M_B + 5a_B (equivalently H0) is. The M_B statement is an equivalent restatement, not a more basic one.
- "Every anchor-calibrated ladder stays mutually consistent (73.02 ± 0.78)": analyses tied to the same NGC 4258 anchor differ by 0.094 mag (SH0ES vs CCHP TRGB), 0.143 mag (vs CCHP JWST-only) and 0.169 mag (SH0ES-side vs CCHP JAGB, overlapping data). That is 50–90% of the gap measured in magnitudes, though only 1.4–2.3σ naively. The "one scale" is a group mean whose internal spread is comparable to the gap, and the exhaustive partition puts CCHP with the early group (M-DECOMPOSER-10).
- The prediction that the arbiter "lands decisively near one calibrator" is untested. The current anchor-free, r_d-free combination lies 0.24–0.66 of the way between the two calibrations [calc, section 6]. A midpoint outcome would turn C7 into C6.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives:** the reframe as an accounting identity. On the published SN compilations, the gap between ladder and inverse ladder or CMB is a disagreement between the anchor-to-SN calibration and r_d. H(z) model freedom with legacy SNe included moves it by ≤ 0.5 km/s/Mpc.
- **What is lost:** the unconditional "not between expansion rates". Below z ≈ 0.1 the pin is only as good as the legacy low-z SN cross-calibration. A homogeneous-sample analysis (preprint) allows a z ≲ 0.09 expansion-rate enhancement that carries 58% of the gap at a 1.65σ-preferred legacy offset, or all of it with DES-only SNe at large error.
- **Doubt about my own attack:**
  - Nájera et al. is new, not refereed, and comes from authors with a prior preference for a local void.
  - Any such low-z H(z) excess must still face the C5 bound: δ ≈ −0.5 over ≳ 430 h⁻¹ Mpc, 84σ in ΛCDM (M-CONSTRAINTS-12). This limits how plausible the third locus is physically, but not whether the data pin it.
  - The legacy offset may itself be read as a third calibration. On that reading, C7's "two calibrations" becomes "calibrations", and the core survives with a wider list.
- **What would settle it:** DEBASS, or another homogeneous low-z SN sample tied to DES-Dovekie, and a same-system ladder vs inverse-ladder M_B comparison.
