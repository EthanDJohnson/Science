# Verdict on C4 (refuter 2, angle: bounds)
claim: Post-recombination new physics, either a modified expansion history H(z) or a low-redshift change in G_eff or standard-candle luminosity, is the dominant cause of the gap.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: cited-evidence
narrowed: Only an ad hoc, non-gravitational (or Cepheid+SN-specific) step in calibrated stellar-candle luminosity of about 0.15–0.19 mag, switching on between lookback times of about 73 and 333 Myr, survives the independent bounds; it is disfavoured at about 1.4–3.3σ by the non-SN H0 probes and has no demonstrated mechanism. Smooth H(z) changes and the Cepheid-only fifth force fail, and so does a universal G step of the size C4 quotes (9–12%).

Units: H0 in km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ (written km/s/Mpc); pressures and bulk moduli in GPa; fluxes in W/m²; temperatures in K; lookback times in Myr; G ratios, exponents and γ are dimensionless; σ is Gaussian-equivalent.

## Strongest attack

C4 bundles three mechanisms, and each one runs into a different independent bound. The dominant-cause claim survives only through the least physical of them.

1. **(i) Smooth H(z): the inverse distance ladder rules it out.** BAO calibrated by the standard r_d, plus uncalibrated SNe Ia anchored at z → 0, give H0 = 67.19 +0.66/−0.64 (4th-order cosmography, no ΛCDM assumed at late times) and 67.67 (flat ΛCDM) [D-48]. That is 5.9σ and 5.3σ below H0DN 73.264 ± 0.806 [calc: calc/falsifier-C4-2_bounds.py, §A]. Any H(z) deformation leaves H0·r_d fixed, so with the standard r_d it cannot move the global H0 to the ladder value [D-70]. Three further points agree:
   - the DESI w0wa fits move H0 *down*, to 63.6–67.5 [D-51];
   - a constant w needs w = −1.194, which violates the null energy condition, with Ω_m 3.6σ below DESI [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-10, verified];
   - the World Cup II authors write that "none of the models benefit from the inclusion of an evolving dark energy equation of state in their ability to ease the Hubble tension" (as quoted in candidates.md).

   Variant (i) cannot be the dominant cause.

2. **(iii) Cepheid-only screened fifth force: the other stellar candles rule it out.** It needs a 0.15–0.19 mag brightening that only Cepheids feel. In the SH0ES JWST hosts, TRGB and JAGB agree with Cepheids to ≤ 0.05 mag [D-26, D-28, as cited in C4's own "weakest link"]. A Cepheid-specific offset of ≤ 0.05 mag is at most about 27% of the 0.188 mag gap, so (iii) cannot carry more than half. This is arithmetic on the dossier's bound.

3. **(ii) Universal G_eff step: stellar-memory and geophysical bounds.** This is the variant C4 leans on. Because a step in G is a change in cosmic time, the Sun, the Earth and every nearby star also lived through it, 73–333 Myr ago.
   - **Red dwarfs (2026 preprint).** Sakstein et al. (arXiv:2610.04048) find that the proposed G-step models are "excluded at significances of 3.1σ and 4.0σ". Those models are "a 4% change in G at a look-back time of 73 Myr and an 11% change at 130 Myr". C4's own figure is G_far/G_near = 0.89–0.91 or 1.09–1.12 at 144–333 Myr [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-11]. That is the 11% class of model, placed just beyond the tested 130 Myr. The memory weakens with lookback time, so the 4.0σ figure is a close analogue for C4's step, not an exact match.
   - **Days per year.** Orbital angular momentum is conserved across the step, so T_year ∝ G⁻². Banik, Desmond & Samaras 2025 (MNRAS 539, 1553) note that the length of a year "would have abruptly increased by about 10%, but the number of days per year has evolved broadly continuously according to geochronometry and cyclostratigraphy".
     - The model's authors reply (Perivolaropoulos & Ruchika 2025, arXiv:2508.04395, eq. 12) that a "fluid-like" Earth has R ∝ G⁻¹. That makes T_day ∝ G⁻² as well, so the day/year ratio stays fixed.
     - **This reply is internally inconsistent.** For a self-gravitating body, R ∝ G⁻¹ holds only for an effective adiabatic index γ = d ln P / d ln ρ = 5/3. "Fluid-like" in the reply means low viscosity, which is a shear property. The radius response is set by the bulk modulus.
     - Earth's deep interior has γ = K/P ≈ 3.9–5.8, which gives d ln R / d ln G ≈ −0.07 to −0.13. The days-per-year ratio therefore jumps by −6.6% for the 4% step and by +14% to +23% (or −14% to −19%) for C4's 9–12% steps. Even an unrealistically soft γ = 2.5 still gives −5.4% for 4% [calc: calc/falsifier-C4-2_bounds.py, §B].
     - So the geochronometric continuity bound stands.
   - **Insolation.** L ∝ G^a with r ∝ 1/G, so the flux at Earth scales as G^(a+2).
     - C4's 9% downward step (the Chandrasekhar sign) cuts the absorbed flux by 104–137 W/m² for a = 4–7. Without feedbacks that is −38 to −55 K at the surface.
     - The opposite-sign 11% step raises it by 209–374 W/m².
     - Banik et al. read this as "likely pushing Earth into a planetary glaciation. However, there was no Snowball Earth episode in the last 500 Myr."
     - Only the 4% model, with the authors' favoured a = 4, gives a no-feedback +17.5 K (an absorbed-flux change of 64 W/m²), which they argue matches the observed "∼20°C cooling" [calc: §C]. So paleoclimate on its own does not decide the 4% case.
   - **Helioseismology.** Banik et al. state that the step would make "the Sun's helioseismic age exceed that of the oldest meteorite samples, but these agree excellently in practice".

   C4's G variant at the amplitude C4 itself requires is therefore excluded or strongly disfavoured on three separate lines: red dwarfs, the days-per-year record and insolation. The 4% Cepheid-inclusive version (Ruchika et al. 2024) remains disfavoured at 3.1σ by a single preprint and by a ~7% jump in days per year.

4. **(ii) as a non-gravitational SN luminosity step: weakest bound, but still in tension.** If SNe Ia alone change, every non-SN H0 should read the "true" early value. Time delays, masers and sirens combine to 71.27 ± 1.93, which is 2.07σ above 67.19 and 1.37σ above 68.50. Adding SBF (which needs no SNe) gives 72.26 ± 1.51: 3.27σ and 2.33σ [calc: §D; matches math: M-CONSTRAINTS-14]. This is tension, not exclusion. A step that hits Cepheids and SNe but spares SBF and TRGB also needs a mechanism that nobody has offered.

## Corrections to details

- C4 sizes the G change at 9–12% at 144–333 Myr lookback (z ≈ 0.01–0.023). The literature's live G-step model (GSM) is a ~4% step at about 73 Myr, with Cepheids also affected (Ruchika et al. 2024, JCAP 06 056; Perivolaropoulos & Ruchika 2025). C4's "Weakest link" and "predictions" should name this version, since it is the one its proponents now defend.
- C4 lists "growth/S8 change" as a prediction of (ii). C4 does not note that the same universal step also predicts a jump in Earth's orbital period (T_year ∝ G⁻²), a solar insolation step and red-dwarf radius anomalies. These are the strongest existing bounds and belong in "evidence against".
- The candidate gives the Wright & Li exponent as a lens figure reading; the math check left it unchecked [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-11]. The sign ambiguity does not rescue (ii): both signs are bounded, cold-side and hot-side respectively [calc: §C].

## What survives and remaining doubt

- **What survives.** Only an SN (or Cepheid+SN) luminosity step that does not come from a universal change in G. It has no known mechanism, and the non-SN probes disfavour it at 1.4–3.3σ, depending on whether SBF is included and whether the true H0 is taken as 67.19 or 68.50.
- **Why weakened rather than refuted.**
  - The decisive G-step bound (red dwarfs) is a single 2026 preprint at 3.1–4.0σ, and it was not tested at C4's exact 144–333 Myr window.
  - The days-per-year constraint rests on Banik et al.'s qualitative "broadly continuously". I did not quantify the fossil record's precision at 73–333 Myr.
  - The non-SN tension stays below 3.3σ, and masers are quoted with statistical errors only.
- **What would settle it.** C4's own decisive test: a calibrator vs Hubble-flow M_B comparison in hosts at 45–100 Mpc with TRGB or geometric distances. Red-dwarf radius tests at longer lookback would also help.
