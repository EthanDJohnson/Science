# Verdict on C1 (refuter 2, angle: bounds)
claim: A distance-ladder systematic, most likely at the SN Ia calibration step shared by SN-based ladders, biases local H0 high by most of the gap; true global H0 is about 67–68.5 km/s/Mpc.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: A ladder-side systematic can still carry most of the gap (true global H0 below the ≈70.2 km/s/Mpc midpoint, with the anchor-free and r_d-free arbiters favouring ≈68.5–70 over 67), but only if it acts at a step that SN-free tracers also share (a common anchor/primary zero point or the Hubble-flow velocity treatment) or if the SN-free ladders all fluctuated high by about 3σ; the "SN Ia calibration step" form named as most likely is disfavoured at about 3–4σ by the H0DN with- and without-SNe comparison and the SN-free probe set.

Units: H0 in km/s/Mpc (astronomical units, not SI); magnitude offsets in mag on the 5 log10 H0 scale; σ is a Gaussian-equivalent significance.

## Strongest attack

C1 names a specific locus, the SN Ia calibration step (calibrator-vs-Hubble-flow standardisation, sub-hypotheses (a), (b), (d)), and a specific truth, global H0 ≈ 67–68.5. An SN-step error only touches routes that pass through SNe Ia. So it has an independent bound: every ladder that uses the same anchors and the same Cepheid/TRGB primaries but replaces the SNe with another Hubble-flow tracer must read the *true* H0. The Local Distance Network (H0DN) ran exactly this test inside one pipeline. Its own abstract says:

> "5) Replacing SNe Ia with galaxy-based indicators changes H0 by less than 0.1 km/s/Mpc, while doubling its uncertainty" [Casertano et al. 2026, A&A 708 A166, arXiv:2510.23823, ACCESS abstract, fetched with fetch_text.py]

Its tabulated variant is V13 "Baseline without SNe Ia", 73.434 ± 1.795 [D-23; RE-03 quote]. This measures the SN-step contribution to the ladder offset directly.

1. **Required size.** C1's stated truth needs a ladder bias of 0.146 mag (truth 68.5) to 0.194 mag (truth 67.0) against H0DN 73.264. Even the boundary-rule minimum (truth 70.2) needs 0.093 mag [calc: runs/2026-10-30-hubble-tension-h0-discrepancy/calc/falsifier-C1-2_bounds.py, §1].
2. **Measured SN-step contribution.** I compared H0DN with and without SNe in the same Pantheon+ version (73.50 vs 73.434). The SN step contributes +0.002 ± 0.047 mag if the no-SN set is treated as a nested subset, or ± 0.058 mag if it is treated as independent. Against the corrected 73.264 the figure is −0.005 mag. The SN step therefore falls short of C1's requirement by:
   - 3.0–4.2σ in the nested treatment (2.5–3.4σ independent) over 0.146–0.194 mag;
   - 3.9–4.1σ (nested) at the 0.188 mag full gap [calc: same file, §2].
3. **SN-free probes must read the truth.** Under an SN-step-only bias, the probes that bypass SNe Ia must sit at 67–68.5:
   - SBF, TF, time delays, masers, sirens: mean 72.39 ± 1.41, which is 2.75σ (truth 68.5) to 3.81σ (truth 67.0) above C1's range;
   - with H0DN-no-SNe in place of SBF and masers, which it contains: 72.63 ± 1.33, 3.11–4.23σ above;
   - with TF dropped because it uses Cepheid zero points: 72.26 ± 1.51, 2.50–3.49σ above.
   
   The χ² goodness-of-fit at the truth is weaker (p = 0.008–0.13 for set B; 8.6×10⁻⁴–0.034 for set A) [calc: same file, §3, §3b].
4. **The escape routes are bounded elsewhere.** To avoid point 2, the bias must sit upstream of the SNe, where it also reaches SBF, TF and masers. The upstream options are:
   - a common anchor scale: a 9.0% coherent error is needed, but NGC 4258 is known to 0.97% (9.4σ), and leave-one-anchor-out gives 73.08–73.61 [D-23, D-32; math: M-MECHANIST-02];
   - Cepheid photometry: crowding is excluded at 5.9σ [D-78; math: M-MECHANIST-02];
   - a primary-indicator-specific error: ruled out because TRGB, JAGB and Cepheid agree on matched hosts and dropping Cepheids or TRGB has "minimal effect" [D-23, D-26];
   - a Hubble-flow velocity treatment common to all tracers (C1(c)): taken as a physical monopole outflow, this is the C5 void, needing δ ≈ −0.46 to −0.51 over R ≳ 430 h⁻¹ Mpc, an 84σ fluctuation [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-12, M-MECHANIST-03]. It survives only as a methodological peculiar-velocity modelling effect (Dong 2026, preprint, D-82: forward 74.30 → 71.37). In that paper the backward model gives 60.83 → 63.25, so the size of the lever depends on the modelling choice.
   
   So the general "ladder systematic" survives only as a combination of contested levers. The locus the heading calls "most likely" is the one this bound hits hardest.

Assumptions I checked:
- The nested-subset formula var(Δ) = var(sub) − var(full) assumes optimal (covariance-weighted) combination, which H0DN uses. The independent-errors version is the conservative bracket, and I report both.
- SBF and masers share NGC 4258 with the ladder. That is upstream of the SNe, so under an SN-step-only hypothesis it does not bias them.
- TF uses Cepheid/TRGB zero points, again upstream. Dropping it (§3b) changes little.
- Asymmetric errors use the side facing the tested value.
- Masers carry statistical errors only (D-32), so including them makes the bound slightly too strong. Dropping them barely moves the set means, because their weight is small.

## Size against the discrepancy

The gap is 0.188 mag (H0DN 73.264 vs CMB-SPA 67.19) and 0.195 mag (SH0ES 73.49). Shift per class that C1's SN-step version produces, with sign:
- **SN-based ladders** (Cepheid-SN, TRGB-SN, JAGB-SN): must fall by −0.146 to −0.194 mag. The measured SN-step contribution, from H0DN with and without SNe, is +0.002 ± 0.047 mag, i.e. about 0% of the gap (95% upper ≈ 0.10 mag, ≈ 50%).
- **SN-free ladders** (SBF, TF, H0DN-no-SNe): shift 0. They must therefore be fluctuations 2.5–4.2σ high, since they read 72.3–73.4.
- **One-step anchor-free** (time delays, masers, sirens): shift 0; they read 71.27 ± 1.93, 1.4–2.2σ above C1's truth. Compatible.
- **CMB, BAO+BBN, inverse ladder**: shift 0, consistent with 67.19–68.5 by construction.
- **Anchor-free and r_d-free arbiters**: 68.5–70.3 ± 1.0–1.3. These favour the top of C1's range or slightly above it, and exclude 73.26 at 2.9–3.8σ.

So an SN-step effect carries at most about half the gap at 2σ, with a central value near zero. That falls short of C1's "most of the gap" (> 50%; 75–100% for the stated 67–68.5).

## Corrections to details

- The prediction-matrix entry "must call SBF a 2.7σ fluctuation" understates the cost. The SN-free set as a whole (SBF, TF, H0DN-no-SNe, time delays, sirens) has to sit 2.5–4.2σ high together, not SBF alone [calc §3].
- "True global H0 ≈ 67–68.5": the independent arbiters (anchor-free and r_d-free) put the global value at 68.5–70.3 ± ≈1.1. The lower end, 67.0, is disfavoured at up to 3.1σ for the Zaborowski variants. A defensible range for C1's truth is ≈ 68–70 rather than 67–68.5 [calc §5]. C1 still holds under the boundary rule (< 70.2), but only marginally for the higher r_d-free variant (70.26 ± 1.04 sits at the midpoint).
- The evidence-for clause compares the one-step mean (1.88σ) with the early mean. Against C1's own lower truth of 67.0 it is 2.21σ [calc §4]. This is minor.

## What survives and remaining doubt

What survives: global H0 sits below the midpoint, and some ladder-side effect carries most of the gap. Several independent bounds support this:
- the CMB, BAO+BBN and inverse-ladder values;
- the GC age (t0 = 13.80 Gyr at 67.19 vs 13.81 ± 0.34 Gyr, while 73.26 is 2.7–3.4σ young) [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-06];
- the anchor-free one-step mean, only 1.4–2.2σ above C1's range;
- the r_d-free arbiters, which exclude 73.26 at about 3–4σ.

What does not survive in its stated form: the locus. The bound singles out the SN calibration step, which C1 calls the most likely site, as the most strongly bounded: H0DN's own SN-free route reproduces 73.4, so an SN-only error is disfavoured at about 3–4σ (2.5σ at the most conservative pairing). A C1-type explanation therefore needs one of three things:
- an upstream common error, already bounded at 6–9σ per lever;
- a methodological Hubble-flow velocity effect acting on every tracer, which is contested and not adopted by H0DN;
- a ≈ 3σ coherent upward fluctuation of the SN-free ladders.

Remaining doubt:
- None of these bounds reaches 5σ, so a refutation is not justified.
- The H0DN no-SN variant comes from the same team and pipeline, uses the pre-correction Pantheon+ version, and carries a χ²/dof of 11.16/23. Its error may be conservative, which would make the bound stronger, or its SBF zero points may share a TRGB problem at NGC 4258 with the SN calibrators (sub-hypothesis (g)), which would weaken it.
- A third-team SN-free ladder at ≤ 1.5 km/s/Mpc would sharpen this bound decisively.
