# Verdict on C1 (refuter 0, angle: magnitude)
claim: A distance-ladder systematic, most likely at the SN Ia calibration step shared by SN-based ladders, biases local H0 high by most of the gap; true global H0 is about 67–68.5 km/s/Mpc.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: A ladder systematic at the SN Ia step can carry only modestly more than half the gap, leaving a true global H0 of about 69.5–70 km/s/Mpc (53–61% of the gap) at a cost of about 1.7–2.2σ in the SN-free channels; the stated 67–68.5 endpoint needs more SN-step bias than the named levers supply even added coherently, and a 2.5–3.2σ upward fluctuation in the same network's SN-free channel.

Units: H0 in km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ (written km/s/Mpc); magnitudes in mag, with Δm = 5 log10(H_a/H_b). Uncertainties are 68% Gaussian. All calculations are in [calc: runs/2026-10-30-hubble-tension-h0-discrepancy/calc/falsifier-C1-0_magnitude.py] and its `.log`.

## Strongest attack

The H0DN network measures the SN Ia rung's contribution internally, and finds it to be zero. Its baseline (73.50 ± 0.81, original Pantheon+ [D-22]) and its no-SNe variant (73.434 ± 1.795 [D-23]) share the anchors and the primary indicators (Cepheid, TRGB). Their difference therefore isolates whatever the SN rung adds. Removing the SN-free information from the baseline gives an SN-channel-only H0 of 73.52 ± 0.91. Treating the two channels as independent, the SN-minus-no-SN offset is **+0.0024 ± 0.0595 mag**. This is conservative: covariance through the shared anchors would only shrink the error. C1's preferred location, the SN step, then needs the following:

| Target | SN-step bias needed | Tension with the measured SN-minus-no-SN offset |
|---|---|---|
| "most of the gap" (> half, 0.094 mag) | 0.094 mag | 1.54σ (one-sided p = 0.062) |
| true H0 = 68.5 | 0.153 mag | 2.53σ (p = 5.7 × 10⁻³) |
| true H0 = 67.19 | 0.195 mag | 3.24σ (p = 6.1 × 10⁻⁴) |

So the claim that the SN rung carries "most of the gap" passes, only just. The stated 67–68.5 endpoint does not: it needs the SN-free half of the same network (SBF plus Hubble-flow masers) to sit 2.5–3.2σ high by chance.

A second, independent magnitude shortfall comes from the named SN-step levers themselves. Taking each at its largest published size and adding them coherently gives two-population/dust standardisation (a) 0.087 mag (Wojtak & Hjorth 70.59 [D-35]) plus the compilation offset (d) 0.038 mag, a total of **0.125 mag**. That moves H0DN 73.264 → **69.15**, short of the 0.146–0.194 mag that 67–68.5 needs. The band is reached only if:
- the environment step (a′, 0.085 mag at an ASSUMED young-fraction difference Δf = 0.5) is also added on top of the two-population model, although both are calibrator-versus-Hubble-flow host-population effects and very likely overlap (0.210 mag → 66.5); or
- the peculiar-velocity lever (c) is added. That lever is not an SN-calibration effect.

Assumptions checked:
- The SN-versus-no-SN test assumes that an SN-rung bias moves the SN channel and leaves the SBF and maser channels unchanged. This is exactly C1's mechanism for sub-hypotheses (a), (b) and (d).
- It does not test (c), which moves every Hubble-flow tracer, or (e), which moves every anchored rung. Both are sized separately above and fail on other grounds (8.7× cosmic variance, and 9.4σ).
- The D-23 variants use the original Pantheon+, so they are compared with the matching baseline 73.50, not with the corrected 73.264.

Overall: C1 can carry "most" of the gap, more than 50% (true H0 below about 70.2), at a cost of about 1.5–1.8σ in each SN-free test (log sections 3 and 7). It cannot carry the 78–100% needed for 67–68.5 without a 2.5–3.2σ fluctuation in the SN-free channel and lever sizes beyond the coherent sum of the named SN-step effects.

## Corrections to details

- **SN-step reach.** C1 (a)+(d) is the only purely SN-calibration pair, and at most it reaches 69.15, not 67–68.5. The "naive sum 0.177 mag, 94%" mixes in (c), which is a peculiar-velocity treatment, not an SN-calibration step.
- **The SBF fluctuation is larger than stated.** The prediction matrix's phrase "must call SBF a 2.7σ fluctuation" is right only at 67.19 (2.75σ); at 68.5 it is 2.2σ. The stronger, network-internal form is the 2.5–3.2σ SN-versus-no-SN offset given above. The combined SN-free set (H0DN no-SN + time delays + sirens) needs p = 0.030 at 68.5 and 1.7 × 10⁻³ at 67.19.
- **Same-amount prediction for TRGB/JAGB.** The row "TRGB/JAGB-SN ladders ↓ same SN-step amount to 67–68.5" is internally inconsistent for CCHP's calibrations, which would land at 62–66.

## What survives and remaining doubt

Three parts of C1 survive:
- a ladder-side systematic carrying more than half the gap;
- a corresponding true H0 of about 69.5–70 (53–61% of the gap);
- the SN step as its most likely location.

On this magnitude test that version costs 1.7–2.2σ in the SN-free tests: at 70.0 the SN-versus-no-SN offset is 1.74σ and the SN-free means are 1.70–1.81σ; at 69.5 these are 2.00σ and 2.05–2.16σ (log section 7). It still counts as C1 under the run's boundary rule (true H0 below 70.2). The specific 67–68.5 endpoint is the casualty.

Remaining doubts:
- **H0DN errors.** The SN-free channel's error (1.8 km/s/Mpc) is dominated by the few SBF calibrators. If the H0DN systematic errors are underestimated, the 2.5–3.2σ figures fall.
- **Peculiar-velocity lever.** If Dong's forward-modelling correction (c) proves right as a methodological effect, it would lower SBF and TF too, and could rescue the 67–68.5 endpoint, but only as a peculiar-velocity systematic rather than an SN-calibration one.
- **Decisive test.** C1's own decisive test, a calibrator-free H0 at σ ≤ 1.2 km/s/Mpc, is unaffected by this attack. It would settle 67–68.5 against 69.5–70 only at σ of about 0.5–0.7 km/s/Mpc.
