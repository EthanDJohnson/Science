# Verdict on C5 (refuter 0, angle: magnitude)
claim: Local structure, a large underdensity or coherent bulk flow around us, makes the locally measured expansion rate exceed the global H0 by most of the gap.
status: final
verdict: refuted
basis: calculation
narrowed: none

## Strongest attack
"Most of the gap" means a local excess of at least half the gap. Against CMB-SPA (67.19) that is ΔH ≥ 3.04 km/s/Mpc (ΔH/H ≥ 4.52%). Against BAO+BBN (68.50) it is ΔH ≥ 2.38 km/s/Mpc (ΔH/H ≥ 3.48%). Each way local structure could supply this fails on size, by its own route:

1. **A ΛCDM void or flow** supplies at most about 15–20% of the gap at 3σ. Half the gap is 7.7σ (BAO+BBN) to 9.8σ (CMB-SPA) of the N-body cosmic variance σ(H0^loc) = 0.31 km/s/Mpc [D-10]. The full gap needs δ = −0.51 (linear) or −0.46 (Bernardeau) over R ≳ 430 h⁻¹ Mpc, which is 84σ of σ_δ = 0.0061 [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-12, verified 16/16].
2. **A void of any dynamics, including enhanced-growth KBC models, is capped by the SN Hubble diagram at well under half the CMB gap.** Converted to a magnitude step, Kenworthy et al.'s 4–5σ exclusion of |δ| > 20% is a step of about 0.076–0.078 mag. That is ΔH/H ≤ 3.6–3.7%, or ΔH ≤ 2.4–2.5 km/s/Mpc: 39–41% of the CMB-SPA gap and 51–53% of the BAO+BBN gap, and this only at a contrast already excluded at 4–5σ. The full gap needs a step of 0.188 mag, 2.5 times what is excluded. The step is kinematic, set by H_loc/H_glob alone. So the cap holds even for models such as Banik et al. 2026, where non-standard gravity makes a given δ produce larger outflows. They still have to produce the 0.188 mag step.
3. **A smooth profile that converges by z ≈ 0.2 (Banik's version) cannot hide that step.** On a mock SN Hubble diagram (1000 SNe, 0.15 mag each), a flat-ΛCDM fit with free Ω_m and a free offset leaves Δχ² = 59–89, about 8–9.5σ, for a void edge at z = 0.15–0.20. The fit also drags the SN-only Ω_m to about 0.15–0.16. The measured SN-only value is Ω_m = 0.322 ± 0.012, which lies on the other side of the CMB value.
4. **A smaller void, with its edge inside the Hubble-flow sample, would show a z_min trend of the wrong sign, and its required contrast is unphysical.** An edge at z = 0.05–0.07 needs an inner δ_lin of −0.93 to −1.61 (δ ≥ −1 is the physical limit). It predicts that the 0.03 < z < 0.10 subsample reads +2.7 to +3.1 km/s/Mpc above the full sample. H0DN observes −0.83 (crude nested σ ≈ 0.29). Only an edge at z ≥ 0.15 produces no trend, and that brings back point 1 or point 2.
5. **A coherent bulk flow, being a dipole, barely moves the monopole H0.** With isotropic sky coverage, a 400 km/s flow biases H0 by −0.009% and a 1000 km/s flow by −0.04%. Even the worst case, with every SN in the flow's hemisphere, needs V ≈ 4100 km/s for the full gap and ≈ 2060 km/s for half. The ΛCDM 3D rms bulk flow is 166 km/s at R = 150 h⁻¹ Mpc.

So no local-structure configuration consistent with the SN Hubble diagram, with ΛCDM statistics, or with the absence of a z_min trend supplies ≥ 50% of the gap. The core claim, "by most of the gap", fails.

## Corrections to details
- In the candidate's evidence-for line, the empirical 0.60% local scatter is from Kenworthy 2019. In the same abstract Kenworthy uses that figure to conclude the opposite of C5: "the distance ladder measurement is not affected by local density contrasts". It is an upper bound, not support.
- Neither the prediction-matrix entry nor the argument considers how a bulk flow acts. A dipole flow shifts a sky-averaged H0 only through anisotropic sky coverage. For "bulk flow" to carry any of the gap, it would need roughly 2000–4000 km/s of coherent motion. The "bulk flow" half of the heading is therefore excluded on size by itself.
- The prediction "time delays at z ≈ 0.5 read ≈ 67" is right in direction, but the observed 71.1 is only 1.1–1.7σ from that. The "weakest link" the candidate names is therefore weaker than the SN-diagram and cosmic-variance arguments above.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- What survives is a subdominant local-structure term: ≈ 0.3–0.9 km/s/Mpc in ΛCDM (1–3σ), and at most ≈ 2.4 km/s/Mpc (≈ 40% of the CMB gap) for a non-standard void at the 4–5σ SN exclusion boundary. That is a contribution under C6 (no single dominant cause), not a dominant cause.
- Caveats:
  - Calc D and calc E use a synthetic SN redshift distribution and fixed per-SN errors (stated assumptions), so their σ-levels are indicative. The decisive empirical constraint is the published Kenworthy SN-diagram bound, which used real data.
  - The Kenworthy bound used the 2019 sample and an LTB model. My conversion from δ to step uses linear and Bernardeau growth, accurate to about 5–10%.
  - The H0DN variant differences use a crude nested-subsample σ, and the V20 "z > 0.06" label is internally inconsistent [D-23].
- Not attacked here: backreaction/timescape (sub-hypothesis d), which redefines "global H0" rather than adding a local outflow. The candidate itself notes that Seifert et al. 2025 make no H0-gap claim. Nor did I attack a hypothetical void reaching beyond z ≈ 0.5. Such a void would conflict with DESI BAO at z = 0.3–0.5 and with the Copernican bound (P < 10⁻³) [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-12].
