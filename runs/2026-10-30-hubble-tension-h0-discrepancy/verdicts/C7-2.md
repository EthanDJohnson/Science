# Verdict on C7 (refuter 2, angle: bounds)
claim: The 'early versus late H0' premise is mis-posed: the conflict is between two absolute calibrations, the geometric-anchor distance scale (M_B) and the sound-horizon ruler r_d, not between expansion rates.
status: final

Units: H0 in km/s/Mpc, r_d in Mpc, magnitudes in mag, ages in Gyr, N_eff and Ω_k dimensionless (astronomical units, not SI). All numbers cited below are printed in [calc: runs/2026-10-30-hubble-tension-h0-discrepancy/calc/falsifier-C7-2_bounds.py] (log beside it) unless another source is named.

## Strongest attack

C7's core rests on one load-bearing step: "BAO + SN shape pin H(z) between z ≈ 0.02 and 2.3, so only the two absolute calibrations can differ." I used independent constraints to attack that step from four sides.

1. **Is H(z) really pinned? Late-time freedom fitted to BAO+SN.** C7 predicts that "no modification of H(z) at 0 < z < 2 changes the gap by more than ≈ 0.5σ". DESI DR2's own extended fits [D-51] contradict that sentence as written. Relative to the ΛCDM DESI+CMB value of 68.17 ± 0.28 [D-45], w0waCDM fits that include SNe move the r_d-side H0 by:
   - −0.66 (Pantheon+);
   - −1.43 (DESY5);
   - −2.26 (Union3).

   That is 0.77, 1.68 and 2.65 times σ_gap (0.85), or 13–44% of the 5.09 gap to H0DN. So the r_d-calibrated value is not pinned to about 1%: it carries a 1–2 km/s/Mpc dependence on the late-time model and the SN sample.

   **But every fit that includes SNe moves H0 down, so the gap widens.** The only upward moves come from fits without SNe: wCDM DESI+CMB gives 69.51, +1.34. Once SN shape is included, no smooth H(z) narrows the gap by more than 0.23σ_gap (wCDM: −0.20, −0.16, −0.83). This matches the run's own exclusion of a w = −1.194 solution, which leaves a 0.06–0.10 mag SN-shape residual [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-10]. So the attack defeats a supporting prediction but not the core. Late-time H(z) can change how big the gap is, but cannot close it. Late-time freedom also leaves the M_B statement intact: the M_B disagreement is read off the SN Hubble diagram itself. The fact that the size of the "H0 gap" depends on the model while the calibration conflict persists is, if anything, an argument for C7's framing.

2. **Is the "M_B" conflict actually the whole H0 gap? A closure test.** If the conflict were purely in M_B, then ΔM_B (ladder minus inverse ladder) should equal 5 log10(H0_ladder/H0_inverse).
   - R22's 73.04 against the inverse ladder's 67.19 and 67.67 [D-48] needs 0.181 and 0.166 mag.
   - The quoted ΔM_B is 0.122 and 0.141 mag [D-21; math: M-STATISTICIAN-04].
   - The residual is +0.059 and +0.025 mag.

   This would be an internal inconsistency if the two M_B values were on one SN system. They are not: the ladder uses Pantheon+ and the inverse ladder uses DES-SN5YR. The residual matches the ≈ 0.04 mag DES5Y–Pantheon+ offset for common SNe [D-85]. C7 itself already flags the naive M_B pair as "not a valid comparison". So the closure test cannot refute C7 on current data. It does make C7's call for a same-SN-system M_B comparison the right one.

3. **Are the "arbiters" really free of both calibrations? The equality scale.** C7 counts r_d-free shape measurements (equality scale, uncalibrated BAO) as neutral arbiters. They are not free of early-universe physics. At fixed measured k_eq/h and Ω_m, the inferred h scales as √(1 + 0.2271 N_eff). Moving the equality-route H0 from 67.19 to 73.26 needs N_eff = 4.45 (from 68.50 it needs 4.12). That is the same direction as an r_d reduction by ΔN_eff, so under that variant both "early" calibrations move together, which is an early-versus-late pattern. ACT DR6 gives N_eff = 2.86 ± 0.13 [D-74], which excludes this at 12.2σ (from 68.50: 9.7σ). The bound therefore closes the loophole: on the surviving variants the equality route stays an arbiter that does not depend on r_d. This is a correction to C7's decisive-test design, not a defeat of the core.

4. **Do the two calibrator groups hold together, and do calibration-free probes break the dichotomy?**
   - **r_d group** (CMB-SPA 67.19 ± 0.38 against DESI BAO+BBN 68.50 ± 0.58): χ² = 3.57/1, p = 0.059, a 1.89σ split. Add the DESI–CMB Ω_m discrepancy of 2.3–2.8σ [D-87]. The group holds, but only marginally.
   - **Anchor group** (SH0ES, CCHP total, SBF, TF): 73.02 ± 0.78, χ² = 2.20/3, p = 0.53. CCHP total sits 1.44σ below SH0ES. CCHP's JWST JAGB value (67.80 ± 2.72) is 1.98σ below SH0ES and 0.11σ from the r_d group. That is a strain on "one anchor scale", but it does not reach C7's own falsifier: "the anchor-calibrated ladders split from each other by more than their errors".
   - **Calibration-free probes:**
     - the one-step mean (71.27 ± 1.93) sits at 68% of the way from the r_d group to the anchor group, with z = +1.93 and −0.84 from the two groups;
     - the r_d-free DESI values sit at 38% (69.6) and at −15% (66.7, which is 3.2σ below the anchor group);
     - the globular-cluster age bound gives t0 = 12.86 Gyr at H0 = 73.26 in ΛCDM, 2.79σ below 13.81 ± 0.34 Gyr [D-55]. That is a weak lean against the anchor side.

   None of these supplies a third value that both calibrations contradict. That would be needed to show a conflict "between expansion rates" rather than between calibrations.

None of the four lines defeats the core. Every independent bound I could bring either agrees with C7's reading or falls inside it.

## Size against the discrepancy

C7 names no cause, so it produces no shift of its own. What it asserts is that the gap lives in two places:
- an M_B offset of 0.146–0.188 mag, against an observed gap of 0.153–0.195 mag in 5 log10 H0 [math: M-DIALECTICIAN-12, M-STATISTICIAN-04];
- an r_d deficit: 134.8–138.6 Mpc needed against 147.0 Mpc, which is 5.7–8.3% [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-02].

How the gap behaves under each class of change:
- **Late-time H(z) freedom fitted with SNe:** it moves the gap by −0.20 to −2.26 km/s/Mpc on the r_d side, so the gap widens by 0–44%, and it never narrows the gap by more than 0.23σ_gap. This agrees with C7's claim that H(z) is not the locus.
- **Local structure:** it would act on the anchor side but needs an 84σ underdensity [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-12; D-77], so its contribution is ≈ 0.
- **Calibration-free probes** (one-step mean, r_d-free BAO, GC ages): they sit inside the 67.5–73.0 bracket and so split no third way.

## Corrections to details

- **The "≤ 0.5σ" prediction is wrong as written.** "No modification of H(z) at 0 < z < 2 changes the gap by more than ≈ 0.5σ" fails: DESI DR2 w0wa fits with SNe change it by 0.77–2.65 σ_gap (−0.66 to −2.26 km/s/Mpc), and w0wa without SNe by 5.4σ_gap. The correct statement is one-sided: no H(z) that fits BAO+SN *narrows* the gap by more than ≈ 0.2σ, while several widen it. C7's "If false: a smooth H(z) change is found that moves the gap" should read "that narrows the gap".
- **"BAO + SN shape pin H(z)" is too strong.** The pinned quantity carries a 1–3% dependence on the late-time model and on the SN sample (the w0wa range across SN compilations is 65.91–67.51). It also assumes flat geometry [D-70 assumptions]. With curvature free, the chronometer-calibrated route does not yet separate the calibrations [Favale et al. 2026].
- **C5 is not removed "by construction".** A local underdensity is an expansion-rate effect on the Hubble-flow sample (it changes a_B). It is excluded by the SN Hubble-diagram bound and by cosmic variance [D-77; math: M-CONSTRAINTS-12], not by the framing. C7 should say "removed by bounds".
- **The equality-scale and r_d-free routes are not free of early-universe physics.** Under ΔN_eff they move together with r_d (N_eff = 4.1–4.45 would push the equality route to 73.26). Only ACT's N_eff = 2.86 ± 0.13 (a 9.7–12.2σ exclusion) makes them clean arbiters against the surviving early-time variants. The decisive test should say this.
- **The "r_d group mutually consistent" prediction is marginal now:** CMB-SPA against BAO+BBN is 1.89σ (p = 0.059), and the DESI–CMB Ω_m discrepancy is 2.3–2.8σ [D-87]. The "one anchor scale" prediction is also under strain from CCHP JWST JAGB (1.98σ from SH0ES), though the anchor-group χ² is fine (p = 0.53).
- **On the M_B comparison:** the 0.025–0.059 mag shortfall of ΔM_B against the H0 gap is the size expected from the SN-system mismatch (≈ 0.04 mag) [D-85], as C7 says. It cannot be used either for or against the core until there is a comparison on one SN system.

## What survives and remaining doubt

The core survives.
- Every independent bound I tested agrees with reading the gap as a conflict between the anchor-calibrated M_B and the sound-horizon r_d:
  - inverse ladder and SN shape [D-70; Efstathiou 2021; Camarena & Marra 2021];
  - DESI extended models [D-51];
  - local-void bounds [D-77];
  - N_eff [D-74];
  - globular-cluster ages [D-55];
  - calibration-free one-step, r_d-free and chronometer probes [D-31, D-49; Favale et al. 2026].
- Late-time expansion-history freedom changes only the size of the gap, and only upward, once SN shape is included.
- No calibration-free probe yet sits outside the 67.5–73.0 bracket, which is what a genuine "expansion-rate" conflict would require.

Remaining doubts:
- C7 is a framing, not a cause. Its empirical content beyond D-70 is thin, and it rests on the D-70 assumptions: no redshift evolution of SN standardisation, flat geometry, FRW homogeneity, and standard BAO.
- If the DESI–CMB Ω_m discrepancy (2.3–2.8σ) grows, the "r_d group" would itself split. That would add a third locus inside the early side, and C7's two-calibration picture would then be incomplete.
- A same-SN-system M_B comparison, and an arbiter free of both calibrations at σ ≤ 1.2 km/s/Mpc [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-15], remain the tests that would give the reframe its teeth.
