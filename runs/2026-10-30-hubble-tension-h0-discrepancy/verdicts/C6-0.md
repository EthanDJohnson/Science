# Verdict on C6 (refuter 0, angle: magnitude)
claim: No single dominant cause: comparable partial contributions (ladder-calibration levers, partial early-universe shifts) plus fluctuation and underestimated errors spread over several measurements produce the gap.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: Only a finely balanced two-sided hybrid survives: a ladder bias L and an early-universe shift E of ≈ 2–3 km/s/Mpc each (each 33–50% of the 6.07 km/s/Mpc gap), plus a helpful fluctuation of about 1σ in each pole, which puts the true global H0 in a ≈ 1.2 km/s/Mpc window, ≈ 69.4–70.6 km/s/Mpc; diffuse error inflation and pure fluctuation do not carry the gap, and the candidate's stated 69–71 range and its 70–71 decisive band reach mostly into C1 or C3 territory.

Units: H0 in km/s/Mpc (astronomical units, not SI); magnitudes in mag; σ is a Gaussian-equivalent significance.

## Strongest attack

C6 is "no part above half" under the run's own boundary rule (candidates.md: a cause is dominant if it carries more than half the gap, i.e. moves the true H0 across the midpoint 70.23 km/s/Mpc). With a gap of 6.07 km/s/Mpc between H0DN and CMB-SPA and pole errors of only 0.81 and 0.38 km/s/Mpc, the balance condition puts a tight limit on the true H0. Without a fluctuation the null holds only *at* the midpoint. With a helpful fluctuation of k σ in each pole it holds in a window [70.23 − 0.81k, 70.23 + 0.38k]: width 1.19 km/s/Mpc at k = 1, and 2.37 km/s/Mpc at k = 2. A joint ≥ 2σ helpful fluctuation has probability 5 × 10⁻⁴. [calc: runs/2026-10-30-hubble-tension-h0-discrepancy/calc/falsifier-C6-0_magnitude.py, §1, §3]

These are the consequences:
1. **The candidate's own stated true H0 of ≈ 69–71 crosses the boundary on both sides.** Without fluctuation the ladder carries 70.2% of the gap at H0 = 69.0 (C1) and the early side carries 62.7% at H0 = 71.0 (C3). Only 59% of the 69–71 range lies inside the 1σ balance window. [calc §2, §3]
2. **The cited part sizes reach the gap only at their top ends.** The ladder levers are given as 1–3 km/s/Mpc and the early shift as 1.5–3 km/s/Mpc, so the maximum sum is 6.00 against a 6.07 gap. Typical mid-range values (L = E = 2.0) leave 2.07 km/s/Mpc (2.3σ of the combined 0.89 km/s/Mpc error) to fluctuation. The low ends (L = 1.0, E = 1.5) need a 3.57 km/s/Mpc fluctuation: 4.0σ, and 59% of the gap, so fluctuation itself would dominate, which the candidate calls "near-eliminated". [calc §4]
3. **Two of the early-time fits C6 cites as its "partial" early contribution are not partial.** Poulin 2026 (71.0) closes 62.7% of the gap and eROSITA+ext (71.4) closes 69.3%. Under the boundary rule these are C3-dominant [D-53, D-94; calc §5; consistent with M-MECHANIST-11, M-EXAMINER-08]. Only the ACT/Planck-bounded fits (69.1–69.9, 31–45%) are partial.
4. **Posterior weight of the balanced region.** Take a flat prior on L ≥ 0 and E ≥ 0, Gaussian pole noise, and a true H0 informed only by SH0ES-independent, r_d-independent probes. The probability that neither side exceeds half is then:

   | probe set | true H0 (km/s/Mpc) | P(C6 balance) | P(ladder > ½) | P(early > ½) |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | one-step only | 71.27 ± 1.93 | 0.056 | 0.31 | 0.70 |
   | one-step + r_s-free (D-49) | 70.25 ± 1.21 | 0.104 | 0.49 | 0.51 |
   | + CCHP, SBF, TF | 70.96 ± 0.92 | 0.081 | 0.28 | 0.77 |

   The maximum of P(balance | H) at any single H is 0.28 (at H = 70.0). With SH0ES alone as the pole it is 0.06–0.12. [calc §6, §7] The probes closest to the true H0 therefore put about 90% of the weight on one side or the other carrying more than half.
5. **The decisive test does not separate C6 from C3.** The candidate's acceptance band for an anchor-free aggregate is "centred at 70–71". At a true H0 of 71.0, P(balance) = 0.017 and P(early > ½) ≈ 0.98, so a result at 71 supports C3/C2, not C6. Placing an aggregate inside the 1σ balance window at ±2σ needs σ ≈ 0.30 km/s/Mpc (0.42%), half the 0.61 km/s/Mpc the candidate quotes. [calc §6b]

The other sub-variants fail on magnitude in the candidate's own accounting, and I confirm this. Sub-variant (b), diffuse errors, needs the local errors inflated ×3.74 or an unmodelled common shift of 4.29 km/s/Mpc [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-13, verified]; inflating the early-side errors ×3 fails at p = 0.0041–0.0086 [math: M-DECOMPOSER-06]. Sub-variant (c), pure fluctuation, needs 6.82σ (combined error 0.891 km/s/Mpc) [calc §1; M-STATISTICIAN-01 gives 6.27σ for SH0ES]. What is left of C6 is therefore sub-variant (a), and that is fine-tuned in the way shown above.

Gap: H0DN 73.264 − CMB-SPA 67.19 = +6.07 km/s/Mpc (0.188 mag); SH0ES − SPA = +6.30. The balanced C6 point, at true H0 ≈ 70.2, implies these shifts:

**Can it carry the whole gap?** In principle yes: L ≈ 3, E ≈ 2.5–3 and a fluctuation of ≲ 1σ sum to 6.07 with no bound violated. In practice it does so only with both partial causes near the 50% ceiling, and only inside a ≈ 1.2 km/s/Mpc window that holds about 6–12% of the posterior from the independent probes.

## Corrections to details

- "True global H0 ≈ 69–71" should be ≈ 69.4–70.6 (1σ balance window). Outside it, by the run's rule, the candidate becomes C1 (below about 69.4) or C3/C2 (above about 70.6) unless the fluctuations exceed 1.5σ.
- "best fits closing 30–67%": the fits above 50% (Poulin 2026 62.7%, eROSITA 69.3%) are C3 outcomes, not partial contributions to C6.
- "≈ 1–3 [ladder] + ≈ 1.5–3 [early]": these sum to the gap only at both upper ends (maximum 6.00 against 6.07). The lower ends leave a 4.0σ fluctuation.
- Decisive test: the acceptance band "centred at 70–71" should be ≈ 69.4–70.6, and the precision needed to land inside it is σ ≈ 0.30 km/s/Mpc (0.42%), not 0.61 km/s/Mpc. A result at 71 favours C3.

## What survives and remaining doubt

The core "no single dominant cause" is not refuted on magnitude. The parts can add to the gap, and the r_s-free probes (69.2–70.3) together with the one-step mean (71.27 ± 1.93) sit near the balance point. C6 survives as a narrow, fine-balanced hybrid, not as a broad "spread over several measurements" null.

Doubts about my attack:
- The posterior weights depend on a flat prior on L and E and on treating the r_s-free and one-step probes as unbiased. Under EDE, θ_*-based r_s-free inferences could shift slightly.
- A broader reading of "comparable contributions", for example letting ladder levers count as separate causes, would widen C6. The run's boundary rule explicitly counts them as one (C1), so I keep the rule.
- Because the boundary is a sharp 50% line, any balanced hybrid is necessarily narrow. This is partly a feature of the slate's definition, not only of the physics.
