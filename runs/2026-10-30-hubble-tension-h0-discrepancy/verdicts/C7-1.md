# Verdict on C7 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: The 'early versus late H0' premise is mis-posed: the conflict is between two absolute calibrations, the geometric-anchor distance scale (M_B) and the sound-horizon ruler r_d, not between expansion rates.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: cited-evidence
narrowed: The H0 conflict is between the geometric-anchor distance scale (M_B) and the sound-horizon ruler r_d, and no change to the post-recombination (z ≲ 2.3) expansion history can reconcile the two; but r_d's leading physical realisation is a 6–9% higher pre-recombination expansion rate, and on current data this partition fits no better than "early versus late", so it is a restatement of that premise rather than a correction of it.

Units: astronomical, not SI. H0 is in km/s/Mpc, r_d in Mpc and magnitudes in mag; fractions are dimensionless.

## Strongest attack

The attack does not touch the reframe's main content. That content is the M_B-versus-r_d reading and the inverse-ladder no-go for late-time H(z), and the literature supports both strongly (see Evidence). The attack goes after the two words that make C7 a *reframe* rather than a restatement: "not between expansion rates" and "mis-posed".

1. **r_d is itself an expansion-rate quantity.** The sound horizon is r_d = ∫ c_s/H dz over z > z_d. The field's standard analysis of what would fix the r_d side names the least-unlikely solution class as an *expansion-rate* change before recombination. Knox & Millea (2020) put it this way: "We single out the set of solutions that increase the expansion rate in the decade of scale factor expansion just prior to recombination as the least unlikely." Aylor et al. (2019), the paper that first framed the tension as distance-ladder r_s against ΛCDM r_s, say the needed change "is likely to be important in the two decades of scale factor evolution prior to recombination."
   - A uniform increase in H(z > z_d) that takes r_d from 147.0 to the 138.6–134.8 Mpc that C7 itself needs is a 6.06–9.05% increase in the expansion rate. That is the same fractional size as the H0 gap, which is 9.04% from H0DN to CMB-SPA [calc: runs/2026-10-30-hubble-tension-h0-discrepancy/calc/falsifier-C7-1_evidence.py, part (b)].
   - So on the r_d branch, which is candidate C3 (EDE, ΔN_eff), the conflict *is* a conflict of expansion rates, located at z > 1000. Only recombination-physics routes (varying m_e, primordial magnetic fields) change r_d without changing H(z).
   - "Not between expansion rates" therefore holds only for the post-recombination expansion history.
2. **"Mis-posed" is stronger than the data allow.** C7's own evidence-against section records two results:
   - the run's grouped-χ² tests find the partitions "early vs late" (P1), "uses r_s vs not" (P2) and "anchored ladders vs rest" (P4) statistically equivalent (within-group p = 0.56, 0.26 and 0.31) [math: M-EXAMINER-06];
   - the exhaustive search over all 511 bipartitions puts "all local vs all early-type" best, at χ² 8.48 on 8 dof [math: M-DECOMPOSER-02], with only a < 2 shift in χ² once a common ladder offset is allowed [math: M-DECOMPOSER-10].

   The only measurements that C7 and the early-versus-late partition treat differently are the anchor-free, r_d-free "arbiters". On current data these lean toward the "late" pole, not toward neutrality: TDCOSMO-2026 at 71.1 +2.4/−2.3 sits +1.55σ from the r_d group and −0.76σ from the anchor group (likelihood ratio 2.3 : 1 for the anchor side), and the one-step mean gives 4.2 : 1 [calc: part (c)]. The data therefore do not show the early-versus-late premise to be *wrong*. C7 is an equivalent and more informative description of the same split, not a correction of an error.

## Evidence

- Knox & Millea 2020, PRD 101, 043533, arXiv:1908.03663 (abstract, fetched with fetch_text): "Measurements of the Hubble constant, and more generally measurements of the expansion rate and distances over the interval $0 < z < 1$, appear to be inconsistent with the predictions of the standard cosmological model" and "We single out the set of solutions that increase the expansion rate in the decade of scale factor expansion just prior to recombination as the least unlikely."
- Aylor et al. 2019, ApJ 874, 4, arXiv:1811.00537 (abstract, fetched). This supports C7's calibration reading: "Type Ia Supernovae, calibrated by classical distance ladder methods, can be used, in conjunction with galaxy survey two-point correlation functions, to empirically determine the size of the sound horizon". It also supports the expansion-rate caveat: "If reconciliation requires a change to the cosmological model, we argue that change is likely to be important in the two decades of scale factor evolution prior to recombination."
- Greene & Cyr-Racine 2022, JCAP 06, 002, arXiv:2112.11567 (abstract, fetched). This supports C7: "This emphasis on one number -- namely $H_0$ -- misses the fact that the tension fundamentally arises from disagreements of distance measurements."
- Efstathiou 2021, MNRAS 505, 3866, arXiv:2103.08723 (abstract, fetched). This supports C7's late-time exclusion: "A dynamics-free inverse distance ladder shows that changes to late time physics are strongly constrained observationally and cannot resolve the discrepancy between the SH0ES data and the base LCDM cosmology inferred from Planck."

## Size against the discrepancy

C7 names no cause, so it produces no shift of its own. Its structural claims, set against the gap of 6.07–6.30 km/s/Mpc (0.188–0.195 mag) to CMB-SPA and 4.99 km/s/Mpc (0.153 mag) to BAO+BBN:

- **r_d-calibrated routes** (CMB, BAO+BBN, inverse ladder). These are mutually consistent at 67.51 ± 0.29 [math: M-EXAMINER-06]. This is confirmed, and is consistent with the literature.
- **Anchor-calibrated ladders.** These sit at 73.02 ± 0.78, but CCHP's anchor-calibrated TRGB/JAGB results (70.39 ± 1.94; JWST-only 68.81; JAGB 67.80) sit 1.5–2σ below SH0ES with the same NGC 4258 anchor [D-24–D-26]. The "single anchor scale" is itself contested inside the anchor family. C7 absorbs this as a zero-point or sample problem at NGC 4258, which is still a calibration problem.
- **Late-time H(z).** The data-preferred w0wa expansion histories move the r_d-side H0 by 0.02–0.07 mag (0.15 mag without SNe), and always downward [calc, part (a)]. They cannot close the gap, which supports C7. But they show the post-recombination history is not "pinned" to zero shift.
- **Arbiters.** Current values are not decisive: likelihood ratio 2.3–4.2 : 1 toward the anchor side for the one-step methods, and 0.58 : 1 for the swing class including r_s-free BAO.

## Corrections to details

- **The prediction "No modification of H(z) at 0 < z < 2 changes the gap by more than ≈ 0.5σ" is ambiguous and partly false.**
  - In units of the gap's own uncertainty, the w0waCDM fits DESI prefers at 2.8–4.2σ move the r_d-side H0 by 0.77–2.65 σ_gap with SNe and 5.4 σ_gap without, always in the widening direction. In tension significance the change is −0.58 to +0.68σ.
  - The cited source (Schöneberg, Poulin et al. 2026, as quoted in the dialectician lens) says "For all models the tension metrics increase by up to 0.5σ". It refers to early-time models with evolving dark energy added, not to "any modification of H(z)".
  - The correct form: no data-allowed post-recombination H(z) change *reduces* the gap; data-preferred changes widen it by up to ≈ 0.7σ in significance, or 0.66–2.26 km/s/Mpc.
- **The argument says "BAO + SN shape pin H(z) between z ≈ 0.02 and 2.3".** They pin the gap's *closure*, not H(z) itself: at fixed r_d, the SN-sample-dependent w0wa fits shift H0 by up to 2.26 km/s/Mpc.
- **The cosmography-independent M_B framing predates Camarena et al. 2023.** Aylor et al. 2019 ("empirically determine the size of the sound horizon" from the ladder) and Camarena & Marra 2021 (MNRAS 504, 5164) both have it. C7's framing is established, not novel.
- **"Removes late-time H(z) (C4(i)) and local structure (C5) by construction."** Neither is removed by construction. Both are removed by evidence: the inverse-ladder no-go [D-70; Efstathiou 2021] and the void-size requirement [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-12]. A local underdensity is a local–global *expansion-rate* difference, so C7's framing would be false, not just incomplete, if C5 held. C7's exclusion of C5 is therefore an empirical bet, which the evidence currently supports.

## What survives and remaining doubt

The substantive core survives and is well supported in the literature (Aylor et al. 2019; Efstathiou 2021; Greene & Cyr-Racine 2022; Camarena & Marra 2021). Under data-allowed post-recombination physics the gap is equivalent to a 0.15–0.19 mag disagreement between the anchor-calibrated SN scale and the r_d-calibrated BAO+SN scale, and no late-time H(z) change closes it.

What does not survive in full:
- **"Not between expansion rates" is too broad.** On the r_d branch, the leading physical route is a 6–9% higher expansion rate before recombination (Knox & Millea; Aylor et al.).
- **"Mis-posed" overstates.** Current data do not prefer C7's partition over "early versus late"; the two are statistically equivalent, and the decomposer's exhaustive search slightly favours local versus early. The arbiters lean, weakly, toward the "late" pole.

Remaining doubt: whether "expansion rates" in the heading was meant to cover only late times. If so, the first narrowing is a wording correction and C7 would be close to "survives". The second point ("mis-posed" against "equivalent restatement") still stands on the partition statistics. I chose weakened over survives because both words carry the claim that this is a reframe *correcting* the premise rather than renaming it.
