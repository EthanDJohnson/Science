# Verdict on C5 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: Local structure, a large underdensity or coherent bulk flow around us, makes the locally measured expansion rate exceed the global H0 by most of the gap.
status: final
verdict: refuted
basis: cited-evidence
narrowed: none

All H0 values are in km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹; distances in Mpc (h⁻¹ Mpc where marked). The gap is 73.264 − 67.19 = 6.074 against CMB-SPA, or 4.764 against BAO+BBN [calc: calc/falsifier-C5-0_magnitude.py]. "Most of the gap" means a share above 50%, which is a local boost of more than about 3.0 km/s/Mpc (≳ 4.5%).

## Strongest attack
The ladder's own Hubble-flow supernovae already reach well beyond any plausible local void, and they return the same high H0. In SH0ES 2022 the absolute magnitude M_B is set by geometric (Cepheid) distances, so it does not depend on velocities. If a void or flow inside z ≈ 0.15 inflated the local expansion rate, Hubble-flow SNe at 0.15 < z < 0.8, measured with that same M_B, would return the global value (≈ 67) and pull the fit down. Instead, SH0ES reports that extending the Hubble flow to z < 0.8 (> 1300 SNe) **raises** H0 by 0.5, and that cutting z < 0.06 "to circumvent concerns about the presence of a hypothesized local void structure" **raises** it by ≈ 0.3. About 63% of the extended sample's weight lies beyond z = 0.15. A void carrying share f of the gap predicts a shift of −0.63 f × 6.07. For f = 0.5 that is −1.9, and for f = 0.8 it is −3.1, where +0.5 is observed. This disfavours "most of the gap" at 2.4σ under a deliberately conservative 1.0 km/s/Mpc nested-fit error (which covers survey cross-calibration offsets of ≈ 0.04 mag), and at about 5σ under a more realistic 0.5. At 2σ the largest share allowed is 39% (or 13%) [calc: calc/falsifier-C5-1_evidence.py].

Four other independent lines of evidence point the same way, each against a different sub-hypothesis:
1. **(a) ΛCDM void or flow.** Wu & Huterer 2017 and Huterer & Wu 2023 find that the sample variance of local H0 is a "nearly negligible fraction" of the gap. The void required would be a 20σ deviation.
2. **Any sharp-edged void inside the Hubble-flow range.** Kenworthy, Scolnic & Riess 2019 exclude |δ| > 20% at 4–5σ. A 50% share already needs δ_lin = −0.255 (Bernardeau −0.244) [calc: calc/falsifier-C5-0_magnitude.py], so "most of the gap" sits inside the excluded region.
3. **(c) KBC void with enhanced (MOND-like) growth.** The pro-void group's own field-level test against CosmicFlows-4 Tully–Fisher distances (Stiskalek, Desmond & Banik 2025, peer-reviewed in MNRAS) prefers a void "less than 70 Mpc", under 10% of the KBC size:
   - the profiles favoured by the Bayesian evidence give 70.4 and 70.2, a void boost of 2.8–3.0 over the Planck background, which is 46–52% of the gap;
   - the only profile that reaches "most" (exponential, 72.1, 77–81%) "is disfavoured by the Bayesian evidence";
   - the preferred models' bulk flows "disagree with recent estimates from CosmicFlows-4".
4. **(b) A non-Copernican Gpc LTB void.** This is the only form that escapes the z < 0.8 test, and only if its edge lies beyond z ≈ 0.8. It needs the observer within ≈ 61 Mpc of the centre (P = 8.6 × 10⁻⁴ for R = 640 Mpc) [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-12], and ΛLTB loses its SN preference once the full sample is used [D-77, D-97].

Every sub-hypothesis that names a mechanism is therefore excluded, or at best limited to about half the gap, by data in hand.

- **Huterer & Wu 2023, arXiv:2309.05749 (review chapter, preprint), abstract (fetch_text):** "the typical local variation in $H_0$ is far smaller than what would be required to explain the Hubble tension; the latter would require a 20-$\sigma$ deviation from the expected sample variance. - **Stiskalek, Desmond & Banik 2025, MNRAS 543 1556 (arXiv:2506.10518), abstract (fetch_text):** "we find the derived velocity fields to prefer a void size of less than 70 Mpc, which is less than 10 per cent of the fiducial size found by Haslbauer et al. … The predicted local Hubble constant is $72.1^{+0.9}_{-0.8}$, $70.4^{+0.4}_{-0.4}$, or $70.2^{+0.5}_{-0.4}$ km/s/Mpc for an initial underdensity profile that is exponential, Gaussian, or Maxwell-Boltzmann, respectively. … The exponential profile does achieve consistency with this measurement at just over $1\sigma$, but it is disfavoured by the Bayesian evidence. The preferred models produce bulk flow curves that disagree with recent estimates from CosmicFlows-4". Full text p. 2: "a local void in theΛCDM framework cannot solve the Hubble tension or explain the KBC void."
- **Böhringer, Chon & Collins 2020, A&A 633 A19 (arXiv:1907.12402), abstract (fetch_text).** This is the strongest observational case *for* an underdensity, and even it is partial:
  - it finds δ ≈ "-0.3 +- 0.15 (-0.2 +- 0.1) in a region with a radius of about 100 (~140) Mpc";
  - inside that region H "will be larger by about 5.5 +2.1-2.8%, which explains part of the discrepancy … If distance indicators outside the local underdensity are included, as in many modern analyses, this effect is diluted."
  - The undiluted share would be 61% (30–84%), but SH0ES's Hubble flow starts at z = 0.0233 (≈ 100 Mpc), at that region's edge.
- **Bulk-flow branch:**
  - A dipole flow cancels in the monopole H0 for a sky-covering sample. The isotropic-sky bias is −0.009% at 400 km/s, and even a one-hemisphere sample needs V ≈ 2060 km/s for half the gap, against a ΛCDM rms of 166 km/s at R = 150 h⁻¹ Mpc [calc: calc/falsifier-C5-0_magnitude.py].
  - Published flow corrections raise local H0 (+0.6 SN, +1.4 SBF) rather than lowering it [D-36].

## Corrections to details
- **The named weakest link is the weak test, not the strong one.** TDCOSMO-2026 (71.1 +2.4/−2.3) sits only +1.7σ from the global 67.19 even at full share, and +0.4σ at a 50% share [calc: calc/falsifier-C5-1_evidence.py]. The time delays do not discriminate. The strong existing test is ladder-calibrated SNe at 0.15 < z < 0.8 (R22 Fits 34–36), which the candidate omits.
- **Part of the "decisive test" has already been run.** "H0 versus Hubble-flow z_min" and "SN convergence at 0.15 < z < 0.4" were done in R22 and in Kenworthy 2019, with null results. Banik's prediction of "convergence to CMB-consistent expansion for z ≳ 0.2" [D-84] is already in conflict with R22 Fit 34, unless the void extends beyond z ≈ 0.8.
- **σ(H0^loc) depends on the selection used.** The candidate's 0.31 (Wu & Huterer, R16 selection) gives 18–20σ. Stiskalek et al. (p. 2) quote ≈ 0.9 km/s/Mpc after Camarena & Marra 2018 for the local cz′, which gives about 6.7σ for the full gap and about 3.4σ for half. Under either value ΛCDM cannot carry most of the gap, but "18–20σ" should be stated as selection-dependent.
- **Evidence missing from both sides.**
  - For: Böhringer et al. 2020, a 5.5% boost inside ≈ 100–140 Mpc, "diluted" for modern samples.
  - Against: Stiskalek, Desmond & Banik 2025, a void < 70 Mpc whose favoured profiles carry about half the gap, with bulk flows that disagree with CF4. That paper is a test by the KBC-void proponents themselves.
  - Both belong in the evidence lists. The unchecked Cai et al. quote can be replaced by the verified Huterer & Wu quote above.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives.** A partial local-structure contribution: an underdensity of δ ≈ −0.2 to −0.3 on ≈ 100–140 Mpc (Böhringer), or the favoured KBC profiles of Stiskalek et al. These allow roughly ≤ 40% of the gap at 2σ by the z < 0.8 test, and about half in the void-friendly velocity-field fit. That is a component of C6, not a dominant cause.
- **The one loophole.** A non-Copernican Gpc void whose edge lies beyond z ≈ 0.8, with the observer near its centre. It escapes the R22 high-z test. It pays a ≈ 10⁻³ placement probability [math: M-CONSTRAINTS-12], loses its preference with the full SN sample [D-77], and would have to affect the z ≈ 0.3–0.7 BAO and time-delay distances too.
- **Doubts about my own attack.**
  1. My z < 0.8 calculation uses equal per-SN weights and an assumed nested-fit error (0.5–1.0). The share bound (13–39%) is approximate, though its sign is not in doubt.
  2. R22 and Kenworthy 2019 come from SH0ES members. However, Huterer & Wu, and Stiskalek, Desmond & Banik (pro-void authors), reach compatible conclusions independently.
  3. Timescape/backreaction (sub-hypothesis d) is not tested here. The Seifert et al. abstract the candidate cites makes no H0-gap claim, so it does not support "most of the gap" either.
