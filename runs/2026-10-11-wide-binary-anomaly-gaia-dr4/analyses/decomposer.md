# Analysis: decomposer (methodical doubt and decomposition)
status: final

## Method applied
Rewrote the question as a tree of sub-questions down to leaves a calculation or a cited result can settle; built a ledger of every premise (measured / derived / assumed / unknown); named the load-bearing premise; sorted leaves into settled vs open. One calibration-transfer calculation with the run's generator tests the leaves that bear most on the slate.

## Findings
1. **The anomaly is a differential (shape) measurement, so only separation-dependent systematics can make or erase it.** Every family-1 and family-2 test normalises to Newton in a high-acceleration or free-normalisation regime (Chae calibrates f_multi at ≈1e-8 m s^-2 [D-15]; Cookson leaves the Newtonian normalisation free [D-29]). A common-mode mass bias b scales γ by 1/b in every bin and cancels in the calibrated ratio; to fake γ = 1.37 by mass alone needs M_est/M_true = 0.730 in the low-g bin relative to the calibration bin, a 27% low-g-only underestimate [calc: runs/2026-10-11-wide-binary-anomaly-gaia-dr4/calc/lens-decomposer_transfer.py]. This strains the brief's "common mass or eccentricity systematic" candidate as a full explanation: it must be a gradient, not an offset. Hernandez & Kroupa's 2.0 vs 1.56 Msun point [D-33] is an offset in an un-normalised comparison and so is a mass-scale issue for that test only.
2. **Hidden triples grow in effect with separation even at fixed f_triple.** In the run's generator (Newton, DR3 noise, HTZ f(e)), the median-based apparent γ from f_triple = 0.2 / 0.3 is 1.097 / 1.137 in the CAL bin (g_N > 10^-8.5) but 1.180 / 1.282 in the TEST bin (10^-10.5–10^-9.5 m s^-2): a differential residual of 1.075 / 1.128 that survives calibration of f on the Newtonian bin if the Newtonian template does not model the triple tail's s-dependence [calc: lens-decomposer_transfer.py]. This lands on Boufourou's claimed full-median bias 1.08–1.13 [D-50] by an independent route and accounts for roughly 23–39% of ln(1.368) [calc: runs/2026-10-11-wide-binary-anomaly-gaia-dr4/calc/lens-decomposer_budget.py].
3. **f_multi transfer alone cannot carry the whole anomaly unless the triple fraction itself rises steeply with separation.** With f calibrated at the CAL bin, reproducing Chae 2024b's Γ = 0.068 under Newton requires a TEST-bin triple fraction larger by Δf ≈ +0.37 to +0.43 [calc: lens-decomposer_transfer.py]. No source in the dossier measures f_multi(s) [D-86, gaps §6]; the RV follow-ups (46% RV-variable at s > 13 kau, Pasquini [D-38]; 27% of 44, Saglia [D-37]; ≲5% in C3PO co-natal pairs [D-27]) bracket but don't settle it, and they come from biased selections [D-68]. Caveat: the generator's f_triple (photocentre-perturbing inner binaries with a_in ≤ 0.3 a(1−e)) is not Chae's f_multi definition, so the Δf is model-relative.
4. **Eccentricity is a ±5–8% gradient lever, not a 37% one, and its sign depends on the truth.** If the true f(e) is a constant power law but the template is the HTZ s-dependent one, the calibrated differential is 1.080 (α = 2), 1.067 (thermal), 1.048 (uniform) [calc: lens-decomposer_transfer.py]. The uncalibrated TEST-bin bias is larger (α = 2: 0.935; uniform: 1.305), consistent with [D-49]. Hwang's α ≈ 1.2–1.3 measured at 1–10 kau [D-41] excludes uniform there; Banik's fitted γ_e ≈ 2 [D-42] would, uncalibrated, push a median result LOW, not high.
5. **Tail truncation at ṽ < √2 cannot hide a real MOND boost of this size from pure binaries.** For pure binaries in the TEST bin, only 1.25% (bz18_simple) or 1.08% (chae2024) of MOND pairs lie above √2, and truncation lowers the MOND median by 0.9% [calc: lens-decomposer_transfer.py]. So brief premise 4 ("robust estimators absorb a real boost") can work only through the triple-component degeneracy in a mixture or a template-calibration choice [D-86], not through the cut itself.
6. **On (nearly) the same catalogue, the pipeline step is larger than the estimator-family step.** Chae 2024b γ = 1.368 [D-22]; Boufourou's full-median reproduction on Chae's catalogue ≈ 1.12–1.20 [D-31]; E2 1.045 and E3 1.05 [D-31]. In ln γ (total gap ln(1.368/1.045) = 0.269), the Chae-pipeline-to-E1 step is 49% (using the deep-bin E1 1.20, the closer like-for-like match to Chae's s ≳ 5 kau bin) to 74% (global E1 1.12), and the E1-to-E2 family step is 51% to 26%; E3 vs E2 differs by ln 0.005 [calc: lens-decomposer_budget.py]. So "same data, different γ" is not mainly the family label: it is Chae's calibration/template implementation versus Boufourou's, which Boufourou does not audit ("We have not audited individual published pipelines" [D-50]).
7. **Family 4's spread is a prior, not the data.** On one 36-pair dataset the deprojection choice alone moves γ from 1.00 to 1.56 [D-17, D-26]: ln 0.445, larger than the whole family-1 anomaly ln(1.368) = 0.313 [calc: lens-decomposer_budget.py]. Family-4 results on DR4 will track the a-prior choice of the group that runs them.
8. **Sample cuts can flip a family-2 verdict.** Cookson's own test reverses from Newton 88× to MOND 12× at d < 150 pc when the ipd_frac_multi_peak = 0 cut is dropped [D-29, D-62]. A cut that removes resolved-but-blended companions acts on the triple tail, so it is the same lever as findings 2–3 acting through selection.
9. Search for a direct measurement of f_multi versus wide-binary separation found none [new: WebSearch, search-summary, SUMMARY "The search didn't turn up a result that directly measures how the triple fraction changes with separation"]; one DR2 paper (Penoyre et al. 2020, arXiv:2003.05467) reports median RUWE falling with pair separation [new: WebSearch on arXiv:2003.05467, search-summary, SUMMARY "median RUWE falls as separation grows"], which concerns angular blending and does not settle the load-bearing premise.

10. **The separation-dependent part of the triple bias comes mostly from long-period inner binaries, which DR4 will only partly flag.** At DR4 noise with f_triple = 0.3, inner binaries with a_in = 0.1–5 au (P_in ≲ 11 yr for ~1 Msun, within ~2× the 66-month baseline) give a calibrated differential TEST/CAL of 1.039; those with a_in = 5–300 au give 1.177; the full log-uniform population gives 1.107 (DR3 noise: 1.134) [calc: runs/2026-10-11-wide-binary-anomaly-gaia-dr4/calc/lens-decomposer_dr4split.py]. Short-period wobble averages out over the baseline; long-period reflex motion enters the proper motion as a near-linear shift comparable to v_c(s) at wide s [D-40, D-85]. So DR4's acceleration solutions and epoch astrometry [D-12] attack the lesser part of the lever unless their curvature sensitivity reaches a_in of tens of au. Brief premise 11 ("DR4 settles it") is therefore not automatic.
11. **A freed eccentricity law can absorb ~7% of γ, not ~37%.** Moving the truth from HTZ to α = 2 lowers the TEST-bin median-based γ to 0.935 [calc: lens-decomposer_transfer.py]; so the Banik+24 fit's γ_e ≈ 2 [D-42] could have absorbed at most a few-to-7% boost in their α_grav. That cannot hide a 1.37 signal, which strains the "null side absorbs a real boost" candidate through this channel.

### 3. Load-bearing assumption
**L5 + L6 together: that the velocity-relevant hidden-companion population (fraction AND inner-period distribution) is the same at ~1 kau, where family-1 is calibrated, and at ≥5 kau, where the anomaly is measured.** If it holds and Chae's triple template is right, Chae's calibrated differential is clean and the robust families must be absorbing a boost (the anomaly survives). If it fails by Δf ≈ +0.4 (finding 3), or if long-period inner companions (a_in 5–300 au) are more common or more perturbing at wide s than templates assume (finding 10), the whole family-1 signal is an artefact. The mixture families do not rely on L5 (they fit f_trip in the test zone) but rely on the triple-tail shape (L6); that is why the two camps cannot convince each other.
**Test:** a separation-resolved census of velocity-relevant companions in wide pairs: multi-epoch RVs (σ ≲ 20 m/s) for a volume-limited sample split at 1 kau and ≥5 kau, plus DR4 epoch-astrometry/acceleration flags; compare f_multi(s) and the a_in distribution. A secondary test: run Chae's own pipeline, unchanged, on a Newtonian mock with a long-period-rich triple population; γ ≥ 1.25 there would settle Q2d.

## What would change my mind
- An audit of Chae's pipeline on a Newtonian mock returning γ ≈ 1.00 (would remove A's pipeline step, pushing weight to B or C).
- A direct, unbiased census showing velocity-relevant companion fraction flat in separation AND an inner-period distribution with few a_in = 5–300 au companions (would weaken A and C, strengthen B).
- An independent injection-recovery (the mechanist's) in which E2/E3 recover γ_inj = 1.4 as ≤ 1.2 (would revive B).
- DR4 F2/F3 results in the scoring bin ≥ 1.2 with a clean Newtonian validation zone.
- A measured orientation dependence of the boost relative to the Galactic field direction.

## Assumptions I relied on
- The run's generator's triple model (log-uniform a_in on 0.1–300 au, stability cut 0.3 a(1−e), photocentre factor with L ∝ m^3.5, baseline averaging) is representative enough to rank which inner periods matter; its f_triple is not Chae's f_multi.
- "Apparent γ" from a median ratio squared is a fair proxy for a family-1 estimator; Chae's acceleration-plane and median templates differ in detail.
- Boufourou's E1 figure on Chae's catalogue (1.12–1.20) is a correct reproduction of a generic full-median on that catalogue [D-31]; the pipeline step depends on it.
- Single-seed generator runs; jitter about ±0.02 in γ assumed by analogy with [D-50].
- The CAL bin (g_N > 10^-8.5 m s^-2) stands in for Chae's ~1e-8 calibration regime; the TEST bin (10^-10.5–10^-9.5) contains the brief's scoring bin.
