# Analysis: constraints (Kant: conditions any valid answer must satisfy)
status: final

## Method applied
Audit of the conditions any answer about the DR3 wide-binary disagreement and the DR4 forecast must satisfy: the bound-orbit velocity bound ṽ ≤ √(2γ) and what the observed tail implies, the MOND-with-EFE prediction γ_MOND(g_N) for several interpolating functions (so "consistent with MOND" has a number), Galactic-tide (Jacobi radius) and flyby limits on the separation window, the f_multi–γ degeneracy, mass-scale and eccentricity systematics, and the statistical-power constraint. Each candidate is checked against each condition with a logged calculation. GR energy-condition tools do not apply here (weak-field, v/c ~ 1e-6); see Lens-specific outputs.

F2. **Exact bound on ṽ for bound orbits under MOND with EFE.** In the EF-dominated QUMOND potential Φ = −(GMν_e/r)(1 + K sin²θ/2) (derived in the script header; it is homogeneous of degree −1), E < 0 and s ≤ r give ṽ² ≤ 2 max_θ η(θ) = 2ν_e. So ṽ_max = √(2ν_e) = 1.766 (simple), 1.737 (MLS), 1.589 (n = 1.5), 1.514 (standard) at g_ext = 1.785 a0, against √2 = 1.414 for Newton; AQUAL gives the same maxima (parallel direction). The scalar-γ form √(2γ) with γ = 1.4228 gives 1.687, so the scalar approximation understates the MOND kinematic ceiling by 4.7% in ṽ [calc: lens-constraints_mond_gamma.py]. Consequence: **any pair with true ṽ > 1.77 is not a bound binary under any interpolating function considered**, so the observed fat tail at ṽ ≈ 1.5–6 [D-08] is contamination (triples, flybys, chance alignments, mass errors, noise) under Newton AND under MOND. Cookson's ṽ < 2.5 cut [D-29] and Manchanda's 2.5 bound [D-81] are generous by a factor 1.4 in ṽ.

No field solver is needed for this quantity. F6. **Newtonian ṽ medians reproduced independently** (own Kepler MC, dN/da ∝ a^-1.6, 6e5 orbits, half-sample agreement ≤ 0.0005): circular 0.6914, uniform 0.5895, thermal 0.5263, α = 1.17 0.5191, α = 1.3 0.5125, α = 2 0.4895; max ṽ 1.4054 < √2 for every law [D-48] [calc: runs/2026-10-11-wide-binary-anomaly-gaia-dr4/calc/lens-constraints_vtilde.py].

F7. **Truncation cannot hide a MOND signal (brief premise 4, kinematic part).** Under pseudo-Newtonian γ = 1.4228 only 0.52% (thermal) / 0.55% (α = 1.3) of bound pairs exceed ṽ = √2, 0.01% exceed 1.6, none exceed 2. A tail-truncated median at √2 compared even with an *untruncated* Newtonian template returns 1.413 (thermal) / 1.412 (α = 1.3) for a true 1.4228, and 1.363 for a true 1.37: the truncation costs ≤ 0.01 in γ [calc: lens-constraints_vtilde.py]. Boufourou's 1.31–1.36 recovery on γ = 1.4 injections [D-50] therefore owes its 5% dilution to something other than the cut (his stated a↔r mixing at the 2 kau boundary is a candidate). Any claim that family 2 suppresses a real boost must name a mechanism other than truncation; none in the dossier does.

F8. **Contamination budget for the full median.** If a fraction f of pairs is moved above the clean median and the template does not contain them, γ_bias = (Q(0.5/(1−f))/Q(0.5))². To fake γ = 1.37 needs f ≥ 0.19 (both thermal and α = 1.3); γ = 1.10 needs f ≥ 0.065; γ = 1.05 needs f ≥ 0.035 [calc: lens-constraints_vtilde.py]. After Chae's high-acceleration calibration [D-15] only the *separation-dependent* part Δf_eff of the velocity-relevant multiple fraction counts, so the artefact candidate requires Δf_eff ≈ 0.19 between ~1 kau and ≳5 kau to explain all of Chae's 1.37, or ≈0.07–0.1 to explain Boufourou's 1.08–1.13 [D-50].

F9. **Robust estimators are robust only to large kicks.** With f = 0.2 triples each kicked by κ = |Δv|/v_c(s) (isotropic, projected): κ = 0.3 → full median 1.028, truncated (<√2) 1.027; κ = 0.5 → 1.073 vs 1.069; κ = 0.75 → 1.146 vs 1.131; κ = 1 → 1.221 vs 1.176; κ = 1.5 → 1.318 vs 1.165; κ = 2 → 1.358 vs 1.093; κ = 4 → 1.391 vs 1.016. A ṽ < 2.5 cut (Cookson [D-29]) gives protection only for κ ≳ 4 (1.075) [calc: lens-constraints_vtilde.py]. So the gap between family 1 and family 2 measures only the large-κ (κ ≳ 1.5) part of the contamination. Small-κ triples (κ ≲ 0.75) bias families 1 and 2 almost equally; only a mixture with the right κ distribution, or removing the triples, corrects them.

F10. **Measurement noise is a separation-dependent bias of the right size.** For a median compared with a noise-free template (per-axis σ_ṽ, Newtonian truth, α = 1.3): σ_ṽ = 0.05 → 1.013; 0.10 → 1.050; 0.15 → 1.114; 0.20 → 1.202; 0.30 → 1.454. Truncation barely helps (0.20 → 1.194) [calc: lens-constraints_vtilde.py]. Because σ_ṽ ∝ d σ_μ √(s/M), a noise-free or under-noised template produces a γ excess that rises toward low g_N. σ_ṽ ≈ 0.27 alone would mimic 1.37. A template with Gaia formal errors underestimated by 30% [D-10] leaves a residual of ≈+0.035 at a true σ_ṽ of 0.13 and ≈+0.13 at 0.26 (read off the same table).

F11. **Eccentricity cannot be the dominant cause.** For a full median with an α = 1.2 (1.3) template, faking γ = 1.37 needs a true α ≈ 0.0 (0.0), i.e. uniform; γ = 1.20 needs α ≈ 0.31 (0.39); γ = 1.10 needs α ≈ 0.69 (0.80). Hwang+22 measure α = 1.32 +0.09 −0.08 at 3–10 kau and 1.17 +0.14 −0.15 at 10–30 kau [D-41]. Required shifts are 6–9σ (1.37) and ≈3σ (1.10). Template α = 1.3 vs true 1.17 gives 1.026; true α = 2 (Banik's fit [D-42]) gives 0.912, *lowering* γ [calc: lens-constraints_vtilde.py]. Eccentricity therefore contributes at most ±0.03 (Hwang-consistent) or −0.09 (Banik-like α in a Hwang-template analysis).

F12. **Hidden-companion mass underestimate.** For one hidden companion with q_in ~ U(0.1, 1) and L ∝ m^3.5 (4.0), M_true/M_est = 1.27 (1.28) on average, up to 1.45 (1.48). With no velocity perturbation it raises the full median by +2.2% (f = 0.1), +4.6% (0.2), +7.1% (0.3), +12% (0.5), and the truncated median by the same (+4.6% at f = 0.2), because the √1.27 ≈ 1.13 boost in ṽ never crosses √2 [calc: lens-constraints_vtilde.py]. It is separation-independent unless f_multi(s) is, so high-acceleration calibration or a validation zone removes it. Even f = 1 cannot reach +37%.

F13. **Statistical floor of a median estimator.** σ_γ ≈ 1.39 γ/√N (pdf at median 1.40, median 0.5125; bootstrap with weights 0.085 at N = 341 vs analytic 0.075; 0.0258 vs 0.0254 at N = 3000). N = 341 → 0.075 (Boufourou's deep bin quotes ±0.07 [D-31], consistent); N = 6389 → 0.017 (Chae 2024b quotes ±0.10 stat for his s/r_M ≳ 0.7 sample of 6389 [D-22]; if all of them sit in his low-acceleration bin, that is about 6× the bare-median floor, so calibration, binning and noise dominate his budget); σ_γ = 0.005 needs N ≈ 77,600 pairs *in the bin*, and 0.01 needs 19,400 [calc: lens-constraints_vtilde.py]. Boufourou's "σ_stat < 0.005 on DR4" [D-52] can hold only for the whole 2–30 kau zone, not for a scoring bin around 1e-10 m s^-2.

Examples, DR3 → DR4, with the noise-free-template full-median bias: G = 15, d = 200 pc, s = 10 kau: σ_ṽ 0.104 → 0.044 (γ 1.055 → 1.009). G = 17, 200 pc, 10 kau: 0.279 → 0.117 (1.39 → 1.07). G = 17, 200 pc, 20 kau: 0.394 → 0.166 (1.79 → 1.14). G = 13 at any d ≤ 300 pc and s ≤ 20 kau stays below 1.05 even in DR3. **So a bright-limited or σ_ṽ-cut sample (σ_ṽ < 0.1, Boufourou [D-31]) is immune to the bias, but a faint-inclusive sample at 200 pc with a noise-free or under-noised template can fake the whole anomaly in the 10–20 kau bins.** The bias is ∝ σ_ṽ² at small σ, so DR4's 2.4× smaller proper-motion errors cut it about 6×. This is a clean DR4 discriminator (see Lens-specific outputs).

F15. **Galactic tides are negligible as a γ systematic.** Jacobi radius r_J = (GM/4A(A−B))^(1/3) = 324,000 au = 1.57 pc for 1.5 Msun (assumed A = 15.3, B = −11.9 km s^-1 kpc^-1). Tidal-to-internal ratio at r = 10 kau: 2.9e-5 in-plane, 9.6e-5 vertical (assumed ρ0 = 0.1 Msun pc^-3); at 30 kau: 7.9e-4 and 2.6e-3 [calc: lens-constraints_sepdep.py]. Tides can't produce ≥ 0.3% of γ at s ≤ 30 kau. Unbound or dissolving pairs enter only as contaminants, and mostly at ṽ > √2 (F2, F9).

(ii) At fixed f_multi the full-median bias grows with s. The ratio 10 kau / 1 kau is 1.364/1.238 = 1.10 (DR3, RUWE-only) and 1.137/1.041 = 1.09 (DR4 flags). So **a calibration at ~1 kau that ignores the growth of κ with s leaves ≈ +0.10 at 10 kau**, rising to +0.12 at 20 kau (1.388/1.238); with σ_logP = 1.5 the ratios are 1.437/1.276 = 1.13 (10 kau) and 1.485/1.276 = 1.16 (20 kau). F17. **GR is irrelevant.** GM/(rc²) = 1.5e-12 and v_c/c = 1.2e-6 at 10 kau (1.5 Msun), so 1PN corrections to γ are ~1e-12 [calc: runs/2026-10-11-wide-binary-anomaly-gaia-dr4/calc/lens-constraints_misc.py]. Energy conditions, quantum inequalities, horizons and chronology have no purchase on this question, so `gr_tensors.py` was not used. The binding constraints are kinematic (F2), statistical (F13) and the Gauss-law MOND identity (F3).

F18. **Perspective effect: a separation- and distance-dependent bias if uncorrected.** Magnitude model (assumed): Δv_p = θ |v_sys|, with v_sys Gaussian at 25 km/s per axis and θ = s/d. The full-median γ is 1.0086 at (10 kau, 100 pc), 1.068 at (20 kau, 100 pc), 1.23 at (30 kau, 100 pc), 1.27 at (20 kau, 50 pc), 1.97 at (30 kau, 50 pc), and 1.018 at (20 kau, 200 pc) [calc: lens-constraints_misc.py]. This agrees with Chae & Yoon that perspective matters mainly at 15–30 kau [D-62]. In my model the uncorrected term *inflates* γ, yet Boufourou's angular correction *raised* his deep bin (0.97 → 1.11) [D-31]. So his correction is either not (only) a perspective removal, or the effect is coherent in a way my random-direction model misses. Open; flagged for the critics.

F19. **The EFE anisotropy gives a systematics-robust observable (MOND, SPECULATIVE).** Applying the exact EF-limit radial boost η(θ) per pair as an instantaneous scalar (approximation): angle-average check 1.4229 (exact 1.4228). Pairs in directions where the EF lies in the sky plane give 1.4267, and EF along the line of sight 1.4117 (contrast only 0.015). But **with the EF in the sky plane, splitting by the angle between the projected separation and the projected EF gives γ = 1.487 (aligned within 30°) against 1.368 (more than 60°), a contrast of 0.12**. The median floor (F13) gives 1.4σ, 2.5σ and 4.3σ for N_bin = 3,000, 10,000 and 30,000 in the deep bin [calc: lens-constraints_misc.py]. Newton, triples, noise and mass errors are isotropic on the sky and predict zero contrast, and perspective is aligned with the separation but not with the Galactic-centre direction. This is the cleanest gravity-vs-artefact discriminator available to DR4 samples. Caveat: orbits in an anisotropic potential don't sample θ uniformly, so the per-pair scalar is an approximation of order the anisotropy itself.

F20. **What the DR3 null results exclude, given the band of MOND predictions.** Boufourou's ΔlnL values imply a quadratic σ_stat of 0.0218 (from ΔlnL(1.4) = 129) or 0.0161 (from ΔlnL(1.0) = 4.8) around γ̂ = 1.05 [D-31]. Deep bin 1.11 ± 0.07 vs 1.42: 4.5σ; vs 1.25: 2.0σ; vs 1.07: 0.5σ. **So the null side's "16σ" is specific to the simple/MLS test-particle prediction. A standard-function MOND (γ ≈ 1.07) is not excluded by any DR3 wide-binary result in the dossier, and the two-body value (1.2–1.3) is excluded only at 2–4σ once the f(e) systematic is included.** Galaxy-scale preference for simple-like ν over standard is a prior from outside this test, labelled and down-weighted per the brief.

F22. **The observed tail implies contamination inside the window.** By F2, every pair whose true ṽ exceeds 1.77 is a contaminant under every gravity law considered. In the inner-binary model (F16) at 10 kau, 48% of triples have κ > 1 and 30% have 0.3 < κ < 1. Since the κ distribution is continuous, a tail population of kicked pairs comes with a comparable in-window population of small-κ pairs, which families 1 *and* 2 both count (F9). For example, f ≈ 0.1 at κ ≈ 0.5–0.6 gives γ ≈ 1.04–1.05 in a truncated median, the size of Boufourou's E2 = 1.045 [D-31]. So the residual of E2 above 1 is *expected* under Newton and is no evidence of a boost. The quantitative tail fraction above ṽ = 2 per bin is not in the dossier (a gap).

### 3. Reference MOND values for the scoring rule (SPECULATIVE, labelled)
- The α_grav → γ conversion when a paper doesn't state its template: use **γ_MOND = 1.416** (exact QUMOND sphere average, simple ν, BZ18 g_ext = 2.142e-10 m s^-2, at g_N = 1e-10 m s^-2 with g_N from 3D r). ### 4. DR4 discriminators derived from the constraints
1. **EF-alignment contrast** (F19): γ(projected separation ∥ projected EF) − γ(⊥) ≈ +0.12 (simple) under MOND, 0 under Newton and every artefact considered. Needs ≳ 10,000–30,000 deep-regime pairs for 2.5–4σ.
2. **Noise scaling** (F14): if the DR3 full-median excess is noise-driven, the same pipeline on DR4 drops it by ≈6× in (γ − 1)_noise. Triple-driven and gravity-driven excesses don't scale with σ_μ.
3. **Magnitude split**: noise bias is concentrated in G ≳ 17 at d ≳ 150 pc; gravity is not. Any family's γ should be independent of the G cut under MOND or Newton, and fall with a brighter cut under the noise artefact.
4. **Acceleration-flag split** (F16): removing DR4 nss_acceleration_astro / epoch-astrometry-flagged pairs should cut the *absolute* full-median bias (≈1.36 → 1.14 at 10 kau in my model). Under MOND the flagged and unflagged clean pairs give the same γ.
5. **Scoring bin vs zone**: under standard-ν MOND the 2–30 kau zone gives 1.048 but the scoring bin 1.074. Under Newton both are 1.00 plus the same bias.

### 5. Validity domains of the models the dossier and this analysis rely on
- **Pseudo-Newtonian G_eff = γG Kepler orbits** [D-05]: an approximation. It is exact only in the Newtonian limit. In the EF-dominated limit the potential is anisotropic (η from 1.354 to 1.560) and orbits precess; the ṽ ceiling differs (1.766 vs 1.687, F2). It is adequate for the median to about the size of the anisotropy averaged over orientations (≈0.015 sky-wide, F19). Not validated for the tail or for 3D orbit fits.
- **QUMOND test particle + point mass in a uniform EF** (BZ18) [D-01]: valid for q = m2/m1 ≪ 1 and EF uniform over the binary (true: Galactic scale ≫ 30 kau). Wide binaries have q ~ 0.5–1, outside the test-particle domain; the two-body solution gives 1.2–1.3 [D-45], the largest theory uncertainty (≈0.15–0.2 in γ).
- **Gauss-law identity (F3)**: exact for the sphere-averaged radial force in QUMOND. It is not an orbit solution, and it does not hold for AQUAL (≈1% different in the EF limit [D-04]).
- **Newtonian ṽ templates**: valid only with the correct f(e) per zone, noise model, mass scale, and the triple κ distribution; each is a separation-dependent input (K5–K8).
- **Inner-binary model (F16)**: crude (circular inner orbits, sinc averaging, threshold detection proxies, assumed σ_logP); valid to perhaps ±50% on the biases.
- **Semiclassical gravity, energy conditions, quantum inequalities**: outside the question (weak field, F17).

## What would change my mind
- A published, noise-matched Chae-type pipeline that still returns ≥ 1.3 in the scoring bin on a bright (G < 15) or σ_ṽ < 0.1 subsample would strain B and raise D.
- A measured rise of the hidden-companion fraction with separation (e.g. DR4 nss_acceleration_astro or epoch-RV multiplicity at 1 kau vs 10 kau) of ≳ 0.15 would move C from strained to surviving as the dominant cause.
- A nonzero EF-alignment contrast in DR4 (≳ 3σ, ≈ +0.1) would make D or E the leading candidate, whatever the per-family medians say. A null contrast at σ ≈ 0.03 would strain D further.
- A full two-body QUMOND or AQUAL solution with the Galactic EF giving ≲ 1.15 at 1e-10 m s^-2 would move the simple-ν prediction into E's band and largely dissolve the "MOND excluded" side of the dispute.
- An injection-recovery (the mechanist's) showing that a truncated or mixture estimator loses ≥ 0.2 of an injected 1.4 *with realistic noise and triples* would contradict F7 and revive D.
- The observed fraction above ṽ = 2 per bin (a dossier gap): a tail well below my model's ~10% at 10 kau would weaken F22 and the in-window contamination argument.
