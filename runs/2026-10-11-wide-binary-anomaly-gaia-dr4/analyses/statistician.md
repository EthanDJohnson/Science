# Analysis: statistician (is the signal real?)
status: final

## Method applied
Reconstruct each published DR3 gamma as numbers (one value per independent dataset), compute local/global significance, tensions within and between estimator families (grouped chi2, with and without shared-data correlation), Bayes-factor bounds and priors, then build per-family DR4 predictive distributions conditional on Newton and on MOND.

## Findings

1. **Local significance shrinks to ~3σ once systematics are included.** Chae 2024b's headline γ = 1.37 [D-22] is 4.1σ above Newton on stat only (γ units) / 4.5σ (Γ units), but 2.9σ (γ) / 3.2σ (Γ = 0.068, σ_down = 0.021) with the quoted systematic added in quadrature. The 5.8σ and 9.2σ in [D-22]/[D-84] are χ² over several bins with stat errors and a self-calibrated f_multi; they are not the single-bin deviation. Yoon+2025 2.9σ [D-23], Hernandez+2024 2.5σ [D-24], Chae 2024a pure 2.6σ [D-21], Chae 2025 MOND regime 2.1σ (4.25σ with transition bins) [D-25]. Chae 2023's 7.2σ uses the top of a 1.33–1.43 variant range with stat-only error [D-20]. [calc: runs/2026-10-11-wide-binary-anomaly-gaia-dr4/calc/lens-statistician_dr3_significance.py]
2. **Look-elsewhere.** No DR3 analysis was blinded [D-18]; the Chae papers report several samples × g_N bins × estimator variants. For N_trials = 5/10/30 the stat+sys 3.21σ becomes 2.72/2.48/2.06σ global (two-sided Šidák). Chae+2026's 4.29σ becomes 3.9/3.75/3.5σ, and it is driven by two systems above Newtonian escape speed; without them it is 1.8–1.9σ [D-26]. [calc: same]
3. **Within-family agreement, between-family disagreement.** grouped_chi2 (symmetric errors = mean of the two sides): F1 (Chae 2024b, 2024a, Yoon+25) mean 1.364 ± 0.084, χ²_within = 0.54/2; F2 (Boufourou E2 global) 1.045 ± 0.021; F3 (Banik+24) 0.990 ± 0.025; F4 (Chae 2025, Chae+26 geometric, C3PO) 1.478 ± 0.139, χ² = 1.47/2. Total within 2.0/4 (p = 0.73), between 28.7/3 (p = 2.5e-6, 4.7σ). Swapping Saad & Ting's 1.00 for the 36 pairs: between 19.6/3 (3.7σ). Using only low-g bins (F2 deep 1.11 ± 0.07, no F3): between 8.5/2 (2.45σ). The disagreement is between estimator families, not within them; a pooled weighted mean (1.04, S = 2.1) would hide this. [calc: same]
4. **Correlation matters and cuts both ways.** F1 members share the El-Badry parent catalogue and Chae's pipeline [D-11]. GLS with ρ = 0/0.5/0.8 gives F1 = 1.364 ± 0.084 / 1.330 ± 0.111 / 1.266 ± 0.108, i.e. 4.3/3.0/2.5σ from Newton and 3.7/2.5/2.0σ tension with F2. For the SAME-data comparison (Chae 2024b median 1.37 vs Boufourou E2 1.045 on overlapping catalogues), correlation increases the estimator-difference significance: 3.3/3.8/4.1σ for ρ = 0/0.5/0.8. So the estimator gap on shared data is ≳3σ and is a real property of the estimators, not noise. [calc: same]
5. **Same data, two models (family 4).** Chae+26 1.60 vs Saad & Ting 1.00 on the same 36 pairs [D-26, D-63] is a 0.60 difference that no statistical error explains (same data → ρ ≈ 1 → the difference is pure model choice). The stat "tension" computed as if independent, 2.2σ, understates this; it is a modelling systematic of ~±0.3 on γ for this family. [calc: same]
6. **Null-side power at low g.** Boufourou's deep bin 1.11 ± 0.07 [D-31] sits 3.7σ below γ = 1.37 and 1.6σ above Newton: the null side's low-g measurement is NOT powerful enough to exclude a modest boost (γ ≈ 1.1–1.2), only the full MOND value. Banik+24's 16σ is a global 2–30 kau template fit [D-28], not a low-g bin. C3PO (s > 3000 au) 1.17 +0.37 −0.23 is 0.5σ from 1.37 and 0.7σ from 1 [D-27]: uninformative. Chae & Yoon's "underpowered" charge [D-64] is right for Cookson (61 pairs) and C3PO, wrong for Banik+24 and Boufourou's global fits. [calc: same]
7. **Bayes-factor bounds.** Chae 2024b stat+sys (z = 3.21, two-sided p = 1.3e-3): max odds 42:1 against a fluctuation; prior needed for P > 0.5 = 0.023. After LEE (N = 10) or with ρ = 0.8 among F1 samples: max odds 6.4:1, prior needed 0.13–0.14. Chae+26 without its two dominant systems: 2.1:1. These bound "real vs fluctuation" only; they do not rank MOND vs a systematic (see candidates). [calc: same]
8. **Settling it.** A 5σ test of Newton (1.00) vs MOND (1.33–1.42 in the scoring bin [D-44, D-43]) needs total σ(γ) ≤ 0.066–0.084. The full-median family has σ_sys ≈ 0.125 (Chae 2024b's own, symmetrised): its z ceiling is 2.96σ no matter how much data, so more DR4 data cannot make F1 decisive unless the systematic falls below ~0.06 (then k ≈ 4.8× the data). Family 4 needs ~260 pairs with σ(γ) ~ 0.2/√k at fixed model, but the deprojection-model spread (~0.3) puts its ceiling below 5σ: k = ∞. [calc: same]

9. **Own injection-recovery (run calculators, DR4 noise, scoring bin log g_N ∈ [−10.5, −9.5), ~9,500 pairs in bin per catalogue, single seeds, jitter ≈ ±0.02 from repeat seeds).** Newtonian truth, f_triple = 0.1/0.2/0.3: uncalibrated full median 1.06 / 1.11–1.15 / 1.23 (reproduces Boufourou's 1.08–1.13 at 0.2–0.3 [D-50]); **Chae-style median with f_multi calibrated at log g_N ∈ [−8.5, −7.0)** 0.97 / 0.99–1.03 / 1.06; empirical-ratio calibration 1.00 / 1.01–1.05 / 1.07; tail-truncated threshold ṽ < √2: 1.05 / 1.12 / 1.15 (DR3 noise 1.06), i.e. NOT unbiased with a generic template, matching Boufourou's own unverified note that E2 gave 1.18 without zone-matched templates [D-50]; trimmed mean 1.07 / 1.17–1.18 / 1.29; mixture 1.02 / 1.01–1.04 / 1.01; simplified projected-only Bayes 1.11 / 1.17–1.19 / 1.24. Sensitivities at f_t = 0.2: thermal truth (template α = 1.25) median 1.18, calibrated median 1.06, mixture 1.04; 10% mass underestimate: median 1.28, mixture 1.15, calibrated median 1.02 (calibration absorbs a separation-independent mass bias into f_multi = 0.30); 1% chance + 1% unbound: mixture 1.02, calibrated median 1.03. MOND truth (bin-median γ_true = 1.28–1.31): calibrated median 0.95–0.97 × truth, mixture 1.01–1.05 ×, threshold 1.05–1.06 ×, uncalibrated median 1.07–1.10 × (1.21 × at f_t = 0.3). [calc: runs/2026-10-11-wide-binary-anomaly-gaia-dr4/calc/lens-statistician_dr4_bias.py]
10. **What finding 9 means.** In a generator where f_triple and the inner-binary period distribution do NOT depend on wide separation, Chae's calibration step removes the triple bias (residual ≤ +0.06). So (i) Boufourou's "full median is biased +0.08–0.13" is reproduced only for an UNcalibrated median; (ii) the Newtonian-regime calibration Chae actually uses does not, in this model, produce γ ≈ 1.37 from Newtonian truth; (iii) for the Chae result to be an artefact under Newton, the artefact must be something the calibration cannot carry over: an f_multi or inner-period distribution that changes with s, a mass bias that differs between bins, or contamination growing with s. Brief premise 2 is therefore the load-bearing link, and no source tests it [D-86; dossier §6 gaps]. Equally, the robust families' Newton-consistent DR3 results are not explained by suppression of a real boost in this model: mixture recovers MOND to within 5%. Under MOND, something not in the generator must suppress the signal in F2/F3. Both camps need an unmodelled effect; the generator cannot choose between them. [calc: same]

11. **Gap decomposition on the same catalogue.** On Chae's own catalogue Boufourou gets robust 1.045 and an implied full-median 1.12–1.20 [D-31, D-90]; Chae 2024b gets 1.37 [D-22]. So of the ≈ 0.33 gap, ≈ 0.07–0.15 is "full median vs robust" (Boufourou's bias), and ≈ 0.17–0.25 is pipeline-specific (calibration, templates, cuts) on the same stars, not sample. Sample choice is not the main driver; but small-sample results are cut-fragile: Cookson's Newton preference reverses to 12× MOND at d < 150 pc without the ipd cut [D-29, D-62].
12. **DR4 conditional predictions (finding for the forecast).** Combining (truth) + (persistent family offset anchored on its DR3 value) + σ_stat scaled by k = N_DR4/N_DR3 ∈ [2, 5] log-uniform + analyst spread gives the matrix in Lens-specific outputs (table 3). Main result: **DR4 per-family values are weakly diagnostic.** The likelihood ratio p(x|MOND)/p(x|Newton) at each conditional median is 0.5–1.9 for F1, 0.4–2.6 for F2, 0.13–25 for F3 and ≈ 1 for F4. Only the mixture family's DR4 value discriminates, and only because its Newtonian conditional is tight. The modal DR4 outcome under either hypothesis is that both camps reproduce their DR3 numbers with smaller error bars (premise 11 answered: no, more data alone does not break the estimator dependence). [calc: runs/2026-10-11-wide-binary-anomaly-gaia-dr4/calc/lens-statistician_dr4_predictive.py]
13. **Base rate (prior, labelled).** Reference class: disagreements between precision methods/estimators on the same sky, where one side's result implies new physics (proton radius 2010–2019: resolved largely by the newer measurements agreeing with the muonic value, i.e. a systematic in older methods; Pioneer anomaly: thermal systematic; Hubble tension: unresolved after a decade; past world-average shifts typically several σ of the quoted errors). In that class, the side claiming a violation of a well-tested law rarely turns out right; I take P(the boost is a property of gravity) ≈ 0.05–0.15 a priori from that class, weighed down further by galaxy-scale MOND evidence entering only as a weak prior per the brief. With the strongest single-bin evidence (42:1 max), posterior ≤ 0.69–0.88; with LEE or correlation (6.4:1), ≤ 0.25–0.53. These are upper bounds against a fluctuation only; they are not evidence for MOND over a systematic. [calc: lens-statistician_dr3_significance.py, section 5]

### Failure modes that apply

- **Multiple comparisons / analysis flexibility:** applies to both camps (samples × bins × estimators × cuts; no blinding [D-18]). Chae 2023's headline is the top of a variant range; Cookson's preference flips with one cut.
- **Optional stopping:** not identifiable from the dossier; papers kept appearing until each camp's estimator was published, which is a mild form.
- **Selection/publication bias:** each camp publishes its own estimator's output; the partisan structure (dossier §7, partisanship flags) means few cross-checks.
- **Winner's curse / regression to the mean:** the Chae-camp low-g central values decline 1.43 (2023) → 1.37 (2024b) → 1.32 (Yoon+25), and Chae+26 falls from 1.60 to 1.21–1.48 without two systems; consistent with first reports overstating.
- **Unmodelled systematics:** the decisive one. Separation-dependent f_multi/inner periods, mass bias by bin, eccentricity template (thermal vs α = 1.3 changes the median by +0.18 [calc]), perspective (moved Boufourou's deep bin by +0.14).
- **Signal fading as data accumulate:** not yet testable (all DR3); DR4 is the first test, but see finding 12.

### What would settle it
- 5σ between Newton and MOND needs total σ(γ) ≤ 0.066–0.084 (Δ = 0.33–0.42) [calc]. All families reach that statistically on DR4; none reaches it on systematics: F1 ceiling 2.96σ at its quoted sys; F4 ceiling below 5σ with the ±0.3 deprojection spread (k = ∞).
- **Real vs likeliest artefact:** a direct measurement of the hidden-companion fraction and inner-period distribution as a function of wide separation, from DR4 nss_acceleration_astro/epoch astrometry and RV monitoring [D-12, D-38]. If f_multi(s) is flat, Chae's calibration is valid in my model and the F2/F3 null results need explaining; if it rises with s by the amount needed (≈ +0.1 in f_multi from 1 to 10 kau gives ≈ +0.06–0.1 in γ in my grid), F1's signal is an artefact.
- **Between the leading explanations:** pre-registered, blind runs of all four estimator families by one team on one DR4 sample (Boufourou-type protocol [D-91], extended to F1 with Chae's calibration), with injection-recovery on catalogues that include s-dependent multiplicity. And ≥ 260 pairs with ≲ 0.1 km/s relative RVs analysed with both deprojection priors.

- A measured f_multi(s) that is flat from 0.2 to 30 kau (DR4 acceleration solutions, RV surveys) would weaken A sharply. In my generator, Chae's calibration then works, and the F2/F3 null results would need a suppression mechanism, which strengthens B.
