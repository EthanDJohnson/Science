# Analysis: dialectician (resolve real contradictions)
status: final

## Method applied
I took the three sharpest contradictions in the dossier, each a case of the same data giving incompatible γ: (C1) one catalogue gives a full-median γ ≈ 1.37 against a robust γ ≈ 1.045; (C2) one set of 36 pairs gives 1.60 against 1.00; (C3) one sample's cuts either remove a fake signal or bias the result toward Newton. For each I state thesis and antithesis with their evidence, and look for the regime or definition under which both hold. Where none exists I say which side must yield. I tested each synthesis with the run's generator (`wide_binary_population.py`, pseudo-Newtonian G_eff, which is a labelled approximation), and each yields a prediction that neither camp made. Units: SI; g_N in m s⁻²; s in au; M in Msun; γ dimensionless.

## Findings
F1. **Scale versus shape (C1 synthesis, calc).** In the generator, a real boost rescales every quantile of ṽ by √γ. The quantile profile γ(p) = (Q_p,obs/Q_p,Newt)² is therefore flat, with S ≡ γ(p = 0.8)/γ(p = 0.3) = 0.995–1.015, both for constant γ = 1.4 and for the chae2024 MOND preset with no triples (DR3 and DR4 noise). Hidden triples make γ(p) rise with p: S = 1.044 / 1.079 / 1.206 at f_triple = 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.3 (DR3, low bin log10 g_N ∈ [−11, −9.5]). An eccentricity mismatch makes it fall: S = 0.835 for a true uniform f(e) against the HTZ template, and 0.979 for thermal. Gravity, triples and f(e) therefore leave three different signatures in one distribution. [calc: runs/2026-10-11-wide-binary-anomaly-gaia-dr4/calc/lens-dialectician_shape.py]

F2. **Triples are not only a tail effect.** At f_triple = 0.2 (DR3) even the 30th percentile shifts: γ(0.3) = 1.133. The cause is that a component with an unresolved companion gets too low a light-based mass (the generator uses M_est = L^(1/3.5)), and that is a pure rescaling. A shape test cannot tell this part from gravity, and a tail-cutting estimator does not remove it. Calibrating at high g_N removes most of it: the same catalogue gives γ(0.3) = 1.096 in log10 g_N ∈ [−8.5, −7.5]. [calc: lens-dialectician_shape.py]

F3. **Triple leverage grows with separation, so a constant-f_multi calibration under-corrects the full median.** For Newton plus triples, the full median in the low and high bins (DR3) is 1.077 vs 1.034 at f = 0.1, 1.166 vs 1.098 at f = 0.2, and 1.251 vs 1.143 at f = 0.3. The low-bin excess (γ − 1) is 1.7–2.3 times the high-bin excess. The calibrated ratio γ_med(low)/γ_med(high) is 1.042 / 1.062 / 1.094, a residual of about +0.31 per unit triple fraction (f ≥ 0.2). The same calibrated ratio taken at p = 0.3 is 1.034 and 1.040 for Newton (f = 0.2, 0.3) and 1.253 for MOND chae2024 + f = 0.2, whose bin truth is about 1.26. A lower-quantile estimator calibrated at high g therefore recovers the truth to within about 0.04 in both worlds. [calc: lens-dialectician_shape.py; lens-dialectician_arith.py]

F4. **Companion-veto ladder (C3 synthesis, calc, DR4 noise, 66-month baseline).** Vetoing on photocentre wobble, a proxy for RUWE or the acceleration solutions, lowers the median γ in both worlds:
- Newton, f = 0.2: from 1.135 to 1.021 (veto at wobble > 0.01 mas).
- MOND chae2024, f = 0.2: from 1.396 to 1.278.
- Constant γ = 1.4, f = 0.2: from 1.556 to 1.426.

Extrapolating linearly in the surviving triple fraction to zero gives 1.004 (Newton f = 0.2), 1.022 (Newton f = 0.3), 1.261 (MOND; bin truth 1.255) and 1.406 (constant 1.4; no-triple bin value 1.364). The slope is 0.65–0.75 per unit triple fraction in both worlds. So "cuts lower γ" is true in both worlds and does not discriminate. The intercept does. [calc: lens-dialectician_shape.py; lens-dialectician_arith.py]

F5. **Splitting the same-catalogue gap (C1).** Chae 2024b's median gives Γ = 0.068, so γ = 1.368 [D-22]. Boufourou's E2 gives 1.045 on Chae's catalogue [D-31], and his implied full-median value on that catalogue is 1.12–1.20 [D-31, D-90]. In ln γ, the estimator change alone accounts for 26–51% of the gap. The remaining 49–74% comes from differences in sample, cuts, template or calibration between Chae's own pipeline and Boufourou's full-median reconstruction. "Estimator artefact" is therefore at most about half of C1. [calc: lens-dialectician_arith.py]

F6. **The F3 surrogate set against Chae's self-calibrated f_multi values.** If the calibrated-median residual is about 0.31 × f, Chae's f_multi values imply residuals of +0.03 (f = 0.10, the RUWE < 1.2 sample, D-35), +0.06 (0.18, the 2024b new sample), +0.13 (0.43) and +0.20 (0.65, Chae 2023) [D-35; calc: lens-dialectician_arith.py]. That reaches about 1.13–1.20 for the high-f_multi Chae 2023 samples (published 1.33–1.43, D-20). It reaches only about 1.06 for the 2024b new sample (published 1.37, D-22) and the RUWE < 1.2 samples (Chae & Yoon > 5σ, D-39). The surrogate is a crude stand-in for Chae's own physical triple model, and the mechanist owns the full reproduction. Within the surrogate, the low-f_multi Chae samples are the hard case for the artefact explanation.

F7. **Family 4: the same 36 pairs, 1.60 against 1.00 (C2).** Saad & Ting reproduce Chae's number inside their own framework by swapping only the semi-major-axis parametrisation: 1.00 [0.81, 1.24] becomes 1.56 [1.38, 1.77] [D-17, D-26]. Abstract: "replacing the semi-major axis with a geometric de-projection of the observed projected separation. This test yields γ = 1.56 +0.21 −0.18, closely matching the result of Chae et al. (2026)" [new: Saad & Ting 2026, arXiv:2603.11015 abstract, full-text via fetch_text, "replacing the semi-major axis with a geometric de-projection of the observed projected separation. This test yields $\gamma = 1.56^{+0.21}_{-0.18}$, closely matching the result of Chae et al. (2026)"]. Chae's two dominant systems are above the Newtonian escape speed [D-26]. Since r ≥ s for every orientation, a system with v3D² > 2GM/s is unbound under Newton whatever deprojection is used. A likelihood that requires every system to be bound can absorb such a system only through γ > 1 (or a mass error). Converting Chae et al.'s own numbers: without the two systems, Γ = 0.042 / 0.057 / 0.085 become γ = 1.21 / 1.30 / 1.48 for g_N < 10^−9.1 / 10^−9.5 / 10^−10.1. With them, Γ = 0.102 / 0.132 give 1.60 / 1.84. [calc: lens-dialectician_massplit.py part (a)]

F8. **The same calibrate-and-transfer structure appears in family 4.** Chae 2025's Bayesian 3D method returns γ = 1.00 ± 0.05 at high acceleration (N = 125) and 1.48 +0.33 −0.23 in the MOND regime (N = 35) [D-25]. So the geometric deprojection passes its Newtonian check at high g, as the full median does (Chae 2024b calibrates at about 1e-8 m s⁻², D-15). The population properties a deprojection prior encodes change between the calibration and test regimes:
- the f(e) index rises from α ≈ 0.1 at about 50 au to 1.2–1.3 above 1 kau [D-41];
- Saad & Ting's a-prior has a break at 5 kau [D-63];
- hidden-companion leverage relative to v_c grows with s [F3];
- RV-variable companions occur in 46% of archival pairs at s > 13 kau, and 3 of the 12 RV-clean pairs there are not bound [D-38].

C1 and C2 are therefore one contradiction at different sample sizes: calibrate at high g and transfer, or model the low-g population directly.

F9. **A mass split at fixed separation is only a weak discriminant (calc).** MOND's boost follows g_N, so at fixed s low-mass pairs should be boosted more. In the generator, the median-γ difference between the lowest and highest M_est terciles at s = 2–3.5 kau and 3.5–6 kau is +0.048 and +0.029 for MOND chae2024 with no triples, and +0.026 and +0.022 with f = 0.2. For Newton it is −0.015 and −0.029 (f = 0.2), and +0.013 and −0.025 (f = 0.3). The between-world difference is about 0.05, at roughly the per-cell noise (about 0.03–0.05 at N = 2e5 total). The point estimates at bin centres would give +0.095 (s ≈ 2.6 kau), but mixing between a and s dilutes this by half. On DR4 this needs at least ~1e5 pairs at 2–6 kau to reach about 3σ. It is a secondary test, not a decisive one. [calc: lens-dialectician_massplit.py]

F10. **The cut dispute already fits the Newton-world ladder.** Cookson's d < 150 pc sample flips from MOND preferred by 12× (no ipd cut) to Newton preferred by 88× (ipd_frac_multi_peak = 0), and the d < 130 pc ipd cut removes about 14% of pairs (1421 → 1216) [D-29, D-62]. In the MOND + triples ladder (F4), removing companions moves γ toward the MOND value (1.26), never toward 1. A post-cut preference for Newton over MOND is what the Newton intercept predicts. Chae & Yoon's point stands: cuts change f_multi, so the calibration must be redone after cutting [D-61, D-62]. But that point applies equally in both worlds and does not turn a Newton intercept into 1.4. Caveat: about 61 MOND-regime pairs [D-64], so this is suggestive, not decisive.

### Synthesis: the three contradictions are one
All three are the same dispute: **calibrate at high g and transfer to low g** (Chae: f_multi in C1, the deprojection prior in C2, the cuts in C3) against **model the low-g population directly** (the robust and mixture families, Saad & Ting, Cookson). Transfer is valid only if three population properties do not depend on separation, and each of them does:
1. f(e) [D-41];
2. hidden-companion velocity and mass leverage relative to v_c(s) [F3; D-40];
3. the unbound and escaper fraction at large s [D-38, F7].

All three push the transferred estimate upward at low g. The f(e) term can push either way (F1: a too-eccentric template biases low, a too-circular one high [D-49]). Two consequences follow:
- The transfer bias should appear even inside the Newtonian regime, as a drift of calibrated γ with s at fixed g_N.
- The shape statistic S separates the triple term (S > 1) from the f(e) term (S < 1) and from gravity (S ≈ 1).

## What would change my mind
- A flat quantile profile (S within about 0.02 of 1) at γ ≳ 1.25 in Chae's low-g bins on DR3 or DR4 data would move me from A/C to B.
- A DR4 veto-ladder intercept above about 1.15 across camps' samples would do the same.
- A physical triple model (Chae's or the mechanist's) under which the calibrated full-median residual stays below about 0.05 at f_multi ≈ 0.1–0.2, together with a published 1.3–1.4 on such a sample, would mean the remainder of the C1 gap is not artefact. That would favour B or a sample effect.
- If both escaper systems prove to be clean single stars with RV-confirmed bound 3D orbits needing γ > 1, C2's verdict reverses.
- If the robust families fail injection recovery for an anisotropic, EFE-shaped boost (not the scalar-G_eff one used here), the "boost is a rescaling" premise of F1 fails. Real QUMOND with the EFE is anisotropic at about 15% [D-02], which would add some shape. The size of that is untested here.

## Assumptions I relied on
- The pseudo-Newtonian "Kepler with G_eff = γ(g_N)G" generator (labelled approximation [D-05]). In it a boost is an exact rescaling of ṽ at fixed orbit; anisotropic EFE dynamics are not modelled.
- The generator's triple model: a_in log-uniform in 0.1–300 au, the stability cut, q_in uniform, L ∝ m^3.5 light-based masses, wobble as a RUWE proxy. Absolute bias sizes (for example 1.17 median at f = 0.2) depend on it. The signs and the scale-versus-shape pattern are less model-dependent. Boufourou's lower 1.08–1.13 [D-50] and the dossier's upper bound of 1.145–1.61 [D-40] bracket ours.
- Calibration is idealised: a clean Newtonian catalogue from the same generator and noise model. Real calibration uses the data's own high-g pairs (Chae) or analytic templates.
- One seed per case. Differences below about 0.03 in γ are not significant.
- The dossier's values and mappings: Chae 2024b as family 1, Boufourou E2/E3 as families 2/3, the 36-pair set as one dataset, and "the same catalogue" for D-22 against D-31 taken as Boufourou describes it [D-11, D-31].
