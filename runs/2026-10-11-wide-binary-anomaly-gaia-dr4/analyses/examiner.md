# Analysis: examiner (Socrates: examine the question before answering it)
status: final

## Method applied
Test whether the question is well posed: define its loaded terms ("real", "artefact", "γ in a bin", "estimator family", "what DR4 will show"), test each hidden premise (support / falsifier / what the question becomes if false), look for a better-posed reframe, and write the hardest checkable questions any answer must survive. Small calculations with the run's calculators check premises that the dossier leaves numerically open.

## Findings

1. **"The MOND prediction in the scoring bin" is not one number; it spans a factor ~1.3 in (γ−1).** At g_N = 1e-10 m s^-2 the parametrised boost is 1.333 (Chae 2024 eq. 22 form), 1.375 (BZ18 simple), 1.061 (BZ18 standard / n = 2) and 1.704 (isolated, no EFE); the EF-dominated orientation range for the simple function is 1.354 (perpendicular) to 1.560 (parallel) around η = 1.423 [calc: runs/2026-10-11-wide-binary-anomaly-gaia-dr4/calc/lens-examiner_premises.py]. Agrees with [D-02], [D-43], [D-44]. Pflamm-Altenburg's full two-body QUMOND gives only 1.2–1.3 in the transition regime [D-45]. So "Chae's γ ≈ 1.4 is what MOND predicts" holds only for the simple-ν family with a one-body EFE approximation; "the null side excludes MOND" excludes that template, not MOND generically (standard-ν γ ≈ 1.06 is inside the null side's own error bars). Consequence: the brief's α_grav → γ conversion rule inherits the template choice, and a result of γ ≈ 1.2 would be read as "MOND-consistent" under Pflamm-Altenburg and "MOND-excluded" under BZ18 simple.
2. **The scoring bin is defined up to a 0.16 dex convention.** Chae's g_N proxy uses r ≈ 1.2 s; using r = s shifts every g_N by log10(1.44) = 0.158 dex. For M = 1.5 Msun, g_N = 1e-10 m s^-2 falls at s = 7,860 au (r = 1.2 s) or 9,432 au (r = s) [calc: same]. The MOND boost moves only 1.314–1.346 (chae2024) across ±0.158 dex, so the bin convention is a small effect on γ_MOND (±0.02) but it changes which pairs are in the bin, which matters for estimator comparisons [D-03].
3. **γ in a velocity-distribution test is a product of degenerate factors, not a measurement of G.** For any ṽ-based estimator, γ_app ≈ γ_true × (M_true/M_est) × E(f(e) template) × T(f_multi, inner-period distribution) × N(noise model). Each factor is individually of order the anomaly: (i) mass: a 27% uniform under-estimate of M_tot would fake γ = 1.37 (1/1.37 = 0.730), and the Hernandez & Kroupa 2.0 vs 1.56 Msun normalisation is a factor 1.282 [D-33][calc]; (ii) eccentricity: template α = 2 with true α = 1.3 gives 1.107; template α = 1.3 with true α = 2 gives 0.903 [calc, from D-48 medians]; (iii) triples: see finding 5. So "real vs artefact" can only be answered *differentially*, by whether the low-g_N bin deviates from a Newtonian control of the same sample, and only if every factor is the same in both bins.
4. **Galactic tides are not a candidate.** In-plane tide ~2.6e-15 m s^-2 and vertical tide ~8.5e-15 m s^-2 at 10 kau, against g_N(1.5 Msun, 10 kau) = 8.9e-11 m s^-2 (ratio ~1e-4) [calc: same; Oort constants and ρ0 = 0.1 Msun pc^-3 are approximate literature values I supplied, not dossier items]. The "real but not a gravity-law effect" option therefore has to come from unbound/flyby pairs or moving-group contamination, not from tides.
5. **The f_multi transfer (premise 2) is the hinge, and it is not scale-free.** With the run's generator (DR3 noise, HTZ eccentricities, Newton), the same f_triple = 0.2 raises the median ṽ by γ_app = 1.085 at 300–700 au but 1.195 at 5–30 kau, because inner-orbit reflex speeds are fixed while v_c falls as s^-1/2 [calc: same]. In the 5–30 kau zone dγ_app/df ≈ +1.0 per unit f_triple (f = 0.1: 1.094; 0.3: 1.307; 0.45: 1.528). If f is calibrated at 0.2 but the true wide-zone value is 0.45, a full-median test returns 1.28; at 0.6 it returns 1.51; if the true wide value is 0.0 it returns 0.84 [calc: same]. So the full-median γ is only as good as the *separation dependence* of f_multi and of the inner-period distribution, known to ±0.05 in f for ±0.05 in γ. Chae's own self-calibrated f_multi spans 0.10–0.65 across samples and cuts [D-35], i.e. the calibration is selection-dependent, which is premise 2's weak point. For comparison, MOND (chae2024 form) with f = 0.2 in the same zone gives γ_app = 1.243 against the Newton f = 0.2 template [calc: same] (diluted below the 1.33–1.37 injected value by the transition-regime pairs and noise).
6. **Separation-dependent nuisance effects are all of the same size as the disputed residual.** Newtonian, no triples, the median ṽ itself is 0.5304 at 300–700 au and 0.5585 at 5–30 kau (ratio² = 1.109), from DR3 proper-motion noise growing relative to v_c and the HTZ α(s) drift [calc: same]. A template that is not noise- and zone-matched therefore carries ~10% in γ by itself, the size of Boufourou's claimed full-median bias [D-50] and of Boufourou's own E2 result without zone-matched templates (1.18, [D-50] unverified).
7. **Truncation cuts do not, by themselves, remove a MOND signal.** The El-Badry 5 Msun proper-motion bound (ṽ ≤ √(5/M_est), without the 2σ allowance) removes 0.01% (Newton) and 0.02% (MOND chae2024) of bound pairs at 5–30 kau; a ṽ ≤ √2 cut removes 0.68% (Newton) and 2.05% (MOND) [calc: same; generator median M_est = 0.93 Msun, lighter than Chae's samples]. So premise 4's worry (robust estimators cut the MOND tail) fails on the cut alone: the MOND information is in the core shift (median 0.5583 → 0.6307, γ_app ≈ 1.276) which both truncated and full estimators see. The disagreement must come from how the templates model the *core* contribution of triples, not from cutting the tail.

8. **No source in the dossier or my searches tests whether the velocity-relevant f_multi depends on outer separation** [D-86] (and the dossier's section 6 gap list: "Whether f_multi depends on separation (premise 2) is untested by any source"). The nearest empirical hint points in the direction that would inflate a high-acceleration-calibrated full-median γ: [new: Tokovinin & Smekhov 2002 A&A (abstract, via WebSearch), search-summary, SUMMARY "spectroscopic sub-systems are more frequent among components of wide visual binaries (~20%) and wide tertiaries (~30%) than among components of close visual binaries (11–18%)"]. Dynamical stability also forbids inner binaries with a_in ≳ 0.3 a_out(1−e_out) at small outer separations, which mechanically lowers the inner-binary fraction in a 300–700 au calibration zone (the run's generator imposes this, f_stab = 0.3). Both make premise 2 (scale-free f_multi) doubtful in the direction of a spurious positive γ; neither quantifies it. A search for a Gaia-era measurement of inner-subsystem fraction vs outer separation returned nothing direct [new: WebSearch, search-summary, SUMMARY "did not find a source that shows the frequency of inner subsystems rising with outer separation"].

9. **DR4 roughly halves, but does not close, the hidden-companion window that drives finding 5.** Order-of-magnitude model (inner pair 1.2 Msun, photocentre factor 0.27, 5σ astrometric-acceleration detection with σ_g = √60 σ_μ/T, resolution 0.5 arcsec, log-uniform a_in): velocity-relevant inner binaries (photocentre speed ≥ v_c(s), P ≥ baseline) span a_in ≈ 2–3 au to 175 au (s = 3 kau), 583 au (10 kau), 1,166 au (20 kau). The undetectable part of that log-window at s = 10 kau is 0.17–0.48 in DR3 and 0.00–0.34 in DR4, depending on G (13–17) and d (100–200 pc); e.g. d = 200 pc, G ≈ 13: 0.35 → 0.17 [calc: runs/2026-10-11-wide-binary-anomaly-gaia-dr4/calc/lens-examiner_dr4window.py]. Caveats: contrast-limited resolution is ignored (faint companions inside 1–2 arcsec are often not resolved), and the real DR4 NSS acceleration thresholds are not public [D-12], [D-85]. Implication with finding 5 (dγ/df ≈ 1): if the hidden velocity-relevant f in the wide zone falls by ~half under DR4 vetting, a full-median γ driven by residual triples should fall by ~0.05–0.1 on the *same pair list*; a real boost should not move. That is a premise-11 test that DR4 can run and DR3 cannot.
10. **Low-g_N bins are also low-mass bins.** g_N ∝ M_tot/s², so at a given s the lowest-acceleration bin is enriched in low-mass (M-dwarf) pairs, where the mass–luminosity relation, metallicity sensitivity and the hidden-companion light ratio differ [D-03], [D-33], [D-65]. A mass bias of 10% that is present only in the low-g_N bin gives γ_app = 1.11 or 0.91 [calc: lens-examiner_premises.py, section C]. Premise 7 ("mass bias is common to all bins") is therefore not safe; the check is γ as a function of M_tot at fixed g_N.
11. **The DR3 "data" are fewer independent measurements than the paper count suggests.** Projected-velocity analyses share the Gaia DR3 astrometry and mostly the El-Badry parent catalogue [D-11]; the 3D-velocity anchors are one 36-pair dataset analysed twice (γ = 1.60 vs 1.00 by deprojection choice alone) [D-17], [D-26], [D-63], plus Chae 2025's 312 pairs [D-25] and Saad & Ting's 57 C3PO pairs [D-27]. The same 81,088-pair catalogue gives 1.37 (Chae, full-median, own f_multi) and 1.045–1.05 (Boufourou, robust) with an implied full-median 1.12–1.20 [D-22], [D-31], [D-90]. So premise 1 ("same data") is half true: within the Chae catalogue the spread is estimator plus triple model, while across groups (Cookson's ipd and 130 pc cut flip a 12× MOND preference into a 548–1501× Newton preference [D-29], [D-62]) sample cuts matter too.

### C. Reframes

- **R1 (premise-is-wrong reframe, preferred): measure the differential, with nuisances fixed externally.** Replace "what is γ in the low-g bin" with "does γ_app(low g_N)/γ_app(Newtonian control) on one pair list differ from 1 when f_multi(s), masses and f(e) are fixed from non-velocity data (RUWE/NSS/RV/resolved companions), not tuned so the control passes?" This is better posed because each multiplicative nuisance cancels unless it varies with s or M, and those variations are separately measurable. It has a clearer DR4 answer: run it on DR4 with and without NSS-acceleration and RV-variability vetting.
- **R2: ask what f_multi(s) would be needed.** Given dγ/df ≈ 1 at 5–30 kau (finding 5), Chae's 1.37 requires a velocity-relevant f in the wide zone ≈ 0.2–0.3 higher than the calibration zone under Newton. This turns the dispute into a measurable astrophysics question (inner-subsystem fraction vs outer separation), which RV surveys (Pasquini 46% at s > 13 kau [D-38]; Saglia 27% [D-37]) already address in small samples.
- **R3: separate "γ > 1" from "MOND".** With template spread 1.06–1.42 (finding 1), the scorable DR4 claim is γ in the bin, and whether it matches MOND is a secondary, template-relative judgement.

### D. Hardest questions any answer must survive (specific, checkable)

1. **f_multi(s):** what is the directly measured (RV-variable + NSS + resolved) fraction of velocity-relevant inner companions at 0.3–0.7 kau versus 5–30 kau in the Chae 2024b sample? An answer claiming "artefact" needs it to rise by ≳0.2; an answer claiming "real" needs it flat within ±0.05 (dγ/df ≈ 1).
2. **Same catalogue, two triple models:** on Chae's 81,088-pair catalogue, does a full median with Boufourou's triple model give 1.12–1.20 and with Chae's MC triple model 1.37? If both hold, the disagreement is the triple model, not the estimator family.
3. **Control with zero free parameters:** does Chae's Newtonian control zone pass when f_multi is fixed from non-velocity data instead of fitted to make it pass? (Boufourou reports +1.4% with his model [D-61].)
4. **Template stated:** what γ_MOND,paper does each paper assume in the scoring bin? If ≤ 1.1 (standard ν) or 1.2–1.3 (two-body [D-45]), are the 16σ MOND exclusions still > 5σ?
5. **Vetting response on a fixed list:** in DR4, does removing NSS-acceleration-flagged, RV-variable and resolved-companion pairs lower the low-g_N full-median γ by more than it lowers the control-zone γ? Artefact predicts a drop of ~0.05–0.1 (finding 9); a real boost predicts no differential drop.
6. **Cut flip:** does Cookson's 12× MOND preference at d < 150 pc without the ipd cut survive DR4 NSS vetting? If it disappears, the ipd cut was removing triples; if it persists, the cut was removing signal.
7. **Mass dependence:** at fixed g_N, is γ independent of M_tot (M dwarfs vs FGK)? A real boost depends on g_N only; a mass-luminosity or companion-light artefact depends on M.
8. **The two escape-speed systems:** do RV monitoring and DR4 epoch astrometry of HD 189739/HD 189760 and TYC 259-236-1/TYC 259-906-1 show inner companions? Chae et al. 2026's family-4 result without them is Γ = 0.042–0.085 [D-26].
9. **Eccentricity freedom:** in Banik-type mixture fits, how much does fixing the eccentricity exponent to Hwang's 1.3 (instead of the fitted ≈2) move α_grav? A shift of ≳0.2 in α_grav would make the 16σ a nuisance-model result.
10. **Independence count:** how many statistically independent DR3 datasets actually bear on the scoring bin? (My count: one shared Gaia projected-velocity sample family, one 36-pair 3D set, Chae 2025's 312 pairs, 57 C3PO pairs.) Any combined credence must not multiply evidence from shared data.

## What would change my mind

- A direct, non-velocity measurement (RV + DR4 NSS + resolved companions) showing the velocity-relevant hidden-companion fraction is flat (±0.05) from ~0.5 kau to 10–30 kau in a Chae-type sample, together with a residual low-g full-median γ ≳ 1.25: this would move weight from A/C to B.
- A full-median analysis on Newtonian mocks with a *correctly specified* f(s) still returning γ ≳ 1.1: that would make the bias intrinsic to the estimator (Boufourou's reading) rather than a mis-transfer (my reading).
- Precision-RV 3D samples (C3PO-type, N ≳ 100 at s > 5 kau) giving γ ≳ 1.3 with independent deprojection priors.
- DR4 vetting on fixed pair lists leaving the low-g γ unchanged while removing ≳ 30% of the hidden window: evidence against A.

## Assumptions I relied on

- The run's generator's triple model (log-uniform a_in 0.1–300 au, stability factor 0.3, photocentre averaging over the DR3 baseline, L ∝ M^3.5) is representative enough for *slopes* (dγ/df, zone contrast); its absolute γ_app values are not those of Chae's or Boufourou's models, and its masses are lighter than the real samples.
- Median-ṽ ratio squared is used as a stand-in for a full-median estimator; real family-1 pipelines bin and calibrate differently.
- The DR4 window calc uses circular inner orbits, a fixed 1.2 Msun inner pair, photocentre factor 0.27 from [D-40], ESA's predicted DR4 errors [D-51], a 5σ acceleration threshold of my choosing and a 0.5 arcsec resolution limit that ignores contrast.
- Oort constants (A ≈ 15.3, B ≈ −11.9 km s^-1 kpc^-1) and ρ0 ≈ 0.1 Msun pc^-3 in the tide estimate are approximate values I supplied; the conclusion (ratio ~1e-4) is insensitive to factors of 2.
- The Tokovinin & Smekhov 2002 numbers are search-summary only and concern spectroscopic subsystems in visual multiples, not Gaia wide binaries; they are a directional hint, not a measurement of f(s).
