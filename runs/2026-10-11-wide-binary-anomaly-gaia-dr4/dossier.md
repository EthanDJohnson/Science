## 1. Established

- [D-01] Banik & Zhao 2018 (MNRAS 480, 2660) model a wide binary in QUMOND as a point mass plus a test particle in a uniform Galactic external field (EF). The EFE enters through g_N = −GM r̂/r² + g_N,ext. Simple interpolating function: ν(y) = 1/2 + √(1/4 + 1/y). QUMOND (Milgrom 2010, MNRAS 403, 886) solves a Poisson equation with source −(1/4πG)∇·[ν(|g_N|/a0) g_N]. (refs: RT-01, RT-15 [abstract-only; BM84/Milgrom-1983 page numbers unverified]) peer-reviewed
- [D-02] In the EF-dominated limit the MOND radial force is anisotropic: g_r = g_N,r ν_ext (1 + K0 sin²θ/2), where θ is the angle between the separation and the EF. The angle-averaged boost is η = ν_ext(1 + K0/3). K0 < 0, so the boost is largest along the EF. For the simple function at g_ext = 1.785 a0 the force boost runs from 1.560 (parallel) to 1.354 (perpendicular), about 15% anisotropy, around the average η = 1.4228. A scalar "γ in a bin" is therefore an orientation and orbit average. (refs: RT-02, RT-19 [calc, reproduces BZ18 Table 3]) peer-reviewed formula; calc-verified numbers
- [D-03] The MOND radius r_M = √(GM/a0) is about 7 kau for 1 Msun and about 11 kau for 2.4 Msun (a0 = 1.2e-10 m s^-2). The boost does not grow much beyond r_M because the EF dominates there. [calc] r_M = 7,031 au (1 Msun), 8,611 au (1.5 Msun), 9,943 au (2.0 Msun). For M_tot = 1.5 Msun, g_N = 10^-9.5 m s^-2 at r = 5,304 au and g_N = 10^-10 m s^-2 at r = 9,432 au (s ≈ r/1.2 gives 4,420 and 7,860 au). The brief's scoring bin therefore corresponds to s ≳ 4–5 kau. (refs: RT-05, RT-21) peer-reviewed + calc
- [D-04] AQUAL (x μ(x) = y) and QUMOND (y ν(y) = x) relate g and g_N in opposite directions. For the simple function in the EF-dominated limit their angle-averaged boosts differ by about 1.2% (1.4056 vs 1.4228). Banik et al. 2024 state the two are almost identical for r ≲ 0.5 r_M [unverified: RC-13 not opened by checker]. (refs: RT-04, RC-13) peer-reviewed
- [D-05] The Chae camp's MOND prediction is a pseudo-Newtonian Kepler model with G → G_eff = γ_g(g_N) G. It is the Chae & Milgrom (2022) fit to circular-orbit AQUAL solutions in the EF (Chae 2024b eq. 22: simple ν, β = 1.1, exponent 1.2 for an EF inclination of 60°, g_ext/a0 = 1.90 ± 0.19, a0 = 1.2e-10 m s^-2). It is Newtonian as s/r_M → 0 and gives G_eff = 1.37 G for s/r_M ≳ 1. Chae calls it "less accurate" for 0.1 ≲ s/r_M ≲ 0.7. Chae states that "numerical solutions for the elliptical motions of binaries under the external field of the Milky Way are not available". The brief's "Keplerian with G_eff = γ(g_N)G" is the same approximation. (refs: RT-06, RT-07 [unverified quotes], RT-09, RT-10, RQ-03) peer-reviewed
- [D-06] Normalised velocity ṽ = v_p / √(G M_tot / s), with v_p the sky-projected relative velocity and s the projected separation (Chae 2024b; Banik & Zhao 2018; Pittordis & Sutherland 2018). M_tot includes any hidden companion. Chae states that f_multi depends on the selection and that f_multi and the inferred gravity are intrinsically degenerate, so Newtonian-regime binaries are needed to fix f_multi [degeneracy quote unverified]. (refs: RT-11) peer-reviewed
- [D-07] Wide-binary eccentricities (Hwang, Ting & Zakamska 2022, MNRAS 512, 3383, Gaia EDR3, d < 200 pc) go from roughly uniform at ~10² au to thermal near 10^2.5–10^2.75 au and superthermal beyond 10³ au: α ≈ 1.2–1.3 for f(e) ∝ e^α at 1–10 kau. At wide separations e < 0.3 is suppressed and e > 0.9 enhanced. The bin values are in [D-41]. (refs: RT-08, RT-16, RQ-14) peer-reviewed
- [D-08] Candidate wide binaries show a "fat tail" in ṽ at about 1.5–6× the circular velocity, which bound pure binaries cannot produce (ṽ ≤ √2). The standard explanation is hidden triples or quadruples: an unresolved or undetected extra star whose closer orbit boosts the apparent relative velocity (Clarke 2020, MNRAS Lett.; Manchanda, Sutherland & Pittordis 2023, preprint). (refs: RT-14, RT-23) peer-reviewed / preprint
- [D-09] For a star with an unresolved companion, the centre of light and the centre of mass move differently, so a single-star astrometric fit degrades. RUWE measures this and can be converted into a photocentre-wobble amplitude (Belokurov et al. 2020) [unverified wording]. Cookson et al. 2026 note that a RUWE cut "will only filter out CBs with periods of months to years". (refs: RT-24, RE-11) peer-reviewed
- [D-10] The El-Badry, Rix & Heintz 2021 catalogue (MNRAS 506, 2269; Gaia eDR3) contains 1.3 (1.1) million binaries within 1 kpc with >90% (>99%) bound probability, at separations from a few au to 1 pc. Chance-alignment probability R is estimated empirically. Its proper-motion cut, Δμ ≤ Δμ_orbit + 2σ_Δμ for a 5 Msun circular orbit (ΔV_orb = 2.1 km/s (s/1000 au)^-1/2), removes "a significant fraction" of unresolved hierarchical triples and truncates the ṽ distribution (Boufourou: ṽ ≤ 2.23/√M_tot, M_tot in Msun) [formula and shift details not confirmed by checker]. Gaia eDR3 formal parallax errors are underestimated by about 30% for isolated well-behaved sources, up to about 80% with a companion within about 4 arcsec, and much more for poor fits. They are reliable at G ≳ 18. (refs: RQ-16, RE-13, RE-14) peer-reviewed
- [D-11] Every DR3 result in this dispute uses the same Gaia (E)DR3 astrometry (34 months), and most use the El-Badry et al. 2021 catalogue as parent. They are therefore not independent measurements of the sky, and the statistician should treat them as correlated. Boufourou's DR3 numbers re-analyse Chae's 2024 catalogue (81,088 pairs, as Boufourou describes it). Chae et al. 2026 and Saad & Ting 2026 analyse the same 36 pairs. (refs: RE-19 [synthesis, unverified], RE-06, RQ-12) synthesis
- [D-12] Gaia DR4 covers 66 months (25 Jul 2014 to 20 Jan 2020, the full nominal mission), against 34 months for DR3. The ESA archive goes down on 25 Nov 2026 at 12:00 CET and re-opens with DR4 on 2 Dec 2026 at 12:00 CET. DR4 publishes all ~2.8 billion processed sources, with a best-suited model per source [RE-02 unverified]. It includes, new, epoch astrometry for all sources in the main astrometric processing (129,625,750,708 rows, 62 TB) and NSS tables: nss_acceleration_astro (3,365,947 rows), nss_two_body_orbit (5,901,111 [unchecked]), and, new, nss_resolved_pair (22,829,105 "physically bound" resolved pairs) and nss_multiplicity (229,083,823). It also has all_source_rvs (312,247,580 rows of mean RVS results). (refs: RE-01, RE-02, RE-03, RE-04, RF-17) official ESA documentation
- [D-13] Gaia DR3 radial velocities exist for 33,812,183 stars to G_RVS ≈ 14. Median formal precision is 1.3 km/s at G_RVS = 12 and 6.4 km/s at 14, with a zero-point trend reaching about 400 m/s at G_RVS = 14 [last two figures not separately grepped]. That is comparable to or larger than wide-binary orbital speeds (~0.1–0.5 km/s at 2–30 kau), so DR3 RVs serve perspective corrections but not relative line-of-sight velocities. Katz et al. 2023 state a plan to publish RVs for all sources to G_RVS ~ 16 in DR4. (refs: RE-17) peer-reviewed
- [D-14] Gaia (E)DR3 median uncertainty in parallax and proper motion is 0.02–0.03 mas (mas/yr) at G = 9–14 and about 0.5 mas at G = 20 (Lindegren et al. 2021, A&A 649, A2). (refs: RE-16) peer-reviewed
- [D-15] Chae calibrates the hidden-multiple fraction f_multi so that "binary internal motions agree statistically with Newtonian dynamics at a high enough acceleration of ≈ 10^-8 m s^-2" and carries it to low acceleration. This is brief premise 2, stated by Chae [that quote not grepped; the 0.25–0.47 literature-range quote verified]. (refs: RQ-04) peer-reviewed (method statement)
- [D-16] Published DR3 results span γ ≈ 1.0 to γ ≈ 1.6 in the low-acceleration regime, depending on group, sample and method. The pro-anomaly analyses are Chae 2023, 2024a, 2024b, 2025; Yoon, Tian & Chae 2025; Hernandez et al. 2024; Chae et al. 2026; Chae & Yoon 2026. The Newton-consistent analyses are Banik et al. 2024; Pittordis & Sutherland 2023; Pittordis, Sutherland & Shepherd 2025; Cookson et al. 2026; Makarov 2026; Saad & Ting 2026 (both papers); Boufourou 2026. Values are in section 2. (refs: RQ-01–RQ-22, RC-01–RC-18, RF-01–RF-22) mixed peer-reviewed / preprint
- [D-17] Within one dataset the inferred γ depends on the model. On the same 36 pairs, a hierarchical Bayesian forward model with an independent semi-major axis gives γ = 1.00 +0.24 −0.19. Replacing it with a geometric deprojection of the projected separation (the Chae et al. approach) gives 1.56 +0.21 −0.18. The sampler, the pooling choice and treating r_⊥ as exact are subdominant. (refs: RQ-12, RC-04, RC-06, RF-06) peer-reviewed (Saad & Ting, OJAp 9, 2026)
- [D-18] No published DR3 wide-binary gravity test that the researchers read describes a blinded analysis. Boufourou's DR4 protocol is the only declared pre-commitment, and it was written after the DR3 results were known. Neither main camp has stated a numerical γ forecast for DR4. (refs: RE-18, RF-10, RF-25 [absence claim, unverified], engineering gaps) synthesis

## 3. Contested or conflicting

- [D-60] **Is there a low-acceleration boost in DR3?** Pro-anomaly side: Chae 2023 (1.43 ± 0.06; variants 1.33–1.43), Chae 2024a (1.49 +0.21 −0.19), Chae 2024b (1.37 +0.10 −0.09 stat +0.16 −0.09 syst), Yoon, Tian & Chae 2025 (1.32 +0.12 −0.11 for g_N ≲ 1e-10), Hernandez et al. 2024 (1.5 ± 0.2), Chae 2025 (1.48 +0.33 −0.23), and Chae & Yoon 2026 (γ ~ 1.3–1.6; "cannot remove" the anomaly). Newton-consistent side: Banik+24 (α_grav = −0.021; MOND excluded at 16σ), Cookson+26 (Newton up to 1500×), PSS25 (Newton "significantly better", not "fully decisive"), Makarov 2026, Saad & Ting C3PO (1.05 / 1.17), Boufourou (1.045–1.05; 1.4 rejected at ~16σ statistical). Most of these share the DR3 astrometry and parent catalogue (D-11). (refs: D-20–D-32, D-39)
- [D-61] **f_multi calibration (brief premise 2).** Side A, Chae & Yoon and Hernandez, Chae & Aguayo-Ortiz (arXiv:2312.03162, preprint, with Chae as co-author): null results skip calibrating the multiple-star fraction on Newtonian-regime pairs. Banik+24's and PSS's samples (s > 2 kau or > 5 kau) cannot calibrate, and an excessive f_multi (0.65) "will necessarily result in reduced effective gravity inferences". Their experiment gives χ²_ν = 7.5 for Newton [unchecked], and refitting to f_multi ~ 0.4 lets AQUAL fit. Side B: PSS25 argue their pre-cut ~37% is "perhaps more realistic" than Banik's f_CB = 0.63. Boufourou does calibrate on the Newtonian regime (validation zone 0.2–2 kau, +1.4% control offset) and still gets γ ≈ 1.05. The researchers' reading (RC-19, a search-summary synthesis) is that Boufourou's residual full-median bias comes from the shape of the triple tail, not from skipping calibration. (refs: RC-01, RC-12, RQ-21b, D-35, D-36, RC-19, RE-08)
- [D-62] **Quality cuts and perspective.** Side A, Cookson+26 (with Banik+24 sec. 5.3, read only via Cookson): Chae lacks a ṽ-uncertainty cut, and a cut removing 20% of pairs "completely eliminates" the rising median-ṽ trend. Their checklist scores Chae 8–11 against 14 for Banik+24 [scores unchecked; the checklist is their own]. Side B, Chae & Yoon: cuts introduce bias, since RUWE removes hierarchical systems. They say the median ṽ falls in the order Chae 2023 > Banik+24 > PSS samples, that perspective matters only in the last bin (15 ≲ r_p ≲ 30 kau), and that the ipd_frac_multi_peak = 0 cut has a "negligible" effect. ## 4. Constraints: theorems, bounds, no-go results, each with its assumptions

- [D-80] **Bound-orbit velocity bound: ṽ ≤ √(2γ)** (ṽ ≤ √2 under Newton). Assumptions: an isolated bound two-body Keplerian orbit with G → γG, v² ≤ 2γGM/r and s ≤ r, M_tot correct and no measurement noise. It fails for triples (inner-orbit velocity), unbound or flyby pairs, chance alignments, mass underestimates and noise. Checked numerically (max ṽ = 1.4019 thermal, 1.4114 for α = 4, against 1.4142) and by the energy argument. It was not found stated as a theorem in a paper. Cookson+26 adopt ṽ < √2 for bound orbits. In the anisotropic MOND EFE case the scalar γ form is itself an approximation (D-02). (refs: RT-17, RQ-09, brief)
- [D-81] **ṽ ≳ 2.5 is too fast for modified gravity** without overshooting galaxy rotation curves (Manchanda et al. 2023, preprint). Cookson+26 use ṽ < 2.5 as the largest value any MOND, TeVeS or emergent-gravity case needs. Assumption: MOND-type boosts calibrated on rotation curves. (refs: RT-14, RF-20)
- [D-82] **EF-dominated asymptote.** The MOND boost saturates at η = ν_ext(1 + K0/3) for separations ≳ r_M. Assumptions: QUMOND (or AQUAL); point mass plus test particle; uniform Galactic EF with g_ext ≈ 1.8–1.9 a0 near the Sun; the chosen interpolating function (simple, MLS or standard give 1.42, 1.37 or 1.07); angle-averaged over the separation–EF orientation. It is not valid in the transition regime (s/r_M ≈ 0.1–0.7) or for full two-body dynamics, where Pflamm-Altenburg finds a lower 1.2–1.3. (refs: D-02, D-43, D-45)
- [D-83] **MOND exclusions from DR3** (each conditional on its model). Banik+24: MOND excluded at 16σ, assuming their QUMOND simple-function EFE orbit templates, a 1.5-dex Öpik close-binary population, line-of-sight contamination, the d < 250 pc and 2–30 kau sample, and a free eccentricity exponent. Cookson+26: modified gravity constrained between a0/10 and 200 a0, assuming the d < 130 pc, RUWE < 1.25, ipd = 0, CMD and ṽ < 2.5 sample; it reverses to 12× MOND at d < 150 pc without the ipd cut. Saad & Ting C3PO: canonical a0 excluded at 2.5σ (simple) and 1.5σ (standard), assuming RV-bound co-natal pairs, mostly in the transition regime, with EFE treatment a flagged systematic. Boufourou: γ = 1.4 rejected at ~16σ statistical, assuming his generative triple model and E3 mixture (single author). (refs: D-27, D-28, D-29, D-31)
- [D-84] **Chae camp's Newton rejections** (each conditional on its model). Chae 2024b: 5.8σ (new sample) and 9.2σ (largest sample), assuming f_multi self-calibrated at ~1e-8 m s^-2 and carried to low g_N, and median-ṽ templates with individual Hwang-type eccentricities. Chae 2024a: ~5σ, assuming the "pure" sample is free of hidden companions. Hernandez et al. 2024: 2.6σ. Chae et al. 2026: "rules out Newton", assuming geometric deprojection, with the result dominated by two systems above escape velocity. (refs: D-21, D-22, D-24, D-26)
- [D-85] **RUWE as a binarity filter.** It is sensitive only to companions with periods of months to years (Cookson+26), and the optimal single-source DR3 cutoff varies from 1.15 to 1.37 across the sky. Inner binaries with P ≫ the baseline, which the research calc says produce velocity perturbations of order v_c(s) (D-40), pass a RUWE cut. DR4 epoch astrometry and nss_acceleration_astro extend this, but by how much is not quantified. (refs: RE-11, RT-22, D-12)
- [D-86] **f_multi–γ degeneracy.** For a ṽ-distribution test from projected data, the fitted f_multi and the inferred gravity are degenerate unless fixed by Newtonian-regime pairs (Chae 2024b) [quote unconfirmed]. Hernandez, Chae & Aguayo-Ortiz show a too-large f_multi lowers the inferred γ. This makes calibration a statement about whether f_multi is independent of separation, which none of the sources tests directly. (refs: RT-11, RC-12)
