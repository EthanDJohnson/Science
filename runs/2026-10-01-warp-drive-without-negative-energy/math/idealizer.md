## Refuted
- None. No load-bearing claim failed.
- Corrections that change no finding or candidate answer:
  - M-05 (F11): ∫\|T_0z\| dV/β is 16.74 m for the eq. (28) sigmoid, not 13.0 m. It only serves as the denominator of the zero net-momentum ratio, which stays at quadrature zero (7e-7 here).
  - M-06 and M-07 (F6, F7, IDEALIZER-C): the "shaped wall" caps (0.084, 0.107) and k = 0.46–0.83 hold for a density that follows \|T_0i\| in angle as well as radius, so they also relax spherical symmetry (I1). With a spherical shaped density they are 0.067, 0.086 and k = 0.36–0.64. Either way the advance/delay margin in F9 is unchanged (≤ 0.2).
  - M-07: 100 m payload mass 4.49e28 kg (23.66 M_J), not 4.48e28 kg (rounding).
  - M-10: the crossing times are for the full diameter.
## Unverified
- **Nonlinear validity.** M-04 to M-09 and M-12 are verified as statements of linear (flat-slice or harmonic-gauge) gravity. The 2024 shell has compactness 2M/R = 0.33–0.67. So IDEALIZER-D's prediction that β = 0.04 violates the NEC (linear cap 0.027 with the sigmoid) is a verified linear-model number, not a check of the published metric. The lens labels it this way.
- **Type-I NEC form (I7).** M-06 verifies |j| ≤ (ρ + p)/2 for isotropic p. I did not check separately that the shift adds no stresses at O(β) in this gauge, which the cap needs. M-04 checked G_0i and ρ_E only. Standard linear theory gives G_ij = 0 at O(β) for a static shift with unit lapse and flat slices, but no log here covers it.
- **Inputs used, not derived:** −g_00 = 0.5793 and lapse 0.761 (toolkit `warp_shell.py`); the statement that the construction leaves g_00 unchanged; Fuchs's 7.6 ns, the checked β = 0.02 and the quotations; Pfister et al.'s strong-field dragging result.
- **M-04:** the lens's specific value −2.4e-3 ε² at its test point was not reproduced, because the test shift and point are not given. The sign is verified for the lens's w everywhere.
- **Housekeeping:** `math/idealizer/run04.out` is a stray console capture from a background run of M-04. The citable log is `M-IDEALIZER-04.py.log`. `math/idealizer/profiles.py` is my own helper (profiles and caps), shared by M-06 to M-08.
## Formalizable
- M-IDEALIZER-03: for x ∈ (0, 1), with s = √(1 − x): (1 − x/2)/s − 1 ≤ 2(1 − s) ⇔ x ≤ 24/25 (equivalently 5s² − 6s + 1 ≤ 0, i.e. s ∈ [1/5, 1)); and (1 − x/2)/s − 1 > 0, 1 − s > 0 on (0, 1).
- M-IDEALIZER-06: for real ρ, p, j: (∀α) ρ + p + 2j cos α ≥ 0 ⇔ ρ + p ≥ 2|j|.
- M-IDEALIZER-05: for real a, b, max_{c ∈ [0, 1]} [c²(a² − 2ab) + b²] = max(b², (a − b)²); and for f ∈ C²[0, ∞) with f′ compactly supported in (0, ∞), ∫₀^∞ (r²f″ + 2rf′) dr = 0.
- M-IDEALIZER-01: with γ = N/√(N² − β²) and 0 ≤ β < N, (N² − β²cos²θ)/(N² − β²) = cos²θ + γ² sin²θ, and the Gaussian curvature of R²[(cos²θ + γ²sin²θ)dθ² + sin²θ dφ²] is γ²/R² at θ = 0 and 1/(γ²R²) at θ = π/2.
- M-IDEALIZER-02 / 01: the linear map (t, z) ↦ (T′, Z′) = (γ(Nt − (β/N)(z + βt)), γz) pulls −dT′² + dZ′² back to −N²dt² + (dz + β dt)².
- M-IDEALIZER-09: for symmetric T and null k with respect to η, (T − ½η tr_η T)(k, k) = T(k, k).
- M-IDEALIZER-10: for 0 ≤ β < N, with g = [[−N² + β², β], [β, 1]], u_s = (1/√(N² − β²), 0) and n = (1/N, −β/N): −g(u_s, n) = 1/√(1 − β²/N²).
