# Analysis: examiner (Socrates: examine the question before answering it)
status: final

## Method applied
Define the question's loaded terms ("superluminal", "move", "without negative energy", "warp drive"), test each hidden premise for support, falsifier and consequence, run the travel-time test this lens owns, look for a better-posed reframe, and list the hardest checkable questions any answer must survive.

## Findings
1. **Fell–Heisenberg's "superluminal" is an interior shift magnitude on one hypersurface, not a moving bubble.** They state: "The shift vector ﬁeld, Figure 2e, is highly radial and asymptotically Minkowskian in the exterior region and highly linear in the interior with a central magnitude of ∼ 1.26, i.e. superluminal." [new: Fell & Heisenberg, arXiv:2104.06488 p. 15, full-text, "highly radial and asymptotically Minkowskian in the exterior region and highly linear in the interior with a central magnitude of ∼ 1.26, i.e. superluminal"]. Only the Hamiltonian constraint on a slice is solved; no translation of the profile in time is specified. With N = 1 and a static profile, |β| = 1.26 > N at the centre makes ∂_t spacelike there (an ergoregion relative to the asymptotic static frame); Eulerian observers stream through and leave the region rather than being carried. They concede that "horizon formations prevent the conﬁgurations from transporting an inertial observer from the subluminal regime to the superluminal regime" [new: same, p. 17, full-text]. Consistent with [D-05]. Travel-time sense: not met as a transport claim; at most a local, region-sized advance for streams crossing it, which Gao–Wald/Olum tie to NEC failure, which the authors concede [D-05].
2. **Lentz's speed is assigned, not derived, and his loophole against the theorems is a claimed "single fastest causal path" premise.** "The relation between the soliton velocity is assigned to be consistent with the shift vector of the soliton's central region (vs =Nz(0, 0))" and "the logistics of soliton travel for observers in the central region reﬂect those in [1]" [new: Lentz, arXiv:2006.07125 p. 6, full-text]. He answers Olum-type theorems with: "the pre-conditions of these proofs are restricted to geometries with a single fastest causal path. No known generalizations of the proofs exist to extensive superluminal mechanisms" [new: same, p. 12, full-text]. Examination: (a) if the soliton translates at v_s = N_z(0,0) > 1 with N = 1 and flat slices, the central Eulerian payload does arrive before exterior light (travel-time sense met, as in Alcubierre); (b) the loophole is moot for the energy question, because Santiago–Schuster–Visser prove NEC failure directly from the unit-lapse, flat-slice form without any fastest-path premise and name Lentz [D-41]; Gao–Wald's theorem is also not stated for a single fastest path [D-60].
3. **Operational superluminality is barred in globally hyperbolic spacetimes regardless of energy.** Krasnikov: "It is argued that under some reasonable assumptions in globally hyperbolic spacetimes the traveller cannot hasten reaching the destination" [new: Krasnikov, PRD 57 4760 (1998), arXiv:gr-qc/9511068, abstract]. So "decide at A, arrive at B before light from A" requires that the geometry along the path was prepared in advance by something outside the traveller's causal future (Everett–Roman control problem [D-13]); the eternal-bubble time advance of Alcubierre/Natário is a property of a pre-existing metric, not of a drive launched from A.
4. **Lentz's own framing names the creation problem.** His abstract lists "diﬃculties associated with constructing a soliton from a nearly ﬂat space–time up to the superluminal phase, where the transported central observers become surrounded by a horizon" [new: Lentz, arXiv:2006.07125 p. 1, full-text]. His positive-energy claim addresses only the energy sign, not creation or control.
5. **Travel-time test, Alcubierre (owned calculation).** With N = 1, ds² = −dt² + (dx − v f dt)², the central payload has dx/dt = v and dτ = dt, so at v = 10 c it reaches α Cen (4.37 ly) in 0.437 yr of exterior and proper time against 4.37 yr for exterior light: a genuine time advance in the brief's sense, but only for a bubble that already exists along the path (Finding 3) [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/lens-examiner_traveltime.py]. "|β| > N" is gauge-dependent: in exterior-frame coordinates the shift is 10 > 1 at the flat centre. The invariant statement is the norm of the comoving Killing vector, −1 + v²(1 − f)², which changes sign at r = 99.45 m (f = 1 − 1/v = 0.9) for R = 100 m, Δ = 1 m: the whole exterior is beyond a Killing horizon relative to the payload, and forward-emitted light from the payload stalls at that radius. For every v < 1 the maximum of v(1 − f) is v < 1, so there is no comoving horizon [calc: same]. Natário's interior also moves at v with N = 1 [D-01, D-02], so the same result holds there.
6. **Subluminal bubbles still give light a time advance; Fuchs et al. Table 1 is a co-minus-counter difference, which resolves the sign question [D-33].** A light ray crossing an Alcubierre bubble along its motion gains exactly 2Rv/c on flat-space light; a counter-propagating ray loses 2Rv/c. At R = 15 m, v = 0.04 c: +1.20 m (4.0 ns) and −1.20 m (−4.0 ns), so the difference is 4Rv/c = 2.400 m = **8.006 ns**, the value of Fuchs et al. Table 1 (8.0 ns) [D-25]. The result does not depend on wall stiffness (σ = 2 and 0.5 m⁻¹ both give 8.006 ns) [calc: same]. So the table's δt is a Sagnac-like co/counter asymmetry, always positive by construction, and the text's "advance" refers to the co-propagating leg. This is our reconstruction from a numerical match: the paper's set-up was not re-read. Consequence: even a subluminal Alcubierre bubble gives light a time advance in Olum's sense, which is why its NEC failure does not depend on v [D-58 Error 26].
7. **The 2024 shell gives no time advance: its frame dragging is swamped by its own Shapiro delay (idealized thin-shell model, ours).** M = 4.49e27 kg (GM/c² = 3.334 m). The flat cavity inside a thin shell at R_s has lapse N_in = √(1 − 2GM/(c²R_s)) = 0.577 (R_s = 10 m) to 0.816 (R_s = 20 m). A forward ray crossing the cavity with interior coordinate shift 0.04 is still delayed by 6.7–12.4 m (22–41 ns) relative to flat space, before adding the exterior Shapiro logarithm (26–44 m to r = 100 m on each side). Any forward advance needs interior shift b > 1 − N_in = 0.18–0.42, which is 4.6–10.6× the published 0.04 [calc: same]. Co-minus-counter in this model is 9–16 ns against the published 7.6 ns: agreement in order only, because the real shift ramps through a thick wall. A thick shell has a lower interior lapse than a thin shell at R₂, so the delay bound is conservative. This matches what Gao–Wald [D-60] and Visser–Bassett–Liberati [D-61] require of NEC-satisfying matter. The warp shell's "0.04 c" is therefore a frame-dragging asymmetry, not superluminality in any sense and not a measured displacement of the payload.
8. **Fell–Heisenberg local advance (ours, length unit assumed 1 m).** A stream crossing their static interior (diameter 12 m) at coordinate speed 1 + 1.26 gains 6.7 m (22 ns) on flat-space light. It is a local advance from a static region, so nothing is carried, and by Olum/Gao–Wald it needs NEC failure, which the authors concede [D-05] [calc: same].
9. **Premise 4 examined in the authors' own words: the 2024 shell is a stationary massive shell plus an interior shift, built in coordinates where nothing depends on time.** "The shell solution will be constructed in comoving coordinates in which the metric tensor does not depend on time" [new: Fuchs et al., arXiv:2405.02709 p. 7, full-text]. They also concede the coasting case needs no shift: "if the warp drive is always at a constant velocity with its passengers having always existed comoving inside the warp drive, then no shift vector is needed since the passengers ui/u0 =vs by definition. Bobrick and Martire discuss this example … which results in a regular matter shell" [new: same, p. 4, full-text]. Their time-delay observable is explicitly a with/against asymmetry: "An example uses photon paths circling a Kerr black hole which has a transit difference depending on traveling with or against the rotation, this same effect occurs here except these paths are straight through the warp bubble center" [new: same, p. 25 fn. 15, full-text], which confirms Finding 6's reading of Table 1. Examination: in a stationary metric whose exterior is Schwarzschild at rest, a payload that stays inside the cavity is at rest relative to the shell and so relative to infinity. "v_warp = 0.04 c" is then an interior frame-dragging amplitude (gauge-invariant, as the 7.6 ns shows), not a displacement of the payload relative to the exterior. Coasting at v relative to some other frame is just a boost of the whole stationary solution, which any massive body allows. This matches Barzegar et al.'s Error 13, that "constant velocity" is ill-defined in the construction [D-31]; that passage itself was not re-read here.

10. **Fuchs et al. name the shift's role as frame dragging, not transport.** "the modified shift vector gave us a warp effect by creating a linear frame dragging inside the shell" [new: Fuchs et al., arXiv:2405.02709 p. 27, full-text]; "This experiment demonstrates a Lense-Thirring effect exists for warp drives with shift vectors, creating a linear frame dragging" [new: same, p. 25, full-text].

### O3. Reframes
- **R1, the operational reframe.** "Can NEC-respecting matter, arranged by a traveller at A, give any signal or payload a net time advance to B relative to light through the asymptotic exterior?" This is better posed: it uses invariant quantities and puts creation in scope. Clearer answer: no, under Gao–Wald (NEC, null completeness, the generic condition) and Krasnikov (global hyperbolicity). The 2024 shell illustrates it: its mass-induced delay of ≥ 6.7 m (22 ns) plus the exterior logarithm exceeds its frame-dragging gain (Finding 7).
- **R2, the frame-dragging reframe.** "What can linear frame dragging inside a massive positive-energy shell do for a payload?" Clearer answer: it produces a with/against light-transit asymmetry (7.6 ns) and nothing else measurable for a comoving payload, because the cavity is flat. A comoving payload's proper time is slowed, not sped (Bobrick–Martire [D-07]).
- **R3, the rocket-benefit reframe.** "Does any positive-energy warp geometry beat a rocket at any figure of merit?" The only candidate merit is acceleration without felt g-force (free-fall cavity). Le's radiative steering keeps the photon-rocket mass law [D-38], and Le himself says the cavity centre's observer "needs a force" [D-72], so even that merit is not established.

### O4. The hardest questions any proposed answer must survive
1. **Travel time, invariantly.** For the claimed payload worldline, in coordinates where β → 0 and N → 1 at infinity, does it reach an exterior-static point before light sent at the same event through the exterior, *including the Shapiro delay of the configuration's own ADM mass*? (For the 2024 shell it does not: the interior shift needs > 0.18–0.42 against 0.04 published; Finding 7.)
2. **Displacement or dragging?** Is the stated velocity a displacement of the payload relative to asymptotic rest observers, or the amplitude of a shift in a stationary metric? Show x_payload(t) in asymptotic rest coordinates.
3. **NEC for every null direction**, including distributional terms at every C⁰ or C¹ junction (Lentz's rhomboid edges, the Fuchs shell boundaries), with a certified or type-I-exact test, not Eulerian ρ.
4. **Does the source exist?** Does T_μν = G_μν/8π obey ∇_μT^μν = 0 with a stated equation of state and sound speeds ≤ c? Is Barzegar's Error 18 (TOV equations not actually solved) answered by an independent re-solve?
5. **Which theorem assumption is broken?** Name it: unit lapse and flat slices (SSV), null completeness, the generic condition or asymptotic flatness (Gao–Wald), or global hyperbolicity (Krasnikov). "A single fastest causal path" is not an assumption of SSV.
6. **Momentum ledger for start and stop.** Where does the change in Bondi/ADM momentum come from, and does the mass cost beat e^(−3L) (Le) or the photon-rocket mass ratio (40.4 for 1 g to α Cen [D-39])?
7. **Causal access to the front wall.** Is the matter that forms the bubble's leading edge in the causal future of the launch event? If not, who placed it?
8. **What does the shift do for the payload** that the identical shell without the shift does not? Proper time, tidal tensor, arrival time at the destination: give numbers for both.
9. **The shift cap.** At compactness 2GM/(c²R) = 0.33–0.67, what is the largest DEC-compatible interior shift, and is it below 1 − N_in? A closed-form or junction-condition answer (the idealizer's thin-shell model) settles whether positive matter could even locally advance light.
10. **Fair cost comparison.** For the same trip time, is the shell's 4.0e44 J rest energy [D-23] compared with the propellant energy of a rocket with the same payload (3.5e23 J [D-39]), not with the payload's kinetic energy?

## What would change my mind
- An independent re-solve showing the published 2024 shell is an exact solution with a stationary exterior at rest *and* a payload displaced relative to infinity; that would revive P4 and move EXAMINER-C toward surviving as a drive.
- A DEC-compatible thin- or thick-shell solution with interior shift > 1 − N_in, or a net advance computed with the Shapiro logarithm included. That would strain EXAMINER-A and contradict the Gao–Wald expectation, so it would point to a broken assumption.
- A certified (interval-arithmetic) NEC pass for the published, unregularized shell, and the resolution of Le's abstract-versus-table tension [D-84].
- A published reading of the Fuchs et al. set-up showing Table 1 is not a co/counter difference. The 8.006 ns match (Finding 6) is our reconstruction.
