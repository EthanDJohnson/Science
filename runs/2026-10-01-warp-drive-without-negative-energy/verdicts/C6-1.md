# Verdict on C6 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: Faster-than-light travel without pointwise negative energy is possible by breaking a hypothesis of the time-advance theorems: asymptotic flatness, null completeness, the generic condition, or stationarity.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: cited-evidence
narrowed: No published theorem yet excludes a non-perturbative, asymptotically flat time advance with pointwise-NEC matter along a strong-field fastest path that crosses no matter and no transverse tide (branch c) or in a null-incomplete spacetime with non-negative mass (branch b); no example exists in either, the weak-field theorem closes both, and breaking asymptotic flatness or stationarity, or violating only the DEC, does not deliver such an advance within the brief's definitions.

## Strongest attack
The heading says FTL without pointwise negative energy "is possible" if any one of four hypotheses is broken. The sources show that two of the four are not hypotheses of the binding theorem, a third is excluded by the brief itself, and the last is already partly closed. What is left is a gap in what has been proven, not a mechanism that is possible.

1. **Stationarity is not a hypothesis of Olum, Gao–Wald or Visser–Bassett–Liberati.** Olum assumes only his travel-time definition and the generic condition on the path P. Visser–Bassett–Liberati (VBL) use retarded Liénard–Wiechert potentials for an arbitrary time-dependent T_μν. Breaking stationarity escapes only the coasting no-go, which says nothing about a time advance. The candidate concedes this for (d) but still lists stationarity in its heading.
2. **"Generic condition" (branch c) needs it at only one point, and Olum says so himself.** C6 keeps (c) "open pending the separate argument that Olum needs the generic condition only somewhere on the path". Olum's paper states this outright: "The generic condition holds whenever there is any normal matter or any transverse tidal force anywhere on P", and the proof uses only "From the generic condition, σ̂ cannot vanish everywhere". The argument C6 calls pending is already in the theorem. So (c) needs a fastest path that touches no normal matter and feels no transverse tide anywhere. In weak fields VBL closes even that case, without any generic condition: the NEC gives g_μν k^μ k^ν = 16πG∫T_μν k^μ k^ν/|x−y| ≥ 0 for every Minkowski-null k, so "the Shapiro time delay in any weak gravitational field is always a delay". In strong fields, Gao–Wald record Galloway's point that their conclusions hold for any spacetime that "fails to contain a null line". Galloway's null-splitting theorem shows that an asymptotically simple vacuum spacetime containing a null line "is isometric to Minkowski space". A non-generic escape therefore needs a null line, and in the vacuum, asymptotically simple case that line forces flat space.
3. **"Null completeness" (branch b): the only explicit escape uses negative mass.** Negative-mass Schwarzschild has T_ab = 0 at every regular point and is null-incomplete, and it does advance light. But the advance has the sign of m, so it exists only because the singularity carries negative ADM mass [calc]. Penrose–Sorkin–Woolgar state the general fact: "positive energy-density focuses null geodesics, and correspondingly retards them, whereas a negative total mass would advance them". They also note that their theorem "is not concerned with what lies behind horizons", so incompleteness hidden behind a horizon does not open the loophole. The brief counts distributional and sheet contributions as part of the energy, so this escape is negative energy. The published positive-energy attempts that used non-smooth sources, Lentz and Fell–Heisenberg (the C5 family), were found to violate the NEC (dossier D-41).
4. **"Asymptotic flatness" (branch a) is excluded by the brief, and the one example fails on its own terms.** The brief requires "The exterior is asymptotically flat". Garattini–Zatrimaylov's own abstract limits their bubble to moving "at a velocity equal to the speed of the expansion of the universe", with the energy conditions "satisfied up to a total divergence term that averages to zero". That is comoving recession, not an advance, and the conditions hold only on average, not pointwise. VBL explain why advances without NEC violation are possible in non-flat backgrounds: cosmic voids give "a Shapiro time advance" only because the comparison is with a denser FRW background. That is a choice of benchmark, not travel faster than light through an undisturbed exterior.

Net: in no branch does the literature or our calculation show a possible mechanism. Branches (b) and (c) are open in the strong-field regime only because no one has proven them closed. VBL themselves say "we do not yet have a fully convincing argument that applies in a non-perturbative setting", and Gao–Wald call their own K′ possibly much larger than K. That is the true residue, and it does not amount to "is possible".

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle:**
  - (a) lies outside the brief's asymptotically flat definition, and its only example fails pointwise by its authors' own statement.
  - (d) and (e) are not escapes at all: Olum and VBL do not assume stationarity, and WEC implies NEC.
  - (b) has an explicit escape only with negative mass in a singularity.
  - (c) is closed at weak field. At strong field, Galloway's null-line rigidity squeezes it.
  - What remains is an unproven negative for a strong-field, matter-free, tide-free fastest path, or for a null-incomplete spacetime with non-negative mass. That makes it not excluded, not possible.
- **In practice:** no construction exists in any branch, so TRL 0, as the candidate itself states.

## Corrections to details
- C6 calls branch (c) "open pending the separate argument that Olum needs the generic condition only somewhere on the path". That argument is not pending: it is in Olum's text, quoted above. The candidate's decisive "Proof for (c)" is already half done in the source.
- The heading lists "stationarity" as a theorem hypothesis to break. None of Olum, Gao–Wald or VBL assumes it, and the body concedes this for (d), so the heading and the body disagree.
- Branch (e) concerns the DEC and the positive-mass theorem, not a hypothesis of any time-advance theorem. It does not belong to the heading's list.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives:** a strong-field gap in what has been proven. No one has published a non-perturbative proof that a pointwise-NEC, asymptotically flat spacetime admits no time advance when the generic condition fails along the whole fastest path, or when the spacetime is null-incomplete with non-negative mass. VBL and Gao–Wald both concede that their arguments are weak there.
- **Remaining doubt:**
  - I grepped Penrose–Sorkin–Woolgar's full text but did not extract its formal hypothesis list. By their own account their theorem excludes nakedly singular spacetimes, so it does not close branch (b) for a naked singularity with non-negative mass. That residue rests only on the cosmic censorship conjecture and on the absence of any example.
- **The brief's travel-time definition matters here.** With positive ADM mass, the far-field term 4M ln(2D/b) grows with the separation D of the rest points, so any finite local advance is overtaken at large enough D (compare M-DECOMPOSER-06). An advance between nearby rest points would still count, so this does not close the branches.
  - Galloway's full null-splitting statement for non-vacuum NEC spacetimes was not quoted. Only the vacuum, asymptotically simple corollary was.
  - If either closes the residue, the verdict moves toward refuted.
