# Verdict on C6 (refuter 0, angle: physics)
claim: Faster-than-light travel without pointwise negative energy is possible by breaking a hypothesis of the time-advance theorems: asymptotic flatness, null completeness, the generic condition, or stationarity.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: No proof yet excludes a strong-field, asymptotically flat spacetime in which a fastest path that is null-incomplete or non-generic everywhere gives a time advance while the NEC holds pointwise and distributionally. But no such spacetime is known, and to first order in G breaking any of the four hypotheses gives no advance without a negative null-energy line integral.

## Strongest attack
The heading says FTL travel "is possible". The body supports only "not yet excluded by a proof", and my calculations close most of what is left.

1. **Weak field: none of the four hypotheses matters.** Take linearized gravity in harmonic gauge (G = c = 1). For a null ray with direction k, emitted at retarded time u and passing at transverse position b, integrating □h̄_ab = −16πT_ab along the ray gives

   ∇⊥² Δt(u, b) = −8π Σ(u, b), where Σ = ∫ T_ab k^a k^b dv.

   This needs no generic condition, no stationarity (the u-dependence comes along) and no null completeness (Σ is distributional, so point and sheet sources are included). Whenever Σ ≥ 0, which the NEC guarantees, Δt is superharmonic in b. By the minimum principle, no ray through a region arrives earlier than every ray on a circle around it. So an advance needs Σ < 0, a negative line integral of the null energy, on some ray.

   I checked this numerically on the zero-net-momentum counter-streaming shell, the current pattern of a "positive-energy warp" [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C6-0_linear_censorship.py]:
   - The point-mass check reproduces the Shapiro slope −4M ln b. The difference between b = 10 m and b = 40 m is −5.545 m numerically and −5.545 m from Shapiro.
   - Every NEC-satisfying case (κ = j/ρ ≤ 1/2) shows no advance at rings of 2R, 3R and 4R. At κ = 0.5 the interior-minus-ring delays are +4.3e-3, +1.06e-2 and +1.53e-2 m.
   - An advance first appears only between κ = 1.5 and 2.0. That is 3–4× beyond the NEC threshold, matching the strong-field finding that the advance threshold sits 21–80× above the NEC threshold.
   - The sign is unchanged on a coarser grid (N = 400).

   This removes branches (b), (c) and (d) wherever |h| ≪ 1. That is also where the run's positive-energy shells operate: their shift cap is β ≲ 0.024.

2. **Branch (b), null incompleteness: the one known example hides negative energy in its singularity** [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C6-0_incompleteness.py]. Schwarzschild with M < 0 is vacuum (gr_tensors: G_ab ≡ 0, so the NEC holds pointwise on r > 0) and null-incomplete (Kretschmann = 48M²/r⁶). It does advance light:
   - for M = −1 m and r₀ = 10 m, T − T_flat = −30.75 m at D = 1e4 m and −49.17 m at D = 1e6 m;
   - rays at r₀ = 100 m are advanced less (−23.0 m and −41.4 m), so the near ray beats the far exterior light;
   - for M = +1 m the same rays are delayed (+34.8 m and +53.3 m).

   So the letter of C6's heading ("without pointwise negative energy") is met. But the Komar mass of every sphere equals M < 0, so the source is a distributional negative mass at r = 0. The brief explicitly counts distributional contributions. Read under the brief's definition, the only working example of branch (b) is negative energy hidden in a singularity, which is a cosmetic escape and not a loophole. The word "pointwise" in the heading is exactly what lets it through.

3. **Branches (a), (d) and (e) fall by the candidate's own admission.**
   - (a): the brief defines FTL against an asymptotically flat exterior, and de Sitter has none. The source [D-75] says the conditions hold only "up to a total divergence term", which is not pointwise.
   - (d) and (e): the candidate says they "remain under Olum and Gao–Wald".

   So the heading lists four hypotheses whose breaking makes FTL "possible", while the body concedes that one is out of scope, two give nothing, and none has a construction (TRL 0). That is an internal inconsistency between heading and body.

  My statement compares rays whose endpoints lie in the asymptotic region. A gauge change alters Δt only by endpoint terms ξ·k, which vanish there in an asymptotically flat gauge. I therefore take the comparison as gauge-invariant up to those endpoint terms, a caveat I list under remaining doubt.

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle:**
  - Weak field: closed for (b), (c) and (d). An advance needs ∫T_kk dv < 0 on some ray.
  - (e): closed by Olum and Gao–Wald, since the WEC implies the NEC.
  - (a): outside the brief's definition.
  - (b) with singularities: works only with negative distributional mass, as in Schwarzschild with M < 0, which the brief's definition excludes.
  - What remains open is strong-field, regular or singular spacetimes in which the fastest path is non-generic along its whole length, or incomplete, and the NEC holds distributionally. No construction exists there, and the run's strong-field shell family shows the opposite: the advance threshold lies 21–80× above the NEC threshold.
- **In practice:** TRL 0. No metric exists, so there is nothing to build.

## Corrections to details
- **Branch (b) has an example after all.** The candidate says "No construction exists in any branch", but negative-mass Schwarzschild is one. It is excluded only because the brief counts distributional (singular) energy, and the heading's word "pointwise" should read "pointwise or distributional".
- **The proof proposed for (c) is mostly unnecessary.** C6's suggested proof that matter crossings supply the generic condition is not needed to first order, because the linear identity needs no generic condition at all.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives** is the narrowed core: the fully nonlinear case has no general proof. Olum and Gao–Wald assume genericity and completeness, and Gao–Wald's K′ can be much larger than K.
- **My linear argument has limits:**
  - it is first order in G;
  - the identity is derived in harmonic gauge, and only the comparison between rays with asymptotic endpoints is gauge-invariant;
  - it compares rays emitted at the same retarded time u.
- A strong-field counterexample cannot be excluded by anything I computed. But no one has exhibited one, and the candidate offers none. That is why the verdict is weakened rather than refuted.
