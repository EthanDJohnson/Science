# Verdict on C1 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: No warp spacetime moves a payload faster than light without NEC violation; positive-energy subluminal shells move payloads only as ordinary massive vehicles.
status: final
verdict: survives
basis: cited-evidence
narrowed: none

## Strongest attack

From the evidence angle the null can fail in two ways: (i) the literature contains a published spacetime that gives a genuine one-way advance between exterior rest points while satisfying the NEC; or (ii) the theorems that C1 relies on do not say what it claims. I tried both.

1. **Counterexample search, 2024–2026.** I searched INSPIRE and arXiv for positive-energy warp claims since 2024. Four candidates came up.
   - **Garattini & Zatrimaylov 2025 (de Sitter warp bubble).** This is the closest thing to a positive-energy warp claim made after 2024. It fails the brief's definitions three ways:
     - the background is de Sitter, not asymptotically flat;
     - the bubble moves "at a velocity equal to the speed of the expansion of the universe", so it is comoving and makes no claim to beat exterior light;
     - its NEC and WEC hold only "up to a total divergence term that averages to zero". That is an averaged statement, not the pointwise NEC the brief requires.
     It is not a counterexample.
   - **Rodal 2026 (GRG 58, 1), peer-reviewed.** It still has NEC violation. Its own abstract reports the "peak NEC violation" only as "more than 60× smaller than Natário" and quantifies "the negative-energy requirement". This supports C1.
   - **Lentz (2020 claim).** It is now contradicted twice: Santiago–Schuster–Visser 2022 (all observers) and Celmaster & Rubin 2025, who find negative energy even in the Eulerian frame.
   - **Fell–Heisenberg.** They concede the all-observer WEC failure themselves [D-05].
   No published metric meets C1's own "if false" condition: a certified pointwise NEC pass plus a computed one-way advance between exterior rest points.
2. **Theorem hypotheses.** The real weakness of (A) is that it is a universal negative. It rests on theorems whose hypotheses a cleverly built spacetime might evade:
   - **Olum** needs the generic condition on the path, and that "S contains no singularities".
   - **Gao–Wald Theorem 1** needs null geodesic completeness and the null generic condition, and it bounds only paths between points outside a possibly much larger K′.
   - **Visser–Bassett–Liberati** is perturbative.
   - C1's own lemma closing the non-generic-path loophole was refuted (M-DECOMPOSER-10).
   These are genuine gaps in proof. But no source exhibits a spacetime that exploits any of them to give an NEC-respecting advance. The three results also cover each other's gaps. VBL's linearized argument needs no generic condition, so it closes the weak-field non-generic case. Olum's local definition closes the finite-region case that Gao–Wald's K′ caveat leaves open, provided the generic condition holds. The generic condition holds automatically wherever the path crosses normal matter, as Olum states.

   An open gap in a proof is not a refutation of the claim. The residue left is a strong-field path that is both non-generic and singular or incomplete, and that is C6's territory, not evidence against C1.
3. **(B) half.** I looked for a source showing that a positive-energy subluminal shell gives its payload anything a plain massive shell boosted to speed v does not.
   - The originators of the framework say the opposite (Bobrick–Martire, quoted below).
   - Fuchs et al. leave acceleration unsolved: moving the shell while growing the shift "requires a negative energy density throughout space", and the alternative they name is mass shedding [D-08].
   - Le's 2026 radiative-steering shells steer "under nonnegative radiation", which is momentum emission, the C3 route.
   - Barzegar et al. question whether the 2024 shell solves Einstein's equations at all [D-31]. Whether or not they are right, the class of valid positive-energy subluminal shells either shrinks or stays one of plain shells, so (B) is not threatened.

## Evidence

- **Olum 1998, PRL 81 3567** (arXiv:gr-qc/9805003, full text via fetch_text):
  - "With this definition (and assuming the generic condition) I prove that superluminal travel requires weak-energy-condition violation." (p. 1)
  - The proof uses only the null contraction: "If the weak energy condition is satisfied, then R_ab K^a K^b ≥ 0, so dθ̂/dv ≤ 0." (p. 5) So what is actually proven is NEC violation on the path, which is the reading C1 uses.
  - "The generic condition holds whenever there is any normal matter or any transverse tidal force anywhere on P." (p. 4)
  - Hypotheses: "Suppose also that S contains no singularities and that the modified region of S obeys the generic condition" (p. 3).
- **Gao & Wald 2000, CQG 17 4999** (abstract):
  - "Both theorems apply to spacetimes satisfying the null energy condition and the null generic condition. The first theorem states that if the spacetime is null geodesically complete, then given any compact set K, there exists another compact set K' such that for any p,q ∉ K', if there exists a 'fastest null geodesic', γ, between p and q, then γ cannot enter K."
  - The second theorem concerns a timelike conformal boundary ("perturbations of anti-de Sitter spacetime"), not asymptotically flat exteriors.
- **Visser, Bassett & Liberati, gr-qc/9908023** (abstract): "We argue that 'effective' superluminal travel, potentially caused by the tipping over of light cones in Einstein gravity, is always associated with violations of the null energy condition (NEC). This is most easily seen by working perturbatively around Minkowski spacetime, where we use linearized Einstein gravity to show that the NEC forces the light cones to contract (narrow)."
- **Low 1999, CQG 16 543** (abstract): "...second to show that it is impossible to construct a warp drive as considered by Alcubierre (1994) in the absence of exotic matter."
- **Santiago, Schuster & Visser 2022, PRD 105 064038** (abstract): the positive-energy claims "are, at best, incomplete since the arguments as presented only assert but do not prove the existence of one set of timelike observers, the comoving Eulerian observers, ..."
- **Celmaster & Rubin 2025, arXiv:2511.18251** (abstract, preprint): "We demonstrate that Lentz's claim is incorrect. We begin with a direct calculation of the energy-momentum tensor of Lentz's warp drive in a Eulerian reference frame, and show that there are spacetime regions where the energy density is negative."
- **Rodal 2026, GRG 58 1** (arXiv:2512.18008, abstract): "its peak NEC violation is more than 60× smaller than Natário ... We quantify the negative-energy requirement via a slice-integrated (on Σ_t) negative-energy volume".
- **Garattini & Zatrimaylov 2025, arXiv:2502.13153** (abstract, preprint): "under certain conditions (namely, that the bubble is moving in the radial direction at a velocity equal to the speed of the expansion of the universe), it is possible for the bubble to have strictly non-negative energy density, with the weak and null energy satisfied up to a total divergence term that averages to zero."
- **Bobrick & Martire 2021, CQG 38 105009** (arXiv:2102.06824, abstract): "we demonstrate that any warp drive, including the Alcubierre drive, is a shell of regular or exotic material moving inertially with a certain velocity. Therefore, any warp drive requires propulsion."
- **Fuchs et al. 2024, CQG 41 095013** (abstract): "we present a solution for a constant-velocity subluminal warp drive that satisfies all of the energy conditions". The positive-energy example its own authors offer is subluminal and coasting.
- **Le 2026, arXiv:2606.22531** (abstract, preprint): "The shells satisfy the strict surface dominant energy condition and steer subluminally under nonnegative radiation."

## In principle vs. in practice

- **In principle.**
  - Faster-than-light travel without NEC violation is excluded by Olum, by Gao–Wald Theorem 1 and by VBL within their hypotheses. No published metric falls outside those hypotheses and still shows an advance.
  - Subluminal positive-energy motion is possible, but only as a massive shell that coasts or is pushed. The originators of the shell framework state this themselves.
- **In practice.** The literature adds nothing that would move a payload faster than light. The engineering numbers for the subluminal shell (M ≈ 4.5e27 kg, SI) are the null's own and were not in dispute here.

## Corrections to details

- **"Olum, Gao–Wald and VBL require NEC failure before any time advance" is too broad.**
  - Olum states his result for the WEC, though the proof uses only R_ab K^a K^b ≥ 0, that is the NEC.
  - Olum also assumes "S contains no singularities", and Gao–Wald Theorem 1 assumes null geodesic completeness. C1 lists the generic condition and the K′ gap among its doubts but omits the no-singularity and completeness hypotheses. A drive whose shift exceeds the lapse can form horizons [D-05], which bears on those hypotheses.
- **Gao–Wald's second theorem concerns timelike (AdS-type) conformal boundaries.** Only Theorem 1 bears on the brief's asymptotically flat setting.
- **Lentz.** C1's "Lentz if his assigned v_s is consistent" understates the case against him. Celmaster & Rubin (preprint) report negative energy density even in the Eulerian frame, which is stronger than an all-observer failure.
- **Missing counterexample candidate.** Add Garattini–Zatrimaylov 2025 to the list of checked candidates. It is excluded by the brief's asymptotic flatness, by its comoving (not faster-than-light) speed, and by its NEC holding only up to a divergence.

## What survives and remaining doubt

Both halves of the core claim survive the evidence attack.
- **(A)** is a well-supported negative, not a theorem free of hypotheses. Its residual loophole is a strong-field fastest path that is non-generic and passes through a singular or null-incomplete region. No paper realizes that loophole.
- **(B)** is the stated view of the framework's own originators (Bobrick–Martire) and of all later work (Fuchs et al., Le).

Open items that could still matter:
- whether Le's observer-robust test finds the regularized Fuchs shell NEC-clean (D-84, unresolved);
- Barzegar et al.'s claim that the 2024 shell is not a solution. It affects the existence of the example, not (B)'s conclusion.
