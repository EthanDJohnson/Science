# Verdict on C3 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: A positive-energy shell starts and stops only by emitting momentum (photon or gravitational-wave exhaust, e.g. Le's radiative steering), at no less than photon-rocket cost for its whole mass.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: cited-evidence
narrowed: An isolated, self-contained positive-energy shell can change its exterior (Bondi) velocity only by emitting momentum, and then at a whole-mass ratio no better than a photon rocket's e^(Δrapidity); with momentum supplied from outside (a beam, a sail, another body) it needs no exhaust and as little energy as its kinetic energy, and the photon-rocket floor is proven for the exterior Bondi velocity, not for the payload cavity's own rapidity.

Units: SI unless marked; Le's formulas in geometric units (G = c = 1). M = 4.511e27 kg (2024 shell plus 1e5 kg payload), v = 0.04 c, as in M-MECHANIST-08.

## Strongest attack
The core sentence makes three claims. The literature supports the first only within the scope its own argument sets: an isolated system.

1. **"Only by emitting momentum" is false once the system is not isolated, and the brief explicitly allows non-isolated propulsion.** The brief's "start and stop" level says "External propulsion (reaction mass, radiation) is allowed but must be counted in mass and energy." C3's conservation argument is stated for "an isolated, asymptotically flat system". Its heading, though, is unconditional. A shell can gain momentum without emitting any:
   - by absorbing or reflecting an external beam, the directed-energy propulsion that the Lubin roadmap proposes for relativistic interstellar probes;
   - by an exchange with another body, such as a gravity assist, or braking against the interstellar medium.

   Le's photon-rocket floor rests explicitly on outgoing flux ("For F ≥ 0, the radiated four-momentum … is future causal"). An incoming beam violates that premise, so the floor does not apply.
2. **"No less than photon-rocket cost" holds only for the mass ratio of a self-contained rocket. It fails for energy, which is the quantity C3 itself bills (3.38e43 J).** My calculation [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C3-1_cost_floor.py] gives:
   - **Optimal reaction-mass rocket.** An on-board rocket with exhaust speed 0.050 c needs 2.0e42 J of exhaust kinetic energy for start plus stop. That is 16.8× (1.23 orders) less than the photon rocket, at a mass ratio of 4.93 against 1.083.
   - **Reflective sail on an external beam.** The beam energy is 0.49 of a photon-rocket leg, and the shell loses no mass on board.
   - **Photon recycling.** With the demonstrated recycling of ~1,500 passes (Bae, search summary), the energy approaches the kinetic-energy floor of 3.25e41 J per leg. That is 0.019 of a photon-rocket leg, 1.72 orders lower.

   So the photon rocket is the floor in mass ratio among self-contained rockets. In energy it is the most expensive of these routes.
3. **Even for Le's isolated, radiation-only construction, the floor is proven for the exterior Bondi velocity, not for the payload cavity's velocity.**
   - Le's Corollary 3.4 is a sharp bound, attained by a straight saturated burn, on the cavity centre's interior rapidity C: m_f/m_i ≤ (cosh(3C/2) + s_i sinh(3C/2))^(−2), with s_i = √(1 − 2m_i/R₀).
   - Le states that the photon-rocket lemma "cannot be applied to (m,C)".
   - For small C this bound exceeds the photon-rocket value e^(−C) whenever 2m_i/R₀ > 8/9, which falls within his allowed range (< 24/25). At 2m/R₀ = 0.95 and C = 0.04, the bound is 0.970 against 0.961 for the photon rocket: about 24% less mass lost for the same change in cavity rapidity [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C3-1_le_center_bound.py].
   - How C maps onto the payload's velocity in the exterior frame (the brief's measure) is not settled by Le. So "no less than photon-rocket cost for its whole mass" is unproven for the payload's own speed change in very compact shells.
   - This does not touch the published 2024 shell: its 2GM/(c²R) is 0.335 at R₂ (0.67 at R₁), below 8/9. There the bound is costlier than a photon rocket.

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle.**
  - Isolated and self-contained: C3 stands. Bondi momentum changes only through flux, and Le's Lemma 2.1 gives the photon-rocket mass floor for the exterior rapidity. His DEC-satisfying burns cost e^(−3L), which is worse.
  - Not isolated: the shell can be pushed or braked by external beams, sails or gravitational encounters with no exhaust at all, at an energy cost that can approach its kinetic energy. So "only by emitting" and "no less than photon-rocket cost" fail as stated.
- **In practice (by 2100):** C3's practical verdict is unchanged. Even the kinetic-energy floor for start plus stop of the 2024 shell is 6.5e41 J = 1.1e21 world-years (10^21.04). Lowering the bill from 10^22.76 to 10^21.04 world-years still leaves it ~21 orders above world energy supply. No external beam or body could deliver γMv = 5.4e34 kg m/s to a 2.4 M_J object.

## Corrections to details
- **"Le's L is unread."** It is now read. Le defines L as the exterior velocity-space path length, with distance equal to relative rapidity, accumulating turns (p. 5, quoted above). So the lens's reading behind 1.27 against 1.083 (M-ENGINEER-08) is confirmed for the exterior Bondi frame.
- **The cavity-centre rapidity obeys a separate, sharp bound (Cor. 3.4).** It is looser than e^(−3C) at finite compactness and beats e^(−C) for 2m/R₀ > 8/9. C3 should not cite Le as showing photon-rocket cost "for the payload".
- **The energy quoted as the floor is not one.** 3.38e43 J is the photon-rocket energy, and a reaction-mass rocket at u ≈ 0.05 c needs 1.23 orders less energy, though 4.9× the mass ratio. C3 should state its floor as a mass ratio, not an energy.
- **Le's abstract also reports "A separate elastic model admits local self-gravitating recoil control with outward emission".** It is still emission, so it is consistent with C3.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives.** The isolated-system core survives, with strong support from the very source C3 cites: no exhaust, no change in Bondi momentum; and the whole-mass ratio is at least e^(Δrapidity) in the exterior frame. Self-propulsion is possible only as a gravitational-wave or photon rocket.
- **What falls.** The unconditional wording ("only by emitting", "no less than photon-rocket cost") fails:
  - for externally pushed shells, which the brief's start-and-stop level admits;
  - for energy rather than mass ratio;
  - possibly for the payload cavity's own rapidity in shells with 2m/R₀ > 8/9.
- **Remaining doubt.**
  - Le is a single-author preprint in its fourth substantially revised version. Self-gravitating settling after a turn is open, and growing normal modes are reported.
  - Whether Le's interior rapidity C corresponds to a payload velocity relative to the asymptotic exterior, at static ends, is not resolved here. If it does not, attack 3 collapses into a gauge statement and only attacks 1–2 stand.
  - Attack 1 rests on ordinary momentum exchange plus one design proposal (Lubin). Recycling factors above 1,000 rest on a search summary.
