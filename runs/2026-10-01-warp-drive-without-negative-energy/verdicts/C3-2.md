# Verdict on C3 (refuter 2, angle: scale)
claim: A positive-energy shell starts and stops only by emitting momentum (photon or gravitational-wave exhaust, e.g. Le's radiative steering), at no less than photon-rocket cost for its whole mass.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: Every start and stop must give the shell's whole mass a momentum γMv per leg. Without an outside momentum source (a self-contained or self-propelled shell), the only way to do that is photon or gravitational-wave exhaust, at no less than the photon-rocket mass ratio (1 + v)/(1 − v) for the whole mass. Outside sources (beamed light, a gravity assist, a push from a base) need no exhaust at all, and their energy floor is the kinetic energy, about 52 times lower at 0.04 c.

## Strongest attack
The heading makes two universal claims: the shell can start and stop "only by emitting momentum", and the cost can be "no less than photon-rocket". Both rest on the argument's first premise: "For an **isolated**, asymptotically flat system, Bondi/ADM 4-momentum changes only through flux at null infinity." The shell does not have to be isolated. The candidate admits this in its own in-principle verdict ("allowed by conservation at the 'start and stop' level, **with external propulsion counted**"). That admission contradicts the heading's "only by emitting".

Once momentum can come from outside the shell, the photon-rocket figure is no longer a floor. I computed the alternatives for the published shell (SI units; M = 4.511e27 kg, R₂ = 20 m) [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C3-2_scale.py]:

1. **Self-contained case: the candidate is right.** With E_ex ≥ |p_ex|c, the one-leg mass ratio is at least γ(1 + v) = 1.040833 at 0.04 c. A rocket with exhaust speed u has mass ratio exp(atanh v / u): 1.0833 at u = 0.5 c, 1.49 at 0.1 c and 3.80 at 0.03 c. All of these sit above the photon floor. A gravitational-wave rocket obeys the same bound, so a start and stop radiates at least 7.69% of the initial mass.
2. **Beamed push: no exhaust, shell mass ratio 1.** A perfect reflector needs about pc/2 = 8.1e42 J of beam energy per leg, half the photon rocket's exhaust. If the photons are recycled N times between the base and the shell, the energy falls as 1/N until it reaches the kinetic-energy floor (γ − 1)Mc² = 3.25e41 J per leg. For start plus stop that floor is 6.49e41 J, against 3.38e43 J for the photon rocket: **52 times less**. The ratio scales as about 2/v.
3. **Gravity assist: no exhaust, and the shell spends no energy.** The deflector must move at U ≥ 5,996 km/s. A grazing pass of a 1.4 M_sun neutron star (R = 12 km) at that relative speed turns the shell through 179.7°, which is enough to reach 0.04 c from rest. The shell survives the pass: the tidal acceleration across its 20 m is 4.3e9 m/s², against a surface self-gravity of 7.5e14 m/s². A 1 M_sun white dwarf gives at most 6,440 km/s per pass. A Sun-like star gives only 64 km/s.
4. **Braking by drag on the interstellar medium (n = 1 cm⁻³, full coupling).** Slowing from 0.04 c to 4e-4 c within 1 ly needs an effective area 2.0e16 m in radius, or 1.4e5 AU. In principle this is braking without exhaust. For a shell of 2.4 Jupiter masses it is absurd in practice.

So the core fails as a universal statement. It survives in the narrower form above: the momentum bill on the whole mass is unavoidable, and the photon-rocket mass ratio is the floor only for self-contained propulsion. The other routes do not open a cheap path in practice. Even the kinetic-energy floor is 1.1e21 world-years (assuming 6e20 J per year). The candidate's practical verdict ("eliminated by 2100") therefore stands, and only its universal "only" and "no less than" clauses are lost.

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle.** Momentum conservation makes a γMv-per-leg bill on the whole mass compulsory, and the candidate's argument is sound on that. Starting and stopping without any exhaust from the shell is allowed when the momentum comes from outside: a beam, a gravity assist or drag. The shell's mass ratio is then 1, and the energy floor is (γ − 1)Mc² per leg rather than about vMc². So "only by emitting" and "no less than photon-rocket cost" are false in general. They are true for an isolated, self-propelled shell, the level Le's construction occupies and the level that "any warp drive requires propulsion" [D-07] points to.
- **In practice.**
  - Every route is out of reach for the published shell. The kinetic-energy floor alone is 6.5e41 J, about 21 orders of magnitude above a year of world energy.
  - A single-bounce beam needs 6.7e7 L_sun for 10 years.
  - A gravity assist needs a neutron star or white dwarf moving at about 6,000 km/s relative to the departure frame.
  - Drag braking needs a capture area 1e5 AU across.
  - The candidate's practical elimination stands. External propulsion moves the gap by a factor of about 50 (1.7 orders of magnitude), not by orders enough to matter.

## Corrections to details
- The heading should be restricted to isolated, self-contained shells, or else "only by emitting momentum" should read "only by exchanging momentum (by exhaust, or with an external source)".
- "World-years" depends on the assumed annual world energy. At 6.0e20 J per year the exhaust is 5.6e22 world-years. The candidate's 5.7e22 implies about 5.9e20 J per year. This is immaterial.
- The other supporting numbers check out:
  - mass ratio 1.0833 and 3.38e43 J at 0.04 c;
  - 1.050 at 0.0239 c;
  - at least 7.7% radiated for a gravitational-wave rocket;
  - payload-to-shell bill ratio of 4.5e22.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives:**
  - the momentum requirement of γMv per leg on the whole mass, shell plus payload;
  - the photon-rocket mass ratio as the floor for any self-contained exhaust, photons or gravitational waves alike;
  - the conclusion that the shell multiplies the payload-alone propulsion bill by about 4.5e22 whatever the mechanism (that ratio is just M_shell/M_payload);
  - the practical elimination.
- **Remaining doubt:**
  - Whether an external push on a strong-field shell keeps its energy conditions during acceleration is unsolved, which is C4's territory.
  - My beam and gravity-assist estimates treat the shell as a test body in flat space, or in Newtonian hyperbolic deflection. That is adequate for the momentum and energy bookkeeping. It does not address the shell's internal response.
