## Strongest attack

The attack from scale is on the figure of merit the reframe proposes, "shift per unit compactness", and on the answer it gives, "about 0.07·C". I also checked whether the counterflow reading costs more than C7 says.

1. **β/C is not a geometry-free number.** I combined the verified two-sheet law (M-ENGINEER-05: β = (4GP/c³)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂)) with P = (M/2)·uγ. This gives β = C·(uγ/c)·(R₂/R₁ − 1), with C = 2GM/(c²R₂). C7's "counterflow law β ~ C·(u/c)" therefore leaves out a geometric factor (R₂/R₁ − 1), which happens to equal 1 for the rebuild (R₁ = 10 m, R₂ = 20 m). At the same counterflow speed u = 0.06 c, β/C runs from 0.003 at R₂/R₁ = 1.05 to 0.24 at R₂/R₁ = 5, where the inner sheet's local compactness (0.84) is still below Buchdahl's 8/9. That is a factor of 80 at fixed C. The candidate's prediction "β_max ∝ C·Δ/R₂" disagrees with its own linear law, which scales as C·u·Δ/R₁. The two agree only for thin walls [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C7-2_counterflow_scale.py].
2. **"About 0.07·C" is not the largest drag positive matter can make.** The NEC cap of the rebuild, β_crit/C = 0.0712 (M-CONSTRAINTS-13), corresponds in the two-sheet law to a counterflow of only u = 0.072 c. The DEC allows any u < c. The same linear law gives β/C = 0.31 at u = 0.3 c, which is 4.4 times the NEC cap, and 0.58 at u = 0.5 c, 8.1 times the cap. These numbers stretch linear theory, but they show that the 0.07·C cap belongs to the Fuchs/rebuild profile, in which a static TOV shell has a shift imposed on it. It is not a bound on counter-streaming positive matter.
3. **C7's flow-energy number is internally inconsistent.** C7 quotes 6.8e34 kg m/s per sign for the actual eq.-(28) profile (M-MECHANIST-03). It then computes E_flow from the two-sheet minimum P = 4.04e34 kg m/s, which gives 0.060 Mc². For the profile it actually describes, the DEC floor is 2cP₊ = 4.05e43 J = 0.100 Mc², and a whole-mass counterflow of 0.100 c, not 0.060 c. The two-sheet value is the geometric minimum, not the Fuchs shell's own requirement.
4. **The cost-against-a-rocket half holds at any of these numbers.** An ideal photon rocket that starts and stops the shell plus payload at 0.04 c needs 3.76e26 kg of propellant. The 1e5 kg payload alone needs 8.3e3 kg, a ratio of 4.5e22. Spinning up the counterflow alone (u = 0.10 c: (γ − 1)Mc² = 2.0e42 J) costs 2.7e21 times the payload-alone photon-rocket energy at 0.04 c, and still moves nothing, because the cavity shift is gauge (M-IDEALIZER-01). Turning the poloidal flow at the wall ends adds a dynamic stress ρu² of 4.9e37 to 1.4e38 Pa. That is 1–4% of the rebuild's own peak tangential stress, 3.9e39 Pa: an extra load, not a new barrier.

None of this defeats the heading. The mechanism (gravitomagnetic frame dragging by counter-streaming currents with zero net momentum) and the redirection of the question toward frame drag per unit of positive matter and toward cost against a rocket both stand. What fails is the scalar figure of merit as C7 states it, and its numerical answer. Those belong in the argument, not the heading.

**Zero net momentum is exact, not only linear.** In the rebuild the shift has compact support inside R₂, and the exterior is static Schwarzschild, so K_ij = 0 there. The ADM momentum P_i = (1/8π)∮(K_ij − K h_ij)dS^j at infinity therefore vanishes at every order in β. The second-order worry in C7's "evidence against" applies to how the momentum density is distributed inside the wall, not to the net value. This strengthens the core.

## In principle vs. in practice

- **In principle:** standard GR. Linear translational gravitomagnetism from counter-streaming DEC-respecting matter makes a uniform interior shift and no exterior momentum. Nothing in that needs negative energy, and the reframe correctly treats it as a gauge-level internal drag, not propulsion.
- **In practice:** the drag needs a 2.4 Jupiter-mass shell at about 1.5e23 kg/m³, circulating at 0.06–0.10 c. Its only payload-relevant effect is a clock deficit and a Sagnac flux. A tabletop analogue is 26 orders short in β (M-ENGINEER-11). As transport, it loses to a photon rocket for the bare payload by a factor of 4.5e22 in propellant mass. TRL 1.

## Corrections to details

- **Counterflow law:** β = C·(uγ/c)·(R₂/R₁ − 1), not β ~ C·(u/c). The two coincide only for R₂ = 2R₁. "Shift per unit compactness" has to be quoted together with the aspect ratio and the counterflow speed.
- **The cap:** "about 0.07·C" is the NEC cap of the toolkit's Fuchs-profile rebuild. It is not "the largest interior frame drag positive matter of compactness C can produce". Linear counterflow with DEC matter allows several times more (β/C ≈ 0.31 at u = 0.3 c for R₂/R₁ = 2). The true positive-matter cap needs the nonlinear solve C7 itself names.
- **Scaling prediction:** "β_max ∝ C·Δ/R₂" should be C·u·Δ/R₁ in the linear model. The two differ for thick walls.
- **E_flow:** 0.060 Mc² (0.060 c) is the two-sheet minimum. For the eq.-(28) profile whose 6.8e34 kg m/s per sign C7 quotes, it is 0.100 Mc² (0.100 c).
- **Zero net momentum:** exact to all orders in the ADM sense, since the exterior has K_ij = 0. Only the internal distribution of the currents is unchecked at second order.

## What survives and remaining doubt

The heading survives: the positive-energy "warp effect" is internal gravitomagnetic frame dragging by counter-streaming wall currents with zero net (ADM) momentum. The engineering question it leaves, how much drag per unit of positive matter and whether that beats a rocket, has the answer "no" for the rocket comparison by about 22 orders of magnitude, whatever the drag. Two doubts remain:
- The largest β per unit C is not settled. It depends on aspect ratio and counterflow speed, and linear theory at C ≈ 0.33 is good only to about a factor of 2.
- Since the drag never carries the payload, its size changes no feasibility verdict.
