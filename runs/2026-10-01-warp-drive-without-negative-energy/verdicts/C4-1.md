# Verdict on C4 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: A compact positive-energy shell, pushed externally, drags its interior inertial frames so the payload accelerates without felt g-forces: the one merit where it might beat a rocket.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: cited-evidence
narrowed: A pushed compact positive-energy shell drags its interior inertial frames only partially, so a co-moving payload still feels a fraction of roughly g00_inside/g00_outside of the shell's acceleration (about 0.6 for the published shell, falling toward about 0.1 only near the Buchdahl compactness and never to zero outside a horizon); that reduction, not g-free acceleration, is the merit that might beat a rocket.

## Strongest attack
The candidate's own strong-field source contradicts "without felt g-forces." Lynden-Bell, Bičák & Katz (1999) is the paper C4 cites as the electromagnetic analogue. It also contains the only exact strong-field gravitational result on linear dragging in the candidate's evidence. That result is a ratio: a free test body inside an accelerated massive shell accelerates *in sympathy but less rapidly than the shell*. Its acceleration relative to the shell's frame is reduced by the factor (V_in/V_out)^-2, which is the interior g00 ratio. This reduction becomes complete only in the limit b → 0, which the authors identify as an extreme Reissner–Nordström black hole. Pfister, Frauendiener & Hengge (2005) is C4's other strong-field source. Its abstract says the linear-dragging results "compare favourably with the corresponding results for rotational dragging". Rotational dragging (Brill–Cohen) is complete only at horizon formation.

So for any shell outside its own horizon, the dragging fraction κ is strictly below 1. A payload that rides with the shell needs a contact force of (1 − κ) times the shell's acceleration. The candidate's linear coefficient κ = 4GM/(c²R) gives 1.33 at R = 10 m, which would mean the payload outruns its own shell. That is the linear theory failing, not "strong dragging." The exact result excludes κ ≥ 1.

Applying the g00 law gives these felt fractions [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C4-1_drag_fraction.py]:
- **Published shell (rebuild centre lapse N = 0.761):** F = N² = 0.58. The payload feels 58% of the push.
- **At the Buchdahl compactness 8/9:** F = 0.11 under the neutral-shell transfer of the law. Under the extremal-charged LBK geometry itself it is 0.31.
- **At the 48/49 cap for DEC matter [D-27]:** F = 0.020.

"Without felt g-forces" is therefore false at every admissible compactness. What survives is a reduction of the felt acceleration by a factor between about 1.7 (published shell) and about 50 (at the DEC compactness cap). Even that range depends on carrying LBK's charged-dust law over to a neutral, externally pushed shell. LBK themselves warn that the pushing stresses "will produce their own gravity".

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle (established GR):**
  - Partial interior dragging by an accelerated massive shell is established. It appears in Einstein's weak-field formulae and in the exact conformastatic solutions of LBK 1999.
  - Complete dragging, which is what "no felt g-forces" requires, needs the horizon limit. That limit is excluded for a neutral shell by Buchdahl-type bounds (8/9 for isotropic matter, 48/49 with the DEC [D-27]).
  - So the in-principle claim survives only as a reduction factor F ≈ g00_in/g00_out, bounded below by about 0.02, and that bound itself depends on carrying LBK's law over to a neutral shell.
  - No time-dependent strong-field solution of a neutral pushed shell exists [D-08] [D-15]. The pusher's own stresses alter the result (LBK p. 6), so even F is model-dependent.
- **In practice:**
  - At the published shell, F ≈ 0.58. Holding the payload to 1 g felt allows the shell 1.73 g. That cuts the α Cen trip from 6.00 to 5.38 yr Earth-frame and from 3.58 to 2.55 yr ship time.
  - Even at the DEC cap (F = 0.02, 49 g), the Earth-frame time only falls to 4.41 yr against the 4.37 yr light floor. The real gain is ship time (0.21 yr).
  - All of this costs a shell 4.5e22 times the payload mass, with the C2/C3 gaps of 22–28 orders unchanged.
  - The candidate concedes that the merit is not specific to warp geometry: any massive shell gives it.

## Corrections to details
- **κ = 4GM/(c²R) and its values 0.67 and 1.33.** These are linear, harmonic-gauge, non-retarded numbers. Einstein's de Donder formulae with retardation give 11/3 rather than 4 (LBK p. 7). LBK's exact static-equivalent result gives κ ≈ 2GM/(c²R) at weak field, (1 + m/b)^-2 ≈ 1 − 2m/b. The weak-field coefficient therefore depends on the pushing mechanism. κ = 1.33 is not "strong dragging"; it is outside the coefficient's domain.
- **"Strong-field models exist in which the interior ... stays flat and test particles are dragged" (Pfister 2005).** This is true, but the same abstract benchmarks the result against rotational dragging. Rotational dragging is incomplete below the horizon (Brill–Cohen fraction 0.24 at C = 1/3 and 0.95 at C = 48/49, by the calc). The source does not support near-complete dragging at C ≈ 0.3–0.7.
- **The prediction "κ → 1" at C ≈ 0.3–0.9.** Under the cited strong-field law, κ is about 0.3–0.9 over that range. The candidate's "well below" holds only near C ≈ 0.9.
- **LBK 1999 cited only as "the EM analogue".** The paper's main gravitational result is the exact reduction factor quoted above, and that result bounds C4 against it.
- **Sub-hypothesis (b) (lapse 0.761 → 83.1 yr).** This is not attacked here. It is an ordinary gravitational redshift that any massive shell provides.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives:** a pushed massive shell partially drags its interior frames. A co-moving payload feels a reduced acceleration, by a factor of about the interior g00 ratio. This is a genuine but modest merit in principle, about 1.7× at the published shell and at most about 50× at the DEC compactness cap. It does not belong to the warp shift.
- **Remaining doubts:**
  - The reduction law is taken from an extremal charged-dust shell balanced in an external field (LBK) and transferred to a neutral, externally pushed shell. That transfer is our assumption.
  - The pusher's stresses and retardation could move F either way.
  - Pfister et al.'s full strong-field formula was not accessible.
  - A nonlinear evolution of a neutral pushed shell, the candidate's own decisive test, would settle the factor. Nothing found suggests it would give F = 0 outside a horizon.
