# Verdict on C2 (refuter 2, angle: scale)
claim: The Bobrick–Martire / Fuchs 2024 warp shell is a valid positive-energy coasting vehicle: all four energy conditions hold for all observers below a compactness-set shift cap.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: In the run's toolkit rebuild (T = G/8π, edge smoothing ≥ 0.6 m, sampled points), the shifted Fuchs shell needs a stress-energy that meets NEC, WEC, SEC and DEC for β ≲ 0.024 at C = 1/3, so it is an energy-condition-clean geometry; it is not shown to be a valid vehicle, because no known matter can carry the stress-to-energy ratio it needs at any size, and no matter model or stability analysis exists.

## Strongest attack
The energy-condition clause holds up as far as the math checks reach. The word "valid" does not. The brief's premise 6 counts a construction as physical only if it has a matter model, the DEC, stability, and "densities and stresses that some matter can bear". The scale attack goes after that last criterion, and does it in a way that changing the size cannot escape.

1. **The load is fixed by compactness and does not depend on size.** The run's engineering gaps (27.6 orders in stress, 5.8 orders in density) are absolute. M-ENGINEER-04 shows they shrink as the shell grows at fixed C, reaching nuclear density at R₂ = 15 km. That route does not help. The quantity a material must bear is the ratio of anisotropic stress to energy density, |p_t − p_r|/ε. TOV self-similarity holds that ratio exactly fixed. In the rebuild it is 0.539 at its peak and 0.0228 averaged over volume, at every scale from R₂ = 20 m to 2e7 m [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C2-2_stress_ratio.py]. A free shell must carry this load in shear: isotropic pressure cannot support it [D-74]. So the matter needs a shear strength of about 0.02–0.5 of its own rest-energy density.
2. **No known matter comes close** (same calc, part E). These ratios are strength divided by ρc²:
   - graphene: 6.4e-10;
   - nanodiamond: 1.5e-9;
   - 1 TPa over diamond: 3.2e-9;
   - neutron-star crust, the strongest solid known: (2–4)e-6. This uses a Coulomb-crystal shear modulus (μ/ε = 1.7e-5 to 4.2e-5) times a breaking strain of 0.1. That strain value is quoted below.

   The resulting shortfalls are:

   | Load measure | vs neutron-star crust | vs graphene (best self-bound material) |
   |---|---|---|
   | peak | 5.1 orders | 8.9 orders |
   | mean | 3.7 orders | 7.5 orders |

   Neutron-star crust also exists only under neutron-star confinement.
3. **The only way to cut the load is to cut the warp.** In weak gravity the load scales as 1.06·C at the peak and 0.045·C in the mean (part C). The shift cap scales as about 0.07–0.08·C (M-CONSTRAINTS-13; lens data). Setting the load equal to what a material can bear caps the interior shift (part G):
   - neutron-star crust: β ≲ 3e-7 c to 8e-6 c (96 m/s to 2.3 km/s);
   - graphene: β ≲ 5e-11 c to 1e-9 c (0.014 to 0.34 m/s).

   The published 0.02 c needs 7e6 m/s. A shell that known matter could hold up has a "warp" no larger than a slow walk.
4. **The cap's high-compactness end is not available in this construction.** The candidate takes its cap estimates to the Buchdahl compactness 8/9 (≲ 0.04–0.08). In the rebuild, the zero-shift shell itself fails the DEC well before 8/9 [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C2-2_dec_vs_C.py]:

   | Smoothing span | max\|p\|/ε at C = 0.45 | at C = 0.5 | DEC fails from |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 1 m | 0.989 | 1.219 | C ≈ 0.46 |
   | 2 m | — | — | C = 0.6 (ratio 1.001) |
   | 3 m | — | — | C ≈ 0.7 |

   The C = 0.5 failure is converged: 1.219 at N = 8001 and 1.223 at N = 16001. Wider smoothing spans spread the matter across most of the 10 m wall. So the "compactness-set cap" is known only for C ≲ 0.45–0.65, which caps β at about 0.03–0.04. The DEC margin also depends on the toolkit's own smoothing choice, which the paper does not print: at C = 1/3 the DEC fails for span_P ≤ 0.5 m (max|p|/ε = 1.15–1.90) and holds from 0.6 m.

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle (established GR).** The metric's required T_μν passes NEC, WEC, SEC and DEC at sampled points for β ≲ 0.024 at C = 1/3. Nothing in established physics forbids a material whose shear strength is a few percent to half of its rest-energy density, and the DEC (|p| ≤ ε) is respected. But no such matter is known. The only relativistic elastic-shell treatment is a weak-gravity one. Barzegar et al.'s Error 18 (that the TOV equations are not actually solved) is unanswered [D-31], and stability is uncomputed [D-72]. So "valid" in the brief's premise-6 sense is unestablished, not shown. The surviving content is an energy-condition-clean geometry.
- **In practice (by 2100).** It is eliminated, and making the shell bigger cannot rescue it. The scale-invariant stress-to-energy shortfall is 7.5–8.9 orders against the best self-bound materials. The run's absolute gaps stand on top of that:
  - 22 orders in mass;
  - 23.8 orders in energy;
  - 27.6 orders in stress at R₂ = 20 m.

  Any shell that known solids could support has β ≲ 1e-9 c, so its interior "warp" is below 1 m/s. TRL 1–2.

## Corrections to details
- **"Cap at the Buchdahl compactness 8/9: ≲ 0.04–0.08 (0.15)".** The rebuilt construction has no clean zero-shift shell there. The DEC fails at C ≈ 0.46 with 1 m smoothing and at C ≥ 0.6–0.7 with 2–3 m smoothing. The cap is defined only up to C ≈ 0.45–0.65, which gives β ≲ 0.03–0.04 (using β_crit/C ≈ 0.066–0.072). An Andréasson/Buchdahl-limit extrapolation assumes a matter profile that this construction does not supply.
- **"Edge must be smoothed over ≥ 1 m".** The threshold for the published shell lies between 0.5 m (fails, 1.152) and 0.6 m (holds, 0.968). The threshold also rises with C, so it is not a fixed tolerance.
- **"No size escapes the trade".** This is right, and the dimensionless form is sharper. The required |p_t − p_r|/ε (0.54 peak, 0.023 mean) is exactly independent of size at fixed C. It is the binding materials figure, more so than the absolute 27.6-order stress gap, which falls as R⁻².

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives:** the narrowed core above. On the rebuild, at sampled points and with the toolkit's smoothing, a static shift below about 0.072·C keeps all four pointwise energy conditions.
- **What fails:** "valid vehicle". On the brief's physicality criteria it is unsupported in principle, for want of a matter model, a stability analysis and source consistency. In practice it is eliminated by a size-independent stress-to-energy shortfall of at least 3.7 orders, even against neutron-star crust.
- **Doubt 1, rebuild only.** Every number comes from the toolkit rebuild, whose smoothing differs from the paper's unprinted spans. A different edge profile changes the peak load, by a factor of 3 between the 1 m and 3 m spans. It leaves the mean load (0.045·C) and the conclusion unchanged.
- **Doubt 2, neutron-star crust ratios.** These are my order-of-magnitude Coulomb-crystal estimates. They are not quoted values.
- **Doubt 3, verdict grade.** I chose "weakened" rather than "refuted" because the energy-condition half of the core claim stands. The scale attack defeats "valid vehicle" only on the brief's materials criterion, not on any law.
