# Verdict on C8 (refuter 2, angle: scale)
claim: The binding obstacle to faster-than-light warp travel is causal access to the bubble's front wall, not the energy sign; operationally, travellers cannot hasten arrival even with negative energy.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: Causal access forbids a traveller from creating or steering an on-demand superluminal bubble, and from hastening the first one-way arrival at an unprepared destination, even with negative energy. But a traveller who lays a Krasnikov tube on a subluminal outbound leg hastens the return arrival, and for that route the negative-energy requirement, not causal access, is the binding obstacle.

## Strongest attack
C8's heading makes two claims. One: causal access is the *binding* obstacle and the energy sign is not. Two: even with negative energy, travellers cannot hasten arrival. Both fail on scale once the route is laid inside the traveller's own causal future, which is exactly the Krasnikov construction.

1. **The source C8 cites for its operational claim says the opposite in its next sentence.** C8 quotes Krasnikov 1998 ("the traveller cannot hasten reaching the destination") but stops there. The same abstract continues: "Nevertheless, it is perhaps possible for him to make an arbitrarily long round-trip within an arbitrarily short (from the point of view of a terrestrial observer) time."
   - Everett & Roman 1997 work out the return explicitly: the ship arrives home at Earth time t_E = Dδ, which "can be made arbitrarily small".
   - The tube is laid by the ship itself on the outbound leg, so all of it lies in the causal future of the launch event. The causal-access problem C8 makes binding does not arise.
   - The return leg B→A arrives before light sent from B when the ship leaves B, through the undisturbed exterior. That is the brief's own travel-time definition of faster than light.
   - So "travellers cannot hasten arrival even with negative energy" is false as stated. It holds only for the first one-way arrival.
2. **Once causal access is bought with an ordinary subluminal leg, the energy is what binds, by 37 to 70 orders of magnitude.** [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C8-2_causal_vs_energy_scale.py]
   - **Cost of causal access.** The outbound leg is a 1e5 kg payload flown to α Cen. An ideal photon rocket that accelerates and stops needs 2.0e21 J of fuel at 0.1c (about 3 world-years of primary energy at 6e20 J/yr). At 0.99c it needs 1.8e24 J, which is the speed a short Earth-clock round trip requires. That is hard, but it is a known kind of problem.
   - **Cost of the tube.** We applied Everett & Roman's estimate E ≈ −α ρ_max D/ε to a tube to α Cen (D = 4.13e16 m, ρ_max = 1 m), with the quantum-inequality (QI) wall ε = 100 L_P and their α = 0.01. It needs |E|/c² = 3.4e74 kg = 1.7e44 M_sun, about 3.7e21 times the critical-density mass inside the Hubble volume (9.2e52 kg). In energy that is 3.1e91 J, 70.2 orders of magnitude above the causal-access leg.
   - **Even ignoring the QI** and allowing a 1 m wall, it still needs 5.6e41 kg = 2.8e11 M_sun (5.0e58 J), 37 orders above the leg.
   - **The sign is needed in every design.** Everett & Roman show the tube itself needs "unphysically thin layers of negative energy density". So for every faster-than-light route that has a known construction (on-demand bubble, or a tube laid by the traveller), negative energy is necessary. Causal access is necessary only for the on-demand bubble.
   - "Binding" therefore points the wrong way. Causal access binds one design, and money and time can buy a way around it. The energy sign and size bind all of them, and nothing can buy a way around them.
3. **Internal tension.** C8's own argument names "pre-placed infrastructure along the route (a Krasnikov tube)" as the real alternative. That concedes a way around the causal-access obstacle that leaves the energy obstacle fully in place, which contradicts "not the energy sign" in its heading.

## Evidence
- **Krasnikov 1998,** PRD 57, 4760, arXiv:gr-qc/9511068. ACCESS: abstract via fetch_text. Verbatim: "It is argued that under some reasonable assumptions in globally hyperbolic spacetimes the traveller cannot hasten reaching the destination. Nevertheless, it is perhaps possible for him to make an arbitrarily long round-trip within an arbitrarily short (from the point of view of a terrestrial observer) time."
- **Everett & Roman 1997,** PRD 56, 2100, arXiv:gr-qc/9702049. ACCESS: full text via fetch_text.
  - Abstract: the Alcubierre observer "can neither create a warp bubble on demand nor control one once it has been created. In addition, such a bubble requires negative energy densities. ... Krasnikov tubes, like warp bubbles and traversable wormholes, also involve unphysically thin layers of negative energy density, as well as large total negative energies, and therefore probably cannot be realized in practice."
  - p. 7: "the ship returns to Earth at a time tE as measured by clocks on the Earth given by tE = D + ∆tr = Dδ ... the interval between departure and return, as measured by observers on the Earth, can be made arbitrarily small by appropriate choice of the parameter δ."
  - p. 17: "one requires negative energies of the order of 10^16 galactic masses just to make a Krasnikov tube 1 meter long and 1 meter wide. For a tube that stretches from here to the nearest star, i.e. D ≈ 4 × 10^16 m, we need E ≈ −10^32 M_galaxy."

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle.** C8's horizon fact is correct: the comoving Killing norm changes sign at r = 99.45 m (M-EXAMINER-02, verified). So an on-demand bubble launched from rest cannot reach its own front wall. But the claim that causal access binds "even with negative energy" fails in principle. A Krasnikov tube is laid entirely within the traveller's causal future and, granted negative energy, gives a genuine return-leg time advance. According to Everett & Roman's abstract, a single tube creates no closed timelike curves, while two tubes can make a time machine. Ruling out the tube needs the energy conditions, the QIs, or chronology protection for the two-tube case. It does not need causal access.
- **In practice.** The causal-access workaround costs 1e21–1e24 J and 4.4–44 yr of subluminal flight for a 1e5 kg payload. That is beyond 2026 capability, but it is a rocket problem. The negative energy is 1e58–1e91 J, 37–70 orders beyond that, against a laboratory capability in which no macroscopic negative energy has been demonstrated (dossier D-16). On any scale the energy requirement binds; causal access does not.

## Corrections to details
- C8 calls Krasnikov tubes "pre-placed infrastructure", which suggests someone other than the traveller must build them. In the Krasnikov and Everett–Roman construction, the traveller's own outbound leg lays the tube.
- C8's "evidence against" says Krasnikov is cited from the abstract only. The abstract's own next sentence is the one that undercuts the operational clause.
- **Our scaling numbers are not exact.** The 70-order gap uses Everett & Roman's order-of-magnitude α and their QI wall ε ≈ 100 L_P. A different α or σ shifts it by a few orders, not by 37.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives.** Causal access is a real and independent obstruction:
  - it bars on-demand creation and steering of a superluminal Alcubierre-type bubble (Everett & Roman);
  - under Krasnikov's "reasonable assumptions" in globally hyperbolic spacetimes, it bars hastening the first one-way arrival at an unprepared destination.
  
  That is a correct and useful reframe of the brief's travel-time definition for launched bubbles.
- **What fails.** The ranking ("binding ..., not the energy sign") and the blanket operational clause both fail.
- **Remaining doubts:**
  - The tube energy estimate is Everett & Roman's rough one (Eq. 51), not a full calculation. Even the generous 1 m wall leaves a 37-order gap, so the ordering is robust.
  - Krasnikov's "perhaps possible" is hedged. Everett & Roman's 3+1 generalization supports it, with the caveat that two tubes make closed timelike curves, which chronology protection [conjecture] might forbid. If it does, the remaining obstacle is again not causal access.
  - I did not examine Krasnikov's "reasonable assumptions" for the one-way case. This verdict does not rest on them.
