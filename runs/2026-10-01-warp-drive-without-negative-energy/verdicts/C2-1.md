# Verdict on C2 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: The Bobrick–Martire / Fuchs 2024 warp shell is a valid positive-energy coasting vehicle: all four energy conditions hold for all observers below a compactness-set shift cap.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: cited-evidence
narrowed: A Fuchs-type shifted matter shell can be built whose geometric stress-energy G/8π passes NEC, WEC, SEC and DEC at every sampled point at interior shift β ≲ 0.024 c. This holds only for some smoothing and buffer choices, the cap depends on the wall profile and not on compactness alone, and the published 2024 construction itself is contested: one independent observer-robust reproduction finds NEC-violating Type-IV stress in its smoothing tail. It has no matter model.

## Strongest attack

The core claim covers "the Fuchs 2024 warp shell" and says "all four energy conditions hold for all observers". The only independent, observer-robust reproduction of the published construction contradicts that. It followed Fuchs's own five-step recipe at the paper's v_s = 0.02.

- **What Le found.** An T. Le, arXiv:2605.25417, v1 (25 May 2026) and v2 (20 Jun 2026), titled "On the boundary cost of source-consistent warp shells". The bulk shell r ∈ [10, 20] m is clean. In the smoothing tail beyond R₂, 22 of 25 probes are Hawking–Ellis Type IV. Type IV has no causal eigenvector, so the NEC fails there and the WEC, SEC and DEC fail with it. The worst probe is at r ≈ 23.9 m. The result does not depend on the smoothing kernel: Le reran it with a variance-matched boxcar moving average, the paper's own kind of filter, and got the same tail.
- **What C2's headline evidence covers.** The "0 of 850" pass covers only r ∈ [10, 20] m, so it never samples the tail. The run's own wider scan (r = 9–24 m) does reach the tail. At β = 0 and at β = 0.02 it flags "robust" NEC/WEC/SEC failures (4 at h = 0.05 m) and DEC failures (4–9) at r = 24.0 m, by the tool's own FD-error test. The constraints lens set these aside as noise, since |T| ~ 1e-12 m⁻² there. That may be right for the rebuild's compact-support smoothing. Even so, the run's evidence does not certify the tail, and the tail is exactly where the independent reproduction finds failure.
- **The clean independent result tests a different object.** Le's later paper (arXiv:2602.18023v6) reports a clean Fuchs row. It tests a "covariant modification and C² regularization" of the shell. It samples only the wall band 0.1 ≤ f ≤ 0.9, which excludes the tail. And its margins are "sampled Type-I slacks, with no global enclosure". So it does not answer the tail result.

Two further points narrow the claim.

1. **The cap is not "compactness-set".**
   - Fuchs's eq. (27) requires a buffer Rb > 0, and the paper never gives its value.
   - In the run's own calculation the cap falls from 0.0239 at Rb = 0 to 0.016 at Rb = 1 m and to 0.010 at Rb = 2 m. Both of those caps lie below the paper's β_warp = 0.02.
   - The cap also moves with the smoothing span (0.022–0.025) and with wall thickness and profile: β_max ≈ k·C·(Δ/R₂), with k = 0.18–0.37, and β_max → 0 for thin walls.
   - So compactness sets the cap only jointly with these construction choices. Whether the published shell passes even at 0.02 depends on a parameter the paper does not state.
2. **"Valid" is not established beyond G/8π.**
   - Barzegar, Buchert & Vigneron (Error 18, preprint) say the construction "is not a solution to Einstein's equations", because the TOV step with isotropic pressure is never solved.
   - Fuchs's own text says the final ρ and p_i are read off G_μν. So the energy conditions are a property of a G-method (geometry-first) stress tensor, with no matter model behind it.
   - Le's v1 standard, which asks for an explicit matter model and frame-independent margins, finds that "none passes the full standard", and singles out the Fuchs shell as failing in its smoothing tail.

What these attacks do not defeat: a shifted shell of this type can satisfy all four conditions pointwise for some smoothing and buffer choices. The run's rebuild shows this, Fuchs's own sampled check agrees, and so does Le's regularized wall-band panel. So the verdict is weakened, not refuted.

## In principle vs. in practice

- **In principle.**
  - Pointwise energy conditions for G/8π below a cap of about 0.02 c are supported for suitably smoothed shells: by the rebuild, Fuchs's sampled check and Le's wall-band panel.
  - They are contested for the published construction, because of Le's tail Type IV.
  - None of this is certified: every test is a sample, and none reaches interval enclosure over the tail.
  - "Valid" in the brief's sense needs a matter model obeying its own equations of motion. None is supplied.
    - Barzegar's Error 18 attacks the source step.
    - Le's elastic-shell equilibria (2605.25417v3) are demonstrated only "in sufficiently weak gravity", whereas this shell has 2GM/(c²R₂) = 0.333.
    - Stability is unaddressed.
  - Under established physics, then, the claim survives as "a G-method metric that passes pointwise tests for some profiles", not as a validated vehicle.
- **In practice.** Unchanged from the candidate's own numbers: 2.4 M_J, peak stress 27.6 orders above 1 TPa, TRL 1–2. Not buildable by 2100.

## Corrections to details

- **"Paper's own speed fails" misreads the paper.**
  - Fuchs et al. claim energy-condition compliance at β_warp = 0.02 (p. 17), not 0.04.
  - The 0.04 c of Table 1 is only the comparison speed for the time-delay study ("Comparison of time delay between different warp models and the matter shell for vwarp = 0.04 c", p. 26).
  - The rebuild's pass at 0.02 therefore agrees with the paper's own energy-condition claim. It does not show the paper overstating its speed. The real tension is the unstated Rb > 0 described above.
- **"0 of 850 sampled points" covers r ∈ [10, 20] m only.** It should not be read as a whole-spacetime pass. The run's 416-point wide scan has flagged tail failures at r ≈ 24 m, which the lens judged to be noise.
- **"Matches Le's sampled Type-I slack of +0.021 … for a regularized shell".** Le's object is covariantly modified and C²-regularized, and he samples only the 0.1 ≤ f ≤ 0.9 band. It is not an independent check of the published shell's tail.
- **The dossier misclassifies arXiv:2605.25417 (D-74).**
  - Its v1 and v2 are a critique of the Fuchs shell's boundary, which is what the brief's lead said.
  - Only v3 is the elastic-shells paper.
  - The brief's lead was correct for v1 and v2, and the dossier's "not a critique of the 2024 shell" holds only for v3.

## What survives and remaining doubt

- **What survives.**
  - Shells of the Fuchs type with a positive ADM mass and a small interior shift can pass all four pointwise energy conditions for the geometric stress tensor at β ≲ 0.02 c.
  - This holds for some smoothing choices: a compact-support filter, Rb ≈ 0.
  - The cap scales roughly with compactness × wall-thickness ratio.
- **Remaining doubt.**
  - Le's tail result rests on a single preprint, carried through v1 and v2 and then dropped in v3 without comment. It may be an artefact of the reproduction's smoothing tail, or a real feature of the published moving-average construction. The rebuild and Le disagree on the tail's magnitude: about 1e-12 m⁻², which looks like noise, against a margin about 100× the bulk margin.
- **What would settle it.** An interval-certified scan of the published moving-average shell that covers r ∈ [R₂, R₂ + 4 m] at β = 0 and at β = 0.02. It would show whether the tail violation is shift-induced, a smoothing artefact, or absent.
- **Untouched by any evidence found.** Whether any matter model sources the shifted shell, and whether it is stable. Until both are settled, "valid vehicle" is not established; only "metric with pointwise energy-condition-compliant G/8π for some profiles" is.
