# Verdict on C1 (refuter 2, angle: scale)
claim: No warp spacetime moves a payload faster than light without NEC violation; positive-energy subluminal shells move payloads only as ordinary massive vehicles.
status: final
verdict: survives
basis: calculation
narrowed: none

## Strongest attack

C1's faster-than-light half rests on two things: the theorems (SSV for unit lapse; Olum, Gao–Wald and Visser–Bassett–Liberati for lapse ≠ 1), and one worked shell family, the toolkit rebuild of Fuchs et al. 2024 with R₁ = 10 m and R₂ = 20 m. In that family the NEC fails at β ≈ 0.0239, while even the interior forward light speed exceeds c only for β > 0.210, a factor of 8.8. The scale attack asks whether this gap is a property of the design, not of physics. If the wall is re-proportioned (thicker, thinner, a smaller cavity, compactness anywhere from 0.01 up to the Buchdahl end), does the NEC-safe shift (what can be achieved) ever reach the shift a light advance needs (what is required)? If some design closed the gap, the worked family would stop illustrating the theorems, and C1's (A) argument would rest on the theorems' hypotheses alone, with their known finite-region and generic-condition gaps.

For the (B) half, the attack asks whether a NEC-clean shift gives a payload anything at engineering scale that a plain massive shell of the same mass does not: travel distance, proper time, or a cheaper propulsion bill.

Result: the gap narrows with design but never closes. The worst case for C1 is about 3.4×, and that is against a necessary condition only. The payload "gains" are bounded by the cavity size and are worth nothing. The core claim stands. The 21–80× and 8.8× margins are profile-specific numbers, not floors.

What the table shows:
- **The minimum.** The smallest factor found is 3.38, for a weak-field shell with a 0.5 m cavity.
- **Thickening the wall helps, but the gain saturates.** The factor runs 4.09 → 3.88 → 3.81 as R₁ goes 2 → 1 → 0.5 m. Approaching the Buchdahl end widens it again (6.4 at C = 0.8, 7.7 at C = 0.85).
- **The trend has a physical reason.** The interior-speed threshold grows like the potential, β_int ≈ (0.6–0.75)·C. The NEC-safe shift is set by whether the wall's momentum density stays below its (ρ + p), which also scales with C. So the ratio has an O(1) floor and does not run off to zero.
- **The thin-shell analytic check agrees.** With interior lapse √(1 − C), β_int = C/2. Against the run's cap ratios of 0.07–0.09, that gives a factor of 5.6–7.1, and 5.6 at C = 8/9 against the generous cap of 0.08 (M-DECOMPOSER-09).
- **The real requirement is larger still.** β_int is only the interior criterion. The net advance at D = 1e3 m needs 21–80× (lens-decomposer_thresholds.py, verified M-DECOMPOSER-07), and the threshold rises with D (M-DECOMPOSER-06).

**2. The payload gains from a NEC-clean shift are cavity-bounded** [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C1-2_payload_gain.py].

The one kinematic effect the shift has on a payload is a shift-ramp kick: u_x is conserved, so the payload ends with speed β/N relative to the shell (mechanism verified in M-MECHANIST-06). From the log:
- **Published shell:** "kick speed beta/N = 0.0314 c ... reaches wall in 1.062 us; max relative displacement 2R1 = 20.0 m".
- **Best thick wall:** "0.1824 c ... max relative displacement 2R1 = 4.0 m".

The payload therefore moves at most one cavity diameter relative to the vehicle, then hits the wall. It gains no travel.

The other effects are equally small:
- **Light asymmetry.** The co- minus counter-propagating light asymmetry across the cavity is 0.8–11 ns.
- **Propulsion bill.** The bill ratio equals the shell-to-payload mass ratio, 4.5e22 to 8.1e22, exactly as for a massive vehicle of that mass. The shift does not enter it (cf. M-MECHANIST-08: "ratio 4.511e22").
- **Proper time.** The lapse of 0.761 is the shell's gravitational potential. It is present with β = 0 too: the log of lens-constraints_shell.py shows "beta = 0.00: interior light speeds +x 0.7611 c, -x 0.7611 c".

**3. Physical scale of the "ordinary massive vehicle"** (verified M-CONSTRAINTS-12): "1.535e23 kg/m³; 6.7e5× ... nuclear; 27.59 orders" above a 1 TPa material strength. In practice even the massive-vehicle mode is unbuildable. That strengthens the "only as" clause; it does not breach it.

## In principle vs. in practice

- **In principle.**
  - **Faster-than-light half.** The theorems carry it, and no design in the scanned family brings the NEC-safe shift within a factor of 3.4 of even the necessary interior threshold. The scan covers cavity 0.5–16 m, wall-to-radius 0.2–0.98, and C = 0.01–0.85.
  - **The scan is not a theorem.** It does not cover other shift shapes (Fuchs's sigmoid is fixed), non-spherical density shaping, or anisotropic stress tuning. The idealizer's shaped-wall linear caps (0.084–0.107, M-IDEALIZER-06/07) are of the same order as my thick-wall caps, so shaping is unlikely to close a factor of ≥ 3.4, but this is not proved.
  - **Subluminal half.** Conservation of ADM/Bondi momentum, together with the cavity bound above, leaves the payload with nothing beyond what a massive shell gives.
- **In practice.**
  - **Faster-than-light half.** It is moot. The NEC-violating routes need semiclassical negative energy 7.1e63× beyond the Ford–Roman bound (M-CONSTRAINTS-09).
  - **Subluminal half.** Positive-energy shells do not even work as massive vehicles. The propulsion energy is 3.38e43 J, 5.7e22 world-years (M-MECHANIST-08), and the wall needs 6.7e5× nuclear density and stresses 27.6 orders beyond any material.

## Corrections to details

- **"NEC fails at β ≈ 0.0239 while a forward light advance needs β > 0.210".** Both numbers are right, but the 8.8× margin is specific to the R₁ = 10 m, R₂ = 20 m profile. With thick walls the margin falls to 3.4–4 (table above), and with thin walls it rises to 25. C1 should cite the design-space floor (≈ 3.4× on the necessary criterion), not the published-profile value, as the robust margin.
- **"Across five masses the advance threshold lies 21–80× above the NEC threshold".** This is true for the fixed published geometry at D = 1e3 m only. It is not a design floor.
- **β_NEC at the published geometry.** My two-line sample gives 0.0248, against the math check's denser 0.0239 (M-CONSTRAINTS-13). The difference is sampling resolution; my values are upper bounds, which can only make the gaps above larger.
- **The 0.04 c used for the photon bill.** It is a cruise speed chosen by the lens, not a shift that is NEC-clean (0.04 > β_NEC). The bill is independent of the shift, so nothing changes.

## What survives and remaining doubt

The core claim survives a scale attack. No NEC-clean shell in the scanned design space gets within a factor of about 3.4 of the shift that a light advance needs even as a necessary condition. The payload effects a NEC-clean shift does produce are bounded by one cavity diameter and a few ns, and leave the propulsion bill at the shell's own mass ratio.

Remaining doubt:
1. **Shapes not scanned.** The design scan uses the toolkit's sigmoid shift, a spherical density, and FD sampling on two lines. A non-spherical or anisotropically stressed wall with a tailored shift was not scanned. Closing it would need an analytic bound on β_NEC/C, the calculation C1's own decisive test names.
2. **Theorem caveats.** The theorems' finite-region gap (Gao–Wald) and the reopened non-generic-path branch (M-DECOMPOSER-10) remain the honest residual for C1's first half. They are inherited, not created, by this scale analysis.
3. **Whether the shell exists.** The (B) half inherits the question of whether the 2024 shell is an exact solution at all [D-31].
