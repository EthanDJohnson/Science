- The brief's start-and-stop level admits external propulsion (C3-1 §1 quotes it). A beamed sail, a mass driver, or a gravity assist delivers γMv = 5.41e34 kg m/s per leg (C3-2, matching M-CONSTRAINTS-10) with a shell mass ratio of exactly 1 [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C3-0_external_push.py] [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/falsifier-C3-2_scale.py].
- What I keep: the momentum bill γMv per leg on the **whole** mass is compulsory by every route, and the null-exhaust floor is a theorem for the isolated case (all three verdicts accept this). The slate's exclusivity line ("C2, C3 and C4 can all hold at once") is now true: the narrowed C3 permits C4's external push.
- One point I do not concede in full: C3-2's gravity assist (a 1.4 M_sun neutron star moving at ≥ 5,996 km/s relative to the departure frame, grazing at 12 km) and drag braking (capture radius 1.37e5 AU) are valid momentum-exchange routes in principle, but C3-0 itself notes no turning angle was computed for ordinary flybys and C3-2 treats the 2.4 M_J shell as a test body. If the cavity rapidity is the exterior rapidity read on redshifted interior clocks, C ≈ L/s, this is 1 − 3L: **Le's exterior e^(−3L) bound, independent of compactness** (slope per unit L = 3.000 at 2m/R₀ = 0, 0.335, 0.667, 8/9, 0.95). ## Deciding observation

The narrowed C3 is a conservation statement, so most rivals are compatible with it (the slate's own exclusivity line). For each rival I name the single result that would split them.

- **Against C1** (null: payloads move only as ordinary massive vehicles). C3 is C1(B)'s "except by emitting momentum" route; they differ only on whether a positive-energy start-and-stop **construction** exists. *Test:* an interval-certified or numerical-relativity check of Le's radiative-steering junction (arXiv:2606.22531v4) through a full burn, testing surface DEC and the Bondi balance m_f/m_i = e^(−3L). Holds → C3 has a worked positive-energy instance, beyond C1(B)'s bare "except by emitting momentum" clause; fails (e.g. the growing normal modes [D-72] destroy the cavity) → C3 reduces to C1(B)'s generic conservation statement. Precision: surface DEC sign at the junction and mass loss to better than the e^(−3L) − e^(−L) gap, 0.074 of the mass per 0.04 c leg (0.96077 − 0.88686). Who: Le, or any GR group with Warpax [D-73]. No date known.
- **Against C2** (the shifted shell is a valid positive-energy coaster). C3 does not need the shift: P_ADM = 0 and M_ADM = 4.511e27 kg at every β [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/lens-constraints_shell.py], so the bill is the same with and without it. *Test:* the nonlinear solve C2 already asks for, extended to second order in β to compute the net wall momentum. Zero → C2's coaster and C3's bill coexist; a nonzero second-order P_ADM at rest-frame M would mean the shifted shell is not at rest, which changes C2's "coast only" grade but not C3. Barzegar Error 18 confirmed [D-31] kills C2 and leaves C3 standing on any static shell (idealizer F3, Le's elastic shells [D-74]). Who: any numerical-relativity group, now.
- **Against C4** (externally pushed shell drags the payload without g-forces). After concession 1 both hold at once; they split only on whether the drag lowers the bill. *Test:* numerical relativity of a compact elastic or fluid shell pushed by a modelled exhaust, recording (i) Bondi mass loss against exterior rapidity and (ii) payload proper acceleration against the shell's. C3 predicts (i) ≥ e^(L) and a payload cavity rapidity C ≈ L/s (clock effect only), whatever (ii) shows; C4 is decided by (ii) (κ → 1). A whole-mass ratio below e^(L) in exterior rapidity refutes C3 (and conservation). Precision: mass ratio to ~1e-3 at 0.04 c (floor 1.0408 per leg); C/L to a few % at 2m/R₀ ≳ 0.3, where 1/s ≥ 1.22. No result expected on any known timeline.
- **Against C6** (FTL by breaking a theorem hypothesis, branch (d) non-stationary phases). C3 says accelerating phases are Bondi-balanced subluminal rocket burns. *Test:* compute the one-way light time between exterior rest points through Le's radiating-shell spacetime during a burn. A delay, or an advance smaller than the flat-space value, favours C3 (and C1); a certified advance with the NEC holding favours C6(d). Who: any GR group, now.
- **Against C7** (the shift is internal frame dragging; no interior rearrangement beats a photon rocket). C7's answer "no" to its reframed question is C3's isolated-case claim, so no propulsion observation splits them. The nearest split is the same second-order net wall-momentum calculation as for C2: C7 predicts zero net momentum; a nonzero value refutes C7's "zero net momentum" premise while C3's bill is unchanged. Who: Warp Factory or `tools/numeric_stress_energy.py` users, now.
- **Against C8** (FTL is barred by causal access, not energy sign). Disjoint domains (C3 is subluminal). *Test:* a launch-from-rest numerical evolution of a positive-energy shell sourced in the causal future of the launch event. C3 predicts Bondi mass loss ≥ e^(L) with no advance; C8 is decided only by whether an advance appears with negative energy allowed, which C3 does not address. No date known.

**The crux in one line:** whether Le's cavity-centre rapidity is just the exterior Bondi rapidity read on redshifted interior clocks (C ≈ L/s, so the photon-rocket floor on whole-mass ratio holds in the brief's exterior measure), decidable by reading Le's junction mapping or by a numerical-relativity burn that records both rapidities.
