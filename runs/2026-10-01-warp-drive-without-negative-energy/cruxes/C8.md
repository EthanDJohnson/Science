# Crux: C8
answers: verdicts/C8-0.md, verdicts/C8-1.md, verdicts/C8-2.md
status: final

Units: c = 1 with years and light-years for timings (SI-derived); geometric (G = c = 1, m⁻²) for stress-energy values quoted from the verdicts; SI (kg, J) for energies.

**Position after the verdicts.** I accept the narrowing all three refuters propose, and I defend C8 only in this narrowed form:

> **C8 (narrowed).** In a globally hyperbolic spacetime where the traveller's activity changes the metric only inside the causal future J⁺(S) of the departure event S (Krasnikov's "utter causality"), the first one-way arrival at an unprepared destination cannot be hastened, *whatever the energy sign*. So an on-demand superluminal bubble fails on causal grounds before the energy question arises. Round trips and routes prepared in advance (a Krasnikov tube laid on the outbound leg) can beat light. For those, NEC/WEC violation is the binding obstacle, and it binds by tens of orders of magnitude.

The heading's ranking, "causal access, not the energy sign", and the blanket clause "travellers cannot hasten arrival even with negative energy" do not survive as general statements. Both are conceded below.

## Response to the verdicts

### C8-0 (physics): the return leg through a self-laid tube
- **Point 1: the tube is laid inside the traveller's own J⁺ and gives a return-leg advance.** *Conceded in full.*
  - I re-derived the timings from C8-0's null vector l_I = −(1 − δ)∂_t − ∂_x [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/crux-C8_timing.py]. For D = 4.37 ly the fastest return reaches home at t = D/v − (1 − δ)D. The return-leg advance over light sent from B at departure is (2 − δ)D, whatever the outbound speed v:
    - 8.696 yr at δ = 0.01;
    - 8.303 yr at δ = 0.1.
  - The home arrival is 0.0437 yr for v → 1 and δ = 0.01, and 39.767 yr for v = 0.1 and δ = 0.1. This reproduces C8-0's 8.696 yr and 0.0437 yr, and C8-2's 39.76 yr and 8.30 yr.
  - Calling the tube "pre-placed infrastructure" was wrong. The traveller lays it himself. In Krasnikov's Example 5 the altered region {x < t} ∩ {0 < x < D} lies inside J⁺(S).
- **Point 2: the tube needs NEC violation.** *Conceded.* C8-0's all-observer NEC minimum is −0.32 m⁻² (geo) at ρ = 8.7 m, with a flat control giving zero violations. It agrees with Krasnikov p. 12 ("requires violation of the weak energy condition") and Everett–Roman. I have no grounds to dispute it. C8 never claimed the tube was energy-free; its error was ranking.
- **Point 3: what binds depends on the trip.** *Conceded as stated.* Causal access is a one-time cost. After the first flight, the energy sign binds every later leg.
- **What C8-0 leaves standing, and I keep:**
  - the one-way, first-arrival core, with Proposition 1 as the geometric reason;
  - the gauge-dependence of |β| > N;
  - the comoving horizon at r = 99.45 m for v = 10, R = 100 m (M-EXAMINER-02, verified);
  - the eternal bubble's advance as a property of a pre-laid metric (M-EXAMINER-01).

### C8-1 (evidence): selective quotation, and Low 1999
- **Point 1: selective quotation of Krasnikov's abstract.** *Conceded.* The abstract's next sentence ("Nevertheless, it is perhaps possible for him to make an arbitrarily long round-trip within an arbitrarily short … time") reverses the operational clause for round trips.
  - **Small correction to C8-1, which strengthens its own case.** C8-1's threshold "a round-trip advance exists iff v_out > 0.5c" assumes an instantaneous return (Δt_return = 0). Through the tube the return *loses* coordinate time (1 − δ)D, so the threshold is v_out > 1/(3 − δ): 0.334 c at δ = 0.01 and 0.345 c at δ = 0.1.
  - At v_out = 0.99c and δ = 0.01 the Earth-clock round trip is 0.0878 yr, not 4.414 yr. No arrival home precedes departure for any v ≤ c [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/crux-C8_timing.py].
- **Point 2: once causal access is solved, the energy sign binds.** *Conceded.* This is the Everett–Roman result, and their abstract says so.
- **Point 3: Low 1999 puts the barrier in the DEC, and Krasnikov's divergence condition is "tentative".** *Partly conceded, partly rebutted.*
  - *Conceded:* "even with negative energy" holds only for sources whose influence stays inside J⁺(S). Matter with superluminal characteristics (which Low ties to DEC violation) is not covered. Neither is matter that breaks global hyperbolicity: Krasnikov's Example 3 hastens arrival with utter causality intact but without global hyperbolicity.
  - *Kept:* this narrowing costs C8 little on the brief's own terms, because the only negative-energy supply the run treats as admissible is semiclassical (C1's eliminated fallback; [D-16], [D-67]), and negative active mass is excluded as speculative [D-81]. Semiclassical negative energy (Casimir, squeezed states, [D-16]) comes from quantum fields with causal propagation. Those are exactly the "causally propagating" sources for which C8-1 itself grants that the claim is "plausible but unproven".
  - So the honest claim is: *with negative energy of the kind admissible physics supplies*, the first one-way flight cannot be hastened. This is reasoning, not a cited theorem. That the divergence condition holds for a semiclassical source is an open step, and I do not claim it is proved.
  - Low's result and C8 are not in conflict on first flights. Low says DEC-respecting matter cannot *construct* a warp drive. C8 says even DEC-violating but causal matter gains no first-flight advance. Both are sufficient conditions for no first-flight advance.
- **Point 4: "the theorems answer no, whatever the energy sign" overstates.** *Conceded.* Only Krasnikov's Proposition 1 is energy-free. Gao–Wald [D-60], Olum [D-59] and Visser–Bassett–Liberati [D-61] assume the NEC or WEC. The corrected sentence is C8-1's own.
- **Fell–Heisenberg [D-05] as support for "even with negative energy".** *Conceded.* It concerns horizon formation during push-through, and it shows nothing about negative-energy launch.

### C8-2 (scale): which obstacle binds, measured in orders of magnitude
- **Point 1: Krasnikov and Everett–Roman grant the return-leg advance.** *Conceded* (as for C8-0 and C8-1).
- **Point 2: causal access costs 2.0e21–1.8e24 J (SI); the tube costs 5.6e41 kg (1 m wall) to 3.4e74 kg (quantum-inequality wall).** *Conceded.* That is 37–70 orders, so on any trip a causal argument does not already forbid, the energy requirement binds. I do not contest the numbers.
  - C8-2's own caveat applies: the gap uses Everett–Roman's order-of-magnitude α and the Eq. 51 estimate. The conclusion survives even the generous 1 m wall.
- **Point 3: internal tension with "the real alternative".** *Conceded.* Naming the tube as the alternative concedes a way round causal access that leaves the energy obstacle in place.
- **One point I keep against the scale framing.** For the *on-demand, first one-way* trip, which is the brief's travel-time sense when a drive is launched from A, no amount of energy of either sign helps under Proposition 1.
  - There the energy gap is not the binding constraint, because the trip is barred with the energy gap set to zero. The ranking "energy binds" therefore holds for prepared routes, and "causal access binds" holds for first flights.
  - Krasnikov p. 10 says this in his own words: devices placed in advance may set up "a regular spaceship service", but "it does not help to outdistance the test particles from M1 in the first flight (i. e. in the flight that would start at P)" [new: Krasnikov 1998, arXiv:gr-qc/9511068 p. 10, ACCESS full-text].
  - Proposition 1 itself reads: "If Mi are globally hyperbolic spacetimes diverging by S, then F1 ⋆ F 2" [new: Krasnikov 1998, arXiv:gr-qc/9511068 p. 9, ACCESS full-text]. Its proof is in his appendix, and I have not re-derived it, as C8-0 also notes.

### Net
- **Lost:**
  - the general ranking;
  - the universality of "even with negative energy" (it now needs causal propagation);
  - the "pre-placed" description of the tube;
  - the use of [D-05] as support.
- **Kept:**
  - a sourced, energy-free no-go for first one-way flights launched on demand (Krasnikov Proposition 1, Everett–Roman);
  - the gauge point (|β| > N is not an advance);
  - the operational reading of the eternal-bubble advance.
- **Status:** C8 becomes a *scope reframe* of the brief's travel-time definition (d). The brief's "two exterior rest points, arrival before light" must specify whether the route may be prepared. If it may not, the question is settled causally before energy enters. If it may, C1's energy-condition answer governs.

## Deciding observation

C8 (narrowed) is a reframe and is mostly compatible with the rivals. For each rival I name the one calculation that would separate them, or say that none does.

- **Against C1 (null: no FTL without NEC violation; positive-energy shells are only massive vehicles).**
  - **Where they agree:** C8 and C1 agree on every NEC-satisfying case.
  - **Where they part:** on whether negative energy, if supplied, suffices for a *launched* one-way advance. C1 is silent there. Its argument needs NEC violation and treats it as the obstacle. C8 says it does not suffice.
  - **The calculation:** a numerical-relativity (or semiclassical backreaction) evolution of an Alcubierre-type bubble launched from rest. The NEC-violating source has characteristic speeds ≤ c, for example a minimally coupled phantom or ghost scalar, or a NEC-violating fluid with subluminal sound speed. The source is seeded only inside J⁺(launch event), and the spacetime is checked to stay globally hyperbolic.
  - **What it measures:** whether the payload reaches a static exterior worldline at distance D before the light ray from the launch event does.
  - **The outcomes:**
    - Any positive first-arrival advance refutes C8's core, and leaves the NEC as the only obstacle, which is C1's framing.
    - Arrival no earlier than light for every source and v confirms C8, and shows the energy sign is insufficient for first flights.
  - **Precision:** the arrival-time difference must be resolved to better than the grid light-crossing time of one cell. Proposition 1 predicts a difference ≤ 0 exactly, so any resolved positive value decides it.
  - **Who and when:** groups already evolving warp spacetimes in numerical relativity (Clough, Dietrich & Khan evolved a warp "containment failure" with a stiff fluid [D-14]; Buchert & Frackowiak give dynamical equations for the Natário class [D-15]) could do it now. No result is expected on any stated timeline.
  - **Analytic version:** a proof that a symmetric-hyperbolic matter system with characteristic cones inside the metric cones meets Krasnikov's divergence condition. That would turn C8's "plausible" step into a theorem.
- **Against C2 (Fuchs/Bobrick–Martire shell as a positive-energy coasting vehicle).**
  - **Compatible.** C8's obstacle applies only when the comoving Killing vector turns spacelike somewhere outside the payload, which is the superluminal case. At the shell's cap of β ≈ 0.024 c no horizon exists, so C8 places no causal bar on C2.
  - **What would separate them:** the sign of the comoving Killing norm g(∂_t, ∂_t) = −α² + β_iβ^i in the shell frame across the rebuild's exterior and wall at β ≤ 0.0239.
    - Timelike everywhere means C8 is silent on C2.
    - A sign change would mean a horizon, so the shell could not be steered or created on demand.
  - **Who and precision:** anyone with `tools/warp_shell.py` could compute it now, needing only the sign of the norm. I expect it to be timelike everywhere, so this is a consistency check, not a live crux.
- **Against C3 (start and stop only by emitting momentum, at no less than photon-rocket cost).**
  - **Compatible.** C3 is subluminal momentum accounting. C8 adds that, even pushed to v → c, a radiatively steered shell gives no first-flight advance.
  - **What would separate them:** extending Le's radiative-steering construction [D-72] to a Krasnikov-type return route, to see whether a shell steered by photon exhaust can *lay* a return path with an advance.
    - C8 (narrowed) predicts this needs NEC violation in the laid structure.
    - C3 makes no prediction about it.
  - **No observation separates them on C3's own claims.**
- **Against C4 (inductive frame dragging gives g-force-free acceleration).**
  - **Compatible**, because it is subluminal and C8 says nothing about it.
  - **No calculation separates them.** The only shared quantity is the comoving Killing norm (as for C2): C4's κ → 1 regime at compactness C ≈ 0.3–0.9 must stay horizon-free for the shell to be pushed from outside. A nonlinear pushed-shell evolution that formed a horizon between payload and pusher would bring C8's control obstacle into C4. Same numerical-relativity groups, no timeline.
- **Against C6 (FTL without pointwise negative energy by breaking a theorem hypothesis: (a) asymptotic flatness, (b) null completeness or distributional sources, (c) the generic condition, (d) stationarity, (e) DEC-violating "superluminal matter").**
  - **This is C8's sharpest rival.** Krasnikov's Proposition 1 uses neither the NEC nor the generic condition. So C8 predicts that branches (c), (d) and (e) can never give a first-flight advance if the spacetime stays globally hyperbolic and the construction is sourced inside J⁺(launch). Only branches that break global hyperbolicity ((b), if null incompleteness comes from a Cauchy-horizon-type breakdown) or causal propagation ((e), if the matter has superluminal characteristics) escape C8.
  - **The calculation:** take any C6 construction offered as a working example, starting with the Garattini–Zatrimaylov de Sitter bubble [D-75] for branch (a), since it is the only one exhibited.
    1. Verify global hyperbolicity.
    2. Compute whether a bubble switched on at a launch event reaches a comoving destination before the light ray from that event.
  - **The outcomes:**
    - A positive one-way advance in a globally hyperbolic, causally sourced construction refutes C8.
    - An advance only when the bubble is laid along the route beforehand (or only cosmological recession) confirms C8's reading of C6's branch (a) as an eternal-metric artefact.
  - **Who and when:** any GR theorist could do it now. Exact null-ray integration in de Sitter is analytic. No timeline is known.
- **Against C7 (the warp effect is internal frame dragging by counter-streaming wall currents).**
  - **Compatible and complementary.** Both are reframes that rest on invariants rather than on |β|: C7 on the Sagnac flux and ADM momentum, C8 on the comoving Killing norm and Proposition 1.
  - **What would separate them:** a nonlinear shifted shell whose comoving Killing norm changes sign at sub-horizon compactness. C7's cap β_max ≈ 0.07·C (corrected, M-CONSTRAINTS-13) predicts this never happens below C = 1.
    - A sign change below the cap would put a causal (C8) obstacle inside C7's "frame-drag only" regime.
    - No sign change leaves them disjoint.
  - **Who:** the same Warp Factory or `tools/numeric_stress_energy.py` solves C7 already calls for. Precision: the sign of the norm.

**Single most decisive item across all rivals:** the launch-from-rest evolution described under C1. It is a globally hyperbolic numerical-relativity run with a causally propagating NEC-violating source confined to J⁺(launch), measuring the payload's arrival at a distant static worldline against the launch light ray.
- Any resolved positive one-way advance refutes C8 and returns the question to the energy sign (C1, C6).
- Arrival never before light confirms C8's first-flight core.
