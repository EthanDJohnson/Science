# Crux: C4

status: final

Units: geometric (G = c = 1) for the compactness C = 2M/R, the dragging fraction κ and the felt fraction F = 1 − κ (both dimensionless); SI for masses (kg), lengths (m), accelerations (m/s², or multiples of g = 9.80665 m/s²) and densities (kg/m³). "Published shell" means the rebuild of Fuchs et al. 2024 (M_ADM = 4.511e27 kg, R₁ = 10 m, R₂ = 20 m, C = 0.333–0.335 at R₂, interior lapse N = 0.761).

All three refuters return `weakened`. They agree on one narrowing, and I accept it. **C4 as stated ("without felt g-forces") is lost.** Below I list what I concede, what I rebut, and what survives.

**Restated C4 (narrowed):** An externally pushed, compact positive-energy shell partly drags its interior inertial frames. A payload at rest in the cavity then feels F = 1 − κ of the push, with 0 < F < 1 at every compactness below a horizon. The reduction depends on mass and compactness, not on the warp shift. It leaves the momentum bill unchanged. As a way to spare a payload g-forces, it is dominated by free fall behind a pushed compact mass.

## Response to the verdicts

### C4-0 (physics, weakened)

1. **"κ = 1 only at the collapse limit R = 2M, and only where the DEC fails" (Pfister et al. 2005, as summarized by Arnfinnsson & Grøn 2014, pp. 2 and 17).** *Conceded.* C4 itself cited Pfister et al. as "strong-field models exist ... test particles are dragged". The same model's dragging factor is 1 only at R = 2M, and its DEC-obeying region has κ < 1. "Without felt g-forces" becomes "with reduced felt g-forces".
2. **"LBK 1999 gives 'less rapidly than the shell itself' for gravity, not an opposing field."** *Conceded.* C4 cited LBK only for the EM analogue. Its gravitational result (weak-field f = 11/3, p. 7, and the strong-field g₀₀-ratio reduction in the abstract) supports partial dragging, and that bounds C4.
3. **"Brill–Cohen proxy: drag fraction 0.24 at C = 0.335, 0.80 at Buchdahl, 0.91 at the static-shell DEC edge C = 0.96."** *Accepted, reproduced.* My re-implementation reproduces Thirring's 2C/3 in the weak field (6.666806e-05 against 6.666667e-05 at C = 1e-4) and gives κ = 1.0000 at C = 1. It also gives F = 0.758 at C = 0.335 and F = 0.086 at C = 0.96 [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/crux-C4_felt_fraction.py]. The proxy is rotational, not translational, which C4-0 itself flags.
4. **"Weak-field coefficient is 11GM/(3c²R), not 4GM/(c²R)."** *Conceded.* That gives κ_lin = 0.61 at R = 20 m, not 0.67 (C4-0 log). The math checks M-MECHANIST-10 and M-IDEALIZER-12 had already confined 4M/R to a linear coefficient. My calculation shows both linear laws go negative in F, which means over-dragging, by C ≈ 0.5 (1 − 2C = −0.333, 1 − 11C/6 = −0.222 at C = 2/3). They cannot be used where C4 used them. The claim that "linear theory has broken down there, so strong dragging is plausible" had the sign of the error wrong: the nonlinear models drag less than the linear ones.
5. **"A rocket-towed compact mass ahead of the payload gives the same comfort with 1e8–1e10 times less mass."** *Conceded on the conclusion. Rebutted in part on the size.* C4-0's tractor (5.877e18 kg at d = 2,000 m, A = 10 g) has to fit inside d. That needs a mean density ≥ 1.75e8 kg/m³, which is degenerate (white-dwarf-like) matter, not an ordinary body [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/crux-C4_felt_fraction.py]. In general the tug's surface gravity must be ≥ A.
   - At ordinary (osmium, 2.26e4 kg/m³) density, the lightest tug for A = 10 g is 3.54e26 kg. That is only 12.7× lighter than the shell, not 1e8–1e10×.
   - Even so, that tug leaves a tide of 1.3e-6 of A over 10 m, against the shell's F ≥ 0.58 at the published compactness. The shell is still dominated on both mass and comfort.
   - The shell's mean density, 1.53e23 kg/m³ [D-22], is 8.7e14× above the degenerate tractor's floor. So the tractor is also far less exotic.
   - The concession stands: the tow beats the shell. The only correction is that "8–10 orders less mass" holds for degenerate tugs, and about 1 order holds for ordinary-density ones.
6. **The Arnfinnsson–Grøn C-metric shell has κ = 1 at every m/R.** *Not used as a rescue.* Its own authors call this "not realistic" (C4-0, p. 16 quote), and it rests on a conical strut. I do not claim it.

### C4-1 (evidence, weakened)

1. **"LBK's exact result: the felt fraction is the interior g₀₀ ratio, and κ = 1 only at an extreme Reissner–Nordström black hole (b → 0)."** *Conceded.* This is the strongest attack, because it uses C4's own source.
   - At the published shell the g₀₀ law gives F = N² = 0.579.
   - The neutral transfer gives 1 − C = 0.667, and the extremal geometry gives (1 − C/2)² = 0.694.
   - Across all nonlinear models at the published compactness, F = 0.579–0.758. The gain 1/F is 1.32–1.73× [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/crux-C4_felt_fraction.py].
2. **"κ = 1.33 at R = 10 m means the payload outruns its shell."** *Conceded.* That number lies outside the coefficient's domain. It is not evidence of strong dragging.
3. **"The prediction 'κ → 1 at C ≈ 0.3–0.9' is unsupported."** *Conceded, and the prediction is replaced.* The corrected "if true" prediction is: in a nonlinear evolution, F lies between about 0.58 and 0.76 at C ≈ 0.33, falls monotonically with C, and stays above zero below a horizon.
   - At the DEC caps the models spread widely. At C = 0.96, F = 0.040–0.270, a best gain of 25×. At C = 48/49 [D-27], F = 0.020–0.260, a best gain of 49×.
4. **"Le's construction requires a force on the payload" [D-72].** *Conceded.* This is now consistent with the narrowed claim: a reduced force, not zero.
5. **The trip arithmetic (α Cen 6.00 → 5.38 yr Earth-frame, 3.58 → 2.55 yr ship time at F = 0.58).** *Accepted as stated by C4-1; not re-derived here.* It quantifies the narrowed merit.
6. **"The law is transferred from an extremal charged-dust shell; the pusher's stresses could move F either way."** *Agreed.* This is the open point, and the deciding calculation below targets it.
7. **Sub-hypothesis (b), clock slowing.** *Conceded as ordinary gravitational redshift,* as C4 already listed. It also links to (a): under the LBK law F ≈ N², so the felt-acceleration reduction is bought with exactly the redshift. A payload that feels less push also runs slower and is more weakly coupled to the exterior.

### C4-2 (scale, weakened)

1. **"κ < 1 wherever the DEC holds; the static thin-shell DEC edge is C = 24/25 = 0.96."** *Conceded.* This duplicates C4-0 point 1.
2. **"A tenfold gain needs C ≈ 0.96 and costs about 5 M_sun of exhaust (9.0e47 J) to save about 2.8 yr of crew time."** *Accepted as stated by C4-2.* It agrees with C4's own concession that "the momentum bill is unchanged at 4.5e22× the payload alone".
3. **"Free fall beside the same pushed mass gives effectively κ = 1 with negligible tides; an osmium body of 3.5e26 kg tows at 10 g."** *Accepted, reproduced.* I get 3.540e26 kg at the surface-gravity limit, with a tide of 1.26e-4 m/s² over 10 m. C4-2's caveats stand: the position is unstable and needs station-keeping, and the exhaust has to be canted.
   - The shell keeps one residual difference. Its cavity is flat and tide-free (Pfister abstract: the interior "stays flat"), and a payload at its centre is neutrally placed, not on an unstable tow point. That is a convenience, not a merit that beats a rocket.
4. **"The merit does not need a shell."** *Conceded.* "The one merit where it might beat a rocket" is withdrawn as a merit over rocket systems. It holds only against a bare rocket whose payload feels the full A, and even there it depends on mass, not on warp geometry.

**Net:** C4 survives only as the narrowed mechanism stated at the top. The proper-acceleration reduction is real but partial: 1.3–1.7× at the published shell, and at most 25–49× near the DEC caps (model-dependent). It is not warp-specific and is dominated by a tow. The unresolved piece is the actual translational F(C) for a neutral, externally pushed shell with physical pushing stresses. No accessible source gives it; Pfister et al. 2005 is paywalled (PAPER TO REQUEST: doi:10.1088/0264-9381/22/22/007 | A model for linear dragging | its fig. 4 and eqs. 48–49, κ(C) in the DEC-obeying region).

## Deciding observation

**Shared calculation.** One numerical-relativity evolution decides most of the pairs below:
- the setup: a neutral, compact, DEC-obeying elastic shell [D-74] at C ≈ 0.33 (the published compactness), pushed from rest by a modelled exhaust, run once with the shift β = 0 and once with β = 0.02;
- what it measures: the proper acceleration of a test payload at the cavity centre, over the shell's acceleration (F); the Bondi momentum balance; and the energy conditions through the push.

What it needs:
- **Precision:** F to σ_F ≤ 0.08 separates the nonlinear band (F = 0.58–0.76) from no dragging (F = 1) at 3σ. σ_F ≤ 0.045 separates the candidate laws (g₀₀/lapse, LBK-extremal, Brill–Cohen proxy) at 2σ [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/crux-C4_felt_fraction.py].
- **Check on the code:** it should reproduce κ → 11C/6 or 2C/3 (whichever matches its pushing mechanism) at C ≲ 0.01.
- **Who:** any numerical-relativity group with an elastic-matter code; Clough et al. [D-14] have evolved warp matter with a stiff fluid. The reading needed first is Pfister et al. 2005, fig. 4 (paywalled).
- **When:** no result is expected on any known timeline (dossier §6: "No accelerating positive-energy warp solution with solved dynamics exists").

**C4 vs C1** (positive-energy shells move payloads only as ordinary massive vehicles). The narrowed C4 sits inside C1's (B), because it concedes that the effect is a property of mass and compactness. The two now differ only if "nothing a massive vehicle cannot" is read to exclude any felt-acceleration reduction.
- Deciding result: F at C ≈ 0.33 with β = 0 against β = 0.02.
- If F(β = 0.02) = F(β = 0), within σ_F ≈ 0.045, C1's (B) holds in full, and C4's merit is plain-shell physics.
- If F < 1 at all (F ≤ 0.76 is expected), C4's narrowed mechanism survives as a massive-vehicle effect C1 allows.
- Only a β-dependent F (|ΔF| > 0.1) would give C4 something warp-specific against C1. Nothing in the run predicts that.

**C4 vs C2** (valid positive-energy coasting shell below a shift cap). The two are compatible. C4 does not need C2's shift. It needs only a static compact DEC-obeying shell, which survives even if Barzegar's Error 18 sinks the shifted solution [D-31].
- Deciding result: whether the pushed, shifted shell (β = 0.02) keeps certified all-observer energy conditions through the acceleration phase.
- If it does, and F is unchanged from β = 0, C2's coasting vehicle and C4's partial dragging hold together.
- If the shifted shell fails the energy conditions during the push while the β = 0 shell does not, C2 is confined to pure coasting, and C4 survives on the unshifted shell.

**C4 vs C3** (start and stop only by emitting momentum, at no less than photon-rocket cost for the whole mass). The two are compatible: C4 is "pushed externally" and concedes the bill. Deciding result: the same evolution's Bondi balance.
- If the emitted momentum equals γMv for the whole shell plus payload while F < 1, both hold. Dragging redistributes the felt force without lowering the cost.
- If the evolution shows the payload's felt-force reduction coming with a lower emitted momentum than γM_total·v, C3 falls and C4 gains a cost merit. This would contradict Bondi conservation [D-69] and is not expected.

**C4 vs C6** (FTL by breaking a theorem hypothesis, including (d), non-stationary accelerating phases). These are claims about different regimes: C4 is subluminal. The one overlap is C6(d).
- Deciding result: in the pushed-shell evolution, trace light rays from exterior rest points through the cavity during the acceleration phase.
- C4 (with C1) predicts Shapiro delay only, with no one-way advance relative to exterior light. The ray tracing must resolve the net one-way time to a small fraction of the Shapiro delay: the run's linear theory puts any advance at ≤ 0.20 of the delay at the NEC cap (candidates.md, C1, lens-idealizer_thickwall.py).
- A one-way advance during acceleration, with a certified NEC pass, would favour C6(d) and make the shell more than a dragging device. Olum [D-59] says this cannot happen with the generic condition, which the wall matter supplies.

**C4 vs C7** (the warp effect is stationary interior frame dragging by counter-streaming currents; no rearrangement lowers propulsion cost). The two agree on cost and on the shift being gauge-like. They differ on whether there is any payload-level merit at all.
- Deciding result: F at the published compactness.
- If F ≥ 0.92, i.e. within 1σ_F of no dragging, because the pusher's stresses cancel the induction (LBK p. 6 warns of this), C4 collapses into C7's "no merit".
- If F ≤ 0.76, as all three nonlinear models predict, C4 adds a real but non-warp felt-acceleration reduction that C7's reframe leaves out. C7 also predicts the shift leaves g₀₀ and B_g unchanged in the cavity, so F should be independent of β. A β-dependent F would refute that half of C7.

**C4 vs C8** (the binding FTL obstacle is causal access to the front wall). These are disjoint regimes: a subluminal, horizon-free shell keeps the payload in causal contact with its walls [D-71].
- The only contact is a trade-off the verdicts exposed. Under the g₀₀ law, F ≈ N², so near-complete dragging (F → 0) needs N → 0, the horizon limit, where the payload's link to the exterior is infinitely redshifted.
- Deciding result: whether any DEC-obeying pushed shell gives F < N_centre² (dragging better than the redshift law).
- If yes, dragging is decoupled from causal isolation, and C8's theme does not touch C4.
- If F ≥ N² always, then strong g-shielding and exterior access trade off exactly. C8's "access, not energy sign" framing then extends to C4's subluminal merit: the more the payload is shielded, the more it is cut off.
- This needs F and N at the cavity centre to about 0.05 at C = 0.6–0.9, from the same evolution.
