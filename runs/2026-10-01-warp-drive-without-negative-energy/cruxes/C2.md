**C2 as I now defend it (narrowed after the verdicts).** A Fuchs-type shifted matter shell exists whose geometric stress-energy T = G/8π passes NEC, WEC, SEC and DEC for all observers at every point tested, including the smoothing tail, at interior shift β ≲ 0.024 c. This holds at C = 1/3 with a thick wall (Δ/R₂ = 0.5), an edge span of at least about 0.6 m and a buffer of Rb ≈ 0. The cap is set by C·Δ/R₂ and the edge profile, not by C alone. The shell coasts as a whole. It is an energy-condition-clean geometry, not yet a valid vehicle: it has no matter model, no stability analysis and no source-consistency proof. By 2100 it is eliminated in practice.

   - **Type IV is excluded there.** Type IV means T has no timelike eigenvector, and that is a frame-invariant property. It therefore cannot occur in that region in any frame, including the frame where the shell moves at v_s.
   - **Checked numerically.** I tested the rebuild's tail exactly, at β = 0 and β = 0.02 and edge spans span_P = 0.6–4.0 m [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/crux-C2_tail.py]:

     - every tail margin (NEC, WEC, SEC, DEC) is ≥ 0;
     - tail max|p|/ε = 0.087–0.116, so the DEC holds with a factor of about 9 to spare;

   - **The tail reaches Le's worst probe.** For spans of 2–4 m, matter does reach r = 23.9 m, Le's worst probe, with ε/ε_peak = 0.024–0.218 there. It is still clean.
   - **What Le's result implies.** His tail violation must come from something his reproduction does that the published recipe, as the toolkit reads it, does not, such as a nonzero g_0x or other off-diagonal component beyond R₂. Le's tail is not a property of "the Fuchs shell" as such.

## Deciding observation

**Core test for C2 (bears on C1 most directly).**
- **What:** an interval-certified, all-observer energy-condition scan (Le's Warpax S-lemma method [D-73]) of the metric generated by the Fuchs group's own public code (Warp Factory `metricGet_WarpShellComoving.m`) at the published grid, spans and an Rb as stated by the code. Run it at β = 0.02 and β = 0, over r ∈ [R₁ − 1 m, R₂ + 15 m] so that the whole smoothing tail is covered.
- **Precision:** it must certify signs at margins well below the bulk worst NEC margin of +2.2e-5 m⁻² (geo) [calc: runs/2026-10-01-warp-drive-without-negative-energy/calc/lens-constraints_shell.py], to about 1e-6 m⁻² (geo). In the tail it must separate a residue of about 1e-12 m⁻² from Le's reported −7.9e-3, roughly 100× his bulk margin in his units.
- **What each result means:**
  - A certified pass everywhere, with the tail type I, favours C2.
  - A certified NEC failure at β = 0.02, or Type IV beyond R₂ that is absent at β = 0, refutes C2 for the published construction. It leaves only the rebuild member.
  - Type IV beyond R₂ that is also present at β = 0 would be a smoothing artefact unrelated to the warp shift.
- **Who and when:** Le (the Warpax author) or the Helmerich/Fuchs group could run it now. No date is given in the dossier.
- **Second leg:** the "valid" half needs an independent Einstein–matter solve with an elastic or anisotropic equation of state [D-74], plus linear stability. Any numerical-relativity group could do it. No timeline is known.

**Against each rival:**

- **C1 (null).**
  - **Where they agree.** C2 is compatible with C1's faster-than-light half: the shell is subluminal, and its NEC cap of 0.0239 lies 21–80× below the advance threshold. C1's own prediction, that Le's test passes at β ≤ 0.023 and fails at β ≥ 0.025, is also C2's.
  - **Where they split.** C1 says the (B) half "inherits every doubt about whether the 2024 shell is a solution". The split is whether any shift-bearing positive-energy shell exists at all, as a certified geometry and then with a matter model.
  - **What decides it:** the certified scan above, followed by the matter-model solve. If no equation of state sustains any β > 0 while keeping the DEC (Barzegar Error 18 confirmed independently), that favours the stronger reading of C1 and eliminates C2. If a certified pass and a matter-model solution are found at β ≈ 0.02, that favours C2. In that case C1(B)'s "only as ordinary massive vehicles" still stands, because C2 claims coasting only.
- **C3 (start and stop by emission).** These are compatible: C2 is about coasting and C3 about changing speed.
  - **What decides it:** a linear stability analysis of the coasting shifted shell with a stated elastic equation of state.
  - **Reading the result:** growing modes at C = 1/3 eliminate C2's "coasting vehicle" while leaving C3's junction construction [D-72] intact. Stability favours C2 and leaves C3 as its propulsion.
  - **Who and when:** any GR or numerical-relativity group, now. No timeline is known.
- **C4 (dragging under push).** C4 needs C ≈ 0.3–0.9, but this construction is DEC-clean only up to C ≈ 0.45–0.8 even at zero shift (C2-0).
  - **What decides it:** a nonlinear pushed-shell evolution at fixed C = 1/3 that measures the payload's proper acceleration with and without the interior shift.
  - **Reading the result:** identical results mean C2's distinctive content, the shift, adds nothing to C4's merit, so C4 stands independently of C2. A difference would make the shift matter during a push, beyond C2's coasting-only claim.
  - **Who and when:** any numerical-relativity group. No timeline is known.
- **C6 (breaking theorem hypotheses for faster-than-light travel).** These are compatible: C2 is subluminal and asymptotically flat.
  - **What decides it:** a general bound on the one-way light-time advance through any NEC-satisfying, asymptotically flat, lapse ≠ 1 shell as a function of compactness.
  - **Reading the result:** if the bound is zero, C2's family is closed to C6. A certified NEC-clean member of the family with an advance would favour C6 and push C2's cap aside.
  - **Who:** GR theorists, now.
- **C7 (reframe as gravitomagnetic solenoid).** C2-0's finding, that the cap is ∝ C·Δ/R₂ and goes to 0 for thin walls, confirms C7's predicted scaling law. I concede that this favours C7's framing of the cap.
  - **What decides it:** whether a nonlinear solve shows any local cavity invariant beyond the Sagnac flux (B_g ≠ 0, or a change in g_00 at fixed matter), or a nonzero second-order net wall momentum.
  - **Reading the result:** if neither appears, C7's reframe stands and C2 is "a clean frame-drag geometry", which is consistent with my narrowed claim. If one appears, the shift does something local and C2's "vehicle" reading regains content.
  - **Tools:** Warp Factory or `numeric_stress_energy.py`.
- **C8 (causal access, not energy sign).** The two address different regimes. A subluminal shell has no horizon, so it lies outside the Everett–Roman and Finazzi–Liberati–Barceló objections [D-71].
  - **What decides it:** a launch-from-rest computation in which the C2 shell's shift is grown from zero by matter in the launch event's causal future, with the energy conditions checked throughout.
  - **Reading the result:** Fuchs et al. state that growing the shift while moving the centre needs negative energy [D-08]. If that is confirmed, C8's broader moral that launching is the obstacle extends even to subluminal shells, and C2 is confined to "built already moving". A clean launch favours C2 over C8's general reframe.
  - **Who and when:** theorists, now. No timeline is known.
