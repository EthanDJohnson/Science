## Response to the verdicts

All three verdicts say C7 **survives** with `narrowed: none`. Their attacks land on the wording of the reframed question and on its numerical answer, not on the identification. I concede most of them. Below is what C7 now claims after the concessions.

**Restated core, after concessions.** In the shell-plus-shift family, the "positive-energy warp effect" is a stationary gravitomagnetic holonomy. It threads the wall and is sourced by a circulating, zero-net-momentum momentum current in positive-energy wall matter. Its only gauge-invariant signature is a Sagnac-type co-minus-counter light-time difference. It gives no local cavity effect, no propulsion and no travel-time advantage. The reframed question is (i) how much holonomy per unit compactness positive matter can make, at a stated aspect ratio, and (ii) whether any interior arrangement lowers the start-and-stop bill below a photon rocket's for the whole mass. The answer to (ii) is no, from ADM/Bondi momentum conservation. The answer to (i) is open: about 0.07·C for the rebuild's imposed-shift profile, and possibly several times that for genuine counterflow.

## Deciding observation

C7 is a reframe, so against most rivals it is compatible rather than exclusive (candidates.md, line 2). For each rival, the test below is the single calculation that would show whether C7's picture is the right description or whether the rival adds content that C7's frame misses. No laboratory experiment reaches any of them: a lab counterflow is about 26 orders short in β (M-ENGINEER-11), and a rotor beside a ring laser is 5.1 orders short. All are calculations that GR or numerical-relativity groups could do now. The dossier gives no expected dates.

**The master test, which bears on C1, C2 and C3.** Solve the Einstein equations with a stated matter model: an anisotropic elastic shell [D-74] carrying an explicit poloidal counterflow of speed u at R₂/R₁ = 2 and C ≈ 0.33.
- Certify the energy conditions with interval arithmetic (Le's Warpax method [D-73]).
- Compute three things: the wall holonomy ∮g_0i dx^i/(−g_00), P_ADM, and the cavity's Killing redshift against u.
- Precision needed:
  - the holonomy to about 10%, i.e. ±0.8 ns around the ≈ 8.2 ns expected at β ≈ 0.024;
  - the redshift change to O(β²) ≈ 6e-4 in √(−g_00).

**vs C1** (null: positive-energy shells move payloads only as ordinary massive vehicles).
- C7 and C1(B) agree on transport. They differ on whether there is a real positive-energy "warp effect" worth reframing around.
- **Favours C7 as the better description:** the master solve gives a nonzero, EC-certified holonomy with P_ADM = 0. The effect exists but is non-propulsive.
- **Favours C1:** no matter model sustains a nonzero holonomy (Barzegar Error 18 confirmed). C7's object is then empty and it reduces to C1(B).
- C1's faster-than-light half is outside C7's frame. A certified one-way advance in any positive-energy metric would defeat both.

**vs C2** (the Fuchs shell is valid, with energy conditions holding below a cap of β ≈ 0.024 ≈ 0.072·C).
- The decider is the energy-condition cap of the self-consistent counterflow solve at R₂/R₁ = 2.
- **Favours C2's profile-specific cap:** the energy conditions fail at β ≈ 0.024 ± 0.003, i.e. u ≈ 0.07 c.
- **Favours C7's mechanism framing:** they hold to β ≳ 0.05 (u ≳ 0.15 c), showing the 0.072·C cap is an artefact of imposing a shift on a static TOV shell, not a property of positive matter.
- Either result leaves C7's identification intact. Only the second shows C7's question (i) to be the one worth asking.

**vs C3** (start and stop only by emitting momentum, at no less than photon-rocket cost).
