# Brief: Warp-bubble shapes that need less energy
slug: 2026-09-29-warp-bubble-shapes | depth: quick (single researcher, run by hand) | type: design | date: 2026-09-29

## Question as asked
"Are there other shapes that cost less energy? I seem to remember a white paper on that, send a research agent out for that paper, please."
(Asked after a smoke test computed the energy of a spherical Alcubierre bubble with radius R = 100 m.)

## Question made precise
- "Shapes" means any warp-drive geometry claimed to lower the energy an Alcubierre-type drive needs. That includes:
  - the bubble's shape function and wall thickness;
  - its topology, for example a toroidal (ring) bubble;
  - modified spatial geometry, such as Van Den Broeck's small outer bubble around a large inner pocket;
  - zero-expansion (Natário-type) flows;
  - time-varying or oscillating bubbles;
  - solitons claimed to need only positive energy.
- "Energy" means the total Eulerian energy (and its negative part) on a t = const slice, unless a paper defines it otherwise. When it does, record which definition it uses.
- "The white paper" is the specific document the user remembers. A strong lead is Harold "Sonny" White (NASA JSC), "Warp Field Mechanics 101" (2011, NTRS 20110015936) and "Warp Field Mechanics 102: Energy Optimization" (2013, NTRS 20130011213). Confirm or rule out, and look for alternatives.
- Admissible physics: GR plus QFT on curved spacetime. Label speculative extensions.

## Hidden premises to test
- That reductions quoted by different papers compare like with like: same speed, bubble radius and wall thickness, and the same energy measure.
- That the claims survived later scrutiny: peer review, published rebuttals, and quantum-inequality limits on wall thickness.
- That a lower-energy shape still allows superluminal travel. Some positive-energy solutions work only below light speed.

## What counts as an answer
For each geometry:
- the paper: authors, year, venue and whether it was peer-reviewed;
- the claimed energy, and how it scales with bubble size R, speed v and wall thickness;
- the baseline it is compared with;
- the key assumptions;
- its status (accepted, disputed or refuted), citing the critique.

## Known constraints, prior attempts, and user-supplied data
Our own calculation, not a literature value (examples/smoke-test-constraints-lens/):
- For Alcubierre's tanh profile, with radius R, wall thickness Δ and speed v (in units of c), E ≈ −v²R²/(18Δ) in geometric units (G = c = 1).
- With R = 100 m, Δ = 1 m and v = 1 that gives −7.5e29 kg (SI).
- Quantum inequalities limit free-field walls to about 50–100 Planck lengths, which raises the requirement to about −1e62 to −1e63 kg (SI) for R = 100 m.
