# Analysis: examiner (Socrates)
status: final

## Method applied
Examine the question before answering it: define its loaded terms ("beam", "bottle", "the puzzle", "explains", "settle"), test each hidden premise (support / falsifier / what the question becomes if false), look for a better-posed reframe, and list the hardest checkable questions any answer must survive. Numbers are SI (s, s⁻¹, T); λ dimensionless.

## Findings
1. The two method classes do not measure the same quantity even in principle: a proton-counting beam measures τn/Br(n → p + X), an electron-counting beam the rate of decays with an electron, a bottle the total disappearance rate Γ_total + Γ_loss [D-06]. So "beam versus bottle" is a comparison of three different observables that coincide only in the SM with no unidentified loss; SM corrections that separate them (bound-state ~4×10⁻⁶, radiative decay still yields p and e) are ≥2750× too small [D-04]. Premise 4 holds.
2. The "beam" side is effectively one data set: BL1 (Yue 2013 is a re-analysis of the same 2005 data, so 2005 and 2013 are not independent [D-07]) plus Sussex–ILL, which this run has not verified [D-22]. Any "beam" average therefore carries the single-experiment risk of BL1 [D-20].
3. The "strong-field" and "proton-counting" partitions have identical membership in the present data (BL1 and Sussex–ILL are the only 4.6–5 T experiments and the only proton-counting ones [D-20], [D-09], [D-10]); no existing measurement can separate a field-dependent new-physics effect from a proton-counting systematic. Only a strong-field non-proton measurement or a weak-field proton-counting measurement can.
4. Partition tests (grouped χ², symmetric errors as stated in the script) [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/lens-examiner_premises.py]:
   - beam (BL1, Sussex–ILL, J-PARC) vs all bottles: between χ² = 16.49/1 (4.06σ), within χ² = 28.80/8 (p = 3.4×10⁻⁴);
   - proton-counting (= strong-field) vs rest: between 21.80/1 (4.67σ), within 23.49/8 (p = 0.0028); unchanged (4.67σ) if J-PARC's stat error is inflated by its internal scale factor 2.30;
   - BL1 alone (Sussex–ILL dropped, since unverified) vs rest: 17.00/1 (4.12σ);
   - four classes (proton beam, electron beam, material, magnetic): between 36.95/3 (5.46σ), within 8.33/6 (p = 0.22). Only the four-class split leaves acceptable within-class scatter.
   So "proton-counting vs everything" fits better than "beam vs bottle" (Δχ²_between ≈ 5.3 for the same dof), but neither two-way split is internally consistent: the non-proton side itself has χ² = 23.4/7.
5. The "second anomaly" is as large as the first on this data: material bottles 880.03 ± 0.49 s vs magnetic traps 877.83 ± 0.28 s, between χ² = 15.07/1 (3.88σ); using only Gravitrap, MAMBO II and Arzumanov, 880.97 ± 0.68 vs 877.83 ± 0.28 s, 4.28σ [calc: lens-examiner_premises.py], consistent with ORNL's grouping ~3σ [D-32], [D-73]. Material values are taken from Serebrov's table only [D-29] and Ezhov is search-summary [D-30]. Consequence: premise 3 ("the bottles are right") cannot hold for both bottle classes at once; at least one bottle class carries an unrecognised ≈3 s shift, about a third of the beam–bottle gap. Same group, two apparatus generations: Serebrov 2004/5 878.5 s vs 2018 881.5 s [D-28], [D-29].
6. J-PARC as discriminator [calc: lens-examiner_premises.py]: as quoted (side-facing asymmetric errors), J-PARC − BL1 = −10.50 s, 2.15σ; J-PARC − UCNτ 0.14σ; likelihood ratio "J-PARC reads the bottle value" : "J-PARC reads the beam value" = 9.9. With its stat error inflated by its internal scale factor √(15.8/3) = 2.30 [D-23], the tension with BL1 falls to 1.74σ and the likelihood ratio to 4.5. Its three low run conditions average 869.6 ± 2.5 s (stat only), ~8 s below UCNτ, and the fourth, 884.8 s, is 0.64σ from BL1 [D-24]. So J-PARC is internally split into a sub-bottle and a beam-like subset, with a background 4–5× the MC prediction [D-25]; its discriminating power is roughly a factor 5–10 in odds, not a decisive test. Premise 5 is strained.
7. Required sizes [calc: lens-examiner_premises.py]: gap = 1.113% (UCNτ vs BL1); missing rate 1.268×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹, loss time constant 7.89×10⁴ s = 0.91 d; the gap is 4.40× BL1's total error, 5.20× its systematic, 5.81× its un-remeasured "unassociated" 1.7 s line [D-20]. Material vs magnetic difference needs only 4.8×10⁻⁶ s⁻¹.
8. BL1's in-situ trap-length test cancels additive (end-region) terms only [D-08]; any constant fractional proton loss (charge exchange during a fixed trapping time, dead-layer or detection inefficiency) or a fluence-monitor efficiency error is multiplicative and passes straight through the slope. So the in-situ test does not constrain the most discussed systematics [D-17], [D-72]. (Examiner's inference from the slope method as described in D-08.)
9. The SM "arbiter" (premise 6) is not independent of a second anomaly. With consistently paired radiative corrections (GS2023 ΔR^V with Vud = 0.97361(32), as in [U-01]; the tool's default) [calc: lens-examiner_premises.py]:
   - PERKEO III λ: τ_β = 878.50 ± 0.88 s, 3.81σ from BL1, 0.73σ from UCNτ;
   - UCNA 877.60 ± 2.36 s; PERKEO II 880.34 ± 1.35 s; PDG 2024 average 879.65 ± 1.61 s (2.91σ from BL1);
   - aSPECT 2024 λ: τ_β = 889.58 ± 3.20 s, 0.48σ from BL1, 3.66σ from UCNτ;
   - aCORN 874.86 ± 7.07 s (uninformative).
   PERKEO III vs aSPECT 2024 differ by 3.49σ in λ [D-42], [D-74]. BL1 implies |λ| = 1.2684, UCNτ implies 1.2770. So the λ data contain a mirror-image anomaly of the same size: the β-asymmetry route predicts the bottle value and the proton-recoil (a-coefficient) route predicts the beam value. The SM arbitrates only once that λ split is resolved. Observation (not a mechanism): the two "beam-like" results, BL1 and aSPECT, both detect protons; the only other proton-detecting λ result, aCORN, is too imprecise (±7 s) to test the pattern [D-41].
10. Exotic branch [calc: lens-examiner_premises.py]: Br_X = 1 − τ_UCNτ/τ_β(PERKEO III) = 0.077 ± 0.106%, one-sided 95% upper limit 0.25%, consistent with CMS 2018 and Dubbers [D-82]; with aSPECT 2024 λ instead, Br_X = 1.32 ± 0.36%, exactly the size the gap needs. Dark decay is therefore not excluded by λ and Vud as such, but only conditional on the β-asymmetry route being right.
11. CKM direction [calc: lens-examiner_premises.py]: to match BL1 with PERKEO III λ, Vud would have to be 0.96855 ± 0.00128, 0.00506 below the superallowed 0.97361 (GS2023). Closing the first-row deficit (−0.00166 [D-47]) would instead need Vud higher by +0.00085, which shortens τ_β by 1.54 s, away from the beam value. The Cabibbo-angle anomaly cannot help a beam-like τ_β; it pushes the other way [D-75].
12. Look-elsewhere over the choice of partition (Šidák, 5 partitions tried) [calc: lens-examiner_premises.py]: proton-counting vs rest 4.67σ → 4.33σ; beam vs bottle 4.06σ → 3.67σ; BL1 vs rest 4.12σ → 3.73σ; material vs magnetic 3.88σ → 3.47σ. Upper bound on odds against "one common value": about 9.6×10³ (proton vs rest), 7.6×10² (beam vs bottle). Premise 1 survives at 3.7–4.3σ, but the beam side is a single data set (Finding 2), so this is a single-experiment tension, not a two-method one.
13. BBN as an extra measurement class the brief's matrix lacks: [new: Melchiorri 2026, Phys. Dark Univ. 53, 102371, abstract (INSPIRE API), "If Neff is fixed to its Standard Model value 3.044 — motivated by the absence of new light species — a 0.1% determination of YP combined with current D/H data already reaches σ(τn) ≃ 1.3 s, providing > 6σ discrimination." and "If Neff is treated as a free parameter ... yielding σ(τn) ≃ 4.3 s at the ∼ 2σ threshold."]. Examiner caveat (own inference): the n/p freeze-out is set by the weak rates, which scale with 1/τ_β, while free decay between freeze-out and nucleosynthesis runs at Γ_total. Under dark decay BBN therefore measures a hybrid, not τn, and under any systematic it merely re-measures τ_β with a 1.3–4.3 s error. It is not an independent arbiter between a systematic and dark decay.
14. Status of the running experiments (searches, 2026-09-30): no BL2 lifetime value found; the BL2 group published H₂ detection in the beam apparatus [new: WebSearch, search-summary, SUMMARY "Detection of molecular hydrogen in a neutron beam lifetime experiment ... investigated systematic effects in the measurement" (PRC 10.1103/nr3b-3dtl; arXiv:2506.01682)]; τSPECT has published only a Mainz storage time constant [new: WebSearch, search-summary, SUMMARY "Operation of τSPECT at the research reactor TRIGA Mainz extracted a neutron storage time constant of τ = 859(16) s"], not a lifetime. No new decisive datum has appeared since the dossier.
15. The bottle average is essentially UCNτ: with the input errors above, UCNτ carries about three-quarters of the inverse-variance weight of the seven bottle results (hand arithmetic: 1/0.29² ≈ 11.9 of a total ≈ 16.3 s⁻²). So "the bottles" at 878.4 s [D-31] mean "UCNτ plus corroboration at the 1–3 s level", just as "the beams" mean BL1 (Finding 2). The core tension is structurally one experiment against one experiment, with the others as weaker corroboration on each side [D-27], [D-20].

### C. Reframes
- **R1: "BL1 against everything" (the premise is wrong).** The question "beam vs bottle" should read "is the proton-counting normalisation of one data set off by 1.1%?". Better posed: one experiment, one number (1.113%, 4.4× its total error), and one decisive class of test (BL2/BL3 with the same method, or any non-proton beam). Clearer answer: yes. Every non-proton method (J-PARC, magnetic, material, A-route SM) sits between 877 and 881.5 s.
- **R2: "Three tensions, not one."** Proton-counting vs rest (4.7σ), material vs magnetic (3.9–4.3σ), λ_A vs λ_a (3.5σ). A useful question: is there any cause that removes more than one? A proton-detection systematic could in principle touch BL1 and aSPECT, which both detect protons, but through different observables (absolute count vs spectrum shape), so a common cause is not established. Dark decay plus aSPECT-λ removes tension 1 and turns tension 3 into its support, but leaves tension 2 (it cannot make material and magnetic traps differ).
- **R3: "What value of τn should CKM, BBN and the PDG use?"** Better posed and more useful. The non-proton results agree only at S ≈ 1.8 (χ² 23.4/7 among the "rest"), so the honest answer is ≈878.4 s with an inflated error, and the SM A-route (878.50 ± 0.88 s) agrees. This does not require settling the cause.

## What would change my mind
- BL2 (same Penning method, measured H₂) reproducing ≈888 s with a sub-second error would weaken A and raise C and D, since the proton-counting method would then be replicated.
- LiNA at ≈888 ± 1 s would move J-PARC to the beam side and make "beam vs bottle" the correct partition again (premise 2 restored).
