# Analysis: constraints (Kant: conditions any valid answer must satisfy)
status: final

## Method applied
Audit of every constraint that a valid explanation of the beam (proton-counting) vs bottle lifetime gap must satisfy: SM master-formula consistency (λ, Vud, radiative corrections), first-row CKM unitarity, dark-decay kinematics (⁹Be, χ stability, ¹¹Be), direct searches, neutron-star structure, n → n′ search limits, and the size each systematic must have against its published error budget. GR tooling (gr_tensors.py) is not applicable: no metric enters this question.

F1. Dark-decay kinematic window, lower edge. Fornal–Grinstein's printed ⁹Be bound is the ⁹Be → ⁸Be + n separation energy: [new: Fornal & Grinstein, arXiv:1801.01124 (PRL 120, 191801), full-text, "The most stringent of such nuclear decay constraints comes from the requirement of 9Be stability, for which ∆M = 937.900 MeV, thus 937.900 MeV< min { Mf′ } ≤Mf < 939.565 MeV."] A later paper states the tighter three-body (⁹Be → α + α + χ) threshold: [new: McKeen, Pospelov & Raj, PRL 125, 231803 (2020), arXiv:2006.15140, full-text, "There is also a lower limit on the χ mass of mχ > 938.0 MeV from the stability of 9Be [10, 14]."] The same paper gives the hydrogen-decay channel that opens whenever the dark final state is lighter than the H atom: [new: ibid., full-text, "If mχ <m H≃mp +me, atomic hydrogen decays to χ +νe with a lifetime [7, 9] τH = 1029 s (10−10 θ )2(me Q )2"] and "the hydrogen lifetime through this channel is constrained to be τH & 1029 s by a search for n→ χγ [12] and a recast of Borexino data [13]" (the "&" is the PDF extraction of "≳"). Recomputed with nuclear masses in [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/lens-constraints_darkwindow.py] (F10).

F11. Hydrogen-decay corollary, same calc: in the single-χ mixing model (F–G eq. 7: ΔΓ = g_n²e²/(8π)(1 − x²)³ m_n θ²), a 1.113% branch needs θ = 6.7×10⁻¹¹ (m_χ = 937.993 MeV) to 1.6×10⁻¹⁰ (938.7 MeV). F12. Neutron-star bound, toy TOV [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/lens-constraints_nstar.py]. Validation: a free-neutron gas gives 0.709 M☉ (the Oppenheimer–Volkoff limit); converged at dr = 0.03 vs 0.02 km. A toy neutron-matter EOS reaching 2.36 M☉ collapses to M_max = 0.687–0.688 M☉ once a free χ (m_χ = 937.993 or 938.543 MeV) is admitted in chemical equilibrium (μ_χ = μ_n). This reproduces D-88's ≈0.7 M☉ independently, and the result barely depends on the neutron EOS, because χ takes over the core. Adding repulsive vector self-interaction for χ restores M_max = 1.13 / 1.62 / 1.90 / 2.06 / 2.21 M☉ at m_V/g = 200 / 100 / 60 / 45 / 30 MeV, matching D-77's Cline–Cornell m_A′/g′ ≲ 45–60 MeV. Caveat: the toy neutron EOS is acausal (max c_s² = 1.30) inside its own maximum-mass star, and the 45 and 30 MeV cases reach c_s² = 1.05 and 1.19, so the escape threshold is approximate (~50 ± 10 MeV). The free-χ stars are causal (c_s² ≤ 0.16). Verdict: the NS bound kills minimal (non-interacting) dark decay robustly within its assumptions (T = 0, beta equilibrium, χ produced in the star, no χ–n interaction). It is evaded by χ–χ repulsion with m_V/g ≲ ~50 MeV. The Born contact estimate of that repulsion gives σ_χχ/m_χ ≈ 1.3–4 cm²/g at m_V/g = 60–45 MeV. That is order unity (the SIDM-bound scale if χ were all of dark matter; a comparison value this lens has not sourced), and the Born approximation is invalid there (α′m_χ/m_V ≈ 1.2–1.7 for g = 1). So the escape is strained if χ is the dominant dark matter, and open if χ is subdominant.

### B. Validity domains of the models relied on
- SM master formula and radiative corrections: V−A at O(α) with O(α²) leading logarithms, and dispersive or lattice γW box. Valid to ~10⁻⁴ in τ_β [D-02, D-99]; the question's 1% regime lies well inside.
- Superallowed Ft: nuclear-structure corrections δNS and δC are the leading model dependence. Their spread (Vud 0.97361–0.97386 across evaluations) moves τ_β by ≤ 0.12 s once ΔR^V cancels (F3). Inside the domain.
- Dark-decay effective theory (Fornal–Grinstein): n–χ mixing below the QCD scale. The mass window relies on nuclear binding energies only. Inside.
- Neutron-star bound: T = 0 Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff (TOV) structure with ideal or toy EOS and chemical equilibrium reached over the star's age. My toy EOS is acausal above ~2.3 M☉ (c_s² = 1.30 at the M_max centre), so it validates the free-χ collapse (causal, c_s² ≤ 0.16) and not the precise escape threshold.
- n → n′: two-level quantum mechanics with adiabatic plus Landau–Zener passage. Valid when the resonance is crossed locally linearly. The SNS magnet geometry is not modelled here.
- UCN storage and neutron optics: established. The "unidentified loss" is by definition outside the modelled budget, so no theory bounds it, only the budgets and the cross-trap consistency (K10).
- GR, semiclassical gravity, energy conditions, quantum inequalities: not engaged (F18).

## What would change my mind
- Nab (or another a-coefficient measurement) confirming λ ≈ 1.267 at ≤ 0.001: K1 and K2 flip. CONSTRAINTS-B and C become consistent with the SM route and A becomes strained (3.2σ). A PERKEO-like λ from Nab would instead leave C with only the Fierz escape.
- BL2 reproducing ~888 s with an independently re-measured fluence and a measured residual-gas composition: the sub-hypotheses of A lose their room.
- LiNA or UCNProBe at ≤ 1–2 s reading ~888 s: the J-PARC 2024 support for A (K11) would be reversed at 9.5σ against UCNτ.
- A published n → n′ reanalysis showing the SNS magnet passage is far more adiabatic than BL1's (by ≳ 70× in |dB/dz|·v): D would re-open.
- A realistic, causal nuclear EOS with χ in which M_max ≥ 2 M☉ is reached without χ repulsion (for example because χ is not produced in the core within the star's age): K8 would drop.
- UCNτ or τSPECT finding a holding-time-dependent loss at 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ (for example marginal trapping at long times): B would gain.
