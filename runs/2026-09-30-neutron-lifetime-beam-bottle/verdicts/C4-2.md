# Verdict on C4 (refuter 2, angle: bounds)
claim: Neutron–mirror-neutron conversion in the 4.6–5 T proton-trap field removes about 1.1% of beam neutrons, lengthening the proton-beam lifetime; bottles and J-PARC read the true value (speculative).
status: final
verdict: refuted
basis: cited-evidence
narrowed: none

Units: SI (fields in T, lengths in m, speeds in m/s, times in s); mass splittings and mixing energies in neV (ħ = c = 1 for masses). Spin-averaged, unpolarized beams throughout.

## Strongest attack
The core claim is that n → n′ conversion **in the 4.6–5 T field of the proton trap** makes the beam read about 1.1% long. That is exactly the model a direct search was built to test, and the search excluded it.

1. **The SNS regeneration search excludes it directly and self-consistently.** Broussard et al. (PRL 128, 212503, 2022) took the model's parameter band from the *measured BL1 field profile*, not from an "equal passages" premise. They then computed the regeneration probability in their own split-pair magnet (4.8 T at the centre, 6.6 T peaks) and found the whole anomaly band excluded for Δm > 10 neV. The candidate's own window is Δm ≈ 277–320 neV: resonance inside the trap, and not more than about 20–40 neV above |µn|·B_centre, because below that the beam reads short (M-MECHANIST-10). That window sits in the middle of the SNS magnet's resonant range. |µn|·4.8 T = 289.5 neV and |µn|·6.6 T = 398 neV, which are the paper's own "289 neV … 400 neV" features.
2. **My own recomputation, with no equal-passage premise, puts the candidate's parameters 10⁴–10⁷ times over the limit.** For each Δm in 278–320 neV I solved for the θ0 that gives τ_beam/τ_β = 1.0113 in a BL1-like 4.6 T solenoid. I then propagated the same (Δm, θ0) through a split-pair model fitted to the SNS magnet (centre 4.81 T, peaks 6.60 T at ±6.36 cm). The predicted apparent transmission is p = 6.3×10⁻⁴ to 0.50, against the measured limit p < 2.5×10⁻⁸ (95% CL). The smallest excess anywhere in the window is 2.5×10⁴, at Δm = 278 neV, and it holds for two trap bore radii and for three collapse points of the absorber [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C4-2_sns_regeneration.py]. My model reproduces the paper's worked example (Δm = 289 neV, θ0 = 5×10⁻³, v = 500 m/s), giving 0.68 in n′ at the magnet centre against the paper's "60 % … on reaching the beam-catcher". That supports the transfer between geometries.
3. **This closes the one loophole the slate left open.** Candidates.md flags that the 70× and 5×10³× factors (M-CONSTRAINTS-13, M-MECHANIST-11) rest on an unverified equal-conversion premise. M-MECHANIST-11 notes that a Landau–Zener crossing at SNS is near-adiabatic (2πΓ ≈ 7), so conversion per passage there is if anything *larger*, not smaller. The explicit profile calculation shows the premise is unnecessary, and it was conservative: p ≫ P_trap².

## Size against the discrepancy   (anomaly questions)
- **Required:** τ_beam/τ_β = 1.0113, i.e. +9.9 s on the proton-counting beam (reference gap 9.65 ± 2.08 s, 1.09%, candidates.md). Every other class shifts by ≈ 0: J-PARC < 0.01 s (M-MECHANIST-11), magnetic traps ≈ 0, material bottles a per-bounce loss that the size extrapolation removes.
- **Allowed by the SNS bound:** with the parameters that produce +9.9 s, the model predicts SNS p ≥ 6×10⁻⁴. Conversely, holding p below 2.5×10⁻⁸ requires P_trap far below 10⁻² everywhere in the window. The crude P ≤ √p cap gives ≤ 1.6×10⁻⁴, a beam shift ≤ 0.14 s (M-MECHANIST-11). My explicit profiles give p that is 10⁴–10⁷ above the limit, so the true cap is lower still. The allowed shift is at most about 1.4% of the gap, and it carries none of it.
- **Sign check:** below Δm ≈ 277 neV the beam reads short (M-MECHANIST-10), so moving Δm down cannot rescue the sign. Below 10 neV the trap field cannot compensate Δm, so the strong-field mechanism is absent. There the UCN anomalous-loss reinterpretation excludes θ0 ≳ 10⁻³ for Δm ≲ 60 neV (Broussard 2022, quoting Berezhiani).

## Corrections to details
- The slate's caveat that the SNS exclusion rests on "equal passages" is misplaced. The exclusion in the source uses the measured BL1 profile and a full density-matrix calculation for the SNS magnet. My independent profile calculation gives regeneration well above P_trap², so the 70× and 5×10³× factors understate the exclusion by orders of magnitude.
- The needed θ0 varies across the window far more than "1.0–2.2×10⁻³". It is 4.8×10⁻⁴ at 278 neV (resonance at the trap edge, Landau–Zener regime) and rises to about 2×10⁻² at 320 neV (adiabatic regime), for the toolkit's BL1-like solenoid. At the upper end the implied per-bounce loss in material bottles, ½sin²2θ0 ≈ 2θ0² ≈ 10⁻³, would dwarf the Fomblin upscatter loss coefficient η ≈ 2×10⁻⁶ that Serebrov 2005 quotes ("the expected UCN loss coefficient η due to up-scattering is about 2·10-6"). The comparison is only order-of-magnitude, because I did not pin down the collision frequency, and the SNS bound already decides the case.
- The dossier's "ORNL/HFIR" label for Broussard 2022 is wrong: the experiment ran on SNS beamline 4A.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **Nothing of the core survives.** The core names the strong-field (4.6–5 T) mechanism, and the dedicated search designed from BL1's own field map excludes it. The Tan inverse variant contradicts UCNτ and J-PARC and fails the A-route bound. The weak-field B′ variant is excluded by PSI over 99.98% of 4π, and it could not act specifically in the proton trap anyway.
- **Remaining doubt:**
  - n′ attenuation in the B4C absorber lowers SNS sensitivity for 400–600 neV. The paper handles this with a B = 0 dataset (p < 5.5×10⁻⁸), and that range lies outside the candidate's 277–320 neV window.
  - Models with a neutron transition magnetic moment (nTMM), or with both Δm and B′ tuned, are explicitly not constrained by Broussard ("does not provide constraints on alternative models … nTMM"). A new model of that kind would be a different candidate, needing its own resonance in the trap and no signal at SNS.
  - My SNS field map is a two-loop fit to the three quoted numbers, not the manufacturer's map. A mismatch of a factor of a few in gradients cannot absorb a 2.5×10⁴ excess.
- **Supplementary, model-dependent:** McKeen et al. (PRL 127, 061805, 2021) state that neutron-star heating limits "compete with laboratory searches … but apply to a range of mass splittings … 19 orders of magnitude larger". C4 needs τ_nn′ ≈ 10⁻⁷–5×10⁻⁶ s, far shorter than the laboratory limits of order 10–10² s. Goldman et al. (EPJC 82, 945, 2022) argue that a mirror millicharge can weaken this bound, so I do not rest the verdict on it.
