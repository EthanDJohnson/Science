# Verdict on C8 (refuter 0, angle: magnitude)
claim: Beam versus bottle is the wrong partition: the split is proton-detecting measurements (essentially BL1, plus aSPECT's λ) versus all others, with one proton-side cause behind both lifetime and λ tensions.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: The data split better as proton-counting versus everything else than as beam versus bottle, but the lifetime tension (BL1 +9.9 s) and the λ tension (aSPECT |a| low by 2.7%) cannot both come from one generic proton-side effect of the right size; only a finely tuned loss of low-energy recoil protons, acting the same way in two very different apparatus, does both, so the default reading is two separate proton-side problems (or C1 plus an unrelated aSPECT issue).

## Strongest attack
The partition half of C8 is a statistical statement, already verified (M-STATISTICIAN-04, M-EXAMINER-01: proton/rest beats beam/bottle by Δχ² = 5.3). My attack is on the other half, the *mechanism* half: "one proton-side cause behind both lifetime and λ tensions". Under that cause the sizes must work out. BL1 needs a 1.11% deficit in absolutely counted protons (+9.9 s). aSPECT needs its fitted a moved by Δa = +0.00285, from −0.10687 (the PERKEO III λ) to −0.10402. The two observables respond to a proton-side effect in structurally different ways:

1. **aSPECT does not see the absolute count.** Its fit has a free normalisation, so a loss that is uniform in recoil energy T (dead layer, backscatter, detector efficiency at fixed post-acceleration energy) gives Δa = 0 exactly, however large it is. That is the class of effect most natural for BL1, which counts protons after ~30 kV acceleration, where the 0–751 eV recoil energy barely matters. So the natural BL1 systematics produce **none** of the λ tension.
2. **aSPECT does not see protons below 50 eV.** It uses no retardation setting below 50 V. A sharp loss of low-energy protons large enough for BL1 (T < 25 eV → 1.11%, +9.9 s) gives **Δa = 0.000000** in aSPECT. A loss sharp enough to move aSPECT by the required +0.00285 (T < 58 eV) removes 3.8% of BL1's protons, giving **+34.6 s**, 3.5× the gap.
3. **Smooth common efficiencies undershoot BL1.** A linear low-energy-weighted loss ε = 1 − k(1 − T/T_max) tuned to aSPECT's Δa needs k = 0.0048 and gives BL1 only **+2.2 s** (22% of the gap). An exponential loss ε = 1 − k·exp(−T/T₀) gives 1.7–2.8 s for T₀ = 50–400 eV and 5.0–6.9 s for T₀ = 25–30 eV. It reaches the gap only in a narrow window, T₀ ≈ 21–22 eV with k ≈ 0.86–1.0 (T₀ = 22 eV: 9.1 s; 20 eV already needs k = 1.25 > 1, which is unphysical). So a single shared ε(T) can reproduce both sizes only when tuned to about ±1 eV in T₀ and pushed close to total loss at low energy.
4. **Time-scaled in-flight losses (residual-gas charge exchange, the one named proton-side mechanism [D-72]) fail by about two orders of magnitude.** BL1 stores protons for ~10 ms. aSPECT protons fly for ~10 µs. At equal gas density the aSPECT loss fraction is 1.11% × 10⁻³ = 1.1×10⁻⁵. Even with all of it placed in the most a-sensitive energy band (dΔa/df = +2.74 at T ≈ 240–250 eV), it gives Δa = +3.0×10⁻⁵, **94× short** of +0.00285. aSPECT would need ~94× BL1's gas density × cross-section, and that is before any energy dependence.
5. **The "one-to-one mapping" is not a prediction of R-strong.** Under R-strong both numbers are artefacts. Across the effect families above, the BL1 shift that goes with the required aSPECT shift runs from 0 to 35 s, depending on shape. So the fact that the biased pair lands on an SM-consistent line, (Δa/|a|)/(Δτ/τ) ≈ 2.1 (SM) against 2.4 observed [M-CONSTRAINTS-04 "two worlds"], is an unexplained coincidence under a detection-side cause. It cannot count as evidence for one. The candidate cites that closure as support. Under R-strong it is a free-parameter accident, and it would be a natural outcome only if τ_β really were ~888 s (a C3/C5-type world), which R-strong denies.

## Size against the discrepancy
Shifts under R-strong with one shared proton-side effect, each with its sign, against the observed gap:
- **Proton-counting beam (BL1).** Needed +9.9 s (tau long; count low by 1.11%). A uniform or detector-type effect can supply all of it but gives aSPECT Δa = 0. A shared ε(T) tuned to aSPECT supplies +2.2 s (linear), +1.7 to +6.9 s (exponential, T₀ ≥ 25 eV) or +34.6 s (sharp cut). Only the T₀ ≈ 21–22 eV, k ≈ 0.9–1 exponential carries the whole 9.9 s.
- **aSPECT a.** Needed Δa = +0.00285 (|a| low by 2.7%). The BL1-sized sharp cut (25 eV) gives 0. The gas-loss analogue gives ≤ +3×10⁻⁵ (≤ 1% of what is needed).
- **aCORN**, also proton-detecting, reads λ = −1.2796 ± 0.0062 [D-41], on the other side of PERKEO III. An aSPECT-like bias would put it about 2.1σ from where it sits ((1.2796 − 1.2668)/0.0062). This is low weight, but it runs against a common proton-side bias.
- **J-PARC, UCNτ and material bottles** have no shift under R-strong, which is consistent. The 3σ material/magnetic split (880.03 ± 0.70 s against 877.82 ± 0.27 s) is not carried by C8 and goes to the null residual.

Verdict on magnitude: a proton-side cause **can** carry the whole lifetime gap. **One** such cause carries both the lifetime and λ gaps only at a fine-tuned point, or needs aSPECT to have about 100× BL1's in-flight loss rate.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives.** The reframe as a partition survives: proton-counting against the rest fits better than beam against bottle, and J-PARC does behave like a bottle.
- **What does not.** The heading's "one proton-side cause" does not survive in generic form. Magnitudes force a choice:
  - a recoil-energy loss tuned to T₀ ≈ 21–22 eV and near-total at T → 0, acting alike in a 10 ms, 4.6 T Penning trap and a ~10 µs transit through a MAC-E filter; or
  - two separate proton-side problems, which reduces C8 toward R-weak/C1 plus an independent aSPECT issue.
- **Why weakened and not refuted.** The tuned solution exists within a physical range (k ≤ 1), so I cannot exclude it.
- **Doubts about my own model.**
  - The aSPECT model is idealised: ideal transmission function, no edge effect, no Fierz term, equal time per setting.
  - Real aSPECT weights more time on 50 V, which would change the fitted sensitivity by an O(1) factor, not the zero results. The zeros come from the ≥ 50 V threshold and the free normalisation, which are structural.
  - I did not model how a recoil-energy-dependent loss would act inside BL1's trap. I assumed the same ε(T) as in aSPECT, which is charitable to C8.
- **Decisive test.** Nab reading a ≈ −0.1040 would support a proton-detection-side link. Even then, magnitude requires the mechanism to be found and to give ~1.1% in BL1-type traps and a 2.7% slope distortion in the spectrum, for example as a low-energy loss measurable by varying the trap or flight time.
