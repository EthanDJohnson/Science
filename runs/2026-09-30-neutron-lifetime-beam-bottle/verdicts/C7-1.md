# Verdict on C7 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: No single dominant cause: a statistical fluctuation plus several modestly underestimated uncertainties (BL1, J-PARC, material bottles), none of which dominates, produce the gap.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: The gap is carried almost entirely by the proton-counting beam (BL1 supplies 80% of it, Sussex–ILL the rest), and the only live null is that BL1's ≈ 10 s excess is a sum of a fluctuation and several modestly underestimated BL1 items, with no single ≈ 10 s effect, plus an independent ≈ 2σ high fluctuation in Sussex–ILL; J-PARC and material-bottle errors contribute nothing to the gap.

## Strongest attack
The heading names three places where modest error underestimates are spread: BL1, J-PARC and the material bottles. It says none of them dominates. The data do not allow that spread, for three reasons.

1. **Two of the three named contributors cannot produce the gap.** The reference gap is proton-counting beam minus all storage: 9.65 ± 2.08 s, 4.63σ (M-STATISTICIAN-02).
   - J-PARC is in neither mean. Moving it by ±5 s changes the gap by 0.00 s.
   - The candidate's own "material +2 s" makes the material bottles read long. Correcting it lowers the storage mean, so the gap widens to 10.10 s (4.92σ) instead of closing.
   - Only BL1 closes the gap. The candidate's own "BL1 +3 to +6 s" leaves 7.18–4.71 s, i.e. 3.44–2.26σ.
   [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C7-1_null_structure.py]
2. **The gap sits in one experiment by construction.** Split the proton-minus-rest difference into each experiment's contribution: BL1 supplies +7.72 s of 9.65 s (80.0%) and Sussex–ILL +1.93 s. Every storage experiment and J-PARC together supply less than ±0.33 s each. For the BL1-minus-UCNτ difference (9.88 s), the least-squares allocation puts 98.4% (9.72 s, a 4.33σ pull) on BL1 and 0.16 s on UCNτ. Making UCNτ carry even half the gap would take a 17.2σ pull on UCNτ. Whatever the mechanism, the gap has one dominant locus: the proton-counting beam, essentially BL1. That contradicts "none of which dominates" read as a statement about where the error lies.
3. **The candidate's own statistical support points to one dominant error.**
   - *Diffuse inflation.* Take a null where every error is underestimated by the same factor (k = 2.15, which sets global χ²/dof = 1). Monte Carlo then gives a probability of only 1.3×10⁻³ for the observed pattern: a between-class χ² ≥ 37.97/3 together with a within-class χ² ≤ 8.36/7.
   - *Heavy tails.* The candidate's heavy-tail form, independent Student-t with ν = 2–4, gives 2.0–6.9×10⁻³ for the same pattern. In the heavy-tail draws that reproduce a proton-vs-rest z ≥ 4.70, a single experiment carries ≥ 50% of the total χ² in 85–90% of cases. Under the diffuse Gaussian this happens in only 24% of cases.
   - So Bailey's heavy-tail model, the base rate C7 cites, produces gaps like this one mostly through one large outlier, not through several modest ones.
   [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C7-1_null_structure.py]

This matches Bailey's own reading of what heavy tails mean: large disagreements "usually reflect unexpected systematic effects". His example is a single unknown effect in one experiment, the LEP railway currents. The precedent in this very quantity also went that way: the 6.5σ lifetime disagreement of 2005–2010 was resolved by specific, identified systematic errors of about −6 s in named experiments, not by diffuse scatter (Serebrov & Fomin 2010, below).

What is left of C7 is narrower. BL1 (with Sussex–ILL) reads long, not through one large overlooked effect but through several modest BL1 items plus a fluctuation. That is a real and distinct possibility, because no single named effect reaches 10 s. But it has one dominant locus, and it is no longer "spread over BL1, J-PARC and material bottles".

## Corrections to details
- "Shift by class: ... J-PARC ±several s by run condition, material +2 s": neither enters the proton-vs-storage gap in a closing direction. J-PARC's internal scatter (χ² 15.8/3) and the material–magnetic split (2.95σ) are separate tensions, not parts of this gap. They cannot be counted among the "several" contributors to it.
- "Every single-cause fit leaves χ² ≈ 23 of storage-internal scatter": this is true, but the leftover scatter comes from the material–magnetic split. It is not a share of the beam–storage gap, so it does not show that the gap itself is diffuse.
- The Bailey base rate P(> 4.63σ) = 0.010–0.044 is the probability of one variable exceeding the threshold. It does not say that the excess is spread across experiments. In the same model the gap-producing draws are single-outlier-dominated in 85–90% of cases.
- The prediction "BL2/BL3 land between the poles (880–886 s)" follows only from the narrowed form (a BL1-specific composite error). It is not a class-level null prediction: under the narrowed form a new, independent apparatus should read close to storage.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What is lost.** The "spread over several experiments, none dominant" reading. By weight the gap is 80% BL1 and about 20% Sussex–ILL. The candidate's own J-PARC and material-bottle terms add nothing or the wrong sign. Diffuse inflation reproduces the observed between-class concentration with p ≈ 10⁻³.
- **What survives.** A narrower null. BL1's excess is a sum of several modest underestimates plus a fluctuation, with no single ≈ 10 s effect to be found, and Sussex–ILL is an independent ≈ 2σ co-fluctuation. Nothing I found excludes it: no named effect reaches the gap (Caylor < 0.5 s), and no BL2/BL3 result exists yet [D-100].
- **Doubt.** The narrowed form overlaps C1's A-BL1 sub-hypothesis. The difference lies only in whether the ≈ 10 s is one effect or several, which BL2/BL3 with a trap-time and field scan could test only if an effect turns up. I chose weakened over refuted because the brief's definition of the null explicitly allows "several smaller effects ... none of which dominates", and several modest BL1 items meet that definition at the level of effects even though they fail it at the level of location.
- **Limits of the Monte Carlo.** It assumes independent errors with unit-scale t (Bailey's fitted scale is not applied) and the statistician's symmetrised inputs. Sussex–ILL's value is an unverified lead [D-22]. Removing Sussex–ILL would make the BL1 share of the gap even larger, so the attack does not depend on it.
