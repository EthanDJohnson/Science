# Verdict on C7 (refuter 2, angle: bounds)
claim: No single dominant cause: a statistical fluctuation plus several modestly underestimated uncertainties (BL1, J-PARC, material bottles), none of which dominates, produce the gap.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: The ~10 s excess of the proton-counting beam results over the non-proton anchors could be a fluctuation plus several modest, same-sign, underestimated systematics inside the proton-counting measurements (BL1, and Sussex–ILL, with none of them dominant). J-PARC and material-bottle errors cannot contribute to this gap, and the null must also absorb an independent, same-sign aSPECT excursion.

## Strongest attack
The attack uses the gap arithmetic and the independent anchors the null must satisfy. Two of the three loci C7 names cannot produce the gap, so "none of which dominates" is false across classes.

1. **J-PARC is not a term in the gap.** The reference gap is proton-counting beam (BL1 + Sussex–ILL) minus all storage, 9.645 ± 2.084 s (4.63σ, SI). Its linear response to a bias b in each class is:
   - BL1: +0.823;
   - Sussex–ILL: +0.177;
   - material bottles (common bias): −0.228;
   - magnetic traps: −0.772;
   - J-PARC: **0.000**.

   An underestimated J-PARC error therefore moves the reference gap by exactly 0 s. Suppose instead the gap is read as "all beams versus bottles". Inflating J-PARC's error by its own internal scale factor (2.295) then *raises* the all-beam gap from 7.56 s (4.02σ) to 9.18 s (4.50σ), because J-PARC is the beam result that sits with the bottles. On either reading, J-PARC's underestimated uncertainty works against the claim that it helps "produce the gap".
2. **The material-bottle contribution has the wrong sign.** C7's own shift table gives material bottles +2 s (reading long). Because material results enter the storage side, this bias *lowers* the gap by 0.46 s. Material bottles contribute −5% of the gap.
3. **So the proton-counting side carries more than 100% of the gap.** Apply C7's own table: BL1 +3 to +6 s, material +2 s, magnetic ≈ 0, J-PARC ±5 s.
   - The named effects explain only +2.0 to +4.5 s.
   - The remaining 5.2 to 7.6 s (2.5–3.7σ_gap) must be a fluctuation, and it can only be a fluctuation of the proton-counting results. The storage side is pinned by UCNτ (±0.3 s), which C7 itself says reads true ("magnetic ≈ 0").
   - Fluctuation plus bias on the proton-counting side comes to 105% of the gap in both cases.

   A cross-class "no dominant contributor" picture is therefore arithmetically impossible. One class dominates: the proton-counting beam.
4. **Independent anchors place the whole gap on the proton side.**
   - Magnetic traps, J-PARC and the SM A-route τ_β (from PERKEO III/UCNA/PERKEO II λ with superallowed Vud) combine to 877.895 ± 0.253 s, with χ² 1.15/4.
   - Dropping all storage data, J-PARC plus the SM A-route alone give 878.64 ± 0.81 s. BL1 sits 3.79σ above this storage-free anchor.
   - Offsets from the full anchor: BL1 +9.80 s (4.34σ), Sussex–ILL +11.30 s (2.33σ), material +2.14 s (2.86σ, which is a separate tension), magnetic −0.08 s, J-PARC −0.70 s.
5. **"Either SM route" contradicts C7's own shift table.** If magnetic traps read true, the a-route τ_β (aSPECT, 887.21 ± 2.93 s) is 3.17σ from the anchor. So C7 must add a fourth underestimated error, a 9.3 s-equivalent excursion in aSPECT. That excursion has the *same sign* as BL1's, and like BL1 it is a proton-detecting measurement. The prediction-matrix entry "either route; no class pattern" is internally inconsistent with "magnetic ≈ 0".
6. **Same-sign structure under C7's own diffuse-inflation mechanism.** Take the truth to be the anchor and inflate all errors uniformly. The three proton-detecting results (BL1, Sussex–ILL, a-route) all land high, with one-sided joint probability:
   - 4.6×10⁻¹¹ at S = 1;
   - 1.9×10⁻⁶ at S = 1.5;
   - 9.9×10⁻⁵ at S = 2;
   - 4.5×10⁻⁴ at S = 2.35.

   Meanwhile every non-proton lifetime anchor is within 0.2σ of the anchor. These are post-selected (I chose the high results), so read them as the size of the coincidence the null must accept, not as a p-value for C7. Even so, they show that the diffuse, class-blind form of the null does not fit the observed pattern.

What the attack does *not* defeat: the null restricted to the proton-counting side. BL1 at 887.7 ± 2.25 s is 2.18σ high if its errors are doubled (one-sided p = 0.015). A 2–3σ excursion made of several same-sign BL1 items plus a stat fluctuation is unlikely but not excluded by any bound I can find. Hence *weakened*, not refuted.

## Corrections to details
- "(BL1, J-PARC, material bottles)" as contributors: only BL1 (with Sussex–ILL) contributes positively. J-PARC contributes 0 s to the proton–storage gap, and material bottles contribute −0.46 s under C7's own +2 s.
- Prediction matrix, SM column, "either route; no class pattern": this is inconsistent with "magnetic ≈ 0". The a route is then 3.17σ off, in the same direction as BL1.
- The J-PARC and material-bottle tensions (J-PARC's internal χ²/DOF 15.8/3; material against magnetic, 2.9σ) are real, but they are separate residual tensions. They are not parts of the beam–bottle gap.
- C7's decisive test says BL2/BL3 land "in between (~880–886 s)". Under C7's own "magnetic ≈ 0", the true τ is 877.9 ± 0.25 s. An intermediate BL2/BL3 reading would therefore mean a surviving proton-method bias, not a diffuse cross-class one.

## What survives and remaining doubt
What survives is a proton-side null: the BL1 (and Sussex–ILL) excess is a 2–3σ fluctuation of several modest same-sign systematics under the field's typical error scale factors of about 2, with no single item near 10 s. This is compatible with every independent bound I checked:
- the SM A route;
- CKM unitarity;
- J-PARC;
- BBN (currently uninformative).

It is distinguished from C1 only by "several items" versus "one item". It must also accept an independent same-sign aSPECT excursion of about 3σ, or else concede C8's proton-detection link.

Remaining doubt:
- The joint probabilities in item 6 are post-selected.
- Heavy-tailed error distributions (Bailey ν = 2–4) would raise them.
- Sussex–ILL (D-22) was not verified in this run.
