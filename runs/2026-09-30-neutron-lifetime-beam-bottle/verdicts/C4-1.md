Units: SI for lifetimes (s) and fields (T). Energies are in neV, with |µn| = 60.31 neV/T. Probabilities are dimensionless.

## Strongest attack
The mechanism in C4's heading has been tested directly, twice, by dedicated experiments. Both published exclusions were built for exactly this model.

1. **The resonance C4 needs.** To resonate inside the 4.6–5 T BL1/Sussex–ILL trap, the splitting has to be Δm ≈ 278–302 neV. For Δm = 280 neV the resonance field is B_res = 4.64 T [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C4-1_regeneration_limits.py].
2. **Broussard et al. 2022 excluded that region.** They did not use an idealised field. They computed the model region that gives the 1% effect using the *measured BL1 magnetic field profile*, with full density-matrix and Landau–Zener evolution, averaged over both polarizations and over the velocity spectrum. The 6.6 T regeneration search then excluded that region for all Δm > 10 neV. The paper's own conclusion is that the result "excludes this explanation for the neutron lifetime discrepancy".
3. **Gonzalez et al. 2024 tightened the limit.** With a 4.70 T field and a Cd absorber, whose resonance reaches µnB = 283 neV and so covers the 278 neV BL1 value, the limit on p_tr is ~80× tighter than in 2022: p_tr < 3.1×10⁻¹⁰ (95% CL). That paper restates that the 2022 search "excluded n→ n′ transitions as an explanation for the neutron lifetime discrepancy".
4. **This closes the gap the slate left open.** Both of the slate's reservations are answered by these sources:
   - the "evidence for" item, which says it was not established that every field-profile variant is closed;
   - the equal-passage premise that the run's math check left unverified (M-MECHANIST-11).

   The exclusion does not rest on the √p premise. It rests on the experimenters' own simulations with the real field maps. One of the model's originators (Berezhiani) co-authored the in-matter oscillation treatment (Kamyshkov, Ternullo, Varriano, Berezhiani 2022) that these analyses cite for the absorber attenuation.
5. **The sub-hypotheses fail independently.** Note that D-Tan and D-weak are not the heading claim anyway.
   - *D-Tan* reverses the sign (bottles short, beam true). It is contradicted by UCNτ and J-PARC reading ≈ 878 s [D-27, D-23].
   - *D-weak* (a B′ mirror field or a peV splitting) is a different parameter region that PSI excludes [D-63, D-90]. It also does not act "in the 4.6–5 T proton-trap field".
6. **The escape routes are also closed.**
   - Δm < 10 neV cannot resonate at 4.6–5 T. It also meets the UCN anomalous-loss limit, θ0 ≳ 10⁻³ excluded for Δm ≲ 60 neV [D-89].
   - Negative Δm, per the source, is "not consistent with the neutron lifetime anomaly".

## Size against the discrepancy   (anomaly questions)
Within the parameter region the SNS experiments allow, the shifts by class are as follows.
- **Proton-counting beam:** the naive-premise cap is ≤ +0.14 s (2022 limit) and ≤ +0.016 s (2024 limit). The published simulations with the real maps exclude the whole 1%-effect band for Δm > 10 neV, so the realistic shift is ≈ 0 s against the required +9.9 s. For Δm below the trap resonance the sign flips and the beam reads short [M-MECHANIST-10], which is the wrong direction.
- **J-PARC:** < 0.01 s.
- **Material bottles:** a negligible per-bounce loss.
- **Magnetic traps:** ≈ 0.

At best the candidate carries ~1% of the gap (with the 2022 limit), and ~0.2% with the 2024 limit, i.e. none of it in practice.

## Corrections to details
- "ORNL/HFIR" in the brief is imprecise. Both regeneration searches ran at SNS; HFIR was only proposed.
- The slate's "evidence for" says that "whether every field-profile ... variant is closed by the SNS search was not established". This is answered by the source: Broussard used the measured BL1 profile, and Gonzalez reports that the falloff profile outside the high-field region does not significantly change the transmission probability.
- The slate cites only the 2022 limit. The current limit is Gonzalez 2024: p_tr < 3.1×10⁻¹⁰ at 4.70 T (2.8×10⁻¹⁰ combined), about 80× stronger.
- The decisive test proposed, a regeneration search at BL1's 4.6–5 T profile with P < 10⁻⁴ per passage, has in effect already been done. The 4.70 T Cd-absorber run implies ~2×10⁻⁵ per passage under the run's premise.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- The heading claim does not survive. Its only viable parameter space (Δm ≈ 260–300 neV, θ0 ~ 10⁻³, positive Δm) is excluded at 95% CL by two independent SNS runs whose analyses used the BL1 field map.
- The residual doubt is model-level, not experimental. A mirror-sector variant in which n′ interacts strongly enough with the absorber to kill regeneration, while still leaving BL1 conversion intact, would evade the test. No such variant is proposed in the dossier, and the published in-matter treatment (co-authored by Berezhiani) is already included.
- I found no published rebuttal of the SNS exclusion by the model's proponents. One WebSearch (search-summary) and lit_search since 2022 turned up none.
- The two-field BL2/BL3 test remains a useful generic check for any field-dependent effect, but it is not needed to dispose of C4.
