# Verdict on C7 (refuter 0, angle: magnitude)
claim: No single dominant cause: a statistical fluctuation plus several modestly underestimated uncertainties (BL1, J-PARC, material bottles), none of which dominates, produce the gap.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: The 9.65 s gap sits almost entirely in the proton-counting beam results (BL1, with Sussex–ILL); C7 survives only as the claim that this proton-side excess is a heavy-tailed fluctuation, or several modest BL1 items adding up, rather than one identifiable effect. J-PARC and the material-bottle scatter contribute nothing to the gap: they are separate, smaller tensions.

## Strongest attack
The null says the gap is shared among several contributors, none of which dominates. The magnitude calculation shows that one locus, the proton-counting beam pole and BL1 in particular, dominates the gap under every way of dividing it. Two of the three contributors C7 names contribute zero to the gap, or the wrong sign [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C7-0_magnitude.py].

1. **J-PARC contributes exactly 0 s.** The reference gap is proton beam (BL1 + Sussex) against storage (8 results), and J-PARC belongs to neither pole, so d(gap)/d(J-PARC) = 0. In the all-beam variant (beam mean 885.88 s), J-PARC's weight is 0.193. If J-PARC's true value were higher than its reading ("underestimated" in the direction that would move it toward BL1), the beam mean would rise, by 0.97 s for 5 s, and the gap would widen. J-PARC's χ² contribution against a single common value is 0.09 of 46.3 (0.2%) (§3, §5).
2. **The material bottles have the wrong sign.** C7's own shift table says the material bottles read high by +2 s. Correcting them by −2 s moves the storage mean by −0.456 s and widens the gap by +0.456 s (§3). The correction does cure the storage-internal scatter: the storage scale factor S falls from 1.85 to 1.10. That makes it a separate tension (material against magnetic), not part of the beam–bottle gap. Their observed position, 2.2 s above the magnetic traps, points the same way: any error that explains that split pushes the gap wider.
3. **Under C7's own numbers, BL1 carries all of the explained part.** Take BL1 corrected by 3 to 6 s, as C7 states, with material −2 s and magnetic 0. The explained part is then 2.0 to 4.9 s of 9.65 s. BL1 alone supplies 2.47 to 4.94 s of that; the material item subtracts 0.46 s. The residual that "fluctuation" must supply is 4.7 to 7.6 s, which is 2.26σ to 3.72σ (two-sided p from 0.024 down to 2.0×10⁻⁴) (§3). At the low end of C7's BL1 range the residual is not a plausible Gaussian fluctuation. At the high end, BL1's correction is 2.67 σ_BL1, 3.2× its whole 1.9 s systematic budget. That is not a "modest" underestimate, and it is the largest single piece.
4. **Where does the fluctuation part live?** The most probable Gaussian division of the gap, with minimum Σ(δ_i/σ_i)², puts each pole's share in proportion to its variance. The proton-beam pole carries 98.7% (9.52 s) with storage errors unscaled, or 95.7% (9.23 s) with them S-scaled. Storage carries 0.13 to 0.42 s (§1). The "statistical fluctuation" part of the null is therefore itself a proton-beam fluctuation.
5. **Even the most "democratic" division keeps the proton side dominant.** Pull every one of the 10 results by the same k of its own σ toward closing the gap. BL1 then supplies 57.2% of the closure, the proton pair 83.7%, the magnetic traps 9.0% and the material bottles 7.3%. Reaching 2σ needs k = 1.69 on all ten at once, with the same sign on each. Here the material bottles must move up, so they would have to read low: the opposite of C7's +2 s (§2). "None dominates" fails in the one allocation built to favour it.

## Corrections to details
- "Several modestly underestimated uncertainties (BL1, J-PARC, material bottles)" is wrong as a decomposition of this gap. J-PARC contributes 0 s. The material bottles contribute −0.46 s under C7's own sign. They belong to other tensions: J-PARC's internal S = 2.29, and material against magnetic at 2.95σ.
- The prediction "BL2/BL3 in between (~880–886 s)" holds only if the BL1 excess comes from biases the proton-counting method shares. If it is a heavy-tailed fluctuation specific to BL1, which is the form the magnitude calculation leaves standing, the same null predicts BL2/BL3 ≈ 878 s, like C1. The matrix entry should carry both.
- C7 lists "Shift by class: … material +2 s" as part of the gap's make-up. In fact it widens the gap (see §3).

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives:** the heavy-tail statistical reading, with BL1 as the tail event. In a Student-t fit with unit scale over all 11 results (§4, §6), BL1's pull is about 4.26:
  - its tail probability for one result is 0.013 to 0.050 (ν = 4 to 2);
  - the chance that some result among the 11 lies that far out is 0.14 to 0.43;
  - both proton-counting results high together (Sussex pull about 2.3) is 2.8×10⁻⁴ to 1.9×10⁻³, or ≤ 0.016 to 0.10 after the 55-pair look-elsewhere.

  So a heavy-tailed BL1 (+ Sussex) excursion is not excluded. SER18's pull of about 3.6 shows that storage has tail events of its own. The diffuse part of the null is real for the total χ²: BL1 holds 36.6%, SER18 23.8% and Sussex 10.7%. But that part explains the storage scatter, not the gap.
- **Doubt:**
  - The Student-t numbers use unit scale; Bailey's fitted scale is not applied, so they are indicative.
  - Once "several items within BL1" (charge exchange, nonlinearity, absorption, halo) is taken as the surviving mechanism, the narrowed null is hard to tell from C1's BL1 sub-hypothesis. Under the schema, rival effects within one method belong to that method's candidate. What distinguishes C7 now is only the claim that the proton-side excess is partly or wholly a fluctuation rather than a correctable bias.
  - I chose weakened over refuted because the heavy-tail form survives the magnitude test. Its cross-class, "none dominates" form does not.
