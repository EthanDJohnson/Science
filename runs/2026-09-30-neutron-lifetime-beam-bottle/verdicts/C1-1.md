# Verdict on C1 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: An unidentified systematic in the proton-counting beam method (BL1, Sussex–ILL) makes it read about 1.1% long; the storage value near 878 s is the true lifetime.
status: final
verdict: survives
basis: cited-evidence
narrowed: none

## Strongest attack
The literature attack that does the most damage is this. The experimenters have now bounded, in situ, each concrete effect C1 names, and each bound is 10–20× smaller than the gap:
- the ⁶Li fluence-monitor efficiency (A1);
- H₂ charge exchange, including the "weakest link" the candidate flags, whether the H₂⁺ product escapes detection (A2-H₂);
- proton detection losses (A3).

C1's "unidentified" systematic is therefore not just unidentified. It has to sit outside every channel that has been measured directly. Against C1 there is also the proton-recoil (a) route to λ, where aSPECT sides with the beam.

The attack does not defeat the core claim, for four reasons:
- The heading asserts an *unidentified* systematic, so the absence of a named effect is already priced into the claim.
- Two routes remain open and unmeasured: a mis-sized large correction (A4), and charge exchange or losses on gases other than H₂.
- The independent evidence that decides between "beam is long" and "storage is short" favours C1. This includes J-PARC, the A-route τ_β, CKM unitarity, and the fact that the proton class is essentially one dataset.
- The a-route evidence is itself in 3.5σ conflict with PERKEO III, so it cannot carry a refutation.

## Evidence
**1. A1 (monitor efficiency) is excluded at its needed size by a direct calibration.** Yue et al., Metrologia 55, 460 (2018), arXiv:1801.03086 (ACCESS: abstract via fetch_text): "With these measurements, the detection efficiency of a neutron monitor based on neutron absorption on 6Li was determined to an overall uncertainty of 0.058 %." The method "involves only the counting of measured rates and is independent of neutron cross sections, decay chain branching ratios, and neutron beam energy."

Because τ ∝ 1/ε₀, 0.058% is 0.51 s. The gap is 9.88 s (1.126%), which is 19× that. Even with the 0.9 s deposit-mass-change term added in quadrature (1.04 s), the gap is 9.5× the budget [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C1-1_named_bounds.py]. C1 needs ε₀ about 1.16% off (M-MECHANIST-02), so A1 would require the 2013/2018 calibration to be wrong by about 20σ.

**2. A2-H₂ is closed on the very point C1 calls its weakest link.** Caylor et al., PRC 112, 065501 (2025), arXiv:2506.01682 (ACCESS: full-text, p. 16):
- "the efficiency for detecting H + 2 is high, and it would have been included along with the protons in the way the BL1 data were analyzed. Based on the quantitative estimates discussed in Section V D, we can state with confidence that charge exchange be- tween trapped protons and molecular hydrogen would have produced a negligible (much less than 0.5 s) effect on the reported neutron lifetime."
- "analysis using a worst-case scenario for the amount of H + 2 contamination produces a shift in the measured neutron lifetime that is less than 0.5 s. We conclude that ... it is unlikely to be the cause of the current disagreement".
- On proton and H₂⁺ efficiencies (p. 12): for gold layers "of 60µg/cm 2 and below, the difference in the detection efficiencies be- tween the proton and H + 2 is<1.5 %".

So the H₂ channel is at most 5.1% of the gap [calc: same file]. The candidate's scenario, "whole gap needs H₂ near 3.7×10⁻⁷ Pa with undetected ions", fails on its second condition.

**3. The BL1 team rejects the rest of Serebrov's list.** Wietfeldt et al., PRD 107, 118501 (2023) (ACCESS: abstract via INSPIRE): "We show that these effects were considered in the original analyses and that our corrections and systematic uncertainties were appropriate. ... None of the issues raised lead us to alter the value of the neutron lifetime reported." The full text was not available (no free copy), so its quantitative residual-gas limits could not be checked. **4. A3 (proton detection).** Backscatter (0.4 s) and Si scattering (0.5 s) together make 0.64 s, a factor of 15.4 short of the gap [calc: same file; budget from D-21, Nico 2005 Table IV].

**5. What remains open, from the literature itself.**
- *Pro-C1:* Byrne et al., EPJA 58, 151 (2022) (ACCESS: abstract via INSPIRE): "In a previous publication we have shown that the discrepancy can be resolved by taking account of electron transfer charge exchange reactions between residual gases and final state protons stored in a quasi-Penning trap. ... These data employed trapping times greater by a factor of a thousand than became conventional in later experiments. The data show that significant event losses occur, probably due to residual gas". Byrne et al., J. Phys. G 46, 085001 (2019) (ACCESS: abstract via INSPIRE): "these losses are significant for neutron lifetime measurements in vacua down to 10−11 mm Hg when 0.1% accuracy in the neutron lifetime is sought." These are gases other than H₂, which Caylor does not bound. Caylor itself notes that "charge exchange producing H + 2 or other ions may be of particular con[cern] for beam experiments". This supports C1's A-gen scope, but only qualitatively: the loss sizes come from trapping times about 10³ times longer than BL1's "typical proton trapping period was 10 ms in duration" (Nico 2005, arXiv nucl-ex/0411041, p. 14, full-text).
- *A4:* the two largest BL1 corrections (⁶Li absorption +5.4 s, trap nonlinearity −5.3 s) would each supply 54–55% of the gap at 100% error, and 108% together at the same sign [calc: same file]. No publication tests or excludes a 2× mis-sizing, so A4 remains a live, unmeasured route.

**6. Independent evidence on "which side is wrong" still favours C1.** These are sourced in the candidate and checked by the math files:
- J-PARC's electron-counting beam is 0.14σ from UCNτ (M-EXAMINER-03).
- The A-route τ_β is 878.70 ± 0.83 s (M-CONSTRAINTS-01/-02).
- The CKM sum is at −1.2σ with storage against −4.6σ with BL1 (M-CONSTRAINTS-04).
- Without BL1 the proton class sits only 2.2σ from the rest (M-EMPIRICIST-03).

The one counter-route is the a-route (aSPECT), which puts C1 at 3.2σ (M-CONSTRAINTS-03). It is itself 3.49σ from PERKEO III (M-STATISTICIAN-10), so one of the two λ families is wrong under every candidate on the slate, and this route cannot single out C1 for refutation.

No new beam result or re-analysis (BL2, BL3, LiNA, Nab a) was found in literature up to 2026-09-30 (lit_search "neutron lifetime beam" since 2025). The only items were Desai 2026 on J-PARC pressure effects and theory papers.

Other classes under C1:
- zero shift in J-PARC, material and magnetic storage, and the A-route SM prediction;

C1 can carry the whole gap only through A4 or an unmeasured residual-gas species. Both are "unidentified" in the sense the heading asserts.

## Corrections to details
- **A2 weakest link.** Caylor 2025 does settle whether H₂⁺ escapes detection. The C1 text treats this as open ("The weakest link is whether the H₂⁺ product escapes detection; Caylor determines that ion detection efficiency"). The answer is no: H₂⁺ is detected with efficiency within 1.5% of the proton's and "would have been included along with the protons". The H₂ sub-route is closed (< 0.5 s). The scenario "whole gap needs H₂ near 3.7×10⁻⁷ Pa with undetected ions", and the prediction that a BL2 trap-time scan shows "+2.6 s per 10 ms" "under Serebrov's reading", should be dropped for H₂. They could apply only to other gases whose product ions are lost.
- **A1 as "plausible".** The mechanist's and dialectician's ranking of A1 as plausible conflicts with Yue 2018's 0.058% monitor-efficiency uncertainty. A1 needs about a 19σ calibration error and should rank least likely, as the decomposer ranks it.
- **Byrne evidence missing.** The "evidence for" list omits Byrne et al. 2019 and 2022 (Sussex–ILL group), which report significant residual-gas losses in proton-trap data. That is the only published claim of a loss mechanism beyond H₂. It is qualitative for BL1's 10 ms trapping.

## What survives and remaining doubt
The core stands. An unidentified multiplicative loss or mis-sized correction in Penning-trap proton counting is still the explanation best supported by the independent arbiters (J-PARC, A-route τ_β, CKM). No source read here refutes it.

What the evidence does do is shrink the room: the monitor efficiency, H₂ charge exchange and proton detection are each bounded at ≤ 11% of the gap. The candidate therefore now rests on two things nobody has measured, either a factor-2 error in the trap-nonlinearity or ⁶Li-absorption correction, or losses on non-H₂ residual gas.

Remaining doubts:
- A pending BL2 or BL3 result reading ≈ 888 s with a characterised residual gas would refute C1.
- So would LiNA or UCNProBe reading ≈ 888 s.
- aSPECT's a-route (3.2σ against C1) is the strongest standing evidence against it, but it is not decisive while PERKEO III and aSPECT disagree at 3.5σ.
- The Wietfeldt 2023 full text, with the residual-gas limits, was not accessible.
