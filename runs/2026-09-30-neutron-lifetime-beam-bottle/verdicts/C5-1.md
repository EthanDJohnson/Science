# Verdict on C5 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: A baryon-number-violating branch n → X⁺e⁻ν̄ of about 1.1% yields an electron but no proton, so proton counting reads long while electron counting and bottles read true (speculative).
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: A ~1.1% proton-less, electron-always branch survives only if its dark products are tuned into a 0.83 MeV-wide mass window (937.900 MeV < 2m_A + 2m_e, i.e. 468.44 MeV < m_A < 469.27 MeV) with an MeV-scale, not "energetic", positron and an X⁺ lifetime below about 1 ms, and only if λ takes the a-coefficient value (|λ| ≈ 1.267). The preprint's own realization (m_A = 370–460 MeV, energetic positron, prompt X⁺ decay) is excluded by nuclear stability.

## Strongest attack
The only source for C5 is Veselský et al. 2025 (arXiv:2507.17340, an unrefereed preprint) [D-95]. Its chain is n → X⁺ e⁻ ν̄ followed by X⁺ → 2A⁰ + e⁺. The preprint fixes the dark masses at "about half the mass of neutron". The only combinations it finds acceptable are m_D = 370–400 MeV (390–460 MeV before its astrophysical cut). That makes the minimal final state of the exotic decay f = 2A⁰ + e⁺ + e⁻ + ν̄, with M_f = 2m_A + 2m_e = 741–921 MeV.

The canonical dark-decay paper, Fornal & Grinstein (PRL 120, 191801), sets two conditions that any such final state must meet.
1. **Proton stability.** The exotic operator "generally gives rise to proton decay via p→n* + e⁺ + ν_e", and this is removed only if M_f > m_p − m_e.
2. **Nuclear stability.** ⁹Be stability requires 937.900 MeV < min{M_f′} ≤ M_f < 939.565 MeV.

The preprint's parameters violate both conditions, by 17–197 MeV [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C5-1_stability.py, Part A]. The on-shell X⁺ is blocked inside nuclei, but a bound neutron can still decay through an off-shell X⁺*, with 100–200 MeV of phase space. Nothing kinematic suppresses it.

I computed that off-shell rate in ¹³⁰Te and compared it with the weakest bound there is: the geochemical, mode-independent nucleon lifetime limit τ > 1.6×10²⁵ yr (Evans & Steinberg 1977). The chain then faces a dilemma.
- **If X⁺ decays within about 1 ms**, bound neutrons decay with τ_N ≈ 10⁷–4×10²⁰ yr, depending on m_X, m_A and the phase-space cut. That is below the limit in every row; for the preprint's m_A = 370–400 MeV the shortfall is at least 4×10⁵ in lifetime. Fast decay is required: an X⁺ with charge +1 and a mass within 0.2% of m_p would otherwise be trapped and counted exactly like a proton in the NIST trap (my assumption: trap holding time ~10 ms).
- **If instead the limit is respected**, X⁺ must live at least 43 s (most conservative row) and typically 10⁶–10¹⁵ s. It then survives into the proton detector, and the proton deficit that C5 relies on disappears.

So C5 as its source builds it cannot produce the ~1.1% proton-less rate the candidate claims.

The only escape is a 0.83 MeV-wide window of m_A just above 468.4 MeV. There, M_f clears Fornal & Grinstein's ⁹Be bound. That window lies outside every mass the preprint studied ("Of the investigated range of DM particle masses 300 – 460 MeV", and it states "Mass of the DM particle is limited from above by half the nucleon mass"). In the window the positron carries less than 1.665 MeV, so the preprint's own mechanism for hiding it from J-PARC ("due to its high energy will leave less visible track") no longer applies.

The narrowed branch also still needs the a-route λ. The PERKEO III (A-route) τ_β = 878.70 ± 0.83 s fails the required Γ(p e ν̄) = 1/887.7 s⁻¹ at 3.8σ [M-CONSTRAINTS-03, verified; cited in C5's own evidence against].

## Evidence
- **Source quotes, Veselský et al.** (arXiv:2507.17340, fetched PDF):
  - "neutron decay results in production of two dark matter particles of about half the mass of neutron";
  - "the charged dark fermionic remnant (here noted as X+) immediately undergoes decay into two (or more) equal mass neutral particles (here noted as A0) and light positively charged particle like positron: X+→ 2A0 + e+";
  - "energetic positron is in principle observable, but due to its high energy will leave less visible track … and thus can go unobserved";
  - "The combinations of DM particle mass mD=370-400 MeV and vector coupling gvD/mvD=0.01-0.015 MeV−1 appear most successful";
  - "simplest variant without such coupling does not fulfill the NS constraints and it is thus excluded";
  - "Obviously baryonic number is not conserved in such neutron decay";
  - "The question is why such charged particle is not seen in experiment."

  The preprint never discusses proton decay or nuclear stability. I grepped the full text as fetched; the only stability-related content concerns neutron stars.
- **Fornal & Grinstein** (arXiv:1801.01124, fetched PDF, p. 2):
  - "The operator O generally gives rise to proton decay via p→n∗ +e+ +νe, followed by the decay of n∗ through the channel (a) or (b) and has to be suppressed";
  - "Proton decay can be eliminated from the theory if the sum of masses of particles in the minimal final state f of neutron decay, say Mf, is larger than mp−me";
  - "The most stringent of such nuclear decay constraints comes from the requirement of 9Be stability, for which ∆M = 937.900 MeV, thus 937.900 MeV< min { Mf′ } ≤Mf < 939.565 MeV."
- **Evans & Steinberg**, Science 197, 989 (1977), abstract via lit_search: "a lower limit of 1.6 × 10^25 years has been obtained for the mean lifetime of the nucleons in the tellurium-130 nucleus. This result is insensitive to the particular mode by which the nucleons decay and therefore provides a rigorous limit on possible baryon number nonconservation."
- **Calculation** [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C5-1_stability.py; log beside it]. SI times, energies in MeV (natural units).
  - **Part A.** For m_A = 370, 400 and 460 MeV, M_f = 741.0, 801.0 and 921.0 MeV. That violates the ⁹Be condition by 196.9, 136.9 and 16.9 MeV, and the proton condition by 196.7, 136.7 and 16.7 MeV. The window compatible with both ⁹Be stability and an on-shell X⁺ chain is 468.439 MeV < m_A < 469.272 MeV. In that window the total kinetic energy of the leptons and A⁰s is below 1.665 MeV.
  - **Part B.** Off-shell X⁺* decay of bound neutrons in ¹³⁰Te (S_n ≈ 8.4 MeV), with Γ_1 = 1.299×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ (Br = 1.14%). With X⁺ lifetime τ_X = 1 ms:
    - the predicted bound-neutron lifetime τ_N is 9.5×10⁶ to 4.1×10²⁰ yr across m_X = 937.6–938.9 MeV, m_A = 370–460 MeV and phase-space cuts of 1 MeV, 10 MeV or none;
    - every row lies below 1.6×10²⁵ yr;
    - the limit needs τ_X > 43 s in the most conservative row (m_X = 937.6 MeV, m_A = 460 MeV, Q-cut 1 MeV), i.e. 4.3×10⁴ times the ~1 ms that BL1 allows;
    - for m_A = 370–400 MeV it needs τ_X ≥ 386 s at the most conservative, and up to 3.9×10¹² s.
  - **Part C.** The maximum X⁺ recoil kinetic energy is 97–2356 eV for m_X = 938.9–937.4 MeV (751 eV at m_X = m_p).
  - **Model caveats.** Point-like weak-type vertex; X⁺* propagator; Γ_X(m*) ∝ Q*^n with n = 2.5–4; a Fermi integral without Coulomb correction. These shift C by orders of magnitude between rows, but no row comes within 10⁴ of compliance.
- **PERKEO II** (Klopf et al., PRL 122, 222503; arXiv:1905.01912):
  - abstract: "exclude a one percent contribution for 95 % of the allowed mass range for the dark matter particle";
  - fetched text: its fit adds "a hypothetical 1 % e+e− branch at 30 keV, 50 keV, 80 keV, or 400 keV" to the summed coincidence spectrum.

  Its signal model is a prompt, fixed-energy e⁺e⁻ pair. So I do not claim it excludes the narrowed window, where the e⁺ comes from a separate, possibly delayed, three-body decay with a continuous spectrum.

## Size against the discrepancy
- **Preprint parameters (m_A = 370–460 MeV).** The branch cannot carry any of the 9.9 s gap. At the 1.1% branching needed, either:
  - nuclei decay by a factor of at least 10⁴ in τ_X (roughly 10⁴–10¹⁸ in rate) faster than even the mode-independent geochemical limit allows; or
  - X⁺ lives long enough to be counted as a proton, so the proton-beam shift is 0 s instead of +9.9 s.
- **Narrowed window (m_A ≈ 468.4–469.3 MeV, τ_X ≲ 1 ms).**
  - Proton-counting beam: +9.9 s (long).
  - J-PARC and bottles: unchanged (≈ 878 s), provided a delayed MeV positron is not counted as a second electron-like event in the J-PARC TPC (not checked).
  - Partial survival: if τ_X is comparable to the proton trap's holding time, the proton-beam shift falls. For example, a counted X⁺ fraction f gives a shift of about 9.9 s × (1 − f).
- **SM route.** The narrowed branch needs τ_β ≈ 887.7 s. The A route disfavours this at 3.8σ [M-CONSTRAINTS-03].

## Corrections to details
- **"Extra positrons or charged X⁺ tracks appear in the J-PARC/LiNA TPC"** (C5 predictions). An X⁺ from free-neutron decay has at most about 0.1–2.4 keV of kinetic energy (751 eV at m_X = m_p) [calc Part C]. Like the recoil proton, it leaves no track. The only visible extra signature is the positron (and its 511 keV annihilation photons). In the surviving window that positron is MeV-scale and electron-like, not "energetic".
- **C5 cites the preprint only as "gives up baryon-number conservation".** It omits the consequence that matters most: the minimal final state lies 17–197 MeV below the Fornal–Grinstein nuclear-stability and proton-stability thresholds. This is a bound that the candidate's matrix column "neutron-star bounds / direct searches" should carry, and it is decisive against the source's own parameters.
- **Neutron stars.** The preprint's neutron-star-viable region (m_D = 370–460 MeV with repulsive vector coupling) does not overlap the nuclear-stability window (m_A > 468.44 MeV). The preprint also notes "maximum mass dropping with increasing DM particle mass". Whether any neutron-star-viable model exists inside the window is therefore undemonstrated.

## What survives and remaining doubt
The generic idea survives only as a finely tuned variant that nobody has yet constructed or tested:
- a proton-less, electron-always 1.1% branch;
- 2m_A inside a 0.83 MeV window below m_n;
- a soft positron;
- τ_X ≲ 1 ms;
- the a-route λ.

**Remaining doubts.**
1. The ~1 ms ceiling on τ_X rests on my assumption about BL1's trap holding time (about 10 ms), not on a quoted figure. A longer holding time would only lower the ceiling and strengthen the exclusion.
2. The off-shell rate uses a generic vertex model. Only an unusually strong suppression of the X⁺ width far off-shell, by more than 10⁴ even in the most conservative row, could reopen the preprint's parameters.
3. No existing search is shown to exclude the narrowed window:
   - PERKEO II and UCNA used a prompt-pair signal model;
   - a delayed MeV positron might instead show up as extra single electron-like events in J-PARC, PERKEO or UCNA data, which nobody has computed.
4. Nab's a coefficient (Δλ/|λ| ≈ 0.04%) remains the clean decider for the narrowed form: a PERKEO-like λ excludes it at ≈ 3.8σ.
