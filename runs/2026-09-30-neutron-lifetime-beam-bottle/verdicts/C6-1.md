# Verdict on C6 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: The β-decay rate of recently produced beam neutrons differs from that of stored UCN (an excited neutron state n* or a field dependence), lengthening beam lifetimes (speculative).
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: cited-evidence
narrowed: Only the n* variant survives, in a window 2×10⁻² s ≪ τγ < 139 s with excitation energy ≲ 21 keV, and only with an extra assumption the claim does not state: either J-PARC's 877.2 s is biased low, or spallation sources populate n* much less than reactor fission sources. Without one of these, J-PARC's younger electron-counting beam contradicts it at about 2.4σ (1.1σ with J-PARC's own scale factor).

Units: SI throughout (times and lifetimes in s or ms, lengths in m, speeds in m/s); energies in keV/MeV.

## Strongest attack

**1. J-PARC's neutrons are younger than BL1's, yet J-PARC reads like a bottle.** C6 is a history effect: the β rate depends on the time since the neutron was produced. Under n*, the n* fraction reaching an apparatus falls as exp(−t/τγ). Every beam experiment measures the composition-averaged rate per neutron, 1/τ = [n_g/τ_g + n_e/τ_e]/(n_g + n_e) (Koch–Hummel eq. 6, τn = −n/ṅ). That holds for BL1 (protons per ⁶Li capture) and for J-PARC (electrons per ³He capture in the same gas), unless n* is captured differently.

- **BL1's flight.** Its neutrons travel "approximately 68 m from the cold source" at NIST NG-6, from a 40 K spectrum. That is 74–84 ms at the mean and most-probable speeds, and 34–170 ms across 400–2000 m/s.
- **J-PARC's flight.** J-PARC's TPC starts 19.3 m from the moderator, and its extracted band is 0.32–0.78 nm, so t_J = 16–40 ms [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C6-1_nstar_evidence.py, section 4]. At equal speed t_J/t_B = 0.28–0.30. Only the extreme corner (the slowest J-PARC neutrons against 2000 m/s BL1 neutrons, 40 against 34 ms) reverses the order.
- **What C6 then predicts.** For every τγ, J-PARC should read at least as long as BL1, about 888 s. It reads 877.2 ± 1.7 (stat) +4.0/−3.6 (sys) s [D-23]. That is 10.5–10.8 s short of C6's prediction: **2.4–2.5σ** on J-PARC's quoted errors, or 1.1σ if J-PARC's error is inflated by its internal scale factor S = 2.29 [calc: same file, section 2; M-STATISTICIAN-05].
- **Other beams.** The other young beam measurements that agree with BL1 (Sussex–ILL) are also reactor-based proton counters.
- **No escape through τγ.** The slate's rescue route fails. A τγ that de-excites n* between BL1 and J-PARC would need J-PARC's neutrons to be older than BL1's (section 3 of the calc). They are younger. What remains are assumptions C6 does not state:
  - (a) J-PARC is biased low by ≈ 10 s. Its four run conditions disagree (χ² 15.8/3 [D-24]), so this is possible, but it is then C6 plus a J-PARC systematic.
  - (b) The initial n* abundance depends on the source: high from reactor fission (BL1, Sussex–ILL), low from spallation (J-PARC). Koch–Hummel leave the initial abundance free (their condition e).
  - (c) The ³He and ⁶Li capture cross sections of n* differ from the ground state.

**2. The field-dependence half of the claim is eliminated.** The Zeeman energy is 3.6–3.9×10⁻¹³ of Q, so a field-dependent SM rate falls short of the needed 1.14% by 3×10¹⁰ (6×10⁹ with the Q⁵ lever) [M-MECHANIST-13; its elimination stands, only the lens's factor was corrected]. The heading's "or a field dependence" branch does not survive.

**3. The n* parameter space is closed on three sides.**
- **Upper edge, from UCNτ.** Blatnik et al. (UCNτ data, holding times 20–4000 s, two loading times) find "no evidence for this model with a γ-ray decay lifetime longer than 139 s at the 95% conﬁdence le[vel]". So τγ < 139 s.
- **Energy edge, from Nab.** Nab measured the free-neutron mass in β decay: "At 90% C.L., our measurement of the neutron mass gives mn < 939.565 + 0.021 MeV, placing a limit on the available extra energy in neutron decay". So ΔEγ ≲ 21 keV wherever n* reaches the Nab volume (t_tr = 22 ms). That applies for τγ ≳ tens of ms, i.e. across the whole surviving window.
- **Lower edge, from the hypothesis itself.** Koch–Hummel require t_beam (≈ 2×10⁻² s) ≪ τγ.
- **What is left.** A window about 3.8 decades wide in τγ (2×10⁻² s to 139 s) at ΔEγ ≲ 21 keV, further cut by the γ searches. It is not closed: Nab itself states "The neutron lifetime discrepancy can be explained by excited neutrons for values of τγ between the vertical grey bands."

## Corrections to details

- **Koch–Hummel's abundance formula is not rate-averaged.** Their eq. (10), n_e/n_g = Δτ/(Δτ_e − Δτ), does not follow from their own eq. (6).
  - Solving τn = (n_g + n_e)/(n_g/τ_g + n_e/τ_e) exactly gives n_e/n_g = (τ_e/τ_g)·Δτ/(Δτ_e − Δτ). The missing factor is τ_e/τ_g = 25/9 = 2.78, which is O(1), not the O(τ⁻¹Δτ²) term they drop.
  - Their eq. (19) abundance (≈ 5.5–6.4×10⁻³) produces only a 3.5–3.6 s shift, not 9.65–9.88 s. The needed abundance is 1.73–1.77×10⁻² [calc: section 1].
  - This is not fatal (the abundance is a free parameter), but it triples the n* population that the γ searches must miss.
- **The slate cannot name J-PARC's prediction.** It says the J-PARC prediction is "not computed" and "long if n* survives the flight". It is computable: J-PARC's neutrons are about 3–4× younger than BL1's, so C6 with a common source abundance predicts J-PARC ≥ BL1 ≈ 888 s.
- **The UCNτ bound is now a number.** The slate's weakest link reads "partial exclusion by UCNτ … region size not read". The number is τγ < 139 s at 95% CL (Blatnik). The Nab limit is ΔEγ ≲ 21 keV at 90% CL (m_n < 939.565 + 0.021 MeV).
- **The decisive test needs revising.** A flight-path scan already exists in effect, between J-PARC at 19 m and BL1 at 68 m, and it runs the wrong way for C6. A pulsed or velocity-selected beam must now also compare sources, fission against spallation.

## What survives and remaining doubt

- **What survives.** An n* with 2×10⁻² s ≪ τγ < 139 s and ΔEγ ≲ 21 keV is not excluded by any direct search I read. Nab says the discrepancy can still be explained between its grey bands.
- **What is lost.** The heading's field-dependence branch is eliminated. The "recently produced beam neutrons" branch, taken as stated (a pure function of time since production), is disfavoured by J-PARC's young beam reading ≈ 877 s.
- **Why weakened and not refuted.**
  - The J-PARC tension is 2.4σ nominal and only 1.1σ once J-PARC's internal scatter (χ² 15.8/3) is priced in.
  - J-PARC is a preprint.
  - The source-dependent-abundance escape is ad hoc but not excluded. Koch–Hummel leave the initial abundance free, and the two proton-beam results (NIST, ILL) are both reactor sources.
- **Decisive tests.**
  - LiNA at J-PARC (≈ 1 s): if it reads ≈ 878 s, the spallation-source escape becomes mandatory.
  - A proton-counting measurement at a spallation source, or an electron-counting one at a reactor, would test the escape directly: C6-with-escape predicts that the source type, not the method, sets the value.
- **Assumption behind the calculation.** My flight-time numbers take BL1's speed spectrum from its quoted 40 K effective temperature, not from a measured fluence spectrum. The ordering t_J < t_B fails only in the extreme corner (J-PARC 0.78 nm against BL1 ≥ 2000 m/s), which carries little of either flux.
