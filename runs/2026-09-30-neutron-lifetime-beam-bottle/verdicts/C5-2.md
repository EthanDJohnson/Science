# Verdict on C5 (refuter 2, angle: bounds)
claim: A baryon-number-violating branch n → X⁺e⁻ν̄ of about 1.1% yields an electron but no proton, so proton counting reads long while electron counting and bottles read true (speculative).
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: A ~1.1% proton-less, electron-always branch survives the nuclear-stability bounds only if the charged X⁺ disappears within ≲10 ms into a final state whose total mass exceeds ≈937.4 MeV (a Fornal–Grinstein-type heavy dark state), which rules out the argument's own mechanism, X⁺ → 2A⁰ + e⁺ with m_A ≈ 370–460 MeV (Veselský et al.).

## Strongest attack
**The mechanism C5 specifies breaks nuclear stability, and nothing can fix that without switching off the effect in the proton beam.**

1. **The bound C5 must meet.** Fornal and Grinstein showed that any neutron final state must be heavier than 937.900 MeV, otherwise bound neutrons in stable nuclei (⁹Be first) could decay. C5's chain ends in 2A⁰ + e⁺ + e⁻ + ν̄ [D-95]. With Veselský's own masses, m_D = 370–460 MeV (370–400 MeV under his strictest constraint), that final state weighs 2m_D + 2m_e ≈ 741–921 MeV. This is 17–197 MeV below the limit.
2. **Keeping X⁺ on-shell does not help.** The on-shell X⁺ can sit in the window 937.389 < m_X < 939.054 MeV [calc], which stops the step nucleus → (A−1) + X⁺ + e⁻ν̄. But a bound neutron still decays through an **off-shell** X⁺. That rate is suppressed only by the Breit–Wigner tail of X⁺, and so it scales linearly with the X⁺ width Γ_X.
3. **BL1 sets a floor on Γ_X.** BL1 traps protons for "a trapping time of order 10 ms", in a well "approximately +800 eV" deep that holds every proton (maximum recoil 751 eV). The trapped particles are then "accelerated onto a detector held at a high negative potential" [new: Nico et al. 2005, ACCESS full-text].
   - An X⁺ has charge +1 and a mass within ~1 MeV of m_p.
   - For m_X ≥ m_p its maximum recoil is below 751 eV, so an X⁺ that survives 10 ms is trapped and counted exactly like a proton.
   - The proton beam then reads the true total rate, not a long one.
   - C5's +9.9 s shift therefore needs τ_X ≲ 10 ms, i.e. Γ_X ≳ ħ/10 ms = 6.6×10⁻²⁰ MeV. Veselský's own wording is stronger still: X⁺ "immediately undergoes decay".
4. **Consequence at that minimum width.** Rates per bound neutron, from the off-shell calculation [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C5-2_offshell_nuclear.py]:
   - **m_D = 370–400 MeV.** Neutrons in ¹⁶O, ²⁸Si, ²⁴Mg and ⁵⁶Fe decay at 5×10⁻²² to 1×10⁻¹⁶ s⁻¹, i.e. partial lifetimes of 3×10⁸ to 6×10¹³ yr. Earth's rocks alone would then release 1.6×10² to 3×10⁷ times Earth's entire measured surface heat flux (47 ± 2 TW). This counts only one neutron per nucleus and only the 1.022 MeV of positron annihilation.
   - **m_D = 460 MeV.** This is the least favourable case: ¹²C is closed, but ¹³C (S_n = 4.95 MeV) is open. The ¹³C in 100 t of scintillator would give 2×10⁸ to 5×10¹² events per day, carrying up to ~14 MeV of e⁺e⁻ energy. Borexino measures 0.217 cpd/100 t above 3 MeV. The excess is 10⁹ to 10¹³.
5. **The other way out closes the mechanism.** Making Γ_X small enough to pass these bounds needs τ_X > 1.6 s at the least favourable m_X and up to 3×10⁵ s (Earth heat flow, m_D = 370–400 MeV; the ratio 1.6×10²–3×10⁷ times 10 ms), or τ_X > 10⁷ to 3×10¹¹ s (Borexino, m_D = 460 MeV). Such an X⁺ survives BL1's 10 ms trap and is counted as a proton.

So there is no value of Γ_X that gives both a long proton-beam lifetime and stable nuclei. The only escape is to change what X⁺ decays into: a final state heavier than ≈937.4 MeV, such as X⁺ → e⁺ + Y⁰ with m_Y ≳ 936.9 MeV. That abandons the argument's A⁰ pairs of about half the neutron mass, and with them the neutron-star fit the preprint was built around.

## Size against the discrepancy
The observed gap is proton beam − storage ≈ +9.9 s, a needed branch of 1.113% [M-CONSTRAINTS-03].

- **Case 1: τ_X ≲ 10 ms, the case C5 needs.**
  - Proton beam +9.9 s (long); J-PARC, bottles and UCNProBe 0 s. This carries the whole gap.
  - It is excluded by 10²–10⁷ (Earth heat flow, m_D = 370–400 MeV) and by 10⁹–10¹³ (Borexino ¹³C, m_D = 460 MeV).
- **Case 2: τ_X large enough to pass those bounds (≥ 1.6 s from heat flow at best; ≥ 10⁷ s from Borexino at m_D = 460 MeV), in every case ≫ BL1's 10 ms.**
  - An X⁺ with m_X ≥ m_p is trapped and counted in BL1, so the proton-beam shift is ≈ 0 s. It carries none of the gap.
  - For m_X < m_p, the fraction with recoil above 800 eV escapes, so the shift is only part of 9.9 s (the fraction was not computed).
- **The heavy-Y variant (narrowed core).** It keeps +9.9 s on the proton beam and 0 elsewhere. It remains subject to the λ route: the A-route τ_β = 878.70 ± 0.83 s fails the required Γ(p e ν̄) = 1/887.7 s⁻¹ at 3.8σ [M-CONSTRAINTS-03 verified].

## Corrections to details
- The argument says only that C5 "needs a charged X⁺ that has not been seen". The sharper cost is that the specified X⁺ decay is incompatible with nuclear stability for any X⁺ lifetime short enough to escape BL1's proton trap.
- The prediction "extra positrons … appear in the J-PARC/LiNA TPC" assumes Veselský's energetic positron (Q_X ≈ 137–197 MeV at m_D = 370–400 MeV). In the only variant that survives here, X⁺ → e⁺Y⁰ with m_Y ≳ 936.9 MeV, the positron carries at most ~1.7 MeV.
- The prediction "UCNProBe's β rate equals its storage rate" should carry a caveat. Bound-neutron decays are not an issue for UCN, but surviving X⁺ are also invisible to UCNProBe only if they decay within the counting window. This does not change the prediction.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives.** The heading claim survives only in the narrowed form, and that form is unchecked: an electron-always, proton-less 1.1% branch in which X⁺ decays within ≲10 ms to e⁺ plus a dark state Y⁰ of mass ≈ 936.9–938.5 MeV.
  - Such a Y⁰ is a Fornal–Grinstein-like dark baryon of nearly the neutron mass.
  - It inherits the neutron-star problem of C3's dark decay (McKeen, Baym and Motta; not computed here), unless it has repulsive self-interactions.
  - It still needs the a-route λ (3.8σ against on the A route).
- **Remaining doubt in my calculation:**
  - The off-shell rate assumes a point-like n → X⁺ vertex and an O(1) nuclear overlap (spectroscopic factor). A form factor or small overlap could lower the rates by 1–2 orders, not the 3–13 orders of margin.
  - The heat-flow comparison assumes that at least 1.022 MeV per decay stays in the Earth. This holds for e⁺ annihilation.
  - The Borexino comparison assumes a substantial share of the events deposit more than 3 MeV. The e⁺ takes most of Q_X up to ~14 MeV, so even a 10⁻³ share leaves a 10⁶ excess.
  - I did not use the invisible-nucleon-decay limits (≳10²⁹ yr), because their event selection may not fit this final state. The printed τ_X > 10¹⁴–10¹⁵ s at 10²⁹ yr is illustrative only.
