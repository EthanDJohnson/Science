# Verdict on C5 (refuter 0, angle: magnitude)
claim: A baryon-number-violating branch n → X⁺e⁻ν̄ of about 1.1% yields an electron but no proton, so proton counting reads long while electron counting and bottles read true (speculative).
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: C5 can produce the 9.9 s proton-beam excess only in a fine-tuned sliver: X⁺ decays in ≲ 0.5 ms and the dark particles have m_A ≈ 468.2–469.3 MeV (outside the source preprint's own 370–460 MeV range), so that bound neutrons cannot decay through an off-shell X⁺. Even there it still needs the proton-recoil (a-route) λ, which the β-asymmetry route disfavours at 3.8σ.

## Strongest attack
The attack is a magnitude-and-kinematics squeeze with two jaws. Between them, no X⁺ lifetime lets the branch carry the gap in the model's own parameter range. [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C5-0_magnitude.py]

1. **The X⁺ looks like a proton to BL1.** The mass window is set by the free-neutron kinematics (m_X < m_n − m_e) and by on-shell ⁹Be stability (⁹Be → 2α + n threshold 1.5727 MeV, AME2020 as used in M-CONSTRAINTS-07). It gives 937.48 < m_X < 939.05 MeV. The X⁺ therefore has charge +1 and a charge-to-mass ratio within 0.084% of the proton's. Its recoil kinetic energy is ≤ 2.17 keV, and ≤ 749 eV for m_X = m_p. The preprint requires "similar kinematic properties as electrons and neutrinos from regular neutron beta-decay", which means m_X ≈ m_p. BL1 traps anything with these properties and counts it exactly as it counts decay protons. Nico et al. 2005: the electrodes "impose a potential well ... of depth approximately +800 eV, which is well above the maximum proton kinetic energy of 751 eV"; "After a trapping time of order 10 ms, the trapped protons are ejected ... and accelerated onto a detector held at a high negative potential"; "The precision lifetime data were taken with trapping times of 5 ms and 10 ms."
   - A **stable or long-lived X⁺** is therefore counted. Across the whole mass window, the most that can escape the well axially is 16.5%, at the lightest allowed m_X, and none escapes at m_X = m_p. The branch then lengthens the proton-beam lifetime by at most **1.60 s against the 9.88 s needed (16%)**, and by 0.00 s at m_X = m_p.
   - To leave ≥ 90% of the branch uncounted, the X⁺ must decay before extraction with **τ_X ≤ 0.5 ms** (5 ms trapping period) or ≤ 1.0 ms (10 ms). At τ_X = 10 ms, BL1 would show only 2.1–3.6 s of the 9.9 s.
2. **A short-lived X⁺ makes ordinary nuclei decay.** The vertices the model needs (n → X⁺e⁻ν̄ at a 1.1% rate, then X⁺ → 2A⁰ + e⁺) also let every *bound* neutron decay through an off-shell X*: (A,Z) → (A−1,Z) + e⁻ν̄ e⁺ 2A⁰. In the preprint's range m_A = 370–460 MeV this releases ≳ 18–200 MeV. Its rate is Γ_bound ≈ Γ₁ · ħΓ_X · C, where C is the narrow-width Breit–Wigner tail. I computed C conservatively: only virtual masses within 1 MeV of the kinematic edge, and the X* phase space suppressed as Q⁴.
   - Setting τ_X ≤ 0.5 ms forces bound-neutron partial lifetimes of at most **τ_N\* = 4.4×10¹⁴–1.1×10¹⁸ yr in ²H** and **1.9×10¹⁶–3.8×10²³ yr per neutron in ¹⁶O**, across m_X = 937.49–938.9 MeV and m_A = 370–460 MeV.
   - Nucleon-decay searches in water reach 10³²-year sensitivity for positron-plus-invisible final states (Super-K, p → e⁺νν > 1.7×10³² yr). That is 8.7–16 orders of magnitude beyond τ_N\*. Put the other way, the Super-K scale forces τ_X > 2.9×10⁶ s in ¹⁶O even in the weakest row (m_A = 460 MeV), 5.7×10⁹ times the BL1 maximum.
   - For m_A ≤ 430 MeV, a nucleon lifetime of only 10²⁵ yr already forces τ_X > 370 s in ¹⁶O and > 2.6×10⁴ s in ²H.
   - A deuteron partial lifetime of 1.1×10¹⁸ yr would make SNO's 1 kt of D₂O (6.0×10³¹ deuterons) a source of 5.3×10¹³ positron events per year, each above 17 MeV.
3. **The two jaws do not meet.** The BL1 jaw needs τ_X ≤ 5×10⁻⁴ s. The nuclear-stability jaw needs τ_X ≳ 10⁶–10¹⁷ s at 10³²-year sensitivity, and still ≳ 0.17 s in the single weakest configuration even at a very weak 10²⁵ yr. No X⁺ lifetime carries more than a small fraction of the gap.

**Escape (why weakened, not refuted).** The off-shell nuclear route closes kinematically if 2m_A + 2m_e > m_n − S_n for every abundant nucleus: m_A > 468.16 MeV for ²H and 468.49 MeV for ⁹Be (61.44 MeV lower, 461.44 MeV, for ¹⁶O). X⁺ → 2A⁰e⁺ itself needs m_A < 469.27 MeV. That leaves a sliver m_A ≈ 468.2–469.3 MeV, about 1 MeV wide, in which:
- X⁺ must still reach τ_X ≤ 0.5 ms with Q_X ≤ 1.6 MeV;
- the positron is soft, not "energetic" as the preprint says;
- the preprint's own neutron-star analysis admits only m_D = 390–460 MeV ("only mass range of 390 – 460 MeV ... lead to mass-radius dependencies which fulfill the maximum NS mass of 2 M⊙"), and 370–400 MeV under its strictest coupling constraint.

I did not close this sliver with a calculation, so the verdict is weakened rather than refuted.

## Corrections to details
- "Yields an electron but no proton, so proton counting reads long" silently assumes the X⁺ is not itself counted. It is. In the only allowed mass window, the X⁺ is a charge +1, proton-mass ion with a sub-keV to 2 keV recoil, and BL1's +800 V trap and 30 kV-class detector count it. C5 therefore needs X⁺ to decay in ≲ 0.5–1 ms, which the candidate never states.
- "Weakest link: the λ route, and the unseen X⁺." The decisive weak link is instead the X⁺ lifetime: the short lifetime BL1 needs is exactly what opens the off-shell nuclear decay.
- The candidate's matrix entry "Neutron-star bounds: not computed" and the direct-searches entry omit nucleon-decay searches, which are the binding constraint.
- The preprint's "energetic positron" (Q_X = 18–200 MeV for m_A = 370–460 MeV) and the surviving escape (Q_X ≤ 1.6 MeV) are mutually exclusive, so the surviving variant is not the preprint's model.

## What survives and remaining doubt
The narrowed C5 needs m_A ≈ 468.2–469.3 MeV (Q_X ≤ 1.6 MeV) and τ_X ≤ 0.5 ms, together with an aSPECT-like λ, which is 3.8σ against the A route. I have not checked whether that corner is excluded by the UCNA or PERKEO II e⁺e⁻ searches (a delayed soft positron would be outside their coincidence signature), by the X⁺ decay rate this small Q_X allows, or by the preprint's neutron-star mass window, which it lies outside.

Assumptions of my attack:
- **Locality.** The n–X–eν vertex acts on bound neutrons as on free ones. This is the standard EFT assumption, and the preprint calls the process "analogous to beta-decay".
- **Narrow width.** The Breit–Wigner tail is used with a deliberately truncated off-shell window (1 MeV), which makes the bound conservative. Including the far off-shell tail (20 MeV window) strengthens it by 10²–10⁶.
- **Nucleon-decay bounds.** Super-K's quoted limit is for p → e⁺νν, not for this final state. For m_A near 460 MeV the positron is below about 18 MeV, where Super-K's positron searches are less direct. The attack survives with only a 10²⁴-yr (¹⁶O) or 10¹⁸-yr (²H) partial-lifetime sensitivity, far below any mode-independent test.
