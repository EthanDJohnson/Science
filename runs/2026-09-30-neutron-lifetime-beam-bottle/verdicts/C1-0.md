# Verdict on C1 (refuter 0, angle: magnitude)
claim: An unidentified systematic in the proton-counting beam method (BL1, Sussex–ILL) makes it read about 1.1% long; the storage value near 878 s is the true lifetime.
status: final

Units: SI throughout (lifetimes in s, pressures in Pa, fractions dimensionless).

## Strongest attack
The magnitude attack asks whether C1 can carry the whole gap in the class where it acts, and whether it spoils any other class. I computed each class's residual under C1, plus how much of the gap each *named* proton-side effect can supply at its quoted size [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C1-0_magnitude.py].

The best line of attack is that no physical effect of the needed size can be found in BL1's own apparatus.

- **Summing every budget line does not reach the gap.** The itemised proton-side and fluence budget lines are fluence 0.5 s, backscatter 0.4 s, Si 0.5 s, H₂ worst case 0.5 s, halo 1.0 s, nonlinearity uncertainty 0.8 s and ⁶Li mass 0.9 s. Stacked linearly rather than in quadrature, they total 4.6 s, 48% of the 9.65 s gap.
- **A1, the fluence monitor.** Two ε₀ determinations exist:
  - Nico 2005 derived ε₀ from the deposit's areal density, the cross section and the solid angle.
  - Yue 2013 re-measured it with the Alpha-Gamma device.

  They differ by only 1.4 s (0.158%). A1 needs 1.09%, which is 6.9× the gap between two largely independent methods and 19× the 0.5 s uncertainty. Both would have to be wrong by about 1.1% in the same direction.
- **A2, residual-gas charge exchange.** Caylor's own loss figure is 0.3% at 1×10⁻⁷ Pa for a 10 ms trapping time. Covering the gap needs H₂ at 3.6×10⁻⁷ Pa if all data were taken at 10 ms, or 4.8×10⁻⁷ Pa for an equal 5/10 ms mix. That is 3.6–4.8× the ion-pump reading Nico reports, and Nico says the trap was cryopumped below that reading. The mechanism also needs every H₂⁺ to go uncounted. Yet BL1 shows no H₂⁺ above a 1% detection limit, and Caylor's worst case is < 0.5 s, i.e. < 5.2% of the gap.
- **A3, proton detection.** The backscatter and dead-layer budgets carry 4.1% and 5.2% of the gap.
- **A4, a mis-sized correction.** Doubling the trap-nonlinearity correction carries 55%.

This attack does **not** defeat the core claim. The claim is explicitly that the systematic is *unidentified*, so showing that the listed effects fall short restates its admitted weakest link ("no named effect reaches 9.9 s at its quoted size"). It does not refute it.

The magnitude test that could refute C1 is whether a single ~1.1% multiplicative error in the proton class leaves any other class out of line. It does not:
- J-PARC sits −0.26σ from C1's prediction.
- The SM A-route τ_β sits +0.41σ from it.
- A common multiplicative factor fits both proton-counting experiments with χ² = 0.079/1.

One named mechanism does reach full size. If the +5.4 s ⁶Li-absorption correction had the wrong sign, the shift would be 10.8 s, 112% of the gap. So "a correction mis-sized or mis-signed" is at least an existence proof that a single bookkeeping error in this analysis could carry the whole effect.

## Corrections to details
- **A4.** The candidate says the ⁶Li-absorption (+5.4 s) or nonlinearity (−5.3 s) correction "at 100% error supplies 54% of the gap". That holds for doubling or omitting either correction (55%). A *sign* error on the absorption correction ("mis-signed", the candidate's own word) is a 10.8 s shift, 112% of the gap.
- **"About 1.1%" depends on which storage value is taken as true.** It is 1.16% for UCNτ or the magnetic traps (10.15–10.17 s) and 0.91% for the material bottles (7.97 s). C1's "storage value near 878 s" implicitly takes the magnetic-trap side of the 3σ material–magnetic split.
- **A1 is weaker than described.** The candidate compares A1 only to the 0.5 s Alpha-Gamma uncertainty. The stronger constraint is that the 2005 areal-density calibration and the 2013 Alpha-Gamma calibration agree to 0.16%, so both would need a common 1.1% error.
- **A2 has a pressure constraint the candidate omits.** Beyond needing the ions to escape, it needs trap H₂ at 3.6–4.8× the reported ion-pump pressure of 1×10⁻⁷ Pa. BL1's own data show no H₂⁺ above the 1% detection limit, which is near the 1.09% needed.

## What survives and remaining doubt
The core survives the magnitude test.
- A single multiplicative error of 1.09% (1.10 ± 0.23% fitted to both proton-counting experiments) in proton counting would carry the entire 9.65 ± 2.08 s gap.
- It would leave J-PARC (−0.26σ) and the SM A-route (+0.41σ) consistent, and would require no shift in any other class.
- Its only magnitude cost is the unexplained material–magnetic 2.2 s split, which is outside C1's claim.

The genuine doubt is that no *identified* mechanism reaches the size at its quoted uncertainty:
- the linear stack of every itemised line gives 48%;
- H₂ charge exchange gives < 5%;
- the fluence monitor needs two independent calibrations to fail together.

Only a gross bookkeeping error, such as a sign error on the ⁶Li-absorption correction, reaches 100%. The candidate's own label "unidentified" absorbs this, but it means C1 cannot yet be tested at its causal link. It rests on BL2/BL3 reading about 878 s.
