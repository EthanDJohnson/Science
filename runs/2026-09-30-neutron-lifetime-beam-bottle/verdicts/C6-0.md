# Verdict on C6 (refuter 0, angle: magnitude)
claim: The β-decay rate of recently produced beam neutrons differs from that of stored UCN (an excited neutron state n* or a field dependence), lengthening beam lifetimes (speculative).
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: Only the time-since-production n* variant survives: it can carry the whole proton-beam gap if at least 1.1% of beam neutrons are n* and τγ is between about 0.3 s and the tens-of-seconds storage bound, but it then predicts that the J-PARC 2024 electron-counting result reads ≥ 887.8 s, which puts it 2.2–2.4σ against that result (about 1σ once J-PARC's internal scatter is allowed for); the field variant is eliminated.

## Strongest attack
There are two parts, both calculations [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C6-0_magnitude.py].

1. **The field variant cannot supply the size of effect.** At 4.6 T, |µn|B = 277.4 neV, which is 3.55×10⁻¹³ of Q. The shift needed is 1.13×10⁻², so the effect falls short by 3.2×10¹⁰, or 6.4×10⁹ with the Γ ∝ Q⁵ lever. At 5 T the shortfall is 2.9×10¹⁰ (5.9×10⁹). This reproduces M-MECHANIST-13 (math/mechanist.md), which the slate already cites. Half of the candidate's "or" clause is therefore dead on magnitude alone.

2. **The n* variant predicts that the J-PARC electron-counting beam reads long, and by at least as much as BL1.** The mechanism works through time since production (Koch–Hummel's premise, quoted below). J-PARC's decaying neutrons are younger than BL1's:
   - **NIST BL1:** the cold source is about 68 m from the proton trap, and the beam is a 40 K pseudo-Maxwellian. The density-weighted mean age of the decaying neutrons is 94.5 ms (median 77.0 ms; 10–90% range 47–155 ms).
   - **J-PARC BL05:** the TPC is about 20 m from the source and uses λ = 2–8 Å, so the ages are 10.1–40.4 ms.

   The n* decays by β emission, only more slowly, so it gives both electrons and protons. An n* population that survives to BL1 therefore survives at least as well to J-PARC. With the same birth fraction f0 at both sources, the predicted J-PARC lifetime depends on τγ (J-PARC error +4.35 s):

   | τγ | Predicted J-PARC | Tension with 877.2 s |
   |---|---|---|
   | ≥ 1 s | 887.8–888.4 s | 2.43–2.57σ (2.16–2.29σ with BL1's error included) |
   | 0.3 s | 890.1 s | 2.96σ |
   | 0.1 s | 895.5 s | 4.21σ |
   | 0.03 s | 927.5 s | 11.6σ |

   The floor of the prediction is J-PARC ≈ 887.8 s, and every shorter τγ makes it worse. This holds for Γe/Γg = 9/25 (Koch–Hummel's toy state) and for Γe → 0 alike.

   So the prediction-matrix cell marked "not computed" for C6 under electron counting is now computed: ≥ 887.8 s, against 877.2 +4.35/−3.98 s observed. On J-PARC, C6 stands exactly where C3 (dark decay) stands, not where C1, C5 or C8 stand.

## Size against the discrepancy
Observed gap: 9.88 s, from BL1 at 887.7 s against magnetic traps at 877.82 s.

The n* fraction a beam must carry is set by the exact relation from Koch–Hummel's Eq. 6, τ = −n/ṅ:

| Assumption | Required n*/n |
|---|---|
| Γe/Γg = 9/25 | 1.74% |
| Γe/Γg = 1/25 | 1.16% |
| Model-independent minimum (Γe → 0) | 1.11% (= Δτ/τ_beam) |

Predicted shift in each class of measurement:

| Measurement class | Predicted shift | Can it carry the gap? |
|---|---|---|
| Proton-counting beam (BL1) | +9.9 s by construction (f0 is a free parameter) | Yes, whole gap |
| Electron-counting beam (J-PARC 2024) | ≥ +9.9 s (≥ 887.8 s); larger for τγ < 1 s | Contradicted at 2.2–2.4σ for τγ ≥ 1 s, 3–12σ for τγ ≤ 0.3 s. About 1.0σ if J-PARC's error is scaled by √(15.8/3) = 2.29 |
| Magnetic traps and material bottles | ≈ 0 s. For a UCNτ-like load of 150 s the storage bias is < 0.02 s for τγ ≤ 50 s and 0.18 s at τγ = 100 s (0.04 s with a 300 s load) | Consistent. Storage lifetimes alone constrain τγ only when it is ≳ 100 s; the birth-time refit (Blatnik) is the sharper storage test |
| Material–magnetic split (880.03 vs 877.82 s) | 0 s | None. Left to C7 |

The allowed window for τγ is about 0.3 s ≲ τγ ≪ ~100 s. Below 0.3 s the J-PARC tension rises above 3σ. Above it, the storage and holding-time data bite.

## Corrections to details
- **Required fraction.** Koch–Hummel's Eq. (10), n_e/n_g = Δτ/(Δτe − Δτ), drops a factor τe/τg that is not small. From their own Eq. (6), the exact result is n_e/n_g = (τe/τg)·Δτ/(Δτe − Δτ). For their 9/25 state the required abundance is therefore 1.74%, not their quoted 5.5×10⁻³: a factor of 2.78. The model-independent floor is 1.11%.
- **Beam age.** The slate and Koch–Hummel take beam ages of 2–25 ms. BL1's decaying neutrons are about 47–155 ms old (mean 94.5 ms), and J-PARC's are 10–40 ms. Beam ages thus differ by about 5×, but in the direction that makes J-PARC read longer than BL1.
- **The slate's inference.** The slate says J-PARC reads long "if n* survives the flight" and calls this unchecked. It is now checked: survival to J-PARC is implied by survival to BL1, given the same birth fraction.
- **Field variant.** The shortfall is 3.2×10¹⁰ (6.4×10⁹ with Q⁵), as the slate already records via M-MECHANIST-13. It is eliminated.

## What survives and remaining doubt
What survives is a narrower n* hypothesis:
- τγ lies between about 0.3 s and the storage bound;
- n* makes up ≥ 1.1% of beam neutrons;
- it accepts a J-PARC prediction of ≥ 887.8 s.

**The J-PARC tension is not decisive.** It rests on a single preprint whose four run conditions scatter with χ²/DOF = 15.8/3. With that scatter treated as a scale factor, the tension is about 1σ.

**A different birth fraction at each facility would escape the J-PARC test,** since J-PARC is a spallation source with a liquid-H₂ moderator and NIST's BL1 sits on a reactor cold source. That escape abandons Koch–Hummel's stated premise that the distinction is time since production, and it adds an untested free parameter. The same holds if n* had a different ³He or ⁶Li capture cross-section: that would shift the flux normalisation and is not part of C6 as stated.

**The storage-side exclusions are unread.** I could not read the numerical extents of Blatnik's green region or of Nab's n* limit. Either could close the remaining τγ window.

**The decisive tests** are a J-PARC or LiNA result at 1–2 s precision, and a beam lifetime measured against neutron age (TOF-resolved, since J-PARC already spans 10–40 ms).
