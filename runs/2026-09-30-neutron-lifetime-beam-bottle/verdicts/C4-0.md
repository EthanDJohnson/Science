# Verdict on C4 (refuter 0, angle: magnitude)
claim: Neutron–mirror-neutron conversion in the 4.6–5 T proton-trap field removes about 1.1% of beam neutrons, lengthening the proton-beam lifetime; bottles and J-PARC read the true value (speculative).
status: final
verdict: refuted
basis: calculation
narrowed: none

## Strongest attack
C4 needs P_trap ≈ 1.11% conversion inside BL1, and to get it the mass splitting must sit just above |µn|·4.6 T = 277.4 neV. The SNS regeneration magnet (Broussard et al. 2022) has a 4.8 T central dip where the B4C absorber sits and 6.6 T peaks at ±6.3 cm. For any Δm in C4's window (278–310 neV), a neutron heading for the absorber therefore crosses the n–n′ resonance as the field rises, and crosses it again on the way out.

The candidate's own "evidence against" estimates the SNS signal as P_trap², on an "equal-passage" premise it marks as unverified. It hoped that premise might be a loophole. It is not. At a crossing the conversion follows Landau–Zener dynamics, and that probability is much larger than BL1's no-crossing adiabatic admixture. So P_trap² understates what SNS should have seen, by between about 7× and 3000×.

I fixed θ0 for each Δm so that BL1 reads 887.7 s against the true 877.82 s. With that θ0, the regeneration SNS should have seen is 9.3×10⁻⁴ to 0.47 (spin- and velocity-averaged). The 95% CL limit is p < 2.5×10⁻⁸, so the prediction is 3.7×10⁴ to 1.4×10⁷ times too large, at every point in the window and for every field-gradient model. Using the source's own full-evolution calculation, Broussard et al. reach the same conclusion: the model is excluded for Δm > 10 neV. The obvious escape, n′ attenuation inside the absorber, recovers at most a factor of about 10³ in the worst case and typically only 1–50%. That is far short of the 3.7×10⁴ needed.

Below 277.4 neV there is no room either: there BL1 itself crosses the resonance and the beam reads short. The quantitative core, a ~1.1% removal of beam neutrons that lengthens the beam lifetime by ~9.9 s, cannot be carried by this mechanism anywhere in its allowed parameter space.

## Size against the discrepancy
Observed gap: +9.88 s, BL1 887.7 s against 877.82 s, where proton beams read long.

- **Proton-counting beam (BL1).** C4 is tuned to give +9.9 s, but only for parameters that predict an SNS regeneration of 9×10⁻⁴ to 0.47. With the SNS limit imposed, P_trap is below ~10⁻⁴ even on the candidate's own conservative cap, so the shift is at most +0.14 s. The true bound is tighter, because the SNS per-passage conversion exceeds BL1's. The mechanism can carry **about 1% or less of the gap, i.e. none of it in practice**. For Δm < 277.4 neV the sign is wrong: the beam reads short (M-MECHANIST-10; source: "Negative values of ∆m are not consistent").
- **Electron-counting beam (J-PARC).** Shift below 0.01 s under any parameters. That is consistent with the data but explains nothing.
- **Material bottles.** If Δm is raised to ≥ 292 neV to shrink the SNS excess, θ0 must be ≥ 8×10⁻³. The per-bounce loss is ½sin²2θ0 ≈ 1.3–5.6×10⁻⁴ (the P_det column), 25–100× the 5.5×10⁻⁶ the candidate quotes. The wall-loss consequences get worse, not better, as the candidate tries to escape SNS by raising Δm.
- **Magnetic traps.** About 0, as C4 says.
- **Direct search (SNS n → n′ → n).** Predicted ≥ 9×10⁻⁴ against an observed limit below 2.5×10⁻⁸. Refuted by a factor of at least 3.7×10⁴.

## Corrections to details
- "4.6–5 T proton-trap field": the source puts BL1's maximum at 4.6 T (≈ 278 neV). If any part of the beam path reached 5 T (301.5 neV), a Δm of 280 neV would be crossed inside BL1 and, by C4's own M-MECHANIST-10, the beam would read short. The window works only because the field tops out at 4.6 T.
- The "equal passage" SNS estimate in C4's evidence-against (a P² of 1.3×10⁻⁴, a 70× shortfall) is not a loophole but an *under*estimate. The SNS magnet crosses the resonance where BL1 does not, so the predicted regeneration is 7× (at 278 neV) up to ~3000× (at 283–286 neV) larger than P_trap².
- The θ0 needed rises steeply above the dip value of 289 neV: 8×10⁻³ at 292 neV and 1.7×10⁻² at 310 neV. The "θ0 = 1.0–2.2×10⁻³" range holds only for Δm ≈ 279–281 neV in this geometry.

## What survives and remaining doubt
Nothing of the quantitative core survives. Mirror-neutron mixing in a strong field cannot remove ~1.1% of BL1's beam neutrons without an SNS regeneration signal at least 3.7×10⁴ times the published limit.

What remains open is mirror physics outside C4 (the weak-field B′ and peV-splitting variants, which are not tied to BL1's field), along with Tan's inverse variant, which the slate treats separately.

Residual doubts in my calculation:
1. The SNS field is a Gaussian double-hump model matched to the three published features, not the manufacturer's map. The result varies by less than 15× across a 4× range of widths and always stays ≥ 3.7×10⁴ above the limit.
2. Multiple crossings are composed incoherently, which is appropriate after velocity averaging.
3. The absorber length is not given, so I bracketed 0.1–1 m.

None of these can close a gap of more than four orders of magnitude, and the experimenters' own full calculation with the measured profiles reaches the same exclusion.
