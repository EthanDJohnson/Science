# Verdict on C8 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: Beam versus bottle is the wrong partition: the split is proton-detecting measurements (essentially BL1, plus aSPECT's λ) versus all others, with one proton-side cause behind both lifetime and λ tensions.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: cited-evidence
narrowed: The lifetime data are better partitioned as proton-counting beams (essentially BL1) against every non-proton method, J-PARC included, and aSPECT's low |a| is a separate tension of similar τ-equivalent size; no evidence supports a single proton-side cause behind both, and each experiment's published proton-side budget argues against one.

Units: SI (lifetimes in s); λ, a and relative shifts are dimensionless (%).

## Strongest attack

The partition half of the heading holds up. The evidence is against the half that makes C8 more than a relabelling, "one proton-side cause behind both". That cause would have to act in two apparatus that share no hardware. BL1 is a Penning-type trap that counts protons absolutely on Si after about 30 kV. aSPECT is a MAC-E retardation spectrometer that measures the shape of the integral recoil spectrum on a silicon drift detector (SDD) at 15 kV. In each of them the cause would have to be tens of times larger than every proton-side item the experiment has itself measured or modelled.

1. **aSPECT's a cannot be moved by an ordinary proton-detection effect, by its own published analysis.**
   - aSPECT states that a is sensitive only to effects with "a dependence on the recoil energy or the retardation voltage".
   - At its detector the proton energy "varies only from 15 keV to 15.75 keV". A detection-efficiency or dead-layer loss of the kind that would bias BL1's absolute count therefore enters aSPECT only through its tiny energy or angle dependence. The overall count-rate amplitude N0 is a free fit parameter, so a uniform loss is absorbed there.
   - aSPECT's Table VII: modelling backscattering and below-threshold losses shifts a by −0.1%. Summed, *all* listed systematic corrections come to δa/a ≈ 1%.
   - R-strong needs aSPECT's |a| to be low by 2.67% (M-DIALECTICIAN-05, verified). That is 27× the proton-detection item and 2.7× the sum of every correction aSPECT applies [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C8-1_evidence.py].
   - **The aSPECT collaboration has already run the test R-strong needs, and the result went against it.**
     - The 2024 reanalysis (Beck et al., PRL 132, 102501) was undertaken because "it could not be ruled out that previously overlooked systematics in i) backscattering and below threshold losses in the detector … could be the reason for the discrepancies in the λ values".
     - The revised detector model, with channelling and charge exchange in the alumina top layer, raised the absolute proton-detection losses by ≈35%. That kind of change would move an absolute proton count such as BL1's. It had "no effect on the final result due to normalization (N0)", and a "differs only marginally from the one published in 2020".
     - This is direct evidence that proton-detection losses and aSPECT's a are decoupled. The one class of cause that would naturally bias both an absolute proton count and a recoil-spectrum shape was examined in the apparatus itself and does not move a.
     - The aSPECT authors' own resolution points elsewhere, to a Fierz term (C3's territory), not to a proton-side systematic. They write that the tension "can be resolved by combining the results of the (b,λ) analyses from these two measurements".
2. **BL1's proton-side items are equally far from 1.1%.**
   - The gap is 9.88 s. That is 24.7× BL1's proton-backscatter uncertainty (0.4 s), 19.8× its Si-scattering uncertainty (0.5 s) and 12.3× its trap-nonlinearity uncertainty (0.8 s). It is even 1.9× the full trap-nonlinearity *correction* of −5.3 s [D-21; calc as above].
   - The one proton-side effect found in a BL1-type trap since then is H₂ charge exchange (Caylor 2025). It gives ~0.3%, about 2.7 s, or 27% of the gap [D-17]. It also depends on how long protons are stored in a trap, and aSPECT has no equivalent of that storage for decay-volume protons.
   - So the two experiments' proton-side budgets fall short by factors of ~12–25 (BL1) and ~27 (aSPECT detection item). The two shortfalls would have to be closed by one effect, and no one has named it. The candidate concedes: "No physical effect linking the two observables has been identified" [candidates.md C8].
3. **The "one-to-one" match does not point to a common cause.**
   - aSPECT's τ_β-equivalent (889.58 ± 3.20 s) sits 0.48σ from BL1 (887.7 ± 2.3 s). But the match is loose: any aSPECT offset from the A route between 5.1 s and 12.9 s would agree with BL1 to within 1σ [calc as above].
   - Two independent systematics of roughly the same size would produce the same "match". So the cited closure evidence (the two "closed worlds" of M-CONSTRAINTS-04) does not tell R-strong apart from two unrelated errors.
   - The closure also fits the reverse reading equally well, in which the proton-side results are right. So it cannot pick out the direction R-strong asserts.
4. **The other proton-detecting a-coefficient experiment is now neutral, not supportive.**
   - aCORN (e–p coincidence, proton time of flight) has been updated. Wietfeldt et al. 2024 give λ = −1.2712 ± 0.0061. That is 0.85σ from PERKEO III and 0.66σ from aSPECT 2024 [calc].
   - The class "proton-detecting λ measurements" therefore holds exactly one precise member, aSPECT. The λ half of the partition is one experiment (decomposer F12), just as the lifetime half is essentially BL1.

Why this is weakened and not refuted: none of these points proves that a common cause is impossible. An unknown effect outside both budgets can always be posited, and Nab (a proton-detecting a measurement) will test the prediction. The partition statistics (Δχ² = 5.3, likelihood ratio ≈ 14 for proton/rest over beam/storage; M-STATISTICIAN-04, verified) stand.

## Corrections to details

- "aCORN, another proton-involving λ measurement, goes the other way [D-41]" is out of date. The current aCORN value (Wietfeldt 2024, −1.2712 ± 0.0061) sits between the routes and, by its authors' own statement, "improves agreement with ... aSPECT". It no longer counts against C8, but it gives no support either (0.66σ against 0.85σ).
- Using aCORN 2024 makes the a route internally consistent (χ² 0.44/1) and raises the λ split to 3.46σ (M-STATISTICIAN-10). This slightly strengthens the partition as a description of the data. It does not bear on the common-cause claim.
- "aCORN … 874.86 ± 7.07 s" (the τ-equivalent from the 2021 value) should be updated to the 2024 λ. That gives roughly 884 s, using dτ/dλ ≈ −1144 s per unit λ (RQ-19). This is my arithmetic and was not run in a script.
- The candidate's prediction that "a new proton-detecting a measurement (Nab) sides with aSPECT" under R-strong holds only if the common cause is generic to proton detection. aCORN, which also detects protons, does not show it. So R-strong has to be restricted to something specific to aSPECT and BL1, which makes the "proton-detecting" label broader than the evidence supports.

## What survives and remaining doubt

- **Survives.**
  - Beam against bottle is the wrong partition: J-PARC is a beam that reads like a bottle.
  - Proton counting (essentially BL1) against the rest fits better by Δχ² ≈ 5.3.
  - A separate 3–3.5σ λ tension (aSPECT against β-asymmetry) has a τ-equivalent of similar size.
  - All of this is the R-weak reading. As the candidate notes, it names no new cause and reduces to C1's A-BL1 sub-hypothesis plus the residual λ tension.
- **Doubt.**
  - I read the aSPECT 2024 reanalysis only for its detector-loss and conclusion passages, not its full revised budget table. Its retardation-voltage revision (item ii) has no BL1 counterpart in any case.
  - "Proton-side" could still mean a transport effect, not a detection effect. But BL1's transport items (trap nonlinearity, end effects cancelled by varying the trap length) have no evident counterpart in a single-pass MAC-E filter.
  - A common cause outside both budgets cannot be excluded by the literature, only shown to be unmotivated. Nab's a value is the direct test, read together with an absolute non-proton rate (LiNA or UCNProBe).
