# Verdict on C8 (refuter 2, angle: bounds)
claim: Beam versus bottle is the wrong partition: the split is proton-detecting measurements (essentially BL1, plus aSPECT's λ) versus all others, with one proton-side cause behind both lifetime and λ tensions.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: The data are better partitioned as "proton-detecting against the rest" than as "beam against bottle" (the gain, Δχ² ≈ 5, comes from J-PARC alone), but the lifetime and λ tensions cannot share one proton-detection cause at the one element the two experiments have in common: BL1 needs an absolute proton deficit that leaves aSPECT's a exactly unchanged, and BL1's in-situ extrapolation removes the energy-dependent detector loss that aSPECT would need; the two tensions therefore stay separate proton-side problems (R-weak) unless a new recoil-energy-dependent loss is found that acts in both a Penning trap and a MAC-E filter.

## Strongest attack

R-strong asks one cause to shift two different observables:
- **BL1 (and Sussex–ILL):** needs an absolute proton-count deficit of 1.1% (+9.9 s).
- **aSPECT:** needs |a| to read 2.7% low (a = −0.10402 measured against −0.10687 from PERKEO III's λ).

The bound comes from how each observable responds to a proton-detection efficiency ε(T), where T is the proton's recoil energy.

1. **aSPECT is blind to the component that moves BL1.** aSPECT fits the shape of the integral spectrum N(U) with a free normalisation. The paper reports "a strong correlation (> 0.9) among the fit parameters N0 and a". My forward model uses the SM recoil spectrum, aSPECT's own transmission function (their Eq. 4, r_B = 0.203) and seven retardation points from 50 V to 600 V. A uniform 1.1% proton loss, which is exactly what BL1 needs, changes the fitted a by **0.000%** [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C8-2_bounds.py].
   - Only a slope in ε(T) moves a. The slope needed is small: ε(T_max) − ε(0) = 0.48% across 0–751 eV. That is 0.64% per keV at the silicon drift detector (SDD), where protons arrive at 15.0–15.75 keV, and it implies a spectrum-averaged loss of only 0.25%.
   - So whatever shifts aSPECT is not the 1.1% absolute deficit BL1 needs. At best one cause could carry two separate components.
2. **The only hardware the two share is proton detection in silicon after post-acceleration, and each experiment bounds that element.**
   - **BL1 removes energy-dependent detector losses in situ.** Nico 2005 says the proton efficiency "requires an extrapolation to remove the dependency on backscattering. Thus, one must measure the neutron lifetime at many values of the calculated backscatter fraction. This value is varied experimentally by acquiring data at several acceleration voltages and by using detectors with different dead layer thickness and composition."
     - Any loss that scales with the proton's energy at the detector (backscatter, dead layer, below-threshold) is extrapolated away.
     - The leftover calculation uncertainty is 0.4 s, which is 25× smaller than the 9.9 s gap.
   - **aSPECT's own model of the same physics is 27× too small.** Its table of Monte Carlo systematics gives "Backscattering/threshold IV F −0.1031 −0.1", a relative change in a of −0.1%. The −2.7% shift is 27× that.
   - The dilemma follows:
     - If the proton-side cause depends on detector energy, BL1's acceleration-voltage and dead-layer extrapolation removes it, so BL1 is not biased.
     - If the cause does not depend on energy, it cannot move aSPECT's a (step 1).
     - Linking the two numerically needs a detector loss falling as E⁻² that is unaccounted in both experiments: about 4.8% at 15 keV and 1.2% at 30 keV [same calc, section E]. That is about 25× beyond BL1's quoted backscatter uncertainty and about 27× beyond aSPECT's modelled effect, and BL1's voltage scan would have exposed it.
3. **aCORN is an independent check, and it pulls against R-strong.** aCORN detects the decay proton in coincidence and gives λ = −1.2796 ± 0.0062 [D-41].
   - If the same proton-side bias seen in aSPECT (Δλ = +0.0096) acted on aCORN, it should read −1.2668. It lies 1.89σ from that, against 0.51σ from the unbiased PERKEO III value.
   - The likelihood ratio is 5.2 : 1 against a generic proton-side λ bias.
   - This is modest, and aCORN's wishbone asymmetry is only weakly sensitive to how proton efficiency depends on energy. It is a disfavour, not a kill.

What is left for R-strong is a loss that depends on the proton's initial recoil energy and occurs before the detector, in both BL1's 4.6 T Penning trap and aSPECT's MAC-E filter.
- aSPECT's largest energy-dependent effects of this kind are its edge effect and its retardation-voltage-dependent background. The edge effect comes from gyroradius clipping on a beam wider than its detector; the background comes from residual-gas ions in the analysing-plane region.
- Both are specific to aSPECT's geometry and vacuum. Neither has a counterpart in BL1's trap, whose proton-loss channels were tested in situ (trap-length variation [D-08]; H₂ charge exchange, Caylor 2025 [RE-19]).
- The candidate itself concedes "No physical effect linking the two observables has been identified".

The single common cause is therefore not just unidentified. At the one physical element the two experiments share, it is bounded out by more than an order of magnitude.

## Corrections to details

- "One proton-side cause biases both an absolute proton count and a recoil-spectrum shape." These are orthogonal: a count deficit leaves the shape fit exactly untouched (calc A). Any common cause must carry two independent components, a uniform 1.1% and a slope of 0.48%, so it is two effects under one label unless a physical law ties them.
- The "decisive test" logic needs one caveat. Nab also extracts a from proton time of flight measured in silicon detectors after post-acceleration. An aSPECT-like Nab result would therefore fit R-strong. It would equally fit a physics reading of the a-route (C2, C3 or C5 with LiNA ≈ 888 s), so the LiNA/UCNProBe half of the test carries the discrimination, as the candidate says.

## What survives and remaining doubt

**What survives:**
- The partition statistic: proton-counting against the rest beats beam against bottle by Δχ² ≈ 5.3. This is driven entirely by J-PARC's placement, at about 2.2σ.
- The descriptive observation that both 3σ-class tensions involve an experiment that detects decay protons.
- The two-worlds closure. It is a consistency statement and makes no causal claim.

**What does not survive in its strong form:** the "one proton-side cause" behind both tensions. It fails at the one element the two experiments share, silicon proton detection. There the attack combines exact cancellation in aSPECT's normalisation-free fit with BL1's in-situ voltage and dead-layer extrapolation.

**Remaining doubt:**
- A loss that depends on initial recoil energy and occurs before the detector, common to a Penning trap and a MAC-E filter, is not excluded by any number here. It is only unidentified, and it would have to differ in size between the two instruments.
- BL1's extrapolation is linear in the calculated backscatter fraction, so a loss that scales very differently could survive it partly.
- The aCORN evidence is below 2σ.

For these reasons the verdict is weakened, not refuted: the candidate collapses to R-weak (BL1 against everything, with a separate aSPECT problem), not to nothing.
