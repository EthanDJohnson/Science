claim: An unidentified systematic in the proton-counting beam method (BL1, Sussex–ILL) makes it read about 1.1% long; the storage value near 878 s is the true lifetime.
status: final

Units: SI (lifetimes in s); λ, Yp, branching ratios and the CKM row sum are dimensionless.

## Strongest attack
From the bounds angle, the question is whether any constraint that neither counts protons in a Penning trap nor stores UCN rules out "τn ≈ 878 s, and the proton beam reads long". The ones available are:
- the SM master formula with λ and Vud;
- first-row CKM unitarity;
- the electron-counting beam (J-PARC);
- space-based lifetimes;
- BBN ⁴He;
- direct searches.

The strongest attack is the **proton-recoil (a-coefficient) route to λ**:
- aSPECT 2024 gives λ = −1.2668(27) [D-42], so τ_β = 889.58 ± 3.20 s [M-CONSTRAINTS-02 verified].
- Against C1's storage pole (878.32 ± 0.43 s) that is 3.49σ. The a-route group mean (887.21 ± 2.93 s) sits 3.00σ from the pole [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C1-2_bounds_ledger.py].
- If the a route were right, the SM would independently put τ_β near 888 s, and C1's "storage is the true lifetime" would have to fail, or else need a proton-less branch, which is C3 and not C1.

A secondary attack is Beck 2024's combined fit with a free Fierz term: τ = 893.7 ± 4.2 s for every method, 3.79σ above UCNτ [M-DECOMPOSER-11 verified].

**Why the attack fails.** The a route is not an independent bound that C1 violates. It is one experiment (aSPECT) in 2.9–3.5σ tension with three mutually consistent β-asymmetry experiments:
- PERKEO III, UCNA and PERKEO II together give χ² = 1.51 for 2 degrees of freedom [M-STATISTICIAN-10 verified; D-15].
- The A route gives τ_β = 878.70 ± 0.83 s, 0.41σ from C1's pole and 4.21σ from the beam pole [calc: same file].
- Every sensible combination of the non-trap, non-storage bounds favours C1's pole:
  - A route + a route + J-PARC (as quoted) + Lunar Prospector: Δχ²(storage − beam) = −13.16.
  - PDG-2024 λ (S = 2.7) + J-PARC with its statistical error inflated by 2.29 + space: Δχ² = −12.37.
  - Only by taking the a route alone, discarding all three A-route experiments, does the ledger turn against C1 (Δχ² = +6.10) [calc: same file].

The Fierz escape needs b = −0.0181 ± 0.0065, a 2.8σ non-zero Fierz term seen nowhere else. It is driven by the same aSPECT/PERKEO split, and it disfavours the beam value too (1.26σ above BL1). So it measures the λ tension, not a bound that selects against C1.

No bound refutes the core. The a-route tension is a cost C1 already concedes in candidates.md ("C1 depends on the A-route λ"), not a hidden one.

**First-row unitarity** [M-CONSTRAINTS-04 verified]:
- UCNτ + PERKEO III gives a row sum of 0.99898(87), −1.2σ.
- BL1 + PERKEO III gives 0.98842(251), −4.6σ.

- Unitarity therefore sides with C1's pole. Raising Vud to close the superallowed Cabibbo deficit moves τ_β further from the beam [M-EXAMINER-07 verified].

**BBN ⁴He** is uninformative. Yeh et al. 2023 (arXiv:2303.04140, ACCESS full-text) write: "∆ τn = 5 s gives ∆ Yp = 0.0010∼ 0.3σobs(Yp), where σobs(Yp) is the observed 4He error". Scaled to the 9.65 s pole separation, ΔYp = 0.0019 = 0.58σ_obs [calc: same file]. BBN therefore neither supports nor bounds C1.

**Direct searches** (n → χγ, n → χe⁺e⁻, n → n′, ¹¹Be) are all null [D-60–D-65]. C1 predicts nulls, so these searches cannot bound it. They only remove rivals.

**No newer datum overturns this.** As of 2026-09-30:
- No BL2 lifetime has been published. The 2025–26 BL2 output is the H₂-detection paper, Caylor et al., PRC 112, 065501 (lit_search, abstract).
- Nab has published no a value. The 2026 Nab papers are detector and commissioning work (lit_search, INSPIRE abstracts, e.g. EPJ Web Conf. 378 04001).

## Corrections to details
- The A- versus a-route split is 2.93σ when the common Vud error is removed [M-STATISTICIAN-10]. My naive quadrature of the two τ_β errors gives 2.79σ because it counts the shared Vud error twice. Candidates.md's 3.2σ "C1 fails the SM route" figure is against UCNτ (877.82 ± 0.30 s). Against the S-scaled all-storage pole it is 3.00σ. The figures agree once the reference is stated.
- The candidate lists Sussex–ILL as part of the biased class. That commits C1 to the generic-method scope (A-gen) more than its heading admits. Sussex–ILL sits 2.24σ from the storage pole, so the A-BL1 scope alone leaves a 2.2σ residual unexplained.
- BBN is absent from the prediction matrix. It should be listed as uninformative (0.58σ_obs between the poles).

## What survives and remaining doubt
The core survives the bounds attack. Every independent constraint with discriminating power sides with the storage pole:
- the β-asymmetry λ route (4.2σ against the beam pole);
- the PDG-average λ route (3.2σ);
- first-row unitarity;
- J-PARC (1.8–2.2σ).

The single contrary bound is the aSPECT a-coefficient route (3.0–3.5σ against C1's pole). C1 survives it only by holding that aSPECT's λ is wrong, for a reason separate from the lifetime systematic. That leaves two tensions unexplained instead of one, which is what C8 exploits.

Remaining doubts:
- **C1's own weakest link, which is outside the bounds angle:** no identified effect reaches the 9.9 s it needs. Caylor's worst case for H₂ charge exchange is < 0.5 s.
- **The decisive tests:**
  - Nab's a coefficient. An aSPECT-like result would turn the SM bound against C1 at high significance [M-CONSTRAINTS-05].
  - A BL2 or BL3 lifetime.
