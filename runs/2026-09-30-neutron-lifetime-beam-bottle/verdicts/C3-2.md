# Verdict on C3 (refuter 2, angle: bounds)
claim: A proton-less, electron-less dark-decay branch of about 1.1% (invisible n → χφ, χχχ or χA′) makes beams measure τ_β while bottles measure the true total lifetime (speculative).
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: An invisible ≈1.1% dark branch survives only as a conditional bet in which the aSPECT (a-coefficient) λ is right and all three β-asymmetry λ measurements share a ≈0.7% error (or a tensor Fierz term above the LHC bound exists), J-PARC 2024 reads ≈10 s low, and χ carries a repulsive dark interaction (m_V/g ≲ 50–100 MeV); combining the current non-proton data in the standard way (PDG scale factor) excludes it at ≈7σ.

Units: lifetimes in s (SI); λ, Vud, b, ε_S, ε_T and branching ratios are dimensionless; masses in MeV (c = 1).

## Strongest attack
Under C3, three measurements with no proton counting at all must equal the proton-beam τ_β ≈ 888 s: (i) J-PARC's electron count, since an invisible channel yields no electron; (ii) the SM master formula τ_β(λ, Vud); and, as C3's own prediction, (iii) any in-bottle β count. Only (i) and (ii) exist today. Both bound Br_X independently of the anomalous method, and together they exclude the required 1.09% far beyond 5σ unless C3 also discards most of the λ data.

1. **World λ, combined in the standard way.** The five current λ measurements (PERKEO III, UCNA, PERKEO II, aSPECT 2024, aCORN; aSPECT 2020 dropped as superseded) average to |λ| = 1.27594 ± 0.00049, with χ² = 13.66/4, so the PDG-style scale factor is S = 1.85 and the scaled error ±0.00090. That gives τ_β(SM) = 879.03 ± 1.20 s and Br_X = 0.081 ± 0.145%, with a 95% upper limit of 0.32%. The needed 1.087% sits **6.95σ** away [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C3-2_bounds.py, §2]. This includes aSPECT and deweights the discordant set as the PDG would. The candidate cites only PERKEO III (9.5–10σ) and the PDG 2024 λ (4.9σ); the PDG 2024 value is a different, earlier average (whether it includes aSPECT 2024 was not checked here).
2. **J-PARC as a second, independent route.** Br_X(J-PARC) = −0.13 ± 0.50%. The needed value sits 2.44σ away as quoted, or 1.90σ with J-PARC's statistical error inflated by S = 2.29 [same calc, §3].
3. **Non-proton combination.** Combining the SM route (world λ, scaled) with J-PARC gives τ_β = 878.90 ± 1.15 s (internal χ² 0.17/1), Br_X = 0.066 ± 0.140% and a 95% upper limit of 0.30%. The needed value is **7.2–7.3σ** away. Inflating the λ error by a further 2× (S_eff = 3.7) still leaves **4.4–4.7σ** [same calc, §6].
4. **The C3 pattern as a joint fit.** C3 requires the proton beam, J-PARC and the SM route to agree on τ_β. With world λ this gives χ² = 15.1/2 (p = 5×10⁻⁴, ≈3.5σ). The same three measurements under C1's pattern (storage, J-PARC and SM agree) give χ² = 0.39/2 (p = 0.82) [same calc, §4–5].
5. **The only way out is the a-route escape the candidate names, and it has its own cost.** With aSPECT 2024 alone, τ_β(SM) = 889.58 ± 3.20 s and Br_X = 1.27 ± 0.36%, which fits. But then:
   - aSPECT and J-PARC disagree with each other: internal χ² 5.26/1 (2.3σ) as quoted, 3.70/1 scaled [§6].
   - PERKEO III, PERKEO II and UCNA, three β-asymmetry experiments with different apparatus, must all be wrong by ≈0.7% in λ. The A group has internal χ² 1.51 [M-CONSTRAINTS-02], so their error would have to be common to all three.
6. **The Fierz escape C-b collides with the LHC.** b_n = −0.0158 needs, from b_n⟨m_e/E⟩ ≈ 0.35 ε_S⟨m_e/E⟩ − 5.15 ε_T⟨m_e/E⟩, either ε_T = +3.07×10⁻³, which is 2.36× the LHC 90% CL bound |ε_T| < 1.3×10⁻³, or ε_S = −0.045, which is 7.8× the bound |ε_S| < 5.8×10⁻³ [same calc, §8; sources below]. The LHC bounds assume heavy new physics (SMEFT), so a light-mediator tensor is not excluded by them. C-b is strained, not dead.
7. **Vud does not rescue it.** Moving Vud toward first-row unitarity (+0.0005 to +0.0009) shortens τ_β(SM) to 878.1–877.4 s, which drives Br_X to ≤ 0 [§7; consistent with M-EXAMINER-07].
8. **Neutron stars.** The minimal model collapses stars to 0.69–0.70 M☉ [M-CONSTRAINTS-09 verified], so C3 needs an added repulsive sector. Published constructions exist (quotes below), so this is a cost, not a refutation. The required m_V/g ≲ 50–100 MeV depends on the EOS [M-CONSTRAINTS-09, unverified threshold]. At the strong end the Born self-scattering is σ/m ≈ 1.3–4 cm²/g [M-CONSTRAINTS-10], in tension with halo and cluster bounds if χ is all of the dark matter. The Born approximation is invalid there, and the tension disappears at m_V/g ≈ 100 MeV.

Why weakened and not refuted: every bound above is conditional on an input C3 explicitly contests. These are the λ from the β asymmetry (the a/A split is a live 3.5σ tension of unknown origin), J-PARC 2024 (a preprint whose own run conditions disagree at χ² 15.8/3), and a minimal neutron-star sector. No single bound kills the invisible channel without assuming one of these. Taken together, they confine C3 to a narrow conjunction of escapes.

## Corrections to details
- C3's evidence-against line cites the PDG-average λ at 4.9σ. With aSPECT 2024 included and the PDG scale factor applied, the current world λ gives 6.95σ (upper limit 0.32%). Adding J-PARC gives 7.2σ. The PDG 2024 λ = 1.2754(13) used by the lens is a different average, and I did not check its input list.
- The Fierz escape C-b is described as 1.6σ from Saul et al. 2020. It also needs ε_T ≈ 3.1×10⁻³, 2.4× the LHC 90% bound (for heavy mediators), or a scalar coupling 7.8× its bound. So C-b is constrained beyond the one b measurement quoted.
- "J-PARC counts electrons, so it also reads τ_β" is right for C-inv. It is exactly this that turns J-PARC into a direct Br_X bound (−0.13 ± 0.50%), which the candidate lists only as a tension.

## What survives and remaining doubt
The invisible channel has no direct laboratory search, and each bound individually rests on a contested input:
- The λ bound collapses if aSPECT's a-coefficient is right and the A-route experiments share an unidentified ≈0.7% error.
- J-PARC is a preprint whose four conditions disagree.
- The neutron-star bound is evaded by published repulsive-sector constructions.

So C3 is not refuted, but only the conjunction in the `narrowed:` line keeps it alive. Under the standard combination of current non-proton data, Br_X < 0.30% (95% CL). The decisive tests are unchanged: Nab's a (an aSPECT-like λ is necessary for C3), LiNA (C3 needs ≈ 888 s) and UCNProBe (C3 needs a β-rate lifetime ≈ 10 s longer than storage). A PERKEO-like Nab result would turn this verdict into a refutation.
