# Verdict on C6 (refuter 2, angle: bounds)
claim: The β-decay rate of recently produced beam neutrons differs from that of stored UCN (an excited neutron state n* or a field dependence), lengthening beam lifetimes (speculative).
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: Only an excited-state variant survives. Either n* is populated equally by every source, has a de-excitation time 0.02 s ≪ τγ ≤ 139 s, and predicts that J-PARC reads about 888 s (2.1–2.2σ against the observed value), or n* is populated by reactor fission but not by spallation, a production dependence the model does not motivate and nobody has tested. The field-dependence variant is excluded.

## Strongest attack
C6 says the decay rate depends on how long ago the neutron was produced. In Koch–Hummel's model (the only concrete version) that time is set by one ordering: t_beam (≈ 2×10⁻² s) ≪ τγ ≪ t_bottle. The ordering applies to every cold-neutron beam, whichever decay product it counts. So in the model's own terms, the J-PARC electron-counting beam must also read about 888 s: its neutrons are likewise measured tens of milliseconds after spallation. The candidate calls this "the slate builder's inference; unchecked". The ordering in Koch–Hummel settles it: if τγ ≫ t_beam, the n* population surviving to the J-PARC TPC equals the initial population, just as it does at BL1.

The independent bounds then close in from three sides:
1. **Storage side (cited).** The UCNτ reanalysis (Blatnik et al., arXiv:2406.10378) excludes τγ > 139 s at 95% CL for any starting n* fraction, assuming the fraction does not depend on how the neutron was produced.
2. **Electron-counting beam (calculation).** Within the remaining window, 0.02 s ≪ τγ ≤ 139 s (3.8 decades), the model predicts J-PARC ≈ 887.7 s. J-PARC measured 877.2 +4.35/−3.98 s. That is 2.15σ against BL1's value and 2.24σ against the proton-beam mean, while J-PARC agrees with UCNτ to 0.14σ. The point-likelihood ratio favouring the storage value over the beam value is about 21 : 1.
3. **The τγ ≈ t_beam escape (calculation).** Suppose J-PARC's neutrons are simply older than BL1's, so that n* has decayed before they are counted. Cutting J-PARC's n* population to ≤ 0.1–0.4 of BL1's needs τγ ≲ 4–55 ms for an age difference of 10–50 ms. BL1's own neutrons would then already be depleted, so the fraction at production must be scaled up by e^{t/τγ}. For t_BL1 = 50 ms and τγ = 10 ms the required fraction is 1.65, which is impossible. BL1's lifetime would also depend strongly on neutron velocity (a factor of about 20 between slow and fast neutrons for τγ = 10 ms). That breaks the model's own ordering and contradicts BL1's single-lifetime analysis.
4. **Field variant (calculation, agrees with M-MECHANIST-13).** |µn|B/Q = 3.5–3.9×10⁻¹³ at 4.6–5 T. Even with the Q⁵ phase-space lever the effect falls short of the needed 1.14% by 5.9–6.4×10⁹. Eliminated.

The attack does not refute C6 outright, for three reasons: J-PARC's tension is only 2.2σ; J-PARC's own four configurations scatter (χ²/DOF = 15.8/3, D-24); and one escape remains. If n* is populated by reactor fission but not by spallation, it evades J-PARC (spallation) and the UCNτ bound, whose LANL UCN come from spallation and whose analysis assumed equal fractions. Every proton-counting beam (BL1, Sussex–ILL) sits at a reactor. That escape is an added, unmotivated assumption, and nobody has tested it.

| Material bottles and magnetic traps | 0 (for τγ ≤ 139 s) | ✓ |

The field variant produces about 10⁻¹² of the gap. With equal production it carries the whole gap in proton beams only by also predicting a long J-PARC result, which is not seen at 2.2σ. With fission-only production it can carry the whole gap, but only through an extra assumption.

## Corrections to details
- The candidate calls "whether n* survives the flight to J-PARC's decay volume" uncomputed. Koch–Hummel's own ordering (t_beam ≪ τγ) forces survival. Only the non-canonical τγ ≈ t_beam regime avoids it, and that regime needs an impossible or strongly velocity-dependent n* population (calc §3).
- The storage-side exclusion in D-93 has a size: τγ > 139 s is excluded at 95% CL for all starting n* fractions, assuming the fraction does not depend on how the neutron was produced.
- The Nab limit is on ΔE (m_n < 939.586 MeV at 90% CL, i.e. ΔE ≲ 21 keV) for an n*-dominated sample. It is not a limit on the n* fraction.
- The field-variant shortfall is 5.9–6.4×10⁹ with Γ ∝ Q⁵ (it matches M-MECHANIST-13; the "~6×10⁹" in the candidate is right).

## What survives and remaining doubt
What survives: an n* with 0.02 s ≪ τγ ≤ 139 s that is populated equally by every source, at 2.1–2.2σ tension with J-PARC; or a fission-only n* that evades both J-PARC and UCNτ. Neither is excluded at ≥ 3σ.

Remaining doubts:
- J-PARC's internal inconsistency limits its weight.
- I did not read the flight times of BL1 and J-PARC from their papers, so calc §3 is parametric (10–50 ms age differences).
- Reactor-based storage experiments (ILL) could test the fission-only escape at holding times of a few hundred seconds, but only if τγ exceeds about 100 s. That was not computed.

The decisive tests:
- LiNA, or a J-PARC run with better background control, reaching ≈ 1 s. This separates 878 from 888 at about 10σ in any variant with equal production.
- A proton-counting beam at a spallation source, or BL2 run against flight path. This tests production dependence directly.
