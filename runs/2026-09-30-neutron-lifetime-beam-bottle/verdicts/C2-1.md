# Verdict on C2 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: An unidentified loss of about 1.2×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ common to material and magnetic UCN traps shortens every storage result; the proton-beam value near 888 s is the true lifetime.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: cited-evidence
narrowed: The beam value can be the true lifetime only if an unidentified storage-only loss is apparatus-dependent, not common: 0.66–1.27×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ across the traps, and smaller in walled traps than in wall-free ones. It must also be 49–140× larger than anything UCNτ's in-situ tests allow, and J-PARC 2024 and the β-asymmetry λ route (together 3.8σ from BL1) must each be wrong for separate reasons.

Units: SI throughout (lifetimes in s, loss rates in s⁻¹).

## Strongest attack
The literature attacks C2 from three independent directions. None of them reaches 5σ, so C2 is not refuted. Together they strip it of both parts of its heading: the loss is not "common", and "the proton-beam value is the true lifetime" is contradicted at 3.8σ by evidence that does not involve UCN storage.

1. **Two measurements that store no UCN both side with storage.**
   - J-PARC 2024 counts electrons in a beam, with no stored UCN, so a storage loss cannot reach it. The authors write: "This result is consistent with bottle method measurements but exhibits a 2.3σ tension with the average value obtained from the proton-detection-based beam method."
   - The SM route from β-asymmetry λ gives the same answer. CMS 2018: "Consistency with that relation and better precision suggest τ_n^favored = 879.4(6) s and g_A^favored = 1.2755(11) as preferred values."
   - PERKEO III's λ, which "confirms recent measurements with improved precision", gives τ_β = 878.70 ± 0.83 s.
   - J-PARC and the A route agree with each other (χ² 0.11/1). Combined, they give 878.65 ± 0.82 s: 3.78σ below BL1 and 4.24σ below the proton class.
   - So C2 needs two further, unrelated errors that storage cannot supply: a separate −10.8 s artefact in J-PARC, and a shared ≈0.7% λ error across PERKEO III, UCNA and PERKEO II, three different apparatuses.
2. **No known mechanism comes near the needed size, and every in-situ test is null.**
   - UCNτ's 2025 systematic table (Musedinovic et al., full text) lists "Depolarization 0.00 +0.07", "Heating 0.00 +0.07" and "Residual gas 0.05 ±0.03" (s).
   - The loss UCNτ would need if BL1 is right (1.268×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹, a 9.88 s shift) is 140× the depolarisation bound, 140× the heating bound, 195× the gas correction and 49× the whole +0.20 s systematic.
   - A constant loss is exactly degenerate with decay in a storage curve (M-MECHANIST-06), so no storage curve can exclude it. But the only effects known to produce a constant loss in a wall-free trap (spin flip, gas, heating) have been measured, and they are 2 orders of magnitude too small.
   - The same group states in its abstract that storage "results are also consistent with the latest most accurate measurements of the neutron decay asymmetry within the Standard Model" (Serebrov 2021).
3. **The loss would not be "common", and its pattern and the historical record both run the wrong way.**
   - If BL1 is true, the storage results need losses from 6.64 to 12.68×10⁻⁶ s⁻¹. A single common rate fits with χ² = 23.2/6 (p = 7×10⁻⁴).
   - The walled (material) traps need less loss than the wall-free magnetic traps: 9.81 ± 0.63 against 12.66 ± 0.38 ×10⁻⁶ s⁻¹, a ratio of 0.77, 3.9σ apart unscaled.
   - An extra loss acting in both kinds of trap should leave the walled traps, which also have wall-loss channels, reading no longer than the magnetic ones. The data show the opposite.
   - The one documented, quantified storage systematic went the other way too: Serebrov & Fomin 2010 found "Systematic errors of about -6 s … in each of the experiments", i.e. the old results read too *long*.
4. **A cost it does not state.** A loss that acts identically on walled and wall-free traps, whatever the field or temperature, is a bulk disappearance of free neutrons. By the brief's definition (τn = 1/Γ_total over every channel) that is part of the true lifetime, which is C3, not a systematic. To stay a systematic, C2 has to make the loss depend on the apparatus. Point 3 shows it does, but in the wrong direction.

Why this is not a refutation:
- J-PARC is a preprint with internally inconsistent run conditions (χ² 15.8/3). With its statistical error inflated it is only 1.74σ from BL1.
- The A route is contradicted by aSPECT's a-coefficient at about 3.5σ.
- If both λ families enter with a PDG-style scale factor (S = 2.79), the SM route is only 2.64σ from BL1, and J-PARC plus that route is 2.94σ.
- No experiment has yet directly compared a β-decay rate with a storage rate in the same trap (UCNProBe) or re-measured the proton-counting side (BL2/BL3).

## Corrections to details
- C2's "evidence for" quotes Serebrov & Fomin 2010 as "Systematic errors of about 6 s". The abstract reads "about -6 s". The sign means the old storage results were too long, which counts against C2, not for it. The candidate's parenthesis half-acknowledges this, but the quote itself drops the sign.
- Against UCNτ's current budget (Musedinovic 2025: depolarisation +0.07 s) the needed rate is 140× the bound. The 130× figure comes from Gonzalez 2021's λ_dp bound. Either way the conclusion is the same.
- "About 1.2×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ common" holds only for the magnetic traps and Serebrov 2005. Across all storage results the needed rate is 0.66–1.27×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹, and the material-class mean is 0.98×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives.** The logical possibility that storage experiments share an unknown, storage-only loss. The storage-curve degeneracy means no storage result can exclude it on its own data. The a-route λ (aSPECT) and the heavy-tail base rates keep the beam value from being excluded at 5σ.
- **What is lost.**
  - "Common": the needed loss varies with the apparatus, in the wrong direction.
  - "Unidentified loss" as the whole story: C2 also needs a separate J-PARC artefact and a shared β-asymmetry λ error.
  - A clear line between systematic and new physics: a truly apparatus-independent loss would be C3.
- **Remaining doubt.**
  - J-PARC is a preprint with a 4–5× background excess.
  - The λ split (A route against a route, 3.5σ) is unresolved.
  - Ezhov and Sussex–ILL values rest on search summaries and leads.
  - A result from UCNProBe (β-rate against storage in one trap), BL2/BL3 or Nab's a would settle it. None has published yet.
