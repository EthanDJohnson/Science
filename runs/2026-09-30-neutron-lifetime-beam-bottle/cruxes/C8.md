## Response to the verdicts

All three verdicts return "weakened", and all three split C8 the same way. The **partition** half survives: proton-detecting results against the rest beats beam against bottle by Δχ² = 5.3, and J-PARC is a beam that reads like a bottle (M-STATISTICIAN-04, M-EXAMINER-01, both verified). The **"one proton-side cause"** half (R-strong) is attacked. I defend the partition, concede most of the cause, and say what is left.

### What I concede

1. **A detector-side common cause is effectively excluded (C8-2 points 1–2, C8-1 point 1). Conceded.**
   - aSPECT fits the spectrum shape with a free normalisation N0. A uniform 1.1% proton loss, which is exactly what BL1 needs, therefore changes the fitted a by 0.000% (C8-2 calc A; C8-0 point 1 agrees).
   - aSPECT's own 2024 reanalysis raised its absolute detector losses by ≈ 35% and found "no effect on the final result due to normalization (N0)" (C8-1, quoting Beck et al. 2024). That is a direct in-apparatus test of the one hardware element BL1 and aSPECT share, silicon detection after post-acceleration, and it went against R-strong.
   - BL1 extrapolates away detector-energy-dependent losses in situ, using several acceleration voltages and dead layers (C8-2, quoting Nico 2005). An E⁻² detector loss that would link the two needs ≈ 4.8% at 15 keV and ≈ 1.2% at 30 keV. BL1's voltage scan would have exposed that, and it is 25–27× beyond both published budgets (C8-2 calc E).
   - I have no rebuttal. "Proton-detection-side" in the heading should read "proton-side, before detection". The detection sub-case is dead.
2. **The "one-to-one mapping" and the two-worlds closure are not evidence for a common cause (C8-0 point 5, C8-1 point 3). Conceded.**
   - Under R-strong both numbers are artefacts, so their landing on an SM-consistent line is a coincidence, not a prediction. Across effect shapes, the BL1 shift that goes with the required aSPECT shift runs from 0 to 35 s (C8-0).
   - The match is also loose: any aSPECT offset of 5.1–12.9 s would agree with BL1 within 1σ (C8-1).
   - I withdraw M-CONSTRAINTS-04 from "evidence for" R-strong. It stays as evidence for the partition only.
3. **aCORN is now neutral, not "the other way" (C8-1 correction). Conceded, with a caveat.**
   - Using aCORN 2024 (λ = −1.2712 ± 0.0061, per C8-1) gives τ_β = 884.48 ± 7.04 s. That is uninformative between 878.5 s and 889.6 s.
   - C8-2's 5.2 : 1 likelihood ratio against a generic proton-side λ bias uses the superseded aCORN 2021 value (−1.2796 ± 0.0062). With the 2024 value, C8-1 reports 0.66σ from aSPECT against 0.85σ from PERKEO III, a likelihood ratio near 1. So C8-2's aCORN point no longer counts against C8.
   - C8-1's larger point stands: the proton-detecting λ class has one precise member, aSPECT. The λ half of the partition is one experiment, as the lifetime half is essentially BL1.
4. **In-flight time-scaled losses cannot be shared (C8-0 point 4). Conceded.**
   - Residual-gas charge exchange is the only named proton-side mechanism. Scaled from BL1's ~10 ms storage to aSPECT's ~10 µs flight, it falls 94× short of the required Δa = +0.00285, even when placed in aSPECT's most a-sensitive band (C8-0 calc).

### What I rebut or keep

5. **C8-0's tuned solution is physical, so R-strong is not excluded.** C8-0 finds that one shared recoil-energy efficiency ε(T) = 1 − k·exp(−T/T₀) with T₀ ≈ 21–22 eV and k ≈ 0.86–1.0 gives both +9.1 s in BL1 and the required aSPECT Δa (k ≤ 1). All three refuters therefore stop at "weakened".
   - This is exactly the loss-before-detection class that C8-2 leaves open ("a loss that depends on initial recoil energy and occurs before the detector … is not excluded by any number here").
   - I cannot name a physical process that produces it in both a 4.6 T Penning trap and a MAC-E filter. I concede it is fine-tuned to about ±1 eV in T₀ and needs near-total loss as T → 0.
   - I rate R-strong as a live but low-prior sub-hypothesis, not the main content of C8.
6. **C8-0's modelling caveat cuts both ways.**
   - C8-0 assumed the same ε(T) in BL1 as in aSPECT and called this charitable to C8. But BL1 stores protons with the full 0–751 eV recoil spectrum in a trap of fixed depth. A low-T loss in BL1, for example low-energy protons lost from the trap or below a timing or transport threshold, need not have aSPECT's shape.
   - So the ±1 eV tuning is a feature of the shared-ε(T) model, not a bound on every two-instrument realisation. It remains unmotivated, which is the real problem, and I do not claim otherwise.
7. **The partition is the robust content, and I keep it as C8's claim.**
   - The whole Δχ² gain comes from placing J-PARC (C8-2), at ≈ 2.2σ. I accept that the partition rests on J-PARC's preprint value and BL1's single dataset.
   - The partition still changes the question the brief asks. The live issue is not "beams against bottles" but two separate proton-side results (BL1's absolute count and aSPECT's recoil shape) against a replicated non-proton cluster. The SM route is not an independent arbiter while those two sit on the same side.
8. **What C8 becomes.** After these concessions, C8's surviving content is R-weak: BL1 against everything, plus a separate aSPECT tension of similar τ-equivalent size. As the candidate already said, R-weak names no new cause and reduces to C1's A-BL1 scope plus the null's residual tensions.
   - What is still distinctive is one sharp but low-prior prediction (R-strong): Nab reads aSPECT-like and LiNA/UCNProBe read ≈ 878 s. No other candidate predicts that pair (see below).
   - C8-2 also points out that Nab detects protons in silicon after post-acceleration. So an aSPECT-like Nab fits R-strong or a physics a-route (C2, C3, C5), and only the lifetime half of the pair discriminates. Accepted; the deciding tests below are built as pairs for that reason.

Net: the partition claim stands, R-strong survives only as a fine-tuned pre-detection loss, and the default reading is two unrelated proton-side problems.

## Deciding observation

The central test for C8 is **Nab's a coefficient read together with an absolute non-proton lifetime (LiNA or UCNProBe)**.
- **Who and when:** Nab is at the ORNL SNS. Its goal is Δλ/|λ| = 0.04%, i.e. σ_a ≈ 0.00015. It was upgraded for precision running in Nov 2025, and no a value exists yet [D-107]. LiNA is at J-PARC, targeting ~1 s, commissioned Feb 2024, with no result date [D-102]. UCNProBe is at LANL, targeting 1–2 s (search-summary), with no date [D-106].
- **What R-strong predicts:** a ≈ −0.1040 together with LiNA ≈ 878 s.
- **Resolving power:** the poles a = −0.10687 (PERKEO-like) and −0.10402 (aSPECT-like) are 19σ apart at Nab precision. A PERKEO-like Nab sits 3.4σ from the aSPECT pole (limited by aSPECT's own ±0.00082). An aSPECT-like Nab sits 12.8σ from the PERKEO pole.

Against each rival:

- **C1 (proton-beam systematic; C8 minus the aSPECT link).**
  - Deciding observation: Nab's a.
  - C1 predicts a ≈ −0.1069; R-strong predicts a ≈ −0.1040. Both predict LiNA and UCNProBe ≈ 878 s and BL2/BL3 → 878 s.
  - A PERKEO-like Nab (3.4σ from the aSPECT pole) favours C1 and collapses C8 to R-weak, i.e. into C1's A-BL1. An aSPECT-like Nab with LiNA ≈ 878 s favours C8, because C1 would then need Nab and aSPECT both wrong for reasons unrelated to BL1.
  - Nab's 0.04% goal is ample. Timing is not given in the dossier.
- **C2 (common storage loss).**
  - Nab does not separate them: both predict an aSPECT-like a.
  - Deciding observation: LiNA, or UCNProBe's β-rate lifetime against its own storage lifetime. C2 predicts ≈ 888 s (a β-rate/storage difference of ≈ 10 s in one trap); C8 predicts ≈ 878 s and equality.
  - LiNA at 1 s: a reading of 888 s sits 8.9σ from the storage pole, favouring C2. A reading of 878 s sits 4.2σ from the proton pole, favouring C8.
  - UCNProBe at 1.5 s: 6.2σ and 3.8σ respectively.
  - No result dates [D-102, D-106].
- **C3 (invisible dark decay).**
  - Same pair as C2. Both C3 and C8 predict an aSPECT-like Nab (or b ≈ −0.016 under C3's Fierz escape), so Nab alone cannot decide.
  - LiNA ≈ 888 s favours C3 (8.9σ from storage at 1 s). LiNA ≈ 878 s favours C8 (4.2σ from the proton pole; 4.7σ once BL3 at 0.3 s shrinks that side).
  - A Nab b-coefficient measurement consistent with 0 at ≲ 0.005 would also close C3's Fierz escape without touching C8. That precision threshold is my reading of the b ≈ −0.0158 requirement; it was not computed.
- **C5 (electron-always X⁺ branch).**
  - This is the hardest pair. C5 and R-strong predict the same LiNA (≈ 878 s), UCNProBe (≈ 878 s) and Nab (aSPECT-like).
  - They differ in whether the proton deficit is physics or apparatus. Under C5 the proton-beam value is the true Γ(n → p e ν̄) and does not move with any proton-side variable. Under C8 it is an artefact of proton handling.
  - Deciding observation: a BL2/BL3 lifetime measured while varying proton-side variables (trap storage time, residual-gas pressure, trap field or depth, acceleration voltage). C8 predicts the value depends on at least one of them and moves toward 878 s. C5 predicts 888 s invariant, with no field, gas or trap-time dependence.
  - Precision: with BL2 at < 1 s per setting, halving a proportional 1.11% loss shifts τ by 4.9 s (3.5σ for two 1 s points), and doubling it shifts τ by 9.9 s (7.0σ).
  - Secondary: C5's positron or charged-track signature in the J-PARC/LiNA TPC (precision not computed). BL2 is "next few years" (search-summary); BL3 has no date [D-100, D-101].
- **C6 (excited or history-dependent neutron, n\*).**
  - Deciding observation: a beam lifetime measured against flight time since production (pulsed or velocity-selected beam, BL2/BL3 or LiNA).
  - C6 predicts a flight-time dependence. C8 predicts none but a dependence on proton-side variables.
  - Required precision not computed; no date. A full reading of the Blatnik/Nab n* exclusions [D-93] would also bear on it.
- **C7 (null: no dominant cause).**
  - Deciding observation: BL2's lifetime at < 1 s, read with whether a named proton-side correction accompanies it.
  - C7 predicts an intermediate value with no identified effect. At 883 ± 1 s, BL2 would sit 4.3σ above the storage pole and 2.2σ below the proton pole; at 881 ± 1 s, 2.5σ and 3.1σ.
  - C8 (either form) predicts BL2 at the storage pole once a proton-side effect is identified. As built, BL2 might still read ≈ 888 s if the cause is generic, and then the proton-side scan above decides.
  - Nab contributes too: C7 treats aSPECT as its own fluctuation and so expects a PERKEO-like Nab, while R-strong expects aSPECT-like.
  - Dates as above [D-100, D-107].
