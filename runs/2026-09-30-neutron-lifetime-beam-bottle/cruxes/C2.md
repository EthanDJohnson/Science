# Crux: C2
answers: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/verdicts/C2-0.md, runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/verdicts/C2-1.md, runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/verdicts/C2-2.md
status: final

Units: SI throughout (lifetimes in s, loss rates in s⁻¹); λ dimensionless. All three verdicts say "weakened". This file accepts that verdict and states the narrowed C2 it defends.

**Narrowed C2, as defended here.** The true lifetime is ≈ 888 s. Storage results are short because of a constant-rate loss that nobody has named. It acts only on stored UCN, and its strength depends on the apparatus: ≈ 1.0×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ in material bottles and ≈ 1.3×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ in magnetic traps. C2 also needs two further conditions:
- the a-coefficient λ (aSPECT) is right, and the β-asymmetry λ family is wrong;
- J-PARC 2024 carries its own error of ≈ −10.5 to −10.8 s.

The word "common" in the heading is withdrawn.

## Response to the verdicts

### C2-0 (magnitude)

1. **No named channel reaches the needed size.** Conceded in full. In UCNτ the named loss lines, summed at their limits, carry 0.44 s, which is 4.3% of the gap. The needed rate is 130× the depolarisation bound, 91× the gas rate UCNτ measured run by run, and 50.7× its whole +0.20 s systematic [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C2-0_magnitude.py]. B-gas, B-mag (depolarisation) and B-mat are therefore not live as sources of the gap, and B-mat cannot act in a trap with no walls. By the brief's test ("name the specific effect and its size against the budget"), C2 fails as a *named* systematic.
   - What survives: a strictly constant-rate loss is exactly degenerate with β decay in a single-exponential storage fit [M-MECHANIST-06, verified]. So no storage curve, and no budget built from the channels anyone modelled, can exclude a channel that was never modelled.
   - That is a statement about what can be excluded. It is not evidence for C2.
   - C2's hidden effect is ≈ 50× UCNτ's budget. C1's is 4.4× BL1's [M-CONSTRAINTS-12]. On priors this ratio favours C1, and I do not contest it.

2. **"Common" fails.** Conceded. The classes need 0.98–1.02×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ (material) and 1.27–1.30×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ (magnetic), a ratio of 0.78. The material and magnetic lifetimes differ by 2.21 ± 0.75 s (2.95σ scaled) [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/crux-C2_deciding_tests.py].
   - Rebuttal on its weight: as C2-0 itself notes, this scatter does not separate C2 from C1. A common-τ fit gives χ² = 23.33 for 6 degrees of freedom (dof), against 23.46 for a common loss rate.
   - The split *does* separate C2 from C3. A bulk invisible decay must be identical in every trap, and candidates.md states that "C3 cannot produce their 2.2 s split". The concession that the loss is not common is therefore what keeps C2 distinct from C3 (see C2-1 point 4 below).

3. **J-PARC gets nothing from C2's agent.** Conceded: C2 supplies 0% of J-PARC's −10.77 s offset. The two-parameter fit gives Δχ² = 4.98, a likelihood ratio of 12 : 1 against C2 (4.5–5 : 1 with inflated errors), and a best-fit τ of 886.0 s.
   - Partial rebuttal: the needed J-PARC artefact has a candidate agent pointing the right way. An unsubtracted gas-borne γ background of 1.23% of S_β would be enough, and that is 0.3–0.5 of the background excess J-PARC observed over its MC prediction [M-MECHANIST-07, verified; D-25].
   - J-PARC's four run conditions disagree (χ² 15.8/3) [D-24]. With its error inflated it sits 1.74σ from BL1 [M-DIALECTICIAN-01].
   - This makes the extra cost smaller. It does not remove it: C2 still needs one more independent error than C1 does.

4. **Corrections accepted.** 38% weaker holds for Gravitrap alone; against the material-class mean the figure is 22–23%. The gas shortfall is 56× at 3σ, or ≈ 600σ in pressure. The best-fit τ is 886.0 s once J-PARC is included.

### C2-1 (evidence)

1. **Two storage-free determinations side with storage.** Conceded that they combine to 878.65 ± 0.82 s, which is 3.78σ from BL1 [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C2-1_evidence.py].
   - Rebuttal on symmetry: the λ cost is not specific to C2. PERKEO III and aSPECT 2024 differ by 3.49σ [crux calc §5; M-STATISTICIAN-10], so one λ family is wrong whatever the answer turns out to be.
     - C1 needs aSPECT's a coefficient wrong. C1's own entry concedes it "fails the SM route at 3.2σ" if the a route is right.
     - C2 needs the A route wrong.
   - What is *not* symmetric, conceded:
     - the A route rests on three mutually consistent experiments (PERKEO III, UCNA, PERKEO II; PDG A-only CL 0.261 [D-15]), and the a route on essentially one (aSPECT);
     - C2 also needs J-PARC wrong.
   - With a PDG-style scale factor on both λ families, the SM route is only 2.64σ from BL1, and J-PARC plus that route 2.94σ [falsifier-C2-1 log].

2. **In-situ tests are null, at 49–195× below the need.** Conceded. Against Musedinovic 2025's table the ratios are 140× (depolarisation), 140× (heating), 195× (gas) and 49× (whole systematic). This is the same concession as C2-0 point 1. The degeneracy defence still stands: every one of these lines covers a *named* channel.

3. **The wrong direction and the historical sign.**
   - Serebrov & Fomin's quote reads "about −6 s". Conceded: C2's "evidence for" dropped the sign. The one documented, quantified storage systematic made results read *long*, so that item moves to evidence *against* C2.
   - The claim that walled traps "should read no longer than magnetic ones" is partly rebutted. A loss that is equal in both trap types shortens both by the same amount. The 2.2 s excess of material over magnetic traps would then be a second-order effect in the walled traps: a residual wall or extrapolation bias that the material bottles carry under C1 as well.
   - So this split is neutral between C1 and C2. C2-2 grants the same point: "This split does not by itself favour C1 over C2". It counts against C2 only if one insists that a single agent produces the whole of both offsets.

4. **A common agent is C3.** Conceded as a dichotomy, and C2 is narrowed to take the apparatus-dependent side of it. What C2 claims is a loss confined to UCN held in a trap, with a size that depends on the trap. It is not a disappearance of free neutrons. That makes C2 a systematic in the brief's sense, but one with no mechanism named: the price is the one conceded under C2-0 point 1.

### C2-2 (bounds)

1. **Storage-free data exclude 888 s unless λ comes from the a route.** Conceded as stated, with the full table:

   | λ route | Proton beam vs J-PARC ⊕ SM | C1 : C2 likelihood ratio |
   |---|---|---|
   | A route | 4.23σ | 7.1×10³ : 1 |
   | PDG λ | 3.38σ | 244 : 1 |
   | a route | 1.22σ | 0.14 : 1 (C2 favoured) |

   Source: [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C2-2_bounds.py].
   - C2's live status is therefore conditional on the λ split. Its strongest single external support is the a route.
   - C2 needs |λ| = 1.26821, which is 14.7σ from PERKEO III on PERKEO III's own error.
2. **"Four independent failures".** Partly rebutted. The λ failure has a counterpart in C1 (aSPECT wrong), and the storage-internal material–magnetic split is shared by every single-cause candidate. Net, C2 needs one failure more than C1: the J-PARC artefact. Its hidden storage effect is also larger relative to budget (≈ 50× against 4.4×). Conceded.
3. **The only channel open in both trap types is gas, and gas is 90× short.** Conceded. C2 is coherent only as two or more trap-specific agents whose rates happen to be similar, or as B-pop (a shared stored-population effect, by analogy with the orthopositronium thermalisation error). Neither has a quantified mechanism.

**Net position.** C2 survives only as a conditional, low-prior hypothesis. It depends on (a) the a-route λ, (b) the constant-loss degeneracy and (c) J-PARC being wrong at ≈ 2.2σ. Every one of its predictions is sharp, and near-term experiments can falsify it at ≥ 5σ.

## Deciding observation

Separations are |prediction A − prediction B| / σ_measurement. They leave out the ±2.04 s uncertainty of the beam pole itself; M-STATISTICIAN-14 includes the pole error and gets 6.2σ for UCNProBe at 1.5 s [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/crux-C2_deciding_tests.py].

**Primary test for all rivals: UCNProBe** (LANL, Krivos and Morris; in-bottle β counting; 1–2 s target, a search-summary figure; no date) [D-106].
- It measures the absolute β-decay rate of the stored UCN, R_β/(ε·N), in the same trap whose storage lifetime it also measures.
- C2 alone among the survivors predicts a gap τ_β − τ_storage ≈ +9.65 s.
- The separation is 9.6σ at 1.0 s, 6.4σ at 1.5 s and 4.8σ at 2.0 s. A 5σ separation needs σ ≤ 1.93 s. Reaching 1.5 s needs the absolute β efficiency times the UCN-count normalisation known to 0.17%.
- It is the one test that goes straight at C2's unnamed loss: that loss is invisible to storage curves (degeneracy), but it cannot touch the β rate per stored neutron.

- **Against C1 (proton-beam systematic).**
  - UCNProBe: C1 predicts β rate = storage rate ≈ 878 s. C2 predicts ≈ 888 s against ≈ 878 s. A gap consistent with 0 at ≤ 2 s excludes C2 at ≥ 4.8σ; a gap of ≈ 10 s excludes C1 equally hard.
  - Runner-up: BL2 (NIST/Tulane; < 1 s; "next few years", search-summary [D-100]). C1 under A-BL1 predicts 878 s against C2's 888 s, which is ≈ 10σ at 1 s. BL2 does not decide against C1 under A-gen, because a still-unfound generic proton-trap effect would make BL2 read 888 s too. That is why UCNProBe is the primary test.
  - Nab (λ at 0.04%) decides the λ condition. An A-like |λ| ≈ 1.276 pushes C2 to ≥ 4σ on storage-free data alone [C2-2].

- **Against C3 (invisible dark decay).** UCNProBe, LiNA, BL2 and Nab all predict the same for C2 and C3 (888 s τ_β, 10 s in-trap gap, aSPECT-like λ). The distinguishing observable is whether the in-trap gap between β rate and storage rate **depends on the apparatus**.
  - C3 is a bulk decay, so it predicts an identical gap, τ_storage − τ_β = −9.65 s (1.24×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹), in every trap and under every trap condition.
  - C2 predicts a gap that depends on the trap: ≈ −7.9 s in material bottles against ≈ −10.2 s in magnetic traps, a 2.21 s difference. It should also vary with some trap parameter (UCN spectrum, field, temperature or wall).
  - Test: in-trap decay-product counting in two trap types. Candidates are UCNProBe and PENeLOPE, the magnetic trap with proton detection (TUM → TRIUMF; no data, no date [D-104]); which trap type UCNProBe uses is not stated in the dossier.
  - Needed precision: ≤ 0.78 s per trap to resolve the 2.21 s trap dependence at 2σ, and ≤ 0.52 s for 3σ. This is beyond UCNProBe's 1–2 s target, so this crux is **not resolvable soon**.
  - A cheaper partial test: a trap-condition scan of one in-trap gap. A dependence favours C2; exact constancy favours C3.
  - Secondary evidence: C3-specific constraints that do not touch C2, namely neutron-star bounds and a Fierz term b ≈ −0.016 in Nab under C3-b. C2 predicts b ≈ 0.

- **Against C5 (electron-always X⁺ branch).** LiNA (J-PARC, Mishima; ~1 s; commissioned Feb 2024; no result date) [D-102].
  - C5 predicts ≈ 878 s (every decay yields an electron). C2 predicts ≈ 888 s (the true lifetime, with no storage involved).
  - That is 9.6σ at 1 s and 4.8σ at 2 s.
  - UCNProBe decides the same way: C5 predicts a gap of 0, C2 a gap of ≈ 10 s.

- **Against C6 (n\*, a rate that depends on the neutron's history).** UCNProBe.
  - Stored UCN have de-excited, so C6 predicts β rate = storage ≈ 878 s (a slate inference, not computed). C2 predicts a ≈ 10 s gap.
  - Backup: a beam lifetime measured against flight time since production (pulsed or velocity-selected beam at LiNA or BL2/BL3). C2 predicts no flight-time dependence; C6 predicts one. Precision and timing have not been computed [D-93].

- **Against C7 (null).** UCNProBe.
  - C7 predicts β rate ≈ storage at 878–882 s, a gap of ≲ 4 s with no class pattern. C2 predicts ≈ 10 s.
  - At 1.5 s precision, 10 s against ≤ 4 s is ≥ 4σ.
  - BL2 is weaker here: at 1 s, 888 s against C7's 880–886 s separates by only 2.0–8.0σ, depending on where in C7's range the value falls.

- **Against C8 (a proton-detection cause).** LiNA.
  - C8 R-strong predicts ≈ 878 s. C2 predicts ≈ 888 s: 9.6σ at 1 s.
  - Nab does **not** separate the two: both need an aSPECT-like a ≈ −0.1040.
  - UCNProBe decides it the same way as LiNA: C8 predicts a gap of 0, C2 a gap of ≈ 10 s.

**Single observation that would kill C2 across the board:** UCNProBe finding an in-trap β rate equal to its storage rate within ≈ 2 s. Near-equivalently, LiNA reading ≈ 878 s at ~1 s, which would add a second storage-free electron-counting determination to J-PARC. A PERKEO-like Nab λ would then close off the last route by which C2 is favoured.
