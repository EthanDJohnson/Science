Units: SI (lifetimes and shifts in s); λ and a are dimensionless. Calculation for this crux: [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/crux-C7_discriminators.py] (log beside it).

**The defended claim, narrowed.** I accept the core the three verdicts converge on. The ≈ 10 s gap sits on the proton-counting side: BL1, with Sussex–ILL. It comes from BL1's total error being underestimated by about 2× (several modest items with the same sign, none ≥ 1/3 of the gap) plus a statistical/heavy-tail excursion. Sussex–ILL has its own ≈ 2σ high fluctuation. No single identifiable effect of ≈ 10 s exists, and the true lifetime is ≈ 878 s. J-PARC and the material bottles are separate, smaller tensions and are no longer part of this candidate's account of the gap.

## Response to the verdicts

**1. "J-PARC contributes 0 s to the gap" (C7-0 §1, C7-1 §1, C7-2 §1). Conceded in full.** The reference gap is proton-counting beam minus storage, and J-PARC is in neither mean, so d(gap)/d(J-PARC) = 0 exactly. In the all-beam reading, inflating J-PARC's error raises the gap from 7.56 s to 9.18 s (C7-2). J-PARC's internal χ² of 15.8/3 [D-24] is a real tension, but a separate one. I drop J-PARC from the list of contributors.

**2. "The material bottles have the wrong sign" (C7-0 §2, C7-1 §1, C7-2 §2). Conceded in full.** C7's own "+2 s" on the material bottles lowers the storage mean, so correcting it widens the gap by 0.45–0.46 s (to 10.10 s, 4.92σ, per C7-1). The material-against-magnetic split (2.95σ scaled, M-STATISTICIAN-09) stays in the null, but as a second, independent tension, not as a share of this gap. The old line "Shift by class: material +2 s" was an error in the make-up of the gap.

**3. "One locus dominates: BL1 carries 80% by weight and 95.7–98.7% of the least-squares allocation" (C7-0 §4–5, C7-1 §2). Conceded as a statement about *location*; rebutted as a refutation of the null.** The brief defines the null as "several smaller effects or underestimated uncertainties, none of which dominates" (candidates.md, schema). That is a statement about *causes*, not about which experiment holds the error. C7-1 accepts this reading explicitly ("several modest BL1 items meet that definition at the level of effects"). What C7 must show is that BL1's ≈ 9.9 s excess does not need one dominant effect:
- *No named effect is large enough.* Caylor's worst case for H₂ charge exchange is < 0.5 s [D-17; C1 evidence against]. The shift is 4.4× BL1's total error, 5.2× its systematic and 5.8× its largest un-remeasured line (1.7 s), and no itemised entry has room for it at < 4σ (M-CONSTRAINTS-12, via C1). The named rivals would have to be 20–25× their budgets (A3) or wrong by 2× (A4) (M-MECHANIST-03, M-DECOMPOSER-12, via C1). This is the evidence the null rests on, and none of the three verdicts disputes it.
- *Spreading the excess over items costs nothing in Gaussian terms, and gains nothing.* BL1's budget adds in quadrature to 2.32 s. Pulling fluence (0.5 s), deposit (0.9 s), "unassociated" (1.7 s) and proton statistics (1.2 s) together at k = 2.30 each closes the gap; the least-χ² spread gives a total pull of 4.22σ against the itemised quadrature (4.36σ against the quoted 2.25 s total), the same as one item [calc: crux-C7_discriminators.py §3]. So the null is not "four independent 2.3σ coincidences" but one statement: *BL1's total error is about 2× too small*. Doubling it puts BL1 2.19σ from UCNτ (one-sided p = 0.014); at 2.3× it is 1.91σ (p = 0.028) [same calc; M-DIALECTICIAN-08 verified]. The field's own scale factors are 1.43–2.29 (M-STATISTICIAN-01, -05).

**4. "Diffuse, class-blind inflation reproduces the between/within pattern only at p ≈ 1.3×10⁻³, and in heavy-tail draws a single experiment carries ≥ 50% of χ² in 85–90% of gap-producing draws" (C7-1 §3). Conceded against the class-blind form; this *supports* the narrowed form.** In Bailey's heavy-tail model, a gap like this one usually comes from one outlying experiment. That is the narrowed null: BL1 as the tail event. C7-0 finds that with 11 results the chance of some result having BL1's pull of ≈ 4.26 is 0.14–0.43 (ν = 4–2). A mixed-up list of contributors across classes is dead. A single BL1 outlier with no single physical cause is not.

**5. "Under C7's own numbers the residual fluctuation is 2.3–3.7σ" (C7-0 §3, C7-2 §3). Partly conceded.** With BL1 corrected by 3–6 s, 4.7–7.6 s remains. On the unscaled BL1 error that is 2.3–3.7σ. That is exactly why the narrowed null must add error underestimation (2× gives 2.19σ for the whole excess, §3 above). The "BL1 +3 to +6 s" figure in the old shift table is withdrawn. The narrowed null claims the whole ≈ 9.9 s as (under-budgeted systematics + fluctuation) in BL1, with no identified single item.

**6. "Sussex–ILL must co-fluctuate, and 'either SM route' contradicts 'magnetic ≈ 0': the a-route τ_β (aSPECT) must also be off by ≈ 3.2σ in the same direction" (C7-1 narrowed, C7-2 §5–6). Conceded; this is the real price of the null.**
- The prediction-matrix entry "either route; no class pattern" is withdrawn. The narrowed null needs the A route (PERKEO III λ) to be right and aSPECT to have its own excursion of ≈ 3.2σ.
- Under unit-scale Student-t with ν = 2–4, BL1 (4.26), Sussex (2.3) and aSPECT (3.17) all high together, independently, has one-sided probability 4.6×10⁻⁶ to 8.2×10⁻⁵ before any look-elsewhere correction; 8.4×10⁻¹¹ if Gaussian. BL1 + Sussex alone: 2.7×10⁻⁴ to 1.9×10⁻³ [calc: crux-C7_discriminators.py §4; matches C7-0 §What survives]. Like C7-2's numbers, these are post-selected: I picked the three high results.
- *Partial rebuttal:* aSPECT against PERKEO III is also a λ tension that *every* candidate except C8 R-strong must absorb. C1 needs the same aSPECT excursion (candidates.md, C1 evidence against, 3.2σ). So this cost counts against C7 *relative to C8*, not relative to C1. The coincidence of three proton-detecting results all reading high is the strongest point against the null. It is the one on which C8 rather than C1 would win.

**7. "The BL2/BL3 prediction '880–886 s' does not follow from a BL1-specific fluctuation" (C7-0 Corrections, C7-1 Corrections, C7-2 Corrections). Conceded.** If the excess is under-budgeted BL1 systematics plus a fluctuation, an independent new apparatus should read near storage, ≈ 878–880 s. An intermediate reading would mean a *method-generic* bias. Under the narrowed null the corrected predictions are:
- BL2/BL3 read 878–881 s, *and* no single systematic scan in BL2/BL3 (trap time, residual-gas pressure, trap field, flux-monitor recalibration, trap length) reveals an effect worth ≥ 1/3 of the BL1 gap (≥ 3.3 s scaled to BL1 conditions);
- LiNA ≈ 878 s;
- UCNProBe β-rate lifetime = its storage lifetime;
- Nab PERKEO-like;
- no flight-time dependence.

**8. "The narrowed null is hard to tell from C1's A-BL1" (all three verdicts). Conceded as largely true; the remaining difference is testable, but only weakly.** C1 says one effect (A1–A4) carries the gap. C7 says no single effect does. When BL2/BL3 read ≈ 878 s they agree, so the difference shows up only in whether one scan or one re-examination of BL1 finds a lever worth ≥ 2/3 of the gap (≥ 6.6 s). See the deciding observation below.

**Net.** The cross-class, "spread over BL1, J-PARC and material bottles" form is lost. What survives is a proton-side null: BL1's error is underestimated about 2× through several modest same-sign items, plus a fluctuation, with no single cause. It must pay for a Sussex co-fluctuation and an independent aSPECT excursion.

## Deciding observation

Precisions come from [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/crux-C7_discriminators.py]. Poles: storage 878.32 ± 0.43 s, proton-counting 887.97 ± 2.04 s (M-STATISTICIAN-02).

- **vs C1 (single proton-beam systematic).** *The size of the largest single effect found in the BL2/BL3 systematic scans, or in a re-examination of BL1.*
  - Observation: trap-time (5/10 ms, and longer), residual-gas pressure, trap-field and ⁶Li/Alpha-Gamma flux-monitor recalibration scans, each converted into a BL1-equivalent shift.
  - C1 predicts one lever ≥ 6.6 s: for example A2's 5 ms against 10 ms difference of ≈ 5.1 s (≈ 6.8 s for an equal mix), or A1's 1.16% monitor efficiency.
  - C7 predicts every lever ≤ 3.3 s, with the identified items together short of the gap.
  - Precision: the 3.3 s separation needs a scan-difference error ≲ 1.1 s for 3σ (1.0 s gives 3.3σ). BL2's < 1 s goal suffices [D-100, search-summary], "next few years", facility not named. BL3 (0.3 s) has no date [D-101].
  - The cheap test in existing data is weak. BL1's own 5 ms against 10 ms split has a difference error of ≈ 2.4 s, so C1-A2's 5.1 s would appear at only 2.1σ.
  - Caveat: if BL2 reads ≈ 878 s as built and no lever is found, C1's A-BL1 and C7 stay tied. C7 then wins only by default (no ≈ 10 s effect is ever identified).
- **vs C2 (common storage loss) and C3 (invisible dark decay).** *UCNProBe's β-rate lifetime minus its own storage lifetime in the same trap* (LANL, Krivos and Morris; 1–2 s target, search-summary; no date) [D-106].
  - C2/C3 predict +9.65 s; C7 predicts 0.
  - The separation is 6.4σ at a 1.5 s difference error, 4.8σ at 2.0 s and 3.9σ at 2.5 s.
  - Co-decisive against C3: LiNA (J-PARC, ~1 s, commissioned Feb 2024, no result date) [D-102]. A reading of 878 ± 1 s is 0.3σ from storage and 4.4σ from the proton pole.
- **vs C5 (electron-always X⁺ branch).** *BL2/BL3 read ≈ 888 s against ≈ 878 s*, plus Nab.
  - C5 predicts BL2 ≈ 888 s with no dependence on field, gas or trap time, and an aSPECT-like λ. C7 predicts BL2 at 878–881 s and a PERKEO-like λ.
  - A BL2 reading of 878.3 ± 1 s is 4.3σ from the proton pole; with BL3 at 0.3 s it is 4.7σ. The ceiling comes from BL1's own error.
  - Nab (ORNL SNS, Δλ/|λ| ≈ 0.04%, no a value yet) [D-107]: a PERKEO-like result is 3.5σ from aSPECT, an aSPECT-like one 12.7σ from the A route.
- **vs C6 (excited neutron n\*).** *The beam lifetime against flight time since production* (a pulsed or velocity-selected beam: J-PARC/LiNA or BL2/BL3 with different flight paths).
  - C6 predicts a dependence; C7 predicts none.
  - Required precision and dates: not computed. The n* de-excitation time is not constrained in the dossier; Blatnik's and Nab's exclusion regions were not read [D-93].
- **vs C8 (proton-detection link, R-strong).** *Nab's a coefficient, read together with LiNA or UCNProBe* [D-107, D-102, D-106]. This is the sharpest crux for C7, because C8 turns the null's three-way coincidence (BL1, Sussex and aSPECT all high) into one cause.
  - C8 R-strong predicts an aSPECT-like Nab (a ≈ −0.1040) with LiNA ≈ 878 s.
  - C7 predicts a PERKEO-like Nab (a ≈ −0.1069). The aSPECT excursion is then just the ≈ 3.2σ fluctuation the null already paid for.
  - At 0.04%, an aSPECT-like Nab sits 12.7σ from the A route and would refute the narrowed null's reading of aSPECT as a fluctuation. A PERKEO-like Nab sits 3.5σ from aSPECT and collapses C8 to R-weak, which is C1-A-BL1 or C7.
  - Nab was upgraded for precision running in Nov 2025; no date for an a value.
