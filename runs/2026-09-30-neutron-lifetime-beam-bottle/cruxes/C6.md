# Crux: C6

status: final

Units: SI (times and lifetimes in s or ms). λ, a, A, branching fractions and n* fractions are dimensionless. Gap reference: BL1 887.7 s against UCNτ 877.82 s, 9.88 s [D-20, D-27].

All three verdicts return **weakened**, and they agree on what survives. I accept their narrowing and defend only the narrowed claim:

> **C6 (narrowed).** An excited neutron n* (Koch–Hummel [D-97]) has a longer β lifetime and de-excites with 0.02 s ≪ τγ < 139 s [Blatnik, via C6-1/C6-2]. It makes up ≥ 1.11% of neutrons at BL1's decay volume, and 1.2–1.7% for Koch–Hummel-like states [C6-0, C6-1]. There are two forms:
> - **C6-eq:** the n* fraction at birth is the same for every source. C6-eq predicts that J-PARC/LiNA read ≈ 888 s or longer.
> - **C6-fis:** n* is made in reactor fission and not in spallation. C6-fis predicts that J-PARC/LiNA read ≈ 878 s.
>
> In both forms the SM A route (τ_β ≈ 878.5 s) and the bottles give the true lifetime.

## Response to the verdicts

**1. The field-dependence variant is eliminated (C6-0 §1, C6-1 §2, C6-2 §4).** Conceded in full. The shortfall is 5.9–6.4×10⁹ with the Q⁵ lever [M-MECHANIST-13; calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C6-0_magnitude.py]. The heading's "or a field dependence" clause is dropped. Any strong-field effect beyond this belongs to C4.

**2. Under equal production, J-PARC must read ≥ 887.8 s (C6-0 §2, C6-1 §1, C6-2 §2). This is the main attack.** I concede the physics and dispute only its weight.
- **Concession.** J-PARC's decaying neutrons are 10–40 ms old against BL1's 47–155 ms, a mean of 94.5 ms [C6-0; C6-1 gives 16–40 ms against 74–84 ms]. Under C6-eq, J-PARC therefore reads at least as long as BL1. I re-derived the prediction [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/crux-C6_tests.py, §1]:

  | τγ | Predicted J-PARC | Tension with J-PARC (quoted +4.35 s error) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 139 s | 887.70 s | 2.41σ |
  | 1 s | 888.41 s | 2.58σ |
  | 0.3 s | 890.28 s | 3.01σ |
  | 0.1 s | 897.62 s | 4.69σ |

  These reproduce C6-0's table to within 0.2 s. For C6-eq the τγ window therefore shrinks to about 0.3 s ≲ τγ < 139 s. Below that, J-PARC excludes it at ≥ 3σ.
- **Rebuttal on weight.** The tension is 2.2–2.6σ on J-PARC's quoted errors [C6-0, C6-1, C6-2], and about 1σ once J-PARC's own scatter is used as a scale factor, S = 2.29 from χ² = 15.8/3 [D-24; M-STATISTICIAN-05]. J-PARC is also an unrefereed preprint [D-23]. All three refuters say this is not a ≥ 3σ exclusion. C6-eq stands exactly where C3 stands on J-PARC, and C3 survives on the same footing.
- **Point on the likelihood ratio.** C6-2 gives ≈ 21 : 1 in favour of the storage value, from J-PARC's point likelihood. That odds figure uses the quoted, unscaled error, so it is an upper bound. The slate's inflated figure for the same comparison is 4.5–5 : 1 [M-STATISTICIAN-06, as quoted in candidates.md for C3].

**3. The τγ ≈ t_beam escape is impossible (C6-2 §3).** Conceded. My calculation agrees in direction [calc: crux-C6_tests.py, §1]:
- At τγ = 0.05 s the needed n* fraction at birth rises to 7.35%.
- BL1's fastest and slowest deciles would then differ by 22.6 s, a velocity dependence that BL1's single-lifetime fit does not show.

The escape from J-PARC does not run through τγ.

**4. The fission-only escape is ad hoc (C6-1 (b), C6-2).** Partly conceded, partly rebutted.
- **Conceded.** Nothing in Koch–Hummel motivates a source dependence, and it adds a free parameter.
- **Rebutted.** It is not excluded, and it is not invented to fit here:
  - Koch–Hummel leave the initial abundance free [C6-1 (b)].
  - Blatnik's τγ < 139 s bound itself assumes fractions "constrained ... to be equal, independent of production mechanism" [Blatnik quote in C6-2]. LANL's UCN come from spallation [C6-2], so under C6-fis the UCNτ bound does not constrain reactor n* at all.
  - Every proton-counting beam that reads long (BL1, Sussex–ILL) is a reactor beam [C6-1, C6-2].
- **Cost.** C6-fis predicts nothing a pure source split does not also give: BL2/BL3 ≈ 888 s, LiNA ≈ 878 s and UCNProBe ≈ 878 s. That is C5's pattern and C1-A-gen's pattern before any correction. Its SM prediction differs from C5's (A route, not a route). I record C6-fis as a live sub-hypothesis of lower prior, not as the main form.

**5. Koch–Hummel's eq. 10 drops τe/τg, so the needed abundance is 1.74%, not 5.5×10⁻³ (C6-0, C6-1).** Accepted as a correction. The model-independent floor is 1.11% (Γe → 0). A larger n* population would have to escape γ searches, but the dossier gives no γ-search limit for ΔE ≲ 21 keV photons, so I cannot turn this into a bound either way.

**6. The storage side is now bounded: τγ < 139 s at 95% CL (Blatnik), and ΔE ≲ 21 keV (Nab) (C6-1 §3, C6-2).** Accepted: these fill the slate's "region size not read" gap. On scope I side with C6-2. Nab's mass bound applies to an n*-dominated sample ("t_tr ≪ τγ", "almost pure excited neutrons"), so it limits ΔE for such a sample and does not directly limit a ~1% sub-population [C6-2's quote]. Nab itself states that the discrepancy "can be explained by excited neutrons for values of τγ between the vertical grey bands" [C6-1's quote]. The window is therefore narrowed, not closed.

**7. SM A route: PERKEO III is itself a young cold beam (C6-1, table).** This is a real gap, which I size here [calc: crux-C6_tests.py, §3].
- The n* share of decays is (f/τe)/((1 − f)/τg + f/τe):
  - 0.633% for Koch–Hummel's τe/τg = 25/9 state at f = 1.74%;
  - 0.047% at τe/τg = 25;
  - → 0 as Γe → 0.
- To keep PERKEO III's A within its 1σ of 0.00021 [D-40], the 25/9 state needs |A_e − A_g| < 0.033, i.e. within 27% of |A0|. Longer-lived n* need no condition.
- This is an untested condition, not a contradiction. It is weakest for short-τe states and vanishes for β-stable n*.

**8. "Only one lens supports C6."** Accepted as an appraisal. It is not evidence against the physics.

**Net.**
- C6-eq's only quantified conflict is J-PARC at about 2.4σ (about 1σ scaled). Its SM, storage and direct-search constraints are all satisfied, provided n* shares A.
- C6-fis has no quantified conflict, but it adds a free source dependence.

## Deciding observation

**Single deciding test against the whole slate: LiNA (J-PARC, Mishima; ~1 s; background expected to fall ×50; commissioned Feb 2024; no result date [D-102]) read together with Nab's a (ORNL SNS; Δλ/|λ| ≈ 0.04%; upgraded Nov 2025; no a value yet [D-107]).**
- C6-eq is the only candidate that predicts LiNA ≈ 888 s *together with* a PERKEO-like Nab (|λ| ≈ 1.2764).
- At 1 s, LiNA separates 888 s from 878 s by 9.9σ of its own error [calc: crux-C6_tests.py, §2].
- Nab at 0.04% separates PERKEO-like from aSPECT-like λ by 18.9σ on Nab's error alone, 12.7σ with PERKEO III's error added (matches M-CONSTRAINTS-05) [calc: crux-C6_tests.py, §4].

Against each rival:

- **C1 (proton-beam systematic).**
  - *Observable:* LiNA at ~1 s [D-102].
  - *Predictions:* C6-eq predicts ≥ 887.7 s. C1 predicts ≈ 878 s at every pressure.
  - *Outcomes:* a reading ≈ 888 s favours C6-eq; ≈ 878 s favours C1 or C6-fis.
  - *Against C6-fis:* the test is BL2/BL3 at NIST (< 1 s / 0.3 s; "next few years" / no date [D-100, D-101]) with its internal scans.
    - C6-fis predicts ≈ 887.7 s, with no dependence on trap length, trapping time, residual gas or the recalibrated flux monitor.
    - C1 predicts either ≈ 878 s (A-BL1), or a trap-time slope of ≈ 5 s per 5 ms under A2 [M-MECHANIST-04].
    - A BL2 reading of 888 s with every C1 diagnostic null favours C6-fis over C1. The residual degeneracy with C1-A-gen ("an unfound effect") is broken only by a proton-counting beam at a spallation source, or an electron-counting beam at a reactor. Under C6-fis the source type, not the method, sets the value. The dossier lists no such experiment.
- **C2 (common storage loss).**
  - *Observable:* Nab's a [D-107].
  - *Predictions:* C2 needs τ_β ≈ 888 s, so |λ| ≈ 1.2668. C6 needs the A route, |λ| ≈ 1.2764.
  - *Outcomes:* 12.7σ separation at the 0.04% goal [calc §4; M-CONSTRAINTS-05]. A PERKEO-like result favours C6.
  - *Runner-up:* UCNProBe (1–2 s, search-summary; no date [D-106]). C2 predicts β rate 888 s against storage 878 s in one trap; C6 predicts both ≈ 878 s, because n* has de-excited within τγ < 139 s of a hundreds-of-seconds storage time.
- **C3 (invisible dark decay).**
  - *Observable:* Nab's a, as for C2. C3 needs an aSPECT-like λ, or b ≈ −0.016 [M-CONSTRAINTS-06]. C6 needs PERKEO-like λ and b ≈ 0.
  - *Why LiNA does not decide it:* LiNA cannot separate C3 from C6-eq, since both predict ≈ 888 s. It separates C3 from C6-fis at 9.9σ.
  - *Runner-up:* UCNProBe. C3 predicts β-rate 888 s against storage 878 s; C6 predicts both 878 s.
- **C5 (electron-always X⁺ branch).**
  - *Observable:* Nab's a.
  - *Why the lifetimes do not decide it:* C6-fis and C5 predict the same lifetime pattern (BL2 888 s, LiNA 878 s, UCNProBe 878 s).
  - *Predictions:* C5 needs Γ(p e ν̄) = 1/887.7 s⁻¹, i.e. aSPECT-like λ. C6 needs PERKEO-like λ.
  - *Outcomes:* 12.7σ separation at the Nab goal. A PERKEO-like Nab excludes C5 at ≈ 3.8σ on the SM route alone [M-CONSTRAINTS-05] and leaves C6 intact.
- **C7 (null, no dominant cause).**
  - *Observable:* BL2 at < 1 s [D-100].
  - *Predictions:* C6 predicts that BL2 reproduces BL1's ≈ 887.7 s, if BL2 runs on a similar reactor cold beam and τγ ≫ 0.1 s, where the age dependence is < 1.1 s [calc §1]. C7 predicts an intermediate value (880–886 s) with no trend.
  - *Outcomes:* against C7's 883 s midpoint the separation is 4.7σ at 1 s [calc §2]. A reading of 888 s favours C6; 880–886 s favours C7.
- **C8 (proton-detecting versus rest).**
  - *Observable:* Nab's a.
  - *Predictions:* R-strong predicts an aSPECT-like a ≈ −0.1040. C6 predicts a ≈ −0.1069.
  - *Outcomes:* a PERKEO-like Nab kills R-strong. C8 then collapses to R-weak (= C1), and the C1 test above takes over.

**C6's own diagnostic (to confirm it, not to separate it).** A velocity/TOF-resolved lifetime in a proton beam is informative only for 0.3 s ≲ τγ ≲ 1 s. There the fastest and slowest BL1 deciles differ by 1.1–3.5 s, so it needs subset errors ≲ 0.5 s. For τγ ≥ 3 s the age signature is < 0.35 s, and no beam can see it [calc §1]. Blatnik's exclusion redone without the equal-production constraint, e.g. with reactor-produced UCN, would test C6-fis directly. That test is not computed or scheduled in the dossier.
