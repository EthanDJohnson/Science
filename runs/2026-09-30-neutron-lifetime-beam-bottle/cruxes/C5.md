## Response to the verdicts

All three refuters return **weakened**, and they converge on the same narrowing. I concede that narrowing in full. What I defend is the phenomenological core, restated here, not the preprint's model.

**Surviving C5 (C5-narrow).** A ~1.1% branch n → X⁺ e⁻ ν̄, followed by X⁺ → e⁺ + (dark final state) with τ_X ≲ 0.5–1 ms. The dark final state has total mass ≳ 936.9–937.9 MeV: either 2A⁰ with m_A ≈ 468.2/468.4–469.3 MeV (C5-0, C5-1), or a single Y⁰ with m_Y ≈ 936.9–938.5 MeV (C5-2). Together with the a-route λ. Every decay yields an electron; ~1.1% yield no proton that survives to BL1's detector.

### C5-0 (magnitude)
1. **"The X⁺ looks like a proton to BL1; a stable or long-lived X⁺ is counted (≤ 1.60 s of 9.88 s)."** Conceded. The candidate silently assumed X⁺ is not counted; the q/m argument (within 0.084% of the proton) and the +800 eV well (Nico 2005, as quoted) make that false unless X⁺ decays before extraction. C5 therefore requires τ_X ≲ 0.5 ms for ≥ 90% of the gap. My own check of the counted fraction (uniform production over a trapping period T, exponential decay) agrees: τ_X = 0.5 ms leaves 10% (T = 5 ms) or 5% (T = 10 ms) counted, so the beam shift is 8.89 s or 9.39 s rather than 9.88 s; τ_X = 5 ms leaves only 3.63–5.61 s [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/crux-C5_tests.py, part 5].
2. **"A short-lived X⁺ makes ordinary nuclei decay via off-shell X⁺*."** Conceded for the preprint's m_A = 370–460 MeV. I have no rebuttal: the locality assumption (the n–X–eν vertex acts on bound neutrons) is the standard one and the preprint itself calls the process "analogous to beta-decay". The margin (8.7–16 orders of magnitude against 10³²-yr sensitivity; still ≳ 0.17 s needed at a very weak 10²⁵ yr) is far beyond any form-factor or spectroscopic-factor escape. The preprint's own realisation of C5 is dead.
3. **Escape sliver m_A ≈ 468.2–469.3 MeV.** Accepted as the only surviving form of the two-A⁰ chain. I also concede the three costs C5-0 lists: the positron is soft (total kinetic energy ≤ 1.665 MeV [calc part 4]), not "energetic"; the sliver lies outside the preprint's neutron-star-viable 390–460 MeV (370–400 MeV strict) range, so no neutron-star-compatible model in the sliver has been shown; and it is not the preprint's model.
4. **Correction: the weak link is τ_X, not "the unseen X⁺".** Accepted. The binding constraints are the X⁺ lifetime window and nuclear stability, and the candidate's matrix should have carried nucleon-decay searches.

### C5-1 (evidence)
1. **Fornal–Grinstein ⁹Be and proton-stability conditions violated by 17–197 MeV.** Conceded; same conclusion as C5-0 by an independent route (¹³⁰Te off-shell rate against the mode-independent 1.6×10²⁵ yr geochemical limit). The two refuters' windows differ slightly (C5-0: m_A > 468.16 MeV from ²H, 468.49 MeV from ⁹Be; C5-1: 468.44 MeV from M_f > 937.900 MeV); I adopt the stricter ⁹Be-based 468.44/468.49 MeV lower edge.
2. **Preprint's J-PARC hiding mechanism ("high energy … less visible track") fails in the window.** Conceded. In C5-narrow the positron is MeV-scale and electron-like.
3. **Correction to the prediction "charged X⁺ tracks appear in the TPC".** Conceded: an X⁺ carries only ~0.1–2.4 keV recoil (751 eV at m_X = m_p) and leaves no track. The only extra visible signature is the positron and its 2 × 0.511 MeV annihilation photons [calc part 4]. I withdraw the X⁺-track prediction.
4. **PERKEO II and UCNA do not exclude the window (prompt, fixed-energy pair model).** I accept C5-1's caution but turn it into a concession of vulnerability: with τ_X ≲ 0.5 ms, on any detector timescale longer than that C5-narrow is effectively n → (dark) + e⁺ + e⁻ + ν̄ with a continuous lepton spectrum below 1.665 MeV. That is close to the C-ee channel of C3, which is excluded as dominant for 32–644 keV with a fixed-energy model [D-61, D-86]. A re-fit of the PERKEO II / UCNA e⁺e⁻ coincidence data with a continuous 4-body spectrum at 1.1% has not been done by anyone; I expect it to be sensitive, but I did not compute it. This is C5-narrow's most exposed flank after λ.

### C5-2 (bounds)
1. **Earth heat flow and Borexino ¹³C exclude τ_X ≲ 10 ms for m_D = 370–460 MeV (by 10²–10⁷ and 10⁹–10¹³).** Conceded; a third independent confirmation that the preprint's chain fails.
2. **Only escape: X⁺ → e⁺ + Y⁰ with m_Y ≳ 936.9 MeV, a Fornal–Grinstein-like dark baryon, inheriting the neutron-star problem.** Conceded. C5-narrow then carries C3's neutron-star liability (M_max 0.69–0.70 M☉ for a free dark baryon [M-CONSTRAINTS-09]) unless the Y⁰ is self-repulsive. Note that if Y⁰ carries baryon number the chain conserves it, so "baryon-number-violating" in C5's heading is no longer essential; the heading's defining content is only "electron always, proton ~1.1% absent".
3. **τ_X ceiling ≲ 10 ms (C5-2) against ≲ 0.5 ms (C5-0).** Not a real conflict: 10 ms is where the effect starts to vanish, 0.5 ms is where ≥ 90% of it survives [calc part 5]. I adopt τ_X ≲ 0.5–1 ms.
4. **UCNProBe caveat.** Accepted; it does not change the prediction (β rate = storage rate).

### What remains and what it still costs
- **Rebutted: nothing substantial.** The one thing all three refuters leave standing is the phenomenological niche: C5-narrow is the only candidate that puts J-PARC with the bottles (as observed, 877.2 s, 2.24σ from the proton pole [calc part 2]) *and* makes BL1's 887.7 s the true Γ(n → p e ν̄) with no proton-side systematic.
- **Still costing C5-narrow:** (i) the A-route λ: C5 needs |λ| = 1.2684 (τ_β = 887.7 s), 0.0080 below PERKEO III and 0.6σ from aSPECT 2024's 1.2668(27) [calc part 1]; the A route disfavours it at 3.8σ [M-CONSTRAINTS-03]; (ii) a mass tuned into a ~1 MeV window, with no model built there; (iii) the neutron-star problem of a near-neutron-mass dark state; (iv) the untested e⁺e⁻ coincidence exposure above. My prior for C5-narrow is low; it survives on the data pattern, not on a model.
- **Unexplored self-consistency issue (not computed).** If τ_X is ~0.5 ms, X⁺ probably survives the much shorter proton transit of a recoil-spectrum experiment such as aSPECT (transit time not in the dossier), so aSPECT's "proton" sample would contain ~1.1% X⁺ with its own recoil spectrum. C5 leans on aSPECT's λ as the true SM λ; whether this admixture biases aSPECT's a, and in which direction, is unknown.

## Deciding observation

**Main decider: Nab's a coefficient (ORNL SNS; Δλ/|λ| ≈ 0.04% goal; upgraded for precision running Nov 2025; no a value yet, no date [D-107]).** C5-narrow needs |λ| = 1.2684 (a-route-like). At 0.04% (σ_λ ≈ 0.00051), a PERKEO-like Nab reading excludes C5-narrow at 4.0σ once C5's requirement carries BL1's 2.25 s error (σ = 0.0019 in λ), and the two λ poles alone are 15.7 σ_Nab apart [calc part 1]. This decides C5 against C1, C6 and C7, but not against C2, C3 or C8-R-strong, which also expect an aSPECT-like Nab (caveat: Nab's a uses proton time of flight, so if X⁺ survives Nab's flight time it may be in Nab's sample too; not computed).

Per rival:

- **C1 (proton-beam systematic).** Deciding test: **Nab a** as above. aSPECT-like (|λ| ≈ 1.268) favours C5; PERKEO-like (≈ 1.2764) favours C1 at ~4σ against C5. Runner-up: **BL2** (NIST/Tulane, < 1 s, "next few years" per search-summary [D-100]) or **BL3** (0.3 s, no date [D-101]). C5 predicts ≈ 888 s with no dependence on gas, field or trap time; 888 ± 1 s sits 8.9σ from storage, 878 ± 1 s sits 3.9σ from BL1 (4.3σ at ± 0.3 s) [calc part 3]. A trap-time scan does *not* cleanly separate them: C5 with τ_X = 1–5 ms also predicts a longer lifetime at longer trapping (1.0–2.0 s between 5 and 10 ms), the same sign as C1-A2 [calc part 5]; only τ_X ≲ 0.1 ms gives a flat ≤ 0.1 s.
- **C2 (common storage loss).** Deciding test: **LiNA** (J-PARC electron-counting, ~1 s, commissioned Feb 2024, no result date [D-102]), equally **UCNProBe** (LANL in-bottle β counting, 1–2 s, no date [D-106]). C5 predicts ≈ 878 s (every decay has an electron); C2 predicts ≈ 888 s (LiNA) and β rate 888 s against storage 878 s in one trap (UCNProBe). A 878 ± 1 s LiNA reading sits 4.4σ from the proton pole; 888 ± 1 s sits 8.9σ from storage (6.2σ at ± 1.5 s) [calc part 2].
- **C3 (invisible dark decay).** Deciding test: **LiNA** (or UCNProBe), as for C2: C3 predicts ≈ 888 s (no electron), C5 ≈ 878 s. J-PARC 2024 already favours C5 over C3 at 2.24σ [calc part 2]. Second, independent: a re-fit of **PERKEO II / UCNA e⁺e⁻ coincidence data** (or LiNA's TPC) for a 1.1% excess of e⁺e⁻ pairs with a continuous spectrum below 1.665 MeV plus 511 keV annihilation photons: present under C5-narrow, absent under C3-inv. Who: the PERKEO II (Heidelberg) or UCNA (LANL) groups; the needed sensitivity is a branching well below 1.1%, which PERKEO II reached for fixed-energy pairs [D-61]; sensitivity to the continuous spectrum and a date were not computed.
- **C6 (n\* state).** Deciding test: **Nab a** (C6 keeps the A-route λ, PERKEO-like; C5 needs aSPECT-like), backed by a beam lifetime against flight time (C6 depends on time since production; C5 flat). Precision as above for Nab; flight-time test precision not computed [C6 entry].
- **C7 (null).** Deciding test: **BL2/BL3**: C5 predicts 887.7 ± ~1 s with no identifiable effect *and* LiNA ≈ 878 s; C7 predicts BL2/BL3 at an intermediate 880–886 s. A BL3 reading at 0.3 s separates 888 s from 883 s by 5 s / 0.3 s ≈ 17 of its own σ, but BL1's 2.25 s error still limits any comparison with BL1 to ≈ 4σ [calc part 3]; significance against the full C7 spread not computed.
- **C8 (proton-detecting vs rest, R-strong).** Deciding test: an **electron-only λ measurement** (β asymmetry, e.g. PERC at the 10⁻⁴ level on asymmetries, under construction [D-108]) together with BL2/BL3. C5 says the true λ is aSPECT-like, so a new electron-side A must also give |λ| ≈ 1.268 (PERKEO III wrong); C8-R-strong says the proton-side a is biased and electron-side A stays at 1.2764. Nab alone does not separate them (both expect aSPECT-like, and Nab detects protons). Also: C8-R-strong predicts BL2/BL3 move to ≈ 878 s once the proton-side effect is found; C5 predicts ≈ 888 s permanently.

**Single most decisive observation for C5 overall:** the combination **Nab a ≈ aSPECT-like AND LiNA/UCNProBe ≈ 878 s AND BL2 ≈ 888 s**, which is C5's unique signature; any PERKEO-like Nab result (≥ 4σ at the 0.04% goal) or LiNA ≈ 888 s ends it.
