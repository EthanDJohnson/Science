## Response to the verdicts

All three verdicts return **survives** with nothing narrowed. Taken together they move C1 from "one of several named proton-side effects" to "an effect outside every channel measured in situ". I concede most of that loss. Point by point:

### C1-0 (magnitude)
1. **The linear stack of every itemised line gives 4.6 s, 48% of the gap.** Conceded. No itemised effect reaches the 9.65 s gap. C1's causal link therefore cannot be tested today, only its outcome (what BL2/BL3, LiNA, UCNProBe and Nab read). This is the heading's own word, "unidentified", but it is a real cost: C1 has a pattern of evidence and no mechanism.
2. **A1 faces a stronger constraint than the 0.5 s Alpha-Gamma uncertainty.** Conceded. The 2005 areal-density calibration and the 2013 Alpha-Gamma calibration differ by 0.158% (1.4 s), and A1 needs 1.09%, 6.9× that. Together with C1-1's Yue 2018 figure (below), A1 is effectively eliminated. I withdraw the mechanist's and dialectician's "plausible" ranking.
3. **A2 needs H₂ at 3.6–4.8× the ion-pump reading.** Conceded, and the conditions are worse than that for A2. Nico 2005 says the trap was *below* the pump reading because of cryopumping. BL1 saw no H₂⁺ above its 1% detection limit.
4. **A sign error on the +5.4 s ⁶Li-absorption correction gives 10.8 s, 112% of the gap.** Accepted as a correction to C1's "54%" figure, which holds only for doubling or omitting a correction. This is the one bookkeeping route shown to reach full size. It is an existence proof, not evidence that such an error occurred.
5. **"About 1.1%" depends on which storage value is true.** Accepted. The needed shift is 1.16% against UCNτ or the magnetic traps and 0.91% against the material bottles. The common factor fitted to both proton-counting experiments, 1.10 ± 0.23%, covers both. C1 takes no side in the material–magnetic 2.2 s split, which it leaves to C7.
6. **A-BL1 needs Sussex–ILL to be a 2.24σ fluctuation (p = 0.025), while A-gen fits both experiments with χ² = 0.079/1.** Accepted. I promote **A-gen** to C1's primary scope. The cost of A-gen: BL2 as built may also read long, so a BL2 reading near 888 s refutes C1 only if BL2's own diagnostics (trap-time, residual-gas and field scans, new monitor) show no effect. I carry this into the deciding test.

C1-0's decisive magnitude test goes in C1's favour. A single 1.09% multiplicative error in the proton class leaves J-PARC at −0.26σ and the A-route τ_β at +0.41σ from C1's prediction, and needs no shift in any other class.

### C1-1 (evidence)
1. **A1 is excluded by Yue et al. 2018, which gives the monitor efficiency to 0.058% (0.51 s), 19× short of the gap.** Conceded. A1 is dropped as a live sub-hypothesis.
2. **A2-H₂ is closed: Caylor 2025 finds H₂⁺ detection efficiency within 1.5% of the proton's, and a worst-case shift below 0.5 s.** Conceded in full. My "weakest link", whether H₂⁺ escapes detection, is settled against C1: H₂⁺ is counted. I drop two things from C1's predictions for H₂: the "whole gap at 3.7×10⁻⁷ Pa" scenario and the "+2.6 s per 10 ms under Serebrov's reading" trap-time prediction. A trap-time dependence survives only for **A2-other**: other gases whose product ions are lost or neutral.
3. **Wietfeldt 2023 rejects the rest of Serebrov's list.** Acknowledged. Its full text, with the residual-gas limits, was not accessible, so it neither closes nor opens A2-other quantitatively.
4. **A3 (backscatter plus Si scattering) is 0.64 s, 15× short.** Conceded. A3 is dropped.
5. **The open routes are A4 (a large correction mis-sized) and A2-other (non-H₂ residual gas).** Agreed. On A2-other I accept the missing Byrne evidence (below), but I do not over-read it. Byrne 2019 calls the losses "significant … when 0.1% accuracy in the neutron lifetime is sought". That is a statement at the 0.1% level, not the 1.1% needed, and it rests on trapping times about 10³ times BL1's 10 ms. So Byrne supports the existence of an A-gen channel qualitatively. No source places it near 1%.
6. **The independent arbiters favour C1: J-PARC 0.14σ from UCNτ, A-route τ_β 878.70 ± 0.83 s, CKM −1.2σ against −4.6σ, and BL1 carrying the proton class.** Agreed; no rebuttal needed.

Corrections accepted: A1 ranks least likely, and Byrne 2019/2022 is added to the evidence for A-gen as qualitative support.

Net effect: C1 now rests on A4 or A2-other. Both are unmeasured, and neither has a quoted size.

### C1-2 (bounds)
1. **The a-route (aSPECT 2024) gives τ_β = 889.58 ± 3.20 s, 3.49σ from the storage pole (a-group mean 3.00σ).** Partly conceded. C1 must hold that aSPECT's λ is wrong for a reason separate from the lifetime systematic. That leaves two unexplained tensions instead of one, which is exactly what C8 exploits.

   Partly rebutted, from the verdict's own ledger:
   - the a-route is one experiment against three A-route experiments that agree (χ² = 1.51/2);
   - every combination of non-trap, non-storage bounds except "a-route alone" favours C1's pole, with Δχ²(storage − beam) between −12.37 and −17.55 [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/falsifier-C1-2_bounds_ledger.py].
2. **The Fierz escape (b = −0.0181 ± 0.0065, τ = 893.7 s for every method).** Agreed that this measures the λ tension rather than a bound against C1. It is 1.26σ above BL1 as well.
3. **Unitarity.** Exact first-row unitarity with the A-route λ implies τ_β = 876.88 s, which sides with C1. No rebuttal needed.
4. **BBN.** Accepted: it is uninformative (0.58σ_obs between the poles) and belongs in the matrix as such.
5. **Sussex–ILL commits C1 to A-gen.** Accepted; see C1-0 point 6.
6. **3.2σ against UCNτ versus 3.0σ against the S-scaled pole.** Accepted: the two figures differ only in the reference.

### What C1 is after the verdicts
C1 now claims:
- an unidentified, multiplicative effect, about 1.10 ± 0.23%, generic to Penning-trap proton counting;
- most plausibly a mis-sized or mis-signed large correction (A4), or charge exchange or neutralisation on a non-H₂ residual gas (A2-other);
- the storage value is the true lifetime, and the A-route λ is right.

Dropped: A1, A2-H₂ and A3.

C1 stands on the independent arbiters (J-PARC, A-route τ_β, CKM), not on a mechanism. Its main liability is the aSPECT a-route.

## Deciding observation
All separations below come from [calc: runs/2026-09-30-neutron-lifetime-beam-bottle/calc/crux-C1_deciding.py] unless marked otherwise.

- **vs C8 (one proton-side cause behind both the lifetime and λ splits): Nab's a coefficient.** ORNL SNS; Δλ/|λ| = 0.04% goal, i.e. σ_λ = 0.00051 and σ_a = 0.00015; upgraded for precision running Nov 2025; no a value and no date [D-107].
  - C1 predicts a PERKEO-like a ≈ −0.10687 (|λ| ≈ 1.2764). C8's R-strong predicts that Nab, which also detects protons, reads aSPECT-like, a ≈ −0.10402.
  - A PERKEO-like result sits 3.50σ from aSPECT, which breaks C8's proton-detection link and collapses C8 to R-weak, i.e. C1's A-BL1/A-gen scope.
  - An aSPECT-like result sits 12.7σ from the A route. On C1's premises it implies τ_β = 889.58 ± 0.84 s, 11.9σ above the storage pole. C1 could survive only by declaring both proton-recoil a experiments biased, which is C8, so C1 as stated falls.

- **vs C5 (electron-always X⁺ branch): Nab's a coefficient**, same experiment and precision.
  - C5 needs Γ(p e ν̄) = 1/887.7 s⁻¹, i.e. the aSPECT-like λ.
  - A PERKEO-like Nab gives τ_β = 878.50 ± 0.84 s, 4.3σ below the proton pole, which excludes C5 and fits C1 (0.19σ from storage).
  - An aSPECT-like Nab refutes C1 as above, and C5 then competes with C3 and C8 on LiNA and UCNProBe.
  - LiNA and UCNProBe cannot separate C1 from C5: both predict ≈ 878 s for both.

- **vs C3 (invisible dark branch): LiNA's electron-counting lifetime.** J-PARC; ~1 s; background expected to fall ×50; commissioned Feb 2024; no result date [D-102].
  - C1 predicts ≈ 878 s; C3 predicts ≈ 888 s (τ_β).
  - A reading of 888 ± 1 s is 8.87σ from the storage pole. A reading of 878 ± 1 s is 4.25σ from the proton pole, and 3.81σ from BL1 alone, because the beam pole's own 2.04 s error caps that side.
  - Nab is co-decisive on λ: a PERKEO-like result holds the A-route bound on the dark branch, Br_X < 0.25% (M-EXAMINER-06), against the ~1.1% C3 needs. C3's Fierz escape, b ≈ −0.016, is tested by Nab's b.
  - LiNA is the direct test because it needs no SM theory chain.

- **vs C2 (common storage loss): UCNProBe's β-rate lifetime against its own storage lifetime in the same trap.** LANL (Krivos, Morris); 1–2 s (search-summary); no date [D-106].
  - C1 predicts a difference of 0 s; C2 predicts ≈ +9.65 s.
  - Separation: 9.6σ at a 1 s differential error, 6.4σ at 1.5 s, 4.8σ at 2 s.
  - The differential form cancels UCN-counting normalisation, and it also discriminates against C3, which predicts the same +9.65 s.
  - Runner-up: LiNA, with the same numbers as for C3, since C2 also needs J-PARC-type electron counting to read ≈ 888 s.

- **vs C7 (no dominant cause): a BL3 lifetime at 0.3 s**, NIST, in construction, no date [D-101], together with BL2's diagnostic scans. BL2 is at < 1 s, "next few years" (search-summary) [D-100].
  - C1 (A-gen) predicts ≈ 878 s once an identified effect of ≈ 1.1% is corrected, or as built with BL3's new monitor and trap. C7 predicts 880–886 s with no identified correction.
  - At 0.3 s: a reading of 883 s sits 8.9σ from the storage pole, and 880 s sits 3.2σ from it. A pole reading of 878.32 s sits 3.2σ below C7's lower edge.
  - At BL2's 1 s the 880 s edge is only 1.5σ away. BL2 alone therefore cannot separate C1 from C7's lower range; BL3 is needed.
  - The effect must be *named* for C1 to win outright. An 878 s reading with no identified cause fits C1's A-BL1 reading and does not refute C7.

- **vs C6 (excited state n*): a beam lifetime measured as a function of time since production** (velocity-selected or pulsed beam, BL2/BL3 or LiNA), read together with the Blatnik/UCNτ and Nab n* exclusion regions [D-93].
  - C1 predicts no dependence on flight time: A4 is a fixed fractional error, and A2-other scales with trapping time, not flight time.
  - C6 predicts a shift that falls with flight time.
  - The required precision is not computed, because no lens computed C6's flight-time curve or J-PARC's flight path. As a floor, each flight-time bin would need an error well below the 9.65 s gap, about 1–2 s, to see a fractional change. That floor is my estimate and is not sourced.
  - No date is known.

- **The single most informative observation overall is Nab's a.** It is the only pending measurement that tests C1's one standing liability, the aSPECT a-route, directly:
  - an aSPECT-like result sits 12.7σ from the A route (11.9σ in τ_β against storage), which would refute C1;
  - a PERKEO-like result sits 3.50σ from aSPECT and 4.3σ in τ_β below the proton pole.

  It also decides C1 against C8 and C5, and against C3 together with LiNA, all at once.
