## Response to the verdicts

**Where the verdicts agree.** All three keep the operative core: no two-sided construction (GJW, MSY, Maldacena–Qi) or processor analogue delivers anything without its boundary coupling, causally ahead of it, or beyond what it can carry. All three also keep "Sycamore-type runs test size winding, not spacetime". C8-0 says *survives*; C8-1 and C8-2 say *weakened* on the wording of the heading. I accept the narrowing below. I defend the core.

### 1. "Teleportation" applied to Maldacena–Qi (C8-1 point 2, C8-2 point 1, C8-0 correction 1): conceded

- MQ is an always-on Hamiltonian coupling, S_int = g Σ∫du O_L(u) O_R(u) (MQ p. 8, quoted in C8-1). It has no measurement and no classical L→R bits.
- So "teleportation" is the wrong noun for MQ. The heading should read: "two-sided wormholes are boundary-coupled transfer with a geometric description". Only the measured GJW/MSY variant is teleportation in the LOCC sense.
- What this costs:
  - The bound "qubits ≲ const × classical bits exchanged" [K-15, Q-17] applies to GJW/MSY one-shot couplings only.
  - For MQ, and for GJW-as-written, the right bound counts bits *or qubits* through a quantum coupling (MSY, quoted in C8-1). Equivalently, the transfer rate is capped by the coupling Hamiltonian's entangling capacity (the small-incremental-entangling bound used in C8-0).
- **Corrected prediction 1:** delivered qubits never exceed the bits or qubits the coupling carries (one-shot protocols), or the coupling Hamiltonian's entangling capacity × time (MQ).

### 2. GJW says the geometric case needs a quantum channel (C8-1 point 1): conceded, and the claim is sharpened

- GJW: "it is essential that the channel ... is a quantum one". A classical, instantaneous measurement leaves a window that is "very small (and not well described by a semiclassical spacetime)" (quoted in C8-1).
- My prediction "fidelity drops to zero when the classical L→R step is cut" was therefore aimed at the wrong control. The verdicts' version is right, and I adopt it:
  - **Control A:** set μ → 0. The fidelity must fall to the decoupled value.
  - **Control B:** replace the coherent coupling by a measure-and-send LOCC version. The fidelity may survive, but the semiclassical-wormhole signatures should not.
- This strengthens the reframe rather than undermining it. The geometric description exists only when a quantum coupling crosses between the two systems, so the wormhole is a description of that coupling's action. It is not an extra conduit.

### 3. "Bookkeeping" and the MQ μ^{2/3} speed-up (C8-1 point 3, C8-2 point 2, C8-0 strongest attack): partly conceded

- **What is conceded.**
  - MQ (p. 28, quoted in C8-2): self-interactions "accelerate the transfer of information from the left to the right system".
  - The wormhole transfer time is shorter than the bare-coupling time 1/μ by (μ/J)^{1/3} for q = 4. That is a speed-up of 2.2×, 4.6×, 10×, 21.5× and 100× at μ/J = 10⁻¹, 10⁻², 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁶ (dimensionless) [calc: runs/2026-10-03-traversable-wormhole-shortcuts/calc/crux-C8_timing_window.py].
  - C8-1's figures (7.3× at 10⁻², 33.8× at 10⁻⁴) differ from these by an O(1) prefactor of about 1.6. Only the scaling is sourced.
  - So "bookkeeping" undersells the bulk description. It predicts a transfer rate, Shapiro-type delays and a scrambling-time window that generic teleportation does not.
  - **Revised wording:** "a geometric description of the coupled boundary dynamics, which adds description but not capability".
- **What is rebutted: "beats the coupling channel" (C8-2's reading).**
  - C8-2 counts the speed-up as beating the channel if the channel means the bare μ-term acting alone (J = 0). I hold that this is the wrong benchmark, for three reasons.
  - *(a) No delivery without the coupling.* At μ = 0 nothing crosses [D-01, D-04]. GJW: "no qubit ... can traverse the bridge ... if the left and right systems are dynamically decoupled".
  - *(b) No delivery before the coupling.* The MQ coupling is an equal-time interaction, so its causal latency is zero (C8-0 point 2).
  - *(c) No delivery beyond the coupling's capacity.* C8-0 bounds the coupling of norm about Nμ/2 by its entangling capacity. The MQ transfer rate stays 15 to 6×10⁴ times below that capacity over N = 100 to 10⁴ and μ = 10⁻² to 10⁻⁴ J (J = ħ = 1). The bound would bite only for μ < 3.2×10⁻⁸ J at N = 100 [calc: runs/2026-10-03-traversable-wormhole-shortcuts/calc/falsifier-C8-0_channel_and_regime.py].
  - So the strongly coupled dynamics uses more of the coupling's capacity than single-bilinear hopping does, but never exceeds it.
  - C8-1's cruder norm-based speed limit agrees: it holds for N ≥ πμ^{−1/3}, i.e. N ≥ 15 at μ = 10⁻² and N ≥ 68 at μ = 10⁻⁴ [calc: runs/2026-10-03-traversable-wormhole-shortcuts/calc/falsifier-C8-1_channel.py].
  - The question "earlier than the channel?" is posed by the brief for two-sided constructions, which have no shared exterior. Its only well-defined reading is (a) to (c). The bare-μ comparison asks a different question: whether chaotic dynamics speeds transfer up. It does, and that does not beat the channel.
- C8-2 itself lists this as its remaining doubt ("one could define the coupling channel as the whole coupled Hamiltonian"). On that reading the claim survives with only the wording corrected. I adopt that reading explicitly.

### 4. "Open for less than a Planck time" [Q-16] (C8-0, C8-2 corrections): conceded

- Q-16 is a proper-time, probe-regime statement for BTZ horizons of order the AdS radius [D-03].
- For horizons much larger than the AdS radius, about the horizon area in AdS units in quanta can pass, and more light fields allow more [D-03].
- So it is not a universal cap. Restated: in the probe regime the GJW window is short and passes few quanta, and the parametric MSY bound [K-15] caps the transfer.

### 5. The 1 kg payload count: confirmed

- mcR/ħ = 2.843×10⁴² quanta for 1 kg through R = 1 m (SI), confirmed by C8-1 and C8-2 and by M-IDEALIZER-12.
- C8-1 adds that this counts quanta of wavelength about R, not bits. Accepted.

### 6. The processor test and its timeline (C8-0 point 2, C8-2 in-practice): conceded in part

- **It cannot test spacetime (C8-0).** The Schwarzian coupling Nα_S/(βJ) (dimensionless) is about 0.01 to 0.03 for Sycamore (N = 7) and for Byun et al. (N = 8). It is 0.14 to 0.31 at the proposed N = 100. A semiclassical bulk (βJ ≥ 3 and coupling ≥ 10) needs N ≳ 1.9×10³ to 4.3×10³ Majoranas [calc: falsifier-C8-0_channel_and_regime.py].
  - This strengthens C8's third clause.
  - It also means my decisive test decides sub-hypotheses (a) and (b), about whether the signatures are generic for chaotic boundary dynamics. It does not approach a test of spacetime. I withdraw any suggestion that it does.
- **The gap is smaller than I stated (C8-2).**
  - Granet et al. (2025) report 2.2×10⁻³ effective and about 10⁻³ component two-qubit error, for chaotic sparse SYK at N = 24, one copy, on trapped ions (quoted in C8-2).
  - The N = 20 gap is therefore 1.5 to 2.1 orders at F = ½, not 2.3 to 3.7.
  - At N = 100 it is 2.9 to 3.5 orders [calc: runs/2026-10-03-traversable-wormhole-shortcuts/calc/falsifier-C8-2_scale.py].
  - The engineer's gate model agrees with the sourced count to within 1.5× (0.18 orders).
  - Two-copy N ≈ 20 is plausibly nearer than "5 to 10 yr". I keep no date of my own; the 5 to 10 yr figure was unsourced.
- **The lever arm is weak (C8-2).** ln N grows by only a factor of 1.54 from N = 20 to 100. A clean log N scrambling-time signature therefore needs N ≳ 50, where the gap is 2.3 to 2.8 orders (2025 hardware, F = ½; sourced and lens gate counts) [calc: falsifier-C8-2_scale.py].

### Narrowed claim I defend

Two-sided wormholes (GJW, MSY, Maldacena–Qi) and their processor analogues are a geometric description of a boundary-coupled transfer:
- nothing passes without the coupling, causally ahead of it, or beyond its capacity;
- only the GJW/MSY one-shot protocols are teleportation in the LOCC sense;
- in MQ the geometry fixes a transfer time (μ/J)^{1/3} shorter than bare hopping, but within the coupling's capacity;
- processor runs at every reachable N (about 10 to 100 Majoranas) test size winding and boundary chaos, not spacetime.

## Deciding observation

C8 is a reframe of the two-sided rows. It is logically compatible with C1, C2, C6 and C7, and orthogonal to C3 and C4: no rival claims that a two-sided construction beats its channel. So for most rivals, the deciding test settles how much of the question C8's reading covers, not which candidate is true. I say so for each.

**The single observation that would decide C8 against every rival**, and the one C8 stakes itself on:
- *What:* a coupled two-sided protocol (MQ-type SYK pair, or GJW/MSY one-shot) in which the delivered quantum information exceeds the coupling's capacity: more qubits than the bits or qubits exchanged, or a rate above the coupling Hamiltonian's entangling capacity.
- *Who:* numerically, at large N, by SYK groups (exact diagonalisation and Schwinger–Dyson, as in Plugge et al.); experimentally, on Quantinuum or IBM hardware.
- *Precision:* compare the transfer rate with the capacity bound, which sits 15 to 6×10⁴ times above the MQ rate (C8-0). A violation by any factor > 1 refutes C8 and C1 prediction 3 together.
- *When:* no date is known. The numerics are feasible now.
