## Response to the verdicts

All three verdicts reach `weakened` on the same ground: C1's heading says "no **admissible**" wormhole, and the brief counts labelled phantom fields and NEC-violating modified gravity as admissible in principle. All three leave the practice leg (iii) and the two-sided "coupling channel" clause intact. I accept the narrowing, and I adopt this core:

> **C1 (narrowed).** Within established physics (GR plus semiclassical quantum fields), no known traversable wormhole beats light through the exterior or its coupling channel, and every throat needs negative null energy in the total stress tensor. With the brief's labelled extensions, shortcut geometries exist in principle, but only with sources that violate the NEC (in the matter or in the effective geometric sector). Under any admissible physics, none could pass 1 kg within about 100 yr.

  - the shortcut ratio is 2.103×10⁻¹⁵ at d = 1 ly.

C1-1 cites rotating Ellis–Bronnikov results (Azad et al. 2024; "indications" only, and tempered by Blázquez-Salcedo et al. 2026) and beyond-Horndeski throats. I have no source that undoes any of these. The instability is settled only for the static ghost-scalar family [D-13] and for thin shells with ordinary sound speeds [D-14].

**Partial rebuttal: what "passes 1 kg in principle" costs.** C1-0 says the b₀ = 1 m phantom throat passes "a near-light-speed 1 kg payload". That holds for the instability budget but not for the payload's integrity at that size. From the same script:

The e-folds needed scale as 1/v and do not depend on b₀; the e-folds available grow only as ln b₀. **So the static ghost-scalar throat passes no slow payload at any size.** C1-0 itself found that 0.1c fails.
- **Lateral tide.** For Φ = 0, R_θ̂l̂θ̂l̂ = −r″/r = −1/b₀² at the throat (geometric), and the boosted lateral tide is γ²v²c²ξ/b₀² (SI). This is my derivation in the script docstring; the dossier sources the Morris–Thorne criterion only as a search summary [Q-52].
  - At b₀ = 1 m and v_min, a 0.1 m body feels 3.97×10¹⁵ m/s² (4.05×10¹⁴ g). That means an internal stress of order 2×10¹⁷ Pa for a 1 kg body. I take ordinary material strengths to be of order 10⁹ Pa; that figure is my assumption, not sourced in the dossier.

### Point 2. Leg (ii) in modified gravity (C1-1 §2, C1-2 §2)

The brief itself defines the modified-gravity extension as one "whose effective stress tensor violates the NEC" (quoted by C1-0, "Brief, Admissible physics"). K-02 needs only local geometry (Hochberg–Visser's dynamic case has no symmetry or asymptotic assumption). So the flare-out theorem forces any metric throat to carry negative effective null energy, whatever the field equations.
  - The physical content is that the NEC violation must come from somewhere: exotic matter, or curvature terms beyond Einstein's.
  - The KKK case is weaker than C1-2 presents it. C1-1 cites Cuyubamba, Konoplya & Zhidenko 2018, who find the Kanti–Kleihaus–Kunz wormhole "unstable against small perturbations for any values of its parameters", which overrides KKK's radial-only stability.

- **Within established physics leg (ii) stands as stated.** K-01 and K-02 are proved, and the post-2016 constructions all use Casimir-type negative null energy [D-01, D-04 to D-08].

## Deciding observation

The narrowed C1 is a claim about **established physics in principle, plus every row in practice**. Each rival is separated from it by a different test.

- **vs C3 (achronal ANEC fails, so shortcuts are possible). This is the main crux.**
  - *Test:* a second-order (back-reacted) computation of FGM 2019's minimum transit time [D-07, Q-13]. A negative sign for t_min − d favours C3; a sign ≥ 0 favours C1. Equivalently, a self-consistent solution of G = 8π⟨T⟩ with ∫T_kk dλ < 0 on a complete achronal null geodesic would refute C1's leg (i) in established physics.
  - *Who:* semiclassical-gravity theorists with FGM's perturbative methods.
  - *Precision:* the sign of t_min − d (geometric units) at second order, resolved against the log terms.
  - *When:* no date is known [D-C3].
- **vs C4 (classical labelled-matter shortcuts).** After the concessions above, C1 and C4 agree in the labelled sector. They now differ on whether the shortcut needs labelled matter at all.
  - *Test:* a curved-space QEI (Fewster–Smith type) evaluated on an Ellis throat. A bound that leaves room for a macroscopic throat (b₀ ≳ 1 m) built from admissible quantum fields favours C4 in established physics. A bound that confines the negative-energy band as the flat-space estimates do (≤ 1.84×10⁻²¹ m at b₀ = 1 m, M-CONSTRAINTS-08) favours C1.
  - *Secondary test, for the labelled sector:* a nonlinear numerical evolution of a stabilised (rotating, or Bronnikov-fluid) one-sided Ellis throat with a 1 kg payload pulse crossing at about 0.5c. Kain-type codes exist [D-13]. No date is known.
- **vs C2 (long Casimir wormholes traversable in principle).**
  - *Compatibility:* the narrowed C1 is compatible with C2 once the DECOMPOSER-B strong variant is dropped. Both say the long wormholes are not shortcuts and cannot pass 1 kg in practice.
  - *What still separates them:* whether a traversable wormhole exists in established physics at all.
  - *Test:* a complete linear stability analysis of MMP in every sector, beyond the O(α) truncation, with growth rates compared with the transit time πℓ/c [D-C5]. A mode growing faster than πℓ/c would favour C1's strong variant; none would favour C2.
  - *Who and when:* semiclassical theorists; Sadhukhan covers one sector [D-C5]. No date is known.
- **vs C6 (the ban is narrower: misaligned one-sided shortcuts escape it).**
  - *Test:* the achronality of complete through-throat null geodesics for finite mouths in a curved (Schwarzschild-like) exterior with rotated or mirror gluing. A complete achronal through-line for every one-sided shortcut would close C6's loophole and restore C1's leg (i) under the conjecture. None in the misaligned case would keep C6's loophole open.
  - *Decisive version:* a self-consistent, mirror-glued, Casimir-supported throat with L < d. Its existence would favour C6 against C1.
  - *Who and when:* mathematical-relativity theorists; no date is known.
- **vs C7 (mouth clock offset makes long wormholes shortcuts).**
  - *Test:* solve MMP self-consistently with the twisted identification (t, x) ~ (t + Δ, x + L), and ask whether stationary solutions exist for Δ > T_thru − d/c.
  - *Reading:* if the solution ceases or the throat lengthens before Δ_s, C1's leg (i) holds dynamically. If a solution survives inside the 2d/c window, the self-consistent achronal ANEC is falsified (C7's P2), which removes C1's in-principle support.
  - *Precision:* existence or non-existence across the window, for example Δ_s = 6.4×10³ yr at d = ℓ for MM parameters (C7).
  - *Who and when:* semiclassical theorists using MMP's lowest-Landau-level framework; no date is known.
- **vs C8 (two-sided wormholes are teleportation with bookkeeping).**
  - *Compatibility:* the two are compatible. C8 restates C1's two-sided clause and adds a claim about the 2022 experiment, on which C1 is silent.
  - *The observation that would refute C1's clause (and C8):* any two-sided protocol that delivers more qubits than the classical bits its coupling exchanges, or that delivers before the coupling acts [K-15, D-01]. The cheapest check is the channel-cut control: fidelity should fall to zero when the classical L→R step is cut.
  - *Who and when:* chaotic sparse-SYK runs at N ≥ 20 on quantum hardware need an error per gate of about 10⁻⁵ (M-ENGINEER-13), plausibly within 5 to 10 yr (the engineer's estimate, unsourced).
