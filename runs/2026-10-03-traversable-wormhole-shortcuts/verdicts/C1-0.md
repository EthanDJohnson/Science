# Verdict on C1 (refuter 0, angle: physics)
claim: No viable option: no admissible traversable wormhole beats light through the exterior or its coupling channel, none stays open without negative null energy, and none could pass 1 kg in practice.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: Within established physics (GR plus semiclassical quantum fields, and granting the self-consistent achronal ANEC conjecture), no known traversable wormhole beats light through the exterior or its coupling channel, every throat needs negative null energy, and none could pass 1 kg within about 100 yr; but once the brief's labelled phantom fields are admitted, a short Ellis throat is an in-principle shortcut that stays open long enough to pass a signal or a near-light-speed 1 kg payload.

Units: SI unless marked; geometric means G = c = 1 (lengths in m). b₀ is the Ellis throat's areal radius, τ_e the e-folding time of its unstable mode, and ε the seed amplitude of that mode as a fraction of the throat's mass scale b₀c²/G.

## Strongest attack

C1 is a conjunction of three legs. Its heading says "no **admissible** traversable wormhole beats light". The brief defines admissible physics for the in-principle horizon as "established physics plus labelled extensions, with unlimited resources", and it names **phantom fields** and NEC-violating modified gravity among the labelled extensions. So leg (i), the no-shortcut leg, has to hold for a labelled classical phantom throat as well as for quantum-supported ones.

C1 does not deny that such throats exist. It sets them aside in one clause: shortcut geometries "need achronal-ANEC violation or labelled phantom matter and are unstable." That clause carries the whole in-principle no-shortcut leg for the labelled sector. I attack it directly. Does the instability actually stop a passage?

1. **The instability is real but finite.** Every static massless ghost-scalar (Ellis) wormhole has exactly one unstable radial mode. For the symmetric throat its e-folding time is bounded to lie in [1/√3, 1/√(11/8)] b₀/c, so τ_e ≥ 0.577 b₀/c (Gonzalez, Guzmán & Sarbach 2009, quoted below). I take the worst case, the fastest growth.
2. **A passage needs few e-folds** [calc: runs/2026-10-03-traversable-wormhole-shortcuts/calc/falsifier-C1-0_ellis_lifetime.py]:
   - A light-speed crossing between static observers at r = 10 b₀ takes T = 6.64×10⁻⁸ s at b₀ = 1 m. That is 34.5 e-folds.
   - The same crossing from r = 3 b₀ takes 9.8 e-folds.
   - The Ellis transit time comes from `wormhole_tools.ProperThroat`. It reproduces C4's T_thru = 6.64×10⁻⁸ s and its ratio 2.1×10⁻¹⁵ at d = 1 ly.
3. **The seeds are tiny.**
   - A 1 kg payload perturbs a b₀ = 1 m throat by about ε = Gm/(c²b₀) = 7.4×10⁻²⁸. That leaves ln(1/ε) = 62.5 e-folds before the mode becomes nonlinear.
   - The margin is 28.0 e-folds. The payload's coupling to the mode could be 1.45×10¹² times larger than this estimate and the payload would still cross first.
   - The ambient seeds are far smaller. CMB energy in a b₀³ volume gives ε = 3.4×10⁻⁵⁸. A single vacuum quantum ħc/b₀ gives ε = 2.6×10⁻⁷⁰, so a throat seeded only by vacuum noise stays open about 160 e-folds, or 92.5 b₀/c.
   - The same margins hold at b₀ = 505 m and 1 km, for 1 kg and for 70 kg (58 to 69 e-folds available against 34.5 needed).
4. **Conclusion of the attack.** Under the brief's own definition, a throat that is "open long enough for that passage" is traversable. A phantom-supported Ellis throat with b₀ = 1 m and mouths 1 ly apart therefore passes a signal, or a 1 kg payload moving near c, with T_thru/T_ext = 2.1×10⁻¹⁵. That is a shortcut by fifteen orders of magnitude, made from a brief-admissible labelled field. "Unstable" does not remove this option in principle.
5. **The instability may not even be generic.** Later peer-reviewed work finds that rotation removes the unstable radial mode in the slow-rotation expansion. Rapidly rotating Ellis–Bronnikov wormholes show no instabilities in the M_z = 2, 3 sectors (Azad et al. 2024; Khoo et al. 2024, quoted below). Both results are partial: second-order slow rotation, and selected sectors. But they cut against treating "unstable" as settled.

So the in-principle no-shortcut leg survives only for *established* physics: semiclassical GR with ordinary quantum fields. There it rests on the self-consistent achronal ANEC conjecture, plus a further step that M-DECOMPOSER-08 shows is unproved for one-sided throats with misaligned mouths, which C1 itself concedes. Legs (ii) and (iii) survive my attack (see below). The core therefore stands only in narrowed form.

Why "weakened" and not "refuted": the brief permits phantom fields only when labelled, and C1's legs (ii) and (iii) are untouched. A reader who takes "admissible" to mean "established" keeps C1's leg (i) intact as an expectation. I cannot refute that reading by calculation, because it rests on a conjecture that has neither been proven nor broken.

## In principle vs. in practice

- **In principle, established physics only** (GR plus semiclassical QFT, no labelled extensions):
  - Leg (i) stands as an expectation, not a theorem. It rests on the self-consistent achronal ANEC conjecture [K-07a] and on the unproved one-sided step (M-DECOMPOSER-08). No counterexample exists.
  - The two-sided half of leg (i), "nothing arrives before the coupling channel", is solid for GJW, MQ and MSY [D-01, K-15].
  - Leg (ii) stands. It is a theorem for the pointwise NEC [K-02] and for ANEC on some null line [K-01], and C1's general static radial-ANEC formula was verified (M-CONSTRAINTS-02).
- **In principle, with labelled extensions:**
  - Leg (i) fails as worded. A labelled classical phantom Ellis throat gives T_thru/T_ext down to 2.1×10⁻¹⁵ at d = 1 ly, and it stays open for 62 e-folds against 34.5 needed for a light-speed 1 kg crossing.
  - Slow crossings (≤ 0.1c) of a static throat do not survive the instability unless rotation or active control removes it. Azad et al. and Khoo et al. give partial evidence that rotation does.
  - Leg (ii) survives even here. A phantom field *is* negative null energy, and in modified gravity the effective stress tensor violates the NEC, which is how the brief itself defines that extension.
- **In practice (about 100 yr):** leg (iii) stands. The phantom throat needs |M| ≈ 1.35×10²⁷ kg of negative mass per metre of throat radius. That is 38 to 57 orders beyond demonstrated negative energy (C4 and M-ENGINEER-02 to M-ENGINEER-04), and no phantom field is known. The cheapest established-physics row (SM-MMP) needs unobserved magnetic charge and fields 34 orders above the record. Nothing in my attack touches this leg.

## Corrections to details

That is ample for signals and for near-light-speed payloads, but not for slow ones. So the clause should read "unstable on a timescale of about b₀/c, which stops slow passage but not light-speed passage."
- **"Strongest variant (DECOMPOSER-B)"**: C1 already flags it as strained, and I agree. Its "every 4D asymptotically flat row is unstable" ignores the rotating Ellis results above. These are labelled phantom rows, and the results are partial.

## What survives and remaining doubt

- **What survives:**
  - All of leg (iii), the in-practice leg.
  - All of leg (ii) in every admissible sense: a throat needs negative null energy, from the matter itself or from the effective source in modified gravity.
  - Leg (i) for two-sided constructions measured against their coupling channel.
  - Leg (i) for one-sided constructions within established physics, but only as a conjecture-dependent expectation.
- **What fails:** the unqualified "no admissible … beats light". With a labelled phantom field, an Ellis shortcut exists in principle and is traversable for a signal or a fast 1 kg payload, despite the instability.
- **Remaining doubts about my own attack:**
  - The seed amplitude ε ≈ Gm/(c²b₀) is an order-of-magnitude model of how the payload couples to the unstable mode. The 28 e-fold margin absorbs any error up to a factor of 10¹².
  - The one-sided Ellis shortcut is the usual approximate gluing of a two-ended solution, valid for d ≫ b₀.
  - Creating the throat would need topology change, which classically requires singularities (Tipler). That falls under the labelled quantum-gravity extension.
  - A quantised phantom field has its own vacuum instability. The brief admits phantom fields only as labelled classical extensions, and I treat them that way.
