# Verdict on C4 (refuter 1, angle: evidence)
claim: Classical exotic-matter wormholes (Morris-Thorne/Ellis, Visser thin-shell and polyhedral) give shortcuts of any designed T_thru/T_ext in principle, given labelled phantom or modified-gravity matter.
status: final
verdict: survives
basis: cited-evidence
narrowed: none

## Strongest attack

The strongest attack from the literature is dynamical. The only member of C4's family whose evolution has been computed with a payload is the Ellis throat held open by a ghost (phantom) scalar, and it is unstable on a timescale of about b₀/c. A payload of ordinary matter always collapses it to a black hole. If that meant the throat cannot stay open long enough for a passage, "gives shortcuts in principle" would fail, because the brief requires the throat to be "open long enough for that passage".

The attack does not carry the core claim. The same sources show that a fast traveller gets through before the collapse, and that tuned ghost pulses can keep the throat open for a while longer. My calculation agrees: a fast crossing uses only a few e-folds of the unstable mode, against the roughly 60 to 90 e-folds needed before a payload-sized seed grows to order one. The ratio T_thru/T_ext is set by the mouth separation d, which is free. So any designed ratio is still reachable, but only as a one-shot transient, unless the throat is actively maintained or a stable equation of state is chosen. That limit falls on a supporting sub-hypothesis, not on the core:
- C4's sub-hypothesis (a), a slow (10 km/s) human crossing of an Ellis throat, is refuted by the instability. It would need about 1.1×10⁵ e-folds.
- The phantom route also carries a cost the candidate does not state: published bounds force a Lorentz-violating cutoff of about 3 MeV, which (on my power-counting reading) pushes controllable phantom throats above about 5×10⁷ m.

Neither point removes the in-principle shortcut.

## Evidence

**1. Ghost-scalar throats have one unstable mode, with e-folding time 0.846 r_throat/c (Ellis member).**
- González, Guzmán & Sarbach 2009, CQG 26, 015010, arXiv:0806.0608, ACCESS full-text:
  - "We prove in this paper that all such solutions are unstable with respect to linear perturbations and possess precisely one unstable mode which grows exponentially in time. Furthermore, we show that the associated time scale is of the order of the areal radius of the wormhole throat divided by the speed of light. In particular, this means that a wormhole whose throat has an areal radius smaller than 1 km, say, decays in a few µs."
  - Table I gives T := τ_unstable/r_throat = 0.846 at γ₁ = 0 (the massless Ellis case), and "the time scale converges to a value of 0.590 r_throat for large values of γ₁".
- Nonlinear follow-up, González, Guzmán & Sarbach 2009, CQG 26, 015011, arXiv:0806.1370, ACCESS abstract: "depending on the initial perturbation the wormholes either expand or decay to a Schwarzschild black hole."

**2. A payload always collapses the Ellis throat, but a fast one gets through first.**
Shinkai & Hayward 2002, PRD 66, 044005, arXiv:gr-qc/0205041, ACCESS full-text:
- (abstract) "For normal matter, such as a traveller traversing the wormhole, collapse to a black hole always results. However, carefully balanced additional ghost radiation can maintain the wormhole for a limited time."
- (p. 8) "Supposing the normal field pulse represents an actual traveller of the traversible wormhole, then he, she or it may go through the wormhole and exit safely into the other universe if the speed is high enough, as can be seen from the Penrose diagram obtained from Fig.9. However the wormhole itself will collapse to a black hole, ending its usefulness for travellers."

**3. My calculation** [calc: runs/2026-10-03-traversable-wormhole-shortcuts/calc/falsifier-C4-1_instability.py]. SI units throughout. The e-folding time is τ_e = 0.846 b₀/c.
- *Unattended lifetime.* A static throat seeded only by Planck-scale fluctuations grows to order one after ln(b₀/l_P) = 80 to 89 e-folds. That is a lifetime of 2.3×10⁻⁷ s at b₀ = 1 m and 1.1×10⁻³ s at b₀ = 4.5 km (67.8 to 74.9 b₀/c). A standing Ellis shortcut must therefore be built within about 70 b₀/c of its use, or actively maintained.
- *C4's own Ellis case* (b₀ = 1 m, T_thru = 6.64×10⁻⁸ s). The transit uses 23.5 e-folds. A 1 kg seed (ε = Gm/(c²b₀) = 7×10⁻²⁸) allows 62.5 e-folds, so the payload passes: amplification is 1.7×10¹⁰ on a seed of 7×10⁻²⁸.
- *C4 sub-hypothesis (a)* (b₀ = 505 m, 10 km/s, 0.16 s, or b₀ = 4.5 km, 1.4 s). The transit uses 1.12×10⁵ or 1.10×10⁵ e-folds, against the 64 to 67 that a 70 kg seed allows. The throat collapses long before the traveller crosses.
- *Tidal-limited human crossing* (1 g over 2 m, Ellis lateral tide γ²v²ξ/b₀² ≤ g, which reproduces C4's 4.5 km at 10 km/s).
  - The e-folds used in crossing the throat region are 2.364 c/v, independent of b₀.
  - The crossing is too slow at v ≤ 0.03c (79 e-folds or more, against 73) and passes at v ≥ 0.1c (24 e-folds or fewer, against 75). The threshold is v ≈ 0.03c.
  - At v = 0.9c the throat needs b₀ ≥ 2.8×10⁸ m and 2.6 e-folds.
  - So a human shortcut through a ghost-scalar throat must be relativistic and large.
- *Phantom-EFT scale* (my inference, medium confidence). The Ellis throat energy-density scale is ρ^{1/4} = 2.2×10¹⁰ eV at b₀ = 1 m and 2.2×10⁶ eV at 10⁸ m. This scale equals the 3 MeV cutoff (source 4 below) at b₀ = 5.3×10⁷ m. Generic EFT power counting puts smaller phantom-scalar throats outside the effective theory's control.

**4. Phantom fields themselves need a Lorentz-violating cutoff.** Cline, Jeon & Moore 2004, PRD 70, 043543, arXiv:hep-ph/0311312, ACCESS abstract: "Assuming only that ghosts interact at least gravitationally, we show that Lambda < 3 MeV for consistency with the cosmic gamma ray background. We also show that theories of ghosts with a Lorentz-conserving cutoff are completely excluded."

**5. Modified-gravity routes have no clean stable example.**
- *Horndeski and beyond Horndeski.* Mironov, Rubakov & Volkova 2019, CQG 36, 135008, arXiv:1812.07022, ACCESS abstract:
  - "Horndeski theories, like many other scalar-tensor gravities, do not support static, spherically symmetric wormholes: they always have either ghosts or gradient instabilities among parity-even linearized perturbations";
  - for beyond Horndeski, "our stability analysis is incomplete".
- *Einstein–dilaton–Gauss–Bonnet.* Cuyubamba, Konoplya & Zhidenko 2018, PRD 98, 044040, arXiv:1804.11170, ACCESS abstract: "more detailed analysis of perturbations shows that the Kanti-Kleihaus-Kunz wormhole is unstable against small pertur[bations]" (the abstract text fetched was cut off at this word).

**6. Static existence is not in dispute.**
- The candidate's own shortcut and energy-condition tables were verified by the math checks (M-EXAMINER-02, M-EXAMINER-03, M-CONSTRAINTS-01, M-CONSTRAINTS-05, M-CONSTRAINTS-15).
- None of C4's numbers is refuted in math/.

## In principle vs. in practice

- **In principle: survives, with qualifiers.**
  - With labelled phantom matter, static designer geometries exist for any mouth separation, so T_thru/T_ext can be made arbitrarily small.
  - The Ellis realisation delivers this only as a transient. Either the throat is built within about 70 b₀/c of use, or it is kept open with tuned ghost pulses (Shinkai & Hayward). The payload must cross at v ≳ 0.03c, and the payload's passage then collapses the throat to a black hole.
  - A long-lived standing shortcut needs a thin shell given a chosen, "somewhat perverse" equation of state (linear, radial stability only, [D-14]), or an unverified beyond-Horndeski solution.
  - The phantom route also requires a Lorentz-violating cutoff of 3 MeV or less.
- **In practice: not viable.** I have no evidence that changes C4's own 38-to-57-order gap to demonstrated negative energy, nor the quantum-inequality failures it lists. On top of these, a human crossing must be relativistic, through a throat of at least about 10⁷ to 10⁸ m.

## Corrections to details

- **Sub-hypothesis (a) is refuted as stated.** A slow (10 km/s) crossing of a 505 m or 4.5 km Ellis throat, taking 0.16 s or 1.4 s, uses about 1.1×10⁵ e-folds of the unstable mode. The throat collapses within about 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻³ s of being left static. A tidal-safe human crossing of a ghost-scalar throat needs v ≳ 0.03c (0.1c and above passes comfortably) and b₀ ≳ 10⁷ m [calc: falsifier-C4-1_instability.py; González et al. 2009, Table I].
- **The "If not" prediction is half met already, and the "If viable" one partly.** C4's "If not" prediction ("ghost throats collapse or expand under a weak positive pulse") is already met for the Ellis sub-hypothesis. Its "If viable" prediction (a stabilised phantom throat surviving a payload) is partly met too, by Shinkai & Hayward's fine-tuned ghost-pulse maintenance, but only "for a limited time".
- **Modified-gravity support is thinner than "given labelled modified-gravity matter" suggests.** Static, spherically symmetric Horndeski wormholes always carry a ghost or a gradient instability. The Einstein–dilaton–Gauss–Bonnet wormhole is unstable. The beyond-Horndeski candidates have an incomplete stability analysis (sources in 5).
- **The phantom label hides a Lorentz-violation requirement.** It needs a cutoff of 3 MeV or less (Cline et al.). On EFT power counting (my inference), phantom-scalar throats smaller than about 5×10⁷ m lie above that cutoff's energy-density scale.

## What survives and remaining doubt

- **What survives.** The core claim: classical exotic-matter geometries, given labelled phantom or modified-gravity sources, admit one-sided shortcuts with any designed T_thru/T_ext as solutions of GR, and the Ellis case demonstrably passes a fast payload before collapsing.
- **Doubt: no one-sided dynamics.** No source evolves a one-sided (single-universe) phantom throat. Mouth-to-mouth scalar and gravitational interaction could add a further instability, but I found no calculation either way.
- **Doubt: my e-fold estimate is order of magnitude.** It assumes the payload excites the unstable mode with amplitude about Gm/(c²b₀).
- **Doubt: thin-shell stability is radial and linear only.** It also rests on choosing the equation of state freely, which the brief's "labelled exotic matter" allows but no microphysics supplies.
