# Verdict on C6 (refuter 2, angle: scale)
claim: Even granting the self-consistent achronal ANEC, the shortcut ban is narrower than stated: it forbids only shortcuts threaded by a complete achronal throat geodesic, so misaligned one-sided shortcuts escape it.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: Granting the conjecture, mouth misalignment opens no escape once the mouths have any finite radius a; a static one-sided shortcut escapes the ban only if it is marginal (time advantage below about 1.5a/c, an artefact of exterior paths wrapping the mouths that aligned mouths share), non-generic (flat-face polyhedral rays), or outside the static flat-exterior case computed here.

## Strongest attack
The escape rests entirely on point-mouth toys (M-IDEALIZER-01/02, M-DECOMPOSER-08). In those toys a ray must pass through a single point and leave in the rigidly fixed direction Ru (or −n for mirror gluing), so a through-ray that keeps its direction exists only along the rotation axis. That rigidity is the size limit a → 0 of the mouth, and it is the step that fails at any finite scale. A real mouth has a radius a > 0, and must have one to pass a payload. A ray can then enter at any point of the mouth sphere at any incidence. The exit direction is no longer forced, and the "aligned" condition can always be met by choosing where the ray enters.

The argument is clean in a static spacetime (Φ = 0, or the optical metric otherwise). There, a complete null geodesic is achronal exactly when its spatial projection is a line, that is, a geodesic that minimises distance between every pair of its points. Take far points x = A − s·u and y = B + t·u. Exterior-only paths are at least the Euclidean distance |y − x| ≈ s + t + D·u long. A through-handle path exists that enters at A + a·n and exits at B + a·Gn, with n chosen so that n·u ≤ 0 and (Gn)·u ≥ 0. Such an n always exists, because two closed hemispheres always intersect. That path has length s + t + L + a·u·(n − Gn) ≤ s + t + L.

So whenever L < d (u = D̂), every minimiser between far points threads the throat, **for every gluing G ∈ O(3)**. Minimisers that pass through a fixed compact set while their endpoints go to infinity on both sides converge to a complete line through the handle. In a smooth spacetime, or one with a C⁰ thin-shell metric, that line is a geodesic. So it is a complete achronal null geodesic through the throat. Graham–Olum's Lemma 1 then forbids it under the conjecture in a generic spacetime.

The decomposer's own counterexamples are caught by this. Its static throat with L = 0.5d and mouths rotated by 10° or 180° is caught, and so is the idealizer's mirror gluing, which had no line "even at L = 0". All of them have lines through the throat as soon as a > 0. The point-mouth threshold (0 for the rotated cases) jumps discontinuously to above d at any a > 0. The loophole is an artefact of the singular conical mouth: the a → 0 limit of these lines is still an achronal null curve, but with a 10° or 180° kink at the mouth, so it is not a geodesic.

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle:** with finite mouths, a static misaligned shortcut with L < d is threaded by a complete achronal throat geodesic, so it falls under the ban exactly as an aligned one does. What remains is a marginal band, L* ≤ L < d + πa for observers on the mouth surfaces, whose time advantage is at most about (π − 1.643)a/c ≈ 1.5a/c. This band exists for aligned mouths too (width (π − 2)a), because the exterior path has to wrap around the mouths. Misalignment widens it by at most 0.36a, on the a = 0.1d sample. On the brief's scales, the largest time advantage in the band is:
  - 1.0×10⁻²⁷ s for the Standard-Model MMP throat (r_e ≈ 2×10⁻¹⁹ m);
  - 5.0×10⁻⁹ s for a 1 m thin shell;
  - 0.075 s for MM's r_e = 1.5×10⁷ m (SI) [calc: same file, section 5].
- **In practice:** unchanged. No one-sided shortcut of any kind is buildable (C1, iii). The escape needs point-like mouths, which cannot pass a payload. A mouth large enough for a payload (a ≥ the size of the payload) closes the escape.

## Corrections to details
- "Finite mouths narrow the loophole by O(a/d) but do not close it" (the C6 prediction and idealizer F2/M1) is wrong in the model computed here. Finite mouths close the misalignment loophole for every L < d. What survives is an O(a) observer-wrap band shared with aligned mouths.
- Idealizer F2: "near-grazing rays give achronal segments of length ∼ d/ε² … but no complete achronal line that crosses the throat". This is contradicted: complete lines exist, and they are not near-grazing (entry n·u = −0.91 in section 3).
- The candidate's own "if it is wrong" test ("finite-mouth geometry restores a complete achronal through-line") is met even before the exterior is curved.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives:**
  - the formal point that Graham–Olum's published Theorem 1 assumes simple connectedness;
  - the marginal band, at most about 1.5a/c of advantage;
  - sub-hypothesis (b), flat-face polyhedral rays with ANEC exactly 0, which are non-generic and so outside Lemma 1, whatever the alignment;
  - the cases computed nowhere here.
- **Not computed:**
  - a curved (Schwarzschild-like) exterior, where corrections are of order GM/c² and a, and the line argument carries over to the optical metric if the via-handle path still wins for far pairs;
  - non-static mouths;
  - smooth handles with Φ ≠ 0;
  - back-reaction.

  The line-existence step (Arzelà–Ascoli limit of minimisers through a compact set) is standard Riemannian geometry, but I applied it, not proved it formally. These gaps are why the verdict is weakened rather than refuted. The heading's mechanism, misalignment as the escape, does not survive at any finite mouth size.
