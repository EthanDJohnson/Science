# Verdict on C4 (refuter 2, angle: scale)
claim: Classical exotic-matter wormholes (Morris–Thorne/Ellis, Visser thin-shell and polyhedral) give shortcuts of any designed T_thru/T_ext in principle, given labelled phantom or modified-gravity matter.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: In principle, classical exotic-matter shortcuts of any designed T_thru/T_ext exist only as solutions whose exotic matter is tuned for stability (for example, Bronnikov et al.'s phantom fluid plus a radial field, or a thin shell with β₀² < 0), not with the canonical ghost scalar or Einstein–dilaton–Gauss–Bonnet throats, which collapse in about b₀/c; and only for a wormhole already supplied by labelled quantum topology change or primordial origin, since creating one classically needs an acausal spacetime.

## Strongest attack
C4 is a claim about what is possible in principle. Unlimited resources allow any amount of exotic mass, so the attack from scale has to be about stability: can the throat stay open long enough to be crossed? It cannot do so with the phantom matter C4 itself puts forward, the ghost scalar of sub-hypothesis (a).

1. **The slow crossing in sub-hypothesis (a) cannot happen.** C4 proposes a 10 km/s crossing of a ghost-scalar Ellis throat with b₀ = 505 m or 4.5 km, taking 0.16 s or 1.4 s. González, Guzmán & Sarbach (GGS) 2009 find exactly one unstable mode. Its e-folding time is τ = 0.846 b₀/c for the massless Ellis throat.
   - The number of e-folds during a crossing is N = πc/(0.846 v), which does not depend on b₀.
   - At v = 10 km/s, N = 1.11×10⁵. The perturbation grows by a factor of 10^48350 before the traveller reaches the other side [calc: runs/2026-10-03-traversable-wormhole-shortcuts/calc/falsifier-C4-2_stability_scale.py].
   - The throat therefore collapses or blows up within microseconds to milliseconds: τ = 1.4 µs at 505 m and 12.7 µs at 4.5 km.
2. **The payload's own mass makes the crossing relativistic and the throat huge.** Model the payload as a seed perturbation δ = Gm/(c²b₀). The throat counts as lost when δe^N reaches 0.1. That leaves only about 70 e-folds.
   - So v ≥ 0.05c, and with the boosted transverse tide (b₀ ≥ γv√(ξ/a), M-IDEALIZER-06):
     - **Human (70 kg, 1 g over 2 m):** b₀ ≥ 7.0×10⁶ m, |M| ≈ 9.5×10³³ kg (4.8×10³ M_☉).
     - **1 kg (20 g over 0.5 m):** b₀ ≥ 7.6×10⁵ m, |M| ≈ 1.0×10³³ kg (5.2×10² M_☉).
   - These throats are about 10³ times C4's 4.5 km and 505 m, and need about 10³ times C4's 3.1 and 0.34 M_☉ of negative mass.
3. **The throat cannot be left unattended.** Infall from the cosmic microwave background (CMB) alone is enough to seed the unstable mode.
   - Lifetime: 3.6×10⁻⁷ s at b₀ = 1 m, 1.4×10⁻³ s at 4.5 km, and about 1.9 s at 6.9×10⁶ m (96–128 e-folds).
   - A ghost-scalar shortcut therefore needs active stabilisation with a growth rate of about 3.5×10⁸ s⁻¹ at 1 m and about 51 s⁻¹ at 6.9×10⁶ m. The mode e-folds 7.4 times within one light-crossing of the throat's circumference.
   - No literature result shows such control is possible.
4. **The modified-gravity alternative fails the same way.** The one asymptotically flat Einstein–dilaton–Gauss–Bonnet (EdGB) wormhole once claimed to be stable (Kanti–Kleihaus–Kunz) is unstable "for any values of its parameters" (Cuyubamba, Konoplya & Zhidenko 2018).
5. **Creation.** The exotic matter does not remove Geroch's purely causal theorem: changing the topology of spacelike sections requires an acausal spacetime. So the classical "design" route presupposes an existing wormhole, from quantum foam or primordial origin, both labelled speculative. Otherwise one must build a closed-timelike-curve region in order to build the shortcut.

The core is not refuted. Bronnikov et al. 2013 give a classical GR wormhole with the Ellis metric that is linearly stable. Its source is a negative-density perfect fluid with a tuned equation of state plus a radial field. Poisson–Visser thin shells can be linearly stable if β₀² < 0 (D-14). Under C4's own licence (labelled exotic matter, GR solved in reverse), a shortcut of any T_thru/T_ext can still be written down. What survives is narrower: specially tuned, non-canonical exotic matter, and a wormhole that has to be supplied rather than created.

## In principle vs. in practice
- **In principle.** The core survives only in narrowed form.
  - Static solutions with any T_thru/T_ext exist; the transit-time table (M-EXAMINER-02/03) is not disputed.
  - The canonical labelled matters do not hold them open: the ghost scalar has τ ≈ 0.85 b₀/c, and EdGB is unstable.
  - Linearly stable versions need a tuned phantom fluid plus a radial field, or β₀² < 0 shells. Their polar and nonlinear stability is not shown.
  - Classical creation needs an acausal spacetime (Geroch). So "design" means reshaping a wormhole supplied by labelled quantum topology change or primordial origin.
- **In practice.** Not viable.
  - No NEC-violating classical matter is known.
  - The ghost route needs b₀ ≳ 10⁶–10⁷ m (10²·⁷ to 10³·⁷ M_☉ of negative mass) for a 1 kg payload or a human, crossing at ≥ 0.05c.
  - It also needs active control at up to 10⁸ s⁻¹.
  - The candidate's own gap to demonstrated Casimir negative energy is 38–57 orders of magnitude (M-ENGINEER-02 to 04). The technology readiness level (TRL) is 0.

## Corrections to details
- **Sub-hypothesis (a)** ("A slow crossing (10 km/s) needs only b₀ ≥ 505 m ... or 4.5 km ... and takes 0.16 s or 1.4 s") is wrong for a phantom scalar. These figures are tide-only. The GGS mode grows 10^48350-fold during that crossing. For a ghost scalar, the self-consistent minimum is b₀ ≈ 7.6×10⁵ m (1 kg) or 7.0×10⁶ m (human), at v ≥ 0.05c. The 505 m and 4.5 km figures hold only for a stable matter model such as Bronnikov et al.'s.
- **"any designed T_thru/T_ext"** holds only for a limited time unless the mouths' relative motion and potentials are controlled.
  - A relative speed of 30 km/s gives a clock-offset drift of 5.0×10⁻⁹ s/s. That offset reaches T_ext for 1 ly after about 2.0×10⁸ yr; at 220 km/s it takes about 3.7×10⁶ yr [calc, section 5].
  - After that the shortcut becomes a time machine (C7's territory). This is long, but finite.
- **Thin shells.** C4 lists "superluminal or imaginary sound speed" as evidence against. Poisson–Visser themselves treat β₀² as a free equation-of-state parameter for exotic matter. So the thin-shell route keeps a linearly stable window, and the narrowed core relies on it.

## What survives and remaining doubt
- **What survives.** Under the brief's "in principle" horizon, with exotic matter labelled and freely designed, classical GR allows static, linearly stable shortcut wormholes with any T_thru/T_ext. The examples are Bronnikov et al.'s fluid-plus-field Ellis throat and thin shells with β₀² < 0.
- **Remaining doubts:**
  - No nonlinear or non-spherical stability result exists for any one-sided shortcut configuration, and none for Visser's cube.
  - Whether a tuned phantom fluid is a consistent field theory is not settled here. It is a quantum and bounds question, not a scale question.
  - Whether active feedback could stabilise a ghost-scalar throat when its mode e-folds 7.4 times per light-crossing of the circumference is open. No source addresses it.
- **Why weakened, not refuted.** This angle cannot exclude the tuned-matter route. The canonical phantom and modified-gravity routes fail, so the claim as worded ("given labelled phantom or modified-gravity matter") is broader than what is shown.
