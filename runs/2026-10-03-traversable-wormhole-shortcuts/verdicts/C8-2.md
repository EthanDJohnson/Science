# Verdict on C8 (refuter 2, angle: scale)
claim: Two-sided wormholes (GJW, Maldacena-Qi) and quantum-processor analogues are teleportation with geometric bookkeeping: they never beat their coupling channel, and Sycamore-type runs test size winding, not spacetime.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: Two-sided wormholes and their processor analogues deliver nothing without their boundary coupling, causally ahead of it, or beyond what it can carry, and the processor runs so far (at most 24 Majoranas in one copy) test size winding and boundary dynamics rather than spacetime; but only the GJW/MSY one-shot protocols are teleportation in the LOCC sense, while in Maldacena-Qi the coupling is a continuous quantum interaction and the wormhole sets a transfer time shorter than the coupling alone would give by about (J/μ)^{1/3}, so the geometry does more than bookkeeping.

## Strongest attack

The attack is on timescales, and it hits the "teleportation" and "bookkeeping" parts of the heading for Maldacena–Qi (MQ). It does not touch the causal core.

1. **MQ is not teleportation.**
   - In MQ the two SYK copies are coupled by an always-on Hamiltonian term μ Σ iψ_L ψ_R, a direct quantum interaction.
   - No measurement takes place, and no classical bits pass from L to R.
   - So three of C8's GJW-type arguments have nothing to act on in MQ: "quanta ≲ const × bits exchanged" [K-15], "fidelity drops to zero when the classical L→R step is cut", and "teleported qubits never exceed the classical bits".
   - The heading puts MQ under "teleportation", which is a category error for one of its two named constructions.
2. **In MQ the geometry speeds transfer beyond the bare coupling.**
   - MQ compare two cases (quoted under Evidence):
     - with J = 0, the coupling alone moves a fermion across in Δu₀ ≈ 1/μ;
     - with J ≫ μ, the signal crosses the wormhole in Δu ∝ 1/t′, and Δu/Δu₀ ∝ (μ/J)^{(1−2Δ)/(2(1−Δ))}.
   - For q = 4 (Δ = 1/4) the exponent is 1/3. The wormhole route is then about 2.2× faster at μ/J = 10⁻¹, 10× at 10⁻³ and 100× at 10⁻⁶, up to O(1) prefactors [calc: runs/2026-10-03-traversable-wormhole-shortcuts/calc/falsifier-C8-2_scale.py].
   - MQ's own words: self-interactions "accelerate the transfer of information from the left to the right system".
   - So "never beat their coupling channel" holds only as "nothing without the coupling, nothing causally ahead of it, nothing beyond its capacity". It fails as "no faster than the coupling alone would carry it".
   - The bulk transfer time π/t′ is a physical prediction, not relabelling. It is a property of the boundary dynamics, as duality requires, but it is the strongly interacting dynamics, not the coupling, that sets the speed.
3. **Why this weakens the claim rather than refuting it.**
   - The speed-up needs μ ≠ 0. Nothing crosses at μ = 0 [D-01, D-04].
   - The coupling acts from the start, so nothing arrives "before" it.
   - By C8's own falsifier ("some protocol delivers information before, or in excess of, its coupling channel"), MQ does not falsify it.
   - The causal core stands. The "teleportation / mere bookkeeping" framing must be narrowed to the GJW/MSY one-shot protocols.

I also tried the engineering numbers. They survive in order of magnitude but are stale; see the corrections below.

## Evidence

- **Maldacena & Qi, arXiv:1804.00491, p. 28** (fetch_text, verbatim): "Let us first set the SYK couplings to zero, J = 0, but keeping a non-zero mass-like coupling µ. In this case, a fermion on one side will move to the other side after a time of order ∆u0 = 1/µ. … Now, let us turn on a relatively large value of J ≫ µ … Then the time to go accross is π in time units measured by t. Via t′, (4.29), this translates into a boundary time of the order of ∆u ∝ 1/t′. Comparing this time to the time ∆u0 in the free theory, we get ∆u/∆u0 ∝ (µ/J)^((1−2∆)/(2(1−∆))), which vanishes in small µ limit if ∆ < 1/2. This means that the self interactions, within each SYK model, accelerate the transfer of information from the left to the right system".

## In principle vs. in practice

- **In principle:**
  - No two-sided construction delivers anything without its coupling, causally ahead of it, or beyond its parametric capacity [D-01, D-02, D-03, K-15]. That part of C8 stands.
  - For MQ, the wormhole sets the transfer speed: (J/μ)^{1/3} faster than the coupling alone, at fixed μ.
  - A 1 kg payload as quanta of reduced wavelength 1 m needs about 2.8×10⁴² quanta. That is far outside any perturbative (probe-regime) treatment.
- **In practice:**
  - The decisive processor test is closer than C8 says. Chaotic sparse SYK with 24 Majoranas has already run on trapped-ion hardware (2025), at an effective two-qubit error of 2.2×10⁻³.
  - Two-copy teleportation at N = 20 needs about 2×10⁴ to 3×10⁴ two-qubit gates. Measured against current hardware, the error gap is about 0.85 to 2.0 orders, not 2.3 to 3.7. The decisive test is nearer than C8's estimate of 5–10 years.
  - The log N scrambling signature is weak, though. Over N = 20 to 100 the scrambling time changes by a factor of only 1.5. So separating "chaotic dual" from "commuting artefact" needs N ≳ 50 for a useful lever arm, where the gap is still 2.3 to 3.5 orders.
  - None of this would test spacetime. The narrowed core's last clause stands at every N that can be reached.

## Corrections to details

- The heading's "teleportation" fits GJW, MSY and the processor protocols, but not MQ. MQ is a continuous quantum coupling with no classical channel. The predictions about classical bits and a cut classical step apply only to the one-shot protocols.
- "Demonstrated effective error ≈ 4.2×10⁻³" is the 2022 Sycamore figure. The 2025 trapped-ion hardware reached 2.2×10⁻³ effective and about 10⁻³ component (Granet et al.). This makes the N = 20 gap about 1.5 to 1.8 orders at F = ½, not 2.3.
- "N ≈ 20 plausibly within about 5 to 10 yr": single-copy chaotic sparse SYK at N = 24 was run in 2025. The two-copy protocol at that size is the remaining step, so the timeline is shorter than C8 states.
- The engineer's gate model 2kN²n_T is no longer unsourced in effect. It agrees with the sourced per-step count to within 1.5× (0.18 orders), excluding TFD preparation.
- "The GJW window is open for less than a Planck time" [Q-16] is a probe-regime statement for horizons of order the AdS radius. Freivogel et al. find that more quanta pass for larger horizons and more light fields [D-03]. It is not a universal cap on how long the window stays open.

## What survives and remaining doubt

- **What survives:**
  - The causal core: no two-sided construction delivers anything without, or causally ahead of, its coupling, or more than the coupling can carry.
  - Processor runs to date test size winding and boundary dynamics, not spacetime.
- **What is narrowed:** "teleportation with geometric bookkeeping" holds for the GJW/MSY one-shot protocols. For MQ, the geometry sets a transfer time shorter than the bare coupling by (J/μ)^{1/3}. Its description has physical content beyond relabelling the channel, even though the speed-up is itself boundary dynamics.
- **Remaining doubt:**
  - One could define "the coupling channel" as the whole coupled Hamiltonian, not the bare μ-term. On that reading the MQ speed-up does not "beat" the channel, and the claim would survive with only the "teleportation" wording corrected. I chose weakened because the brief compares against "the boundary coupling", and MQ themselves describe the interactions as accelerating the transfer.
  - The O(1) prefactors in the MQ ratio are unknown. Only the scaling is sourced.
  - The F = 0.05 fidelity criterion is my assumption.
  - The N² scaling of gates per step, from Jordan–Wigner string length, extrapolates from a single sourced point at N = 24.
