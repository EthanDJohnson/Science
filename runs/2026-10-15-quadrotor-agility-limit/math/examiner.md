## Refuted
- M-EXAMINER-06: the equal-per-cent latency sensitivity is wrong. At s = 6 m, a = 39 m/s^2, tau = 40 ms the stop bound is v = 20.1295 m/s, and tau -> 36 ms gives v = 20.2748 m/s, i.e. **+0.722 per cent**, not +0.66 per cent (the first-order prediction 10*|e_tau| = 0.7192 per cent confirms it). The range (+5.233) and braking (+4.513) figures are exact. Corrected form: "+5.23 / +4.51 / +0.72 per cent". This touches F10 and the quantitative wording of EXAMINER-A and EXAMINER-E, but changes nothing structural: range > braking >> latency still holds, latency is still 6-7 times smaller rather than 7-8, and the attainable-increment ranking (M-EXAMINER-07) is untouched.

## Unverified
- M-EXAMINER-09: whether the stated e_s/e_a and e_tau ranges in F2a describe one common set of four cases. The closed form e_s/e_a = (1 - e_tau)/(1 + e_tau) (verified in M-EXAMINER-01) forces |e_tau| = 0.036 to pair with e_s/e_a = 1.0747, not with the stated minimum 1.155; the stated minimum 1.155 pairs with |e_tau| = 0.0719, which is the value I compute at (s = 6 m, a = 39 m/s^2, tau = 40 ms). My derivation and the lens's part ways only in which cases the quoted extremes come from: the analysis names "all four dossier parameter cases" without listing them, and §B of its script also contains a separate "dodge" bound whose elasticities may supply the -0.036. The structural claim of F2a (e_s + e_a = 1 identically; range and braking are exact substitutes) is unaffected and verified.
- Not checked, because they are not mathematics: the pi/4 phase-margin budget and the v <= w_c R/m speed map in F5 (the lens labels the latter as its own construction, with R and m unsourced); whether the relevant half-angle in F2b is the horizontal or the vertical field of view. On the second point, a pitch rotation moves the flight direction within the *vertical* field of view, so for a D435i-class sensor (about 87 deg H x 58 deg V) the cutoff would be g tan(29 deg) = 5.44 m/s^2 and v_cap(6 m) = 8.08 m/s rather than 9.81 m/s^2 and 10.85 m/s [log: math/examiner/M-EXAMINER-02.py.log]. ## Formalizable
- M-EXAMINER-01: for s, a > 0 and tau >= 0, let v(s,a,tau) be the unique positive root of v*tau + v^2/(2a) = s. Then v(ks, ka, tau) = k v(s,a,tau) for all k > 0; with W = sqrt(tau^2 + 2s/a), (s/v) dv/ds = (W+tau)/(2W), (a/v) dv/da = (W-tau)/(2W), (tau/v) dv/dtau = -tau/W; hence e_s + e_a = 1 and e_s/e_a = (1 - e_tau)/(1 + e_tau). Finite-dimensional calculus, no physics left.
- M-EXAMINER-03: for TWR > 1, a_h(TWR) = g sqrt(TWR^2 - 1) satisfies d ln a_h/d ln TWR = TWR^2/(TWR^2 - 1) > 1 with limit 1 at infinity; and for d, a > 0 the bang-bang mean sqrt(a d)/2 has elasticity exactly 1/2 in a.
- M-EXAMINER-04: the map tau |-> (pi/4) R/(m tau) is an involutive-style exact inverse of v |-> (pi/4) R/(m v) on the positive reals; pure algebra.
- M-EXAMINER-05: for alpha in (0,1), the Clopper-Pearson lower bound at k = n is alpha^(1/n), it is strictly increasing in n with limit 1, and min{n : 0.05^(1/n) >= 0.9} = 29. Also: the exact binomial power function n |-> P(X <= c(n) | p = 0.8) is nondecreasing on the checked grid. Finite, decidable statements.
- M-EXAMINER-02: for 0 < phi < pi/2, a_fov(phi) = g tan(phi) is strictly increasing and sqrt(2 a_fov s) is its monotone transform; theta(a) = atan(a/g) with theta(g) = pi/4 exactly.
