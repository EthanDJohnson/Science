# Verdict on C3 (refuter 0, angle: evidence + calculation on the candidate's own statistics)
claim: The binding term is in the task: prior information about the course, a map or trained track, which the unseen-course metric forbids.
status: final
verdict: refuted
basis: calculation
narrowed: none

## Strongest attack

C3 has no ablation behind it — it says so itself ("no one has run it") — so its whole case is one
effect size: a seen/unseen speed ratio of 2.58x (the "firm within-apparatus leg") to 3.5x (the
grouped leg, z = 9.9, M-STAT-04). Three attacks remove that effect size. All of them run on the
candidate's own numbers; my script reproduces M-STAT-04's published baseline exactly (within
2.974/2, 5.280/3, 0.467/1; between 104.21/2, p = 2.35e-23, z = 9.89; group means 26.16, 7.38,
33.05 m/s) before perturbing it
[calc: runs/2026-10-15-quadrotor-agility-limit/calc/falsifier-C3-0_split.py].

**1. The split is a cross-unit artefact: measured in the metric's own quantity it is 1.2–1.9x, not
2.6–6.4x.** The brief defines agility as "the maximum **average** speed", and warns: "Where a source
reports only peak speed, peak acceleration, or performance on a course the system was tuned on, that
is a different quantity and must be recorded as such." M-STAT-04's seen group is dominated by
*peaks* — 27.8 and 32.2 m/s, which [D-20] labels "27.8–32.2 m/s **peak**" — while its unseen group is
A-20's "average speed". Computing the split inside each class instead of across them:

| class (the metric's class is the mean class) | seen | unseen | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| mean / lap-average speed | 16.05 m/s (Swift 12.88, A2RL press 20.0) | 8.37 m/s (A-20: 10, 7) | **1.919** |
| mean, full-text rows only | 12.88 m/s (Swift) | 8.37 m/s | **1.540** |
| mean, with SUPER on the unseen side (below) | 12.88 m/s | 11.19 m/s | **1.152** |
| C3 as published (seen peaks over unseen means) | 26.16 m/s | 7.38 m/s | 3.544 |

A uniform peak-to-mean conversion gives the same answer: the run's own verified bang-bang ratio
(A-34: 13.0/6.5, 19.5/9.8, 27.9/14.0, mean 1.994) takes the grouped ratio from 3.544x to 1.772x —
an inflation of exactly 2.00x — and the between-group chi2 from 104.21/2 (z = 9.89) to 14.03/1
(z = 3.57). The conversion is not imported: C3's own two numbers imply peak/mean = 26.16/12.88 =
2.031 on a seen gate track, within 1.8 per cent of A-34's factor 2. A factor of 1.5–1.9x is inside
the slate's hardware-elasticity cluster — the dodge-bound range elasticity of +1.000 buys 1.9x for
a 1.9x range increase [M-STAT-06, M-SIMULATOR-08], and T/W at +0.527 buys 1.54–1.92x for 2.3–3.4x
thrust (1.54^(1/0.527) = 2.27, 1.919^(1/0.527) = 3.44) — so course
knowledge stops being the largest term. That is C7, not C3.

**2. The "firm within-apparatus leg" compares a best case with a reliability threshold.** Swift's
12.8829 m/s is 3 x 75 m / 17.465 s, its **best three laps** [M-EMP-03], with no stated success rate
(Swift won 15 of 25 races, 60 per cent [A-10], which C3 concedes is a win rate). The 5–7 m/s it is
set against is the band where A-20 still meets about 90 per cent. Like for like — best case against
best case — the ratio is 12.8829 / 10.0 (A-20's fastest demonstrated average) = **1.288x**, not
2.58x; the reliability threshold alone contributes a factor 1.429 of the quoted 2.58x. There is no
mapped-track speed anywhere in the evidence base certified at 90 per cent, so the reliability-matched
version of C3's comparison cannot even be formed from the published record.

**3. A published onboard-only result in an *unknown* environment beats the best mapped-track mean,
with no map.** SUPER (Ren et al., *Science Robotics* 10, eado6187, 2025; in the dossier at low
confidence as [D-39] and omitted from the slate's grouping) is a 280 mm-wheelbase MAV at T/W > 5.0
with onboard 3-D LiDAR and the map built online, reporting "autonomous flights at speeds exceeding
20 meters per second" while "avoiding thin obstacles and navigating narrow spaces". That is 2.710x
the unseen-clutter group's geometric mean of 7.38 m/s and 1.552x Swift's mapped 12.88 m/s, bought by
a sensor change and nothing else. Even on the most conservative reading — 20 m/s as a peak, halved by
the same factor 2 — the implied unseen mean of 10 m/s leaves at most 12.88/10 = **1.29x** for course
knowledge. This also defeats C3's impossibility argument (M-EMP-04): of its four routes to 2.58x, the
dodge-bound range route needs 2.58x usable range (6 m -> 15.5 m), and that is precisely the route a
long-range 3-D LiDAR supplies — with the demonstration already published, and the paper's own abstract
naming "strong sensing capability for detecting obstacles at a distance", not prior information,
among its three enabling terms.

**4. The same numbers separate better by sensor class than by course knowledge.** On the eight
onboard-only rows (A-20, A-29, A-26 x2, A-31, SUPER), the course-knowledge partition gives
between-group chi2 = 53.26 with within-group 2.974/2 and 25.153/4; the sensor-range-class partition
of the *same eight numbers* (long-range LiDAR or monocular gate detection vs the short-range D435i)
gives between-group chi2 = 71.78 with within-group 4.325/3 and 5.280/3 — better separation and less
within-group scatter. A z-value is evidence for the variable that labels a partition only if rival
labellings of the same data do worse; here C1's variable does better. The run's own M-DIAL-03 points
the same way: a latency-and-feature-spacing bound over-predicts the observed split (6.70 against
2.78–3.22), so course *geometry* alone can carry the whole gap with no information term.

## In principle vs. in practice

*In principle* a prior map is information the unseen metric does forbid, and C3's mechanism is real:
Swift matches gate detections "to the known track layout" to correct VIO drift [D-11, D-12].
*In practice* that is a repair for one specific failure mode — short-range depth sensing plus
drifting VIO — and SUPER shows the same failure repaired on the vehicle side, on an unknown course,
to a speed above the mapped result. C3's mechanism therefore acts *through* C1's range/FoV channel
and C2's drift channel; it is not a term parallel to them, which is why it cannot be "first" under
the brief's definition (the gain from relaxing one limit with the others held fixed).

## Corrections to details

- C3 reports the seen group at 26.2 m/s and its within-apparatus mapped figure at 12.88 m/s without
  noting that the first is a peak and the second a mean; the factor 2.03 between them is the factor
  the headline split turns on.
- "no hardware parameter changing between the rows" (quoted from M-DIAL-10) is false as grouped: the
  unseen rows are one depth camera (D435i, 6–10 m usable, ~90 deg HFoV) and the seen rows are
  monocular gate detection at 1.5 m gate scale in a 100x30 m hall [A-26]; admitting SUPER changes the
  sensor class outright.
- The grouped test puts a two-element *mocap peak* group (30.0, 36.4 m/s) into a test about course
  knowledge, although the brief rules mocap results out as evidence about this question.
- A-29's unseen values (8, 5.3 m/s) are maxima, not averages, so the unseen group is itself
  unit-mixed; I kept them only in the peak class.

## What survives and remaining doubt

What survives is far weaker than the heading sentence, and is a sub-clause of C1/C2 rather than a
rival to them: *for a stack with a short-range depth camera and drifting VIO, a prior gate map is
worth of order 1.2–1.9x in mean speed — comparable to, not larger than, a proportional increase in
usable sensing range.*

Remaining doubt, stated plainly. (i) SUPER's full text is paywalled, no free copy exists, and the
speed's definition (peak or mean), the clutter density of the >20 m/s run, the trial counts and the
success rate are not established from the abstract; D-39 also notes an erratum of unknown content.
This is why I built the conservative arm (20 m/s read as a peak), in which the attack still holds at
1.29x. (ii) In the *peak* class the split survives at 4.60x without SUPER and 3.16x with it — so a
defender could say the information effect lives in peak speed. But peak speed is explicitly not the
brief's metric, the unseen peak rows are two 2026 preprints without trial counts (one on a
perturbed-*gate* track), and the seen peaks are instantaneous maxima on the straights of a 100x30 m
hall, i.e. a course-geometry quantity. (iii) The factor-2 peak-to-mean conversion is exact only for a
rest-to-rest bang-bang profile, which is why the load-bearing route is the one that needs no
conversion at all: Swift's own measured lap mean against A-20's own average speeds, 1.29–1.54x.
(iv) C3's decisive test (the course-knowledge ablation) remains unrun and is still worth running — but
on the present record the task-side factor is 1.2–1.9x, not 2.6–6.4x, and an unseen-course flight
faster than every mapped-track mean already exists, so prior information is not what binds first.
