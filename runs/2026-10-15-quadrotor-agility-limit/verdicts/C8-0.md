status: final

narrowed: No published result measures the brief's three-part metric — no per-speed trial count reaches the n = 29 that certifies p >= 0.90, and the time-optimal denominator needs its convention (flying-start vs rest-to-rest, and full-information vs receding-horizon) stated — so the slate's ranking rests on verified models and model-error results rather than on a measured sensitivity; but the ranking itself is well defined, computable and computed.

## Strongest attack

C8's second conjunct is the load-bearing one: if "binds first" were not a well-defined partial
derivative, no ranking could exist and the reframe would stand whatever the data situation. That
conjunct is false, and its own cited support proves it false.

**(1) The partials exist in closed form, and C8's evidence *is* those partials.** C8 defect (3)
says "The partial derivative does not exist as an operation. Perception range and braking
acceleration are exact substitutes in the stop bound (e_s + e_a = 1 identically)". I re-solved the
stop bound v·tau + v²/(2a) = s from scratch and differentiated it: dv/ds = sqrt(a)/sqrt(a tau²+2s),
dv/da and dv/dtau likewise finite and closed-form; the elasticities are exactly (W+tau)/(2W),
(W-tau)/(2W) and -tau/W with W = sqrt(tau² + 2s/a) [calc:
runs/2026-10-15-quadrotor-agility-limit/calc/falsifier-C8-0_partials.py]. The identity e_s + e_a = 1
is *Euler's theorem*: v(ks, ka, tau) - k·v(s, a, tau) simplifies to exactly 0, so v is homogeneous
of degree one in (s, a), and the identity is a statement **about** two partial derivatives that
therefore exist. A sum rule on two derivatives is not the non-existence of either. C8 cites a
computed sum of partials as proof that the partials cannot be computed; that is an internal
inconsistency, and the run's own math check wrote them out explicitly ("(s/v) dv/ds = (W+tau)/(2W),
(a/v) dv/da = (W-tau)/(2W), (tau/v) dv/dtau = -tau/W", M-EXAMINER-01, verdict `verified`).

**(2) "Exact substitutes" is false in the only sense that would block a ranking.** Perfect
substitutes means a constant marginal rate of substitution. Here (dv/ds)/(dv/da) depends on tau
(its tau-derivative is not identically zero), and at the dossier operating point (s = 6 m,
a = 39 m/s², tau = 40 ms, v = 20.1295 m/s) the two elasticities are **distinct and strictly
ordered**: e_s = 0.5360 > e_a = 0.4640 > |e_tau| = 0.0719, with e_s/e_a = 1.1550 and
e_s/|e_tau| = 7.45. Over a box of 80 operating points covering the dossier's own ranges
(s = 3–12 m, a = 20–44.3 m/s², tau = 5–130 ms) the strict ordering e_s > e_a > |e_tau| holds at
**80/80 points**, and e_s > e_a everywhere in the box. So the ranking of the three named limits in
the brief's own stop bound is not merely well defined, it is *regime-invariant* across the whole
parameter box C8 draws on.

**(3) The one-at-a-time operation C8 calls unavailable has already been executed on a verified
plant.** The simulator lens perturbed one limit at a time in the plant while the controller kept
its nominal model (bandwidth retuned by its closed-form 40 deg phase-margin rule), on a 17-state
model whose verification log shows angular-momentum drift 7.10e-15, free-fall error 2.84e-14 m and
RK4 order 4.005 [runs/.../calc/lens-simulator_sweep.py.log]. Recomputing the arc elasticities
ln(v2/v1)/ln(x2/x1) from its printed speeds (gate tol 0.20 m) gives motor_tau -0.2116 (local)
and -0.3328 (wide), tau_vio -0.1554 / -0.1936, tau_imu -0.0618 / -0.1755, thrust +0.2971
[same calc]. These are distinct numbers spread 4.81x, and the ordering of the three shared axes is
the same for local and for wide perturbations. M-SIMULATOR-07 independently re-derived this
elasticity table and returned `verified`. A reframe that says the derivative cannot be taken has to
explain a verified table of taken derivatives.

**(4) "Not yet an observable" is a category claim its own decisive test refutes.** C8's decisive
test is "29 flawless (or 46 with one failure) flights per speed bin at 5, 7, 9 and 11 m/s, about
120–185 flights, a 2–3 week session on one airframe". Costed on the simulator lens's own 98.315 m
course, 29 x 4 = 116 traversals is **1554 s = 25.9 min of cumulative air time**, about 8.6 battery
packs of flight energy, roughly 14 h of range time at 8 trials/hour, and 0.05^(1/29) = 0.9019 >= 0.90
[same calc]. A quantity measurable in 26 minutes of flying on hardware that already exists is
*unmeasured*, not unobservable. The brief also fixes the operational meaning C8 rejects: "It is a
sensitivity, and it is therefore computable — perturb one limit in a model and measure the change"
(brief.md, "Binds first"), and its "What counts as an answer" admits "a verified model" as an
evidence class. C8 answers a question about measurement that the brief did not ask as the whole
question.

## In principle vs. in practice

*In principle:* the ranking exists. The agility metric is a function of the limit parameters; where
the function is smooth, the partials exist (shown in closed form), and where it is a discrete
pass/fail threshold the simulator lens used the standard device of the largest passing speed U*,
whose arc elasticity is well defined and span-invariant. Nothing in C8 shows non-differentiability;
it shows a sum rule and a coupling, both of which presuppose differentiability.

*In practice:* C8's surviving point is real and it bites. The slate's ranking is a model sensitivity,
cross-checked against exactly one hardware-anchored number (the planner thrust-to-weight sweep,
+0.565 against the model's +0.527). The brief's three-part metric has never been measured: A-20's
per-speed trial counts were not retrieved, A-27's best row is 38/43, and no published bin reaches
29. So a reader is entitled to the ranking as a *model* ranking and not as a measured one — which
is the narrowed claim above, and which the slate already states as its fact (i) and (iii).

## Corrections to details

- **"elasticities differing by a factor of 20 (IMU path -0.062 ..., VIO path -0.132 ...)"** is wrong
  on both readings. The elasticity ratio is 0.132/0.062 = **2.13x**; the per-millisecond derivative
  ratio at the geometric-mean delays (1.0 ms vs 20 ms) is **9.39x**, which is the 9.3905 that
  M-SIMULATOR-07 verified. Neither is 20 [calc, Test E].
- "range and braking are exact substitutes" should read "jointly homogeneous of degree one, so their
  elasticities sum to 1" — they are not perfect substitutes (non-constant MRS) and they are strictly
  ordered, e_s/e_a = 1.155 at the operating point.
- Defect (4)'s aerodynamic leg is better stated than C8 states it: the sign problem is not only that
  the h-force term helps (kh V² = 4.0 N at 20 m/s, 54 per cent of weight, M-SIMULATOR-09 verified)
  but that *removing* drag in the plant also hurts — the sweep's row `kd x 0.0` falls to U* = 8.875
  m/s against 14.562 m/s nominal. The limit is genuinely not one signed quantity; the published
  sentence understates its own case.
- The trial-count arithmetic and the 10 m/s multiplicity claim were already refuted by M-STAT-11 and
  M-STAT-12; C8 carries the corrections in its own text, so these are not new.

## What survives and remaining doubt

What survives is a metrology claim, not a non-existence claim: the brief's metric is unmeasured at
the reliability it specifies, the denominator needs two conventions stated (the 12 per cent
flying-start/rest-to-rest gap is real, M-SIMULATOR-01), two of the four limits the question names
are mis-levelled, and the right reporting form is a Clopper–Pearson lower bound at a stated n plus
directional derivatives along attainable engineering moves. All of that is a repair list, and a
metric with a repair list and a 26-minute measurement protocol is an observable awaiting
observation. C8's conclusion — that the asked-for ranking does not exist — is defeated by
calculation: the partials exist in closed form, are strictly ordered at 80/80 points of the
dossier's own parameter box, and have been computed one at a time on a verified plant with an
ordering invariant to perturbation span.

Remaining doubt, honestly stated. First, my ordering box uses the analytic stop bound, which is one
model among several; if the true metric is a pass/fail threshold with a non-monotone dependence on
some limit, the arc elasticity could change sign with the step size. I tested only span invariance
(local vs wide) on three axes, and found it, but I did not test it on the perception-range axis,
whose +0.986/+1.082 pair comes from the lens's own log rather than from a run I executed. Second, the
simulator sweep log exited 124 (timed out at 585 s), so its later sections are incomplete; the rows I
used are printed and were covered by M-SIMULATOR-07's verification, but I did not re-execute the
plant myself. Third, C8 could be re-read as the weaker "no ranking *measured on hardware* exists",
which is true and is the narrowed form I grant — but that reading is not the heading sentence, and it
does not support the reframe's conclusion that the question is unanswerable.
