# Verdict on C7 (refuter 0, angle: simulation)
claim: No single limit dominates: the leading candidates sit within a factor of about two, and which one is first changes with regime.
status: final
verdict: refuted
basis: calculation
narrowed: none

## Strongest attack

C7's cluster is built from **arc elasticities taken in the wrong direction and over intervals
that do not contain the hardware operating point.** The brief defines "binds first" as the limit
*whose relaxation, holding every other limit fixed, produces the largest improvement in the
agility metric*. C7's quoted numbers are mostly the opposite experiment: motor time constant
39.1 -> 78.2 ms (making the actuator *worse*), per-rotor thrust 6 -> 7.5 N against a measured
hardware value of 8.5 N (starting from a *crippled* vehicle), VIO latency 40 -> 130 ms against a
measured 20 ms. Re-run one limit at a time in the *improving* direction, at the measured hardware
values, on the simulator lens's own verified 17-state plant, and the cluster separates into a
ranking:

| relaxation (one at a time, dodge task, s = 6 m, tau = 50 ms) | speed gain |
|---|---|
| perception range, factor 2 (6 -> 12 m) | **+117.8 %** |
| per-rotor thrust, factor 2 (8.5 -> 17 N, T/W 4.62 -> 9.25) | +28.3 % |
| motor time constant, factor 2 (39.1 -> 19.55 ms) | +5.9 % |
| perception latency, factor 2 (50 -> 25 ms) | +5.5 % |
| unmodelled drag kd, factor 2 (1x -> 0.5x) | +1.8 % |

Largest/second = **4.16**, largest/third = **19.9**
[calc: runs/2026-10-15-quadrotor-agility-limit/calc/falsifier-C7-0_factor2.py]. At the smaller,
local 20 per cent relaxation the ratio is 2.23 on gains and 2.12 on elasticities — already above
C7's own trigger — and the three non-perception arms give +1.9 %, +0.6 % and -0.1 %, the last two
inside the bisection resolution, i.e. *no measurable gain at all*
[calc: .../calc/falsifier-C7-0_relaxation.py].

The second clause — "which one is first changes with regime" — fails too, and for a reason that
is proved rather than sampled. On the dodge bound v = s/(tau + 2 sqrt(r/a)) the range elasticity
is **exactly 1 for every parameter value** while the acceleration elasticity is **strictly below
1/2 and falls monotonically as a grows** (M-DIAL-06, verified: "proved 0 < e_a < 1/2 and
|e_tau| < 1 over the whole domain (so e_s always dominates)"). My own evaluation reproduces this
and shows the gap *widening* with thrust: e_a = +0.419 at the measured a_h = 44.26 m/s^2,
+0.393 at T/W 9.1, +0.283 at T/W 72, with the hard cap v -> s/tau = 120 m/s as a -> infinity
[calc: .../calc/falsifier-C7-0_factor2.py]. On the stop bound the same ordering is forced:
e_s - e_a = tau/W > 0 for every tau > 0 (M-EXAMINER-01, verified). So in *both* geometric bounds
C7 itself uses, no choice of range, latency, clearance or acceleration makes an in-vehicle limit
first. The ranking is parameter-independent; it does not "change with regime" anywhere inside
the regime the brief's metric defines (unseen course, reading (a)).

C7's one regime where another limit leads — the **seen** course with full preview — is the regime
the metric excludes, and even there relaxation buys nothing: bisecting U* on the 6-gate slalom at
the 0.50 m tolerance gives U* = 18.125 m/s at the hardware values, and +20 per cent thrust,
-20 per cent motor_tau and -20 per cent tau_vio all return 17.4-18.0 m/s, i.e. zero or slightly
negative gain, because the reference family's own acceleration ceiling (U_feas = 18.339 m/s,
M-SIMULATOR-03 verified) binds before any hardware limit does
[calc: .../calc/falsifier-C7-0_relaxation.py].

## Evidence

**Reproduction first.** The simulator lens's dodge table reproduces exactly on my own
re-implementation against its verified plant (angular-momentum drift 7.10e-15, free-fall error
2.84e-14 m, RK4 order 4.005): 18.139 / 16.920 / 15.320 / 29.412 / 8.541 m/s for
(s, tau) = (6, 20 ms), (6, 50), (6, 100), (10, 50), (3, 50), all five within 0.002 per cent of
the logged values, and the 3 -> 6 m range elasticity +0.9862 against the lens's +0.9863
[calc: .../calc/falsifier-C7-0_relaxation.py.log]. So the numbers I attack with are the
candidate's own plant, not a substitute.

**C7's own cited spreads all exceed two,** by the math checks it cites:
2.326 (M-EMP-11 verified, and flagged there: "2.326 > 2, so the brief's literal 'within a factor
of two' condition is met only on midpoints (1.67), not on endpoints"), 3.992
(M-SIMULATOR-07 verified, the seen-course table C7's leg (iii) rests on — leg (iii)'s own text
says "largest over smallest 3.99"), 3.050 (M-EXAMINER-08 verified, the ten bounds), and 1.87-7.57
for the dodge range elasticity against its rivals (M-SIMULATOR-12). My measurements add 2.23
(local 20 per cent), 4.16 (factor of two) and 5.26 (relaxation to the next hardware step).
Seven of seven spreads are above two; the heading sentence asserts the opposite
[calc: .../calc/falsifier-C7-0_factor2.py.log].

**The unseen-course closed-loop elasticity C7 needs does not exist.** The lens's look-ahead-
limited course runs fail at U = 5 m/s for *every* s_look from 3 to 40 m, so its look-ahead
elasticity row is `+nan` in `lens-simulator_lookahead.py.log`. C7's leg (iii) therefore
substitutes seen-course numbers for the metric's regime, which is the substitution its own
"evidence against" line concedes ("a null that averages over regimes hides the one result
everyone reproduces").

## In principle vs. in practice

*In principle:* a tie is possible — if two limits had equal elasticities and both were relaxable,
"which binds" would have no answer. C7's leg (ii) is the honest version of this: on the stop
bound e_s + e_a = 1 identically, so range and braking acceleration are near-substitutes
(M-EXAMINER-01 verified). But near-substitutes within *one* mechanism (the product a x s of the
geometric bound) is not a tie *between rival candidates*: both terms belong to the
perceive-then-manoeuvre geometry, and the in-vehicle limits C1's rivals name (motor bandwidth,
estimation latency, aero error) are 10-20x behind in my table.

*In practice:* the relaxations are not equally available either, which makes the ranking worse
for C7, not better. Thrust is already saturated at the hardware point on the seen task (+0.0025
elasticity across 8.5 -> 10 N in the lens's log, reproduced as a zero-to-negative U* change in my
part B), and motor_tau below about 20 ms buys nothing in this architecture because the cascade's
own 60 rad/s gain cap binds (lens finding 7; my -20 per cent motor_tau arm gives -0.10 per cent,
within resolution). Perception range has no such saturation: e_s = 1 holds for every s.

## Corrections to details

- C7 leg (ii): "a 1 per cent gain in either buys the same 0.5 per cent of speed" is true only in
  the zero-latency limit. At the measured values e_s = 0.5355 and e_a = 0.4645 (M-STAT-05
  verified), so the split is 0.536/0.464, not 0.5/0.5; e_s > 1/2 > e_a for every tau > 0.
- C7 leg (iii) quotes "motor time constant -0.462" and "thrust-to-weight +0.527" as if they were
  measured at the hardware operating point. They are arc elasticities across 39.1 -> 78.2 ms and
  6 -> 7.5 N respectively; at the hardware values and in the improving direction the same model
  gives ~0.00 for both.
- C7 leg (iv)'s range/half-FoV factor of 1.25 uses the corrected half-FoV figure +4.67 per cent;
  the lens's original +10.5 per cent was refuted (M-MECH-03, FoV row). The corrected pair is a
  tie, but again inside the single perception term, not across candidates.
- My own part-1 arms of magnitude below ~0.7 per cent (motor_tau -0.10 per cent, aero
  +0.58 per cent) sit at the bisection resolution of that script and should be read as "no
  measurable gain", not as signed effects; the factor-2 script uses one consistent bracket and
  10 bisection steps for every arm.

## What survives and remaining doubt

What survives of C7 is weaker than its heading and does not contradict a ranking: (a) the
second-place term, acceleration/thrust authority, is not negligible — a factor of two of extra
thrust still buys +28 per cent of dodge speed, so a report that says "only perception matters"
would be wrong; (b) range and braking acceleration enter the stop bound as near-substitutes, so
the *two* geometric terms cannot be separated from each other at the margin; (c) outside the
metric's regime (seen course, full preview) the in-vehicle limits do lead, which is a statement
about C3/C5's regime, not about the brief's reading (a). None of these is "no single limit
dominates": in the regime the metric selects, one term leads by 4.2x on equal relaxation and by
a margin that is *proved* to hold over the whole parameter domain.

Remaining doubt, stated honestly. (i) The dodge task's e_s = 1 is structural — the manoeuvre
time is independent of range for a single obstacle at fixed clearance — so on dense clutter where
the required clearance grows with speed the range elasticity could fall below 1; no hardware
measurement of it exists, while the thrust elasticity does have one anchor (+0.565, A-16). If a
dense-clutter measurement returned e_s ~ 0.6, the top pair would come back inside a factor of
two and C7 would revive at the margin — though not its "changes with regime" clause. (ii) My
part B is capped by the reference family rather than by hardware, so it shows only that
in-vehicle relaxation is inactive there, not that it would stay inactive with a time-optimal
reference. (iii) All of this is a verified-model result, not a hardware demonstration; the
decisive 500-flight sweep C7 itself specifies has not been run by anyone.
