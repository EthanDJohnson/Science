# Verdict on C2 (refuter 0, angle: simulation, with a decisive piece of evidence)
claim: State estimation binds first through drift and feature loss during the manoeuvre, not through its latency, and today is repaired only by prior track knowledge.
status: draft
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: Estimator *breakdown* (loss of feature tracks and divergence of classical VIO), not latency and not graded drift, is what estimation contributes, and it binds above roughly 15 m/s on a world-referenced track rather than at the 7-10 m/s unseen-course operating point, where the drift elasticity is only -0.02 to -0.19 against perception range's +0.99 to +1.08; and the repair is not confined to prior track knowledge, because a camera-free IMU-plus-thrust odometry already matches a known-gate VIO (0.56 m vs 0.48 m ATE on a 19.4 m/s racing track).

## Strongest attack

C2's damage channel needs a world-frame target to miss, and the brief's metric removes it.

I supplied the elasticity that C2 admits it does not have ("no measured VIO drift in m per m";
the simulator lens's loop "has latency and white noise but no drift"). I added a position-drift
channel of rate rho (metres of estimate error per metre flown) to the lens's own verified
17-state plant and cascaded controller, and measured the largest flyable reference rate U* in two
reference architectures:

* **GLOBAL** — the reference is a world-frame trajectory, so the whole accumulated drift
  rho x 98.315 m reaches the gate error. This is the *mapped, seen-track* regime (Swift,
  MonoRace), the one in which "repaired only by prior track knowledge" is a sensible sentence.
* **RELATIVE** — the gate is perceived at look-ahead s_look and its target is stored in the
  frame of the estimate at that moment, so only rho x s_look reaches the gate. This is the
  brief's *unseen-course* regime: the vehicle has no world-frame target, and the drift is
  common-mode between the obstacle and the state estimate.

| rho (m/m) | U* global (m/s) | U* relative (m/s) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 18.062 | 18.062 |
| 0.005 (0.5 %/m) | 12.438 | 18.062 |
| 0.02 (2 %/m) | fails at every speed >= 4 m/s | 17.500 |
| 0.05 (5 %/m) | fails | 14.688 |
| 0.20 (20 %/m) | fails | fails |

Relative-mode elasticities d ln U*/d ln rho: **-0.023** across 0.5 -> 2 %/m and **-0.191**
across 2 -> 5 %/m, against the same plant's **+0.986 to +1.082** for perception range and
-0.105 to -0.267 for VIO latency [calc: runs/2026-10-15-quadrotor-agility-limit/calc/falsifier-C2-0_drift.py].
So at any drift rate a working VIO actually has, drift in the metric's own regime is a factor
5 to 40 below range, and comparable to or *below* the latency term C2 declares eliminated —
which inverts C2's internal ranking ("not through its latency but through drift"). The closed
form cross-checks the simulation: the two budgets differ by exactly the ratio lap length /
look-ahead = 98.315/6.0 = **16.39**.

The one hardware demonstration in this regime confirms the architecture I modelled: A-20 counts
the goal reached within 5 m at the end of a 40 m line, i.e. it tolerates **12.5 per cent of
distance flown** in global error, 2 to 25 times any drift rate a tracking VIO exhibits.

This is an internal inconsistency as well as a calculation. C2 asserts that drift is "repaired
only by prior track knowledge" *and* that drift binds in a regime where prior track knowledge
is forbidden. But prior track knowledge is only needed where a world-frame target exists; where
it is forbidden, the global modes C2 relies on ([D-06]: "global position and yaw drift
unboundedly") are also unpenalised. The dossier's own theory row says so: "Observability is
existence of information, not accuracy or latency, so it does not by itself bound agility"
[D-06].

**Second attack, on the "only by prior track knowledge" clause: refuted by a published
camera-free estimator.** On a real autonomous racing drone at a top speed of about 70 km/h
(19.4 m/s), 6 s per lap, T265 camera plus Jetson TX2:

| Algorithm | OpenVINS | SVO | Gate-IO | TLIO | IMO (theirs) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| racing ATE_T (m) | 98.20 | 47.20 | 0.48 | 1.21 | 0.56 |
| racing ATE_R (deg) | 99.00 | 123.00 | 2.20 | 3.30 | 2.80 |

Gate-IO is the prior-track-knowledge repair ("fuses the detection of the gate corners with IMU
measurements in an EKF ... The world coordinates of the gate"). IMO uses **no camera at all** —
an EKF driven by the IMU with a learned relative-displacement module fed collective-thrust and
gyro measurements — and reaches 0.56 m against Gate-IO's 0.48 m, while beating classical VIO by
a factor 84 to 175. A repair that is a *platform-dynamics* prior, not a *track* prior, exists,
is onboard-grade, and the brief explicitly admits off-board-trained policies run onboard. The
"only" in C2's heading sentence is false.

**Third attack, on the feature-loss leg as a graded limit.** Priced as IMU-only coasting against
the lens's own measured tolerance (survives sigma_v = 0.50 m/s and sigma_p = 0.10 m at U = 12 m/s,
fails at 1.00 m/s and 0.20 m): with an ICM-42688-P-class noise density of 6.9e-4 m/s^2/sqrt(Hz)
and a 20 mg post-calibration residual bias, a coast must last **2.49 s** to reach delta v =
0.5 m/s, and a 0.3 s coast costs 0.060 m/s and 0.009 m — two orders below tolerance
[calc: .../calc/falsifier-C2-0_drift.py]. A blur event lasts the exposure (1-10 ms at 500 deg/s,
by C2's own M-MECH-07 geometry). So feature loss does not hurt through the integration of
inertial measurements over the blur; it hurts only if the filter *diverges*, which is a
reliability failure mode, not the continuous "limit whose relaxation produces the largest
improvement" that the brief's "binds first" is defined as.

## In principle vs. in practice

In principle: the unobservable modes of visual-inertial estimation are three translations and
global yaw [D-06]; none of them is penalised by a metric scored on a course perceived onboard and
never seen before, so in principle drift cannot be the first limit there. In practice: classical
VIO *does* break at racing speed (47-98 m ATE above), so estimation does set a wall — but at
19.4 m/s on a world-referenced track, not at the 7-10 m/s where the unseen-course success rate
actually falls off, and it is already repairable without a map.

## Corrections to details

* C2 cites the perception-aware-planner study (arXiv:2603.04305v1, 55 -> 100 per cent) as "the
  nearest thing to a controlled sensitivity study". It moves the *planner's perception objective*
  in RotorS simulation, which is evidence that planning for estimability helps a given stack, not
  that estimation is the binding hardware limit; and it is a simulation result with 20 trials,
  below the >= 29 trials C2's own decisive test demands.
* C2's sub-hypothesis (2b) elimination is sound and my runs support it: tau_vio from 5 to 130 ms
  never destabilises the loop, it only costs speed. That part of C2 I do not contest.
* The claim that drift "has no self-correction" because global position and yaw are unobservable
  is true of the global modes only; velocity, roll and pitch are observable [D-06] and are the
  states the unseen-course loop consumes.

## What survives and remaining doubt

What survives is narrower than the heading sentence in two ways, and both matter for the ranking.
(i) The mechanism is estimator *breakdown*, not graded drift: at drift rates a working estimator
has (0.5-5 %/m), the unseen-course elasticity I measured is -0.023 to -0.191, an order of
magnitude below perception range's +0.986 to +1.082 on the same verified plant, so drift does not
"bind first" at the metric's operating point. (ii) The repair is not confined to prior track
knowledge: a camera-free IMU-plus-thrust odometry reaches 0.56 m ATE against the known-gate
VIO's 0.48 m on a 19.4 m/s track.

Remaining doubt, stated honestly, and it is why this is `weakened` and not `refuted`. The 98.20 m
and 47.20 m ATEs are a measured hardware fact at 19.4 m/s that no rival candidate's mechanism
explains, and an effective error of that size (about 0.85 m per m of path over a 115 m lap) would
break even my relative-reference architecture, which fails somewhere between 5 and 20 %/m. So
estimator divergence under feature loss is a genuine wall, and it sits inside the speed band the
metric's numerator would have to reach if perception range were relaxed. My attack shows it is
second in line at 7-10 m/s, not that it is absent. Two further limits on my own attack: my
relative-reference model assumes the obstacle target is re-anchored at every perception update
(true of A-20's architecture and of body-frame learned policies, not of a plan-then-track pipeline
that integrates a world-frame trajectory), and the 5-20 %/m failure bracket for relative mode is
resolved only to a factor of four because each closed-loop run costs 2.7 s of compute.
