# Verdict on C4 (refuter 0, angle: simulation)
claim: No hardware limit is active; what binds is the robustness of the perception-estimation-policy stack plus the margin designers deliberately leave inside the envelope.
status: final
verdict: refuted
basis: calculation
narrowed: none

## Strongest attack

C4's two load-bearing quantitative moves — "a system running at a quarter to a half of every
limit is limited by none of them", and the prediction that "no single hardware relaxation at its
*attainable* increment moves the unseen-course metric by more than about 20 per cent" — both fail
on C4's own apparatus: the simulator lens's verified 17-state plant and the run's own verified
geometric anchors. I reproduced the lens's dodge ceilings on that plant to 0.00–0.13 per cent
before using them, then pushed them.

**1. A single hardware relaxation at an attainable increment moves the metric by +74 per cent,
not 20.** On the verified plant with the controller keeping its nominal model, the largest speed
that clears a 0.5 m obstacle by 0.75 m rises 16.920 -> 29.450 m/s when detection range goes
6 -> 10 m: elasticity +1.085, gain **+74.1 per cent**; 3 -> 6 m gives +98.1 per cent
[calc: runs/2026-10-15-quadrotor-agility-limit/calc/falsifier-C4-0_hardware_active.py]. Range is
hardware by C4's own taxonomy — its decisive test lists "(ii) wide-FoV or gimballed camera" and
"(iii) T/W raised 4.1 -> 8" as the hardware arms — and the increment is attainable: the dossier
records a 3-D LiDAR system reported above 20 m/s in unknown space [D-39]. C4's own falsification
threshold is exceeded by a factor of 3.7.

**2. At the operating point the hardware-capped ceiling is 0.96–1.36x the speed, not 2–7x.**
Taking the two hardware numbers the run verified independently — usable stereo depth of 4.1–5.8 m,
where depth noise equals a 0.1 m obstacle's own size at a 50 mm baseline [M-MECH-06 verified], and
the lateral acceleration a body-fixed 58 deg vertical FoV at the 30 deg up-tilt of the [A-20]
platform permits, a_u = g tan 59 deg = 16.33 m/s^2 [M-MECH-02 verified] — and flying the dodge on
the verified plant at those settings gives ceilings of 11.569 m/s (4.1 m, full a_h), 16.368 m/s
(5.8 m), 14.254 m/s (6 m, FoV-capped) and **9.550 m/s** (4.1 m, FoV-capped). Against the measured
unseen-course record, 8/10 at 7 m/s and 6/10 at 10 m/s [A-20], the margins are 1.36x and 0.96x.
A bound that is 4 per cent *below* the speed at which success has already collapsed is an active
hardware limit, and C4's "2–7x above the operating point" is false in that band
[calc: same file].

**3. Internal inconsistency: C4 reads its own bound table at the wrong speed.** Its headline
"a quarter to a half of every limit" is the 5 m/s row, where the system passes 10/10 — not the
metric's ceiling. C4 itself prints 7 m/s at 0.21–0.65 and 10 m/s at 0.31–0.93 of the ten bounds.
At 10 m/s the system sits at 0.932 of the *lowest* bound, 6.8 per cent short of it, and the two
lowest of the ten are the thrust bang-bang mean (10.7301 m/s) and the FoV-capped stop bound
(10.8499 m/s) — both hardware bounds — agreeing to 1.11 per cent [M-EXAMINER-08 verified,
recomputed in my calc]. The brief's metric is the *maximum* speed at >= 90 per cent success, so
the comparison C4 needs is at the ceiling, where its own numbers invert its conclusion.

**4. Thrust authority is not inert either.** At fixed 6 m range, cutting per-rotor thrust
8.5 -> 6.0 N (T/W 4.62 -> 3.26) lowers the dodge ceiling 16.920 -> 14.406 m/s (-14.9 per cent),
and 4.5 N gives 12.616 m/s [calc: same file]. The lens's seen-course baseline, which C4 cites for
its margin clause, fails by loss of control with the mixer saturated 32.0 per cent of control
steps and peak rotor thrust at 8.46 of 8.5 N available [simulator F5] — i.e. with the deliberate
margin convention in place, the thrust bound is still being touched.

## In principle vs. in practice

Not a feasibility question, but the two readings matter here. *In principle*, C4 is right that a
point-mass bound computed at 5 m/s leaves room: 0.47 of the lowest bound. *In practice*, the
metric is defined at the 90-per-cent ceiling, and when the same bounds are evaluated there, with
the actual sensor range and the actual camera cone of the platform that set the record, they are
active to within 4 per cent. C4's conclusion is an artefact of evaluating a bound at a speed the
vehicle is not limited at.

## Corrections to details

- C4 asserts "no single hardware relaxation at its attainable increment moves the unseen-course
  metric by more than about 20 per cent". Measured on the verified plant: +74.1 per cent for
  6 -> 10 m of range, +98.1 per cent for 3 -> 6 m.
- "the bounds sit 2–7x above the operating point" (slate table, C4 row). Lowest hardware-capped
  ceiling on the A-20 geometry is 9.550 m/s, i.e. 1.36x over 7 m/s and 0.96x over 10 m/s.
- The 0-vs-85-per-cent architecture result [A-14] carries a Vicon-plus-EKF state estimate in its
  real-world rows and stated trial counts are inferred, as C4's own evidence-against records; it
  is also a *seen* track, so it is controller evidence at a different operating point from the
  one C4's bound argument uses.

## What survives and remaining doubt

What survives is weaker than the heading and is not C4's claim: architecture and policy
robustness are a large term in this problem, and at speeds well below the ceiling (3–5 m/s, where
the record is 10/10) nothing in the hardware envelope is close to active. The margin clause also
has a genuine model-side anchor — the platform's own time-optimal profile is untrackable at a
quarter of its acceleration budget [simulator F11]. But "no hardware limit is active" and "the
bounds sit 2–7x above the operating point" are the claim's load-bearing content, and both are
defeated by calculation on C4's own plant and anchors, as is its stated falsification criterion.
C4 also concedes, in its own evidence-against, that it "leaves the 5–10 m/s unseen ceiling
unexplained by any quantity" — and the hardware caps above explain that band to within 4 per cent.

Remaining doubt, stated honestly: (i) my dodge is a single manoeuvre with the lens's chosen
geometry (0.5 m obstacle, 0.25 m vehicle radius, 0.75 m of needed displacement); the manoeuvre
time scales as sqrt(need), so a smaller obstacle raises every ceiling and the 9.55 m/s figure is
geometry-dependent, while the *elasticities* in range are not (v is exactly homogeneous of
degree 1 in range at fixed need and latency). (ii) The simulator's own look-ahead-limited course run failed
as a measurement [simulator F13], so neither C4's bounds nor my refutation of them is the brief's
three-part metric; they are in the same currency as each other, which is what makes the comparison
fair, but neither is hardware. (iii) A real detector would shorten usable range further, which
moves the hardware caps *down*, i.e. against C4. (iv) None of this shows architecture is unimportant;
it shows the claim that hardware is inactive is false at the speed the metric is defined.
