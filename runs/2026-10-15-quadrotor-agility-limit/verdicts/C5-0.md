# Verdict on C5 (refuter 0, angle: simulation)
claim: The actuator envelope binds first on seen courses at 15-20 m/s: thrust authority and the constant-altitude torque ceiling, not the motor time constant.
status: final
verdict: refuted
basis: calculation
narrowed: none

## Strongest attack

Angle used: **simulation** (the simulator lens's own 17-state plant, `calc/lens-simulator_model.py`,
unmodified; its scripts reproduce — my repeated rows match its log to the printed digit). Evidence
and scale were available but weaker: the slate already records that no paper argues for the actuator
axis, and the envelope's numbers are datasheet-grade and not in dispute. What is in dispute is where
the envelope sits relative to the operating point, and that is a simulation question.

C5's two positive sub-hypotheses are both measured **off the operating point**, and when the same
model is evaluated *at* the measured hardware values, in the relaxation direction the brief's
definition of "binds first" requires, the actuator envelope has elasticity **zero**.

1. **(5a) is an elasticity to a thrust bound below the installed one.** C5's headline +0.527 is the
   arc 6.0 -> 7.5 N per rotor, i.e. T/W 3.26 -> 4.08, *both below the plant's installed 8.5 N
   (T/W 4.6227)*. Relaxing the installed limit gives e = **+0.0025** (8.5 -> 10 N, loose gate
   tolerance) and U* does not move at all (18.050 -> 18.050 m/s): +17.6 per cent of installed thrust
   buys +0.041 per cent of mean speed [calc: runs/2026-10-15-quadrotor-agility-limit/calc/falsifier-C5-0_envelope.py].
   The hardware anchor C5 leans on is the same kind of quantity: A-16 sweeps the **planner's** T/W
   2.5 -> 3.6 on a platform of T/W about 4 whose "planner bound [is] deliberately below the platform
   limit" [dossier A-16, D-36]. Both legs of (5a) therefore measure sensitivity to a *commanded*
   thrust bound, not to the actuator envelope. That is C4's claim, not C5's.

2. **The row C5 needed was never run, and it kills (5a).** The lens's own sweep script contains
   section `[5] THRUST-TO-WEIGHT` at the tight (0.20 m) gate tolerance — the tolerance it says is the
   one with "the resolution to measure HOW MUCH" — but the run died on the 585 s timeout (exit 124)
   before printing it, so **every thrust number in C5 comes from the loose tolerance only**. I ran
   the missing section, with the speed grid extended above the nominal reference-family ceiling so
   the thrust arm could not be censored by it (U_feas rises 18.34 -> 19.96 -> 21.92 m/s as f_max goes
   8.5 -> 10 -> 12 N). Result: 8.5, 10 and 12 N per rotor give the **identical** run —
   U* = 14.875 m/s, v = 16.165 m/s, peak rotor thrust 7.32 of 8.5 N, mixer saturation **0.0 per
   cent**. Gain from +41 per cent of installed thrust: **0.00 per cent**.

3. **Neither envelope term is even touched at the binding point.** The mixer's `sat` flag covers both
   clipping paths (f_i > f_max *and* the f_i >= 0 torque-infeasibility branch). At the tight-tolerance
   binding speed it is 0.0 per cent with 14 per cent of installed thrust unused: at the operating
   point that sets the speed, neither thrust authority (5a) nor the constant-altitude torque ceiling
   (5b) is active. The 32 per cent saturation C5 cites is a loose-tolerance run at U* = 18.050 m/s =
   98.4 per cent of the reference family's own feasibility ceiling, where the chosen reference demands
   |a| = 42.87 m/s^2 = **96.9 per cent of a_h by construction**. Saturation there is a property of the
   reference family at its ceiling, not evidence of a binding envelope — and relaxing thrust there
   gains nothing either (point 1).

4. **"Not the motor time constant" fails at the measured value in both readings.** C5's exculpation
   of (5c) rests on the arc 5 -> 10 ms, two to eight times *below* the measured 39.1 ms. At the
   measured value, with the controller retuned by the model's own closed-form rule, cutting the lag
   gives +15.80 per cent of mean speed at 19.55 ms (e = -0.212) and **+24.89 per cent at 10 ms**
   (16.165 -> 20.188 m/s, frac of t_opt 0.740 -> 0.924) — and that is a *lower* bound, because the
   model caps the rate gain at 60 rad/s for architectural reasons ("every actuator-bandwidth result
   at motor_tau below about 20 ms is a LOWER bound", `lens-simulator_model.py`). At the measured
   values |e(motor_tau)| / e(installed thrust) = **84**. If instead the gains are held fixed at the
   baseline 39.938 rad/s, faster motors make it *slower* (-10.5 per cent at 19.55 ms, -15.1 per cent
   at 10 ms) [calc: .../calc/falsifier-C5-0_fixedgain.py] — in which case the beneficiary of the
   relaxation is the achievable rate-loop bandwidth, i.e. an architecture limit (C4), still not the
   envelope. Either way the gain goes somewhere other than thrust or torque authority. And with the
   bandwidth freed, raising thrust 8.5 -> 12 N changes mean speed by **-0.77 per cent**.

This meets the brief's own falsification test for a candidate twice over: "a candidate is refuted if
relaxing it changes the metric little, or if relaxing a rival changes it more" — relaxing the
installed envelope changes the metric by 0.00-0.04 per cent, and relaxing the rival C5 explicitly
eliminated changes it by +15.8 to +24.9 per cent, inside C5's own stated regime (seen course,
15-20 m/s, 0.75 kg, T/W 4.6).

**(5b) separately.** The 0.990 N m hover-collective ceiling is arithmetically right (M-CONSTRAINTS-02
verified, brute-forced to 8e-16 N m), but it is not an actuator-envelope quantity: at T = mg the
ceiling is `l_eff m g`, *independent of f_max*, so no motor, propeller or battery change moves it,
while the global maximum `l_eff 2 f_max` = 1.97 N m is available at a 1.05 g climb. C5 says so itself
and calls the fix "pure firmware". A limit that is independent of the installed hardware and
removable in firmware is a trajectory/controller choice, which is C4's thesis; grouping it under "the
actuator envelope" is an internal inconsistency in the heading. Where (5b) does act quantitatively it
acts *inside* C1's look-ahead bound as blind time, raising e_tau from -0.056 to -0.212 against
e_range +0.53 to +0.64 in the same expression [M-CONSTRAINTS-09 verified] — a multiplier on the
perception term, not a first-binding limit of its own.

## In principle vs. in practice

Not a feasibility question, but the two readings matter here. *In principle*, a thrust ceiling must
bind eventually: the elasticity is +0.527 between T/W 3.26 and 4.08, so a platform built at T/W near
3 is thrust-limited, and the brief's hidden premise 4 is correctly answered with a number rather than
hand-waving. *In practice*, every onboard platform in the evidence base carries T/W 4.1-4.5 [A-13]
and the plant modelled here 4.62, which is past the knee: from T/W 4.08 to 4.62 the model gives
e = +0.27 (3.4 per cent of speed) and above 4.62 exactly zero. So the envelope is a *historical*
limit that current hardware has already cleared, which is the opposite of "binds first".

## Corrections to details

- C5's "-0.212 at the measured value" for motor_tau is right as an arc elasticity (I get -0.2116),
  but it is quoted as evidence *against* (5c) while being 84 times the elasticity of the quantity C5
  promotes. The sign of the comparison is inverted in the candidate's own numbers.
- "exactly 0.000 across 5 -> 10 ms" reproduces (+0.0000), but 5-10 ms is not the measured value, so
  it supports no conclusion about the 39.1 ms platform.
- The +0.527 should be labelled T/W 3.26 -> 4.08, below the modelled platform's own T/W, and at the
  loose gate tolerance only.
- C5 presents (5a) and (5b) as one axis; they have opposite dependence on installed thrust — (5a) is
  a function of f_max alone, (5b) is independent of f_max.

## What survives and remaining doubt

What survives is much narrower than the heading and belongs to other candidates: (i) the envelope is
*touched* (32 per cent of control steps clipped, peak 8.46 of 8.5 N) when a reference is flown at
97 per cent of a_h, so a planner that commands near the ceiling will saturate — an argument for the
design margin of D-36/C4, not for the envelope being the first-binding limit; (ii) the hover-collective
torque ceiling 0.990 N m / 396 rad/s^2 and the 164-201 ms thrust-vector reversal are correct physics
and worth keeping as the blind-time term inside C1's look-ahead bound; (iii) on a T/W-3 airframe
thrust authority would be first-order.

Remaining doubt, stated honestly. (a) My numbers come from the candidate's model, which omits battery
sag, thrust-vs-voltage droop and aerodynamic rotor torque — all of which would shrink the *effective*
envelope at 15-20 m/s and could revive (5a); the omission is the lens's, acknowledged in its model
card, and it cuts in C5's favour, so a hardware thrust sweep could still overturn my result. (b) The
motor_tau arm's gain comes through the retuning rule, and at fixed gains its sign reverses; I do not
claim (5c) binds first, only that C5's positive claim fails in every arm I ran. (c) The reference
family itself caps speed at 18.34 m/s at nominal T/W; I removed that censoring for the thrust arm
(the arm it would have favoured) and it still gained nothing, but the motor_tau arm's +24.9 per cent
is measured only 0.9 per cent above that ceiling and is itself gain-capped, so its true size is
unmeasured. None of (a)-(c) restores a positive thrust or torque elasticity at the measured values.
