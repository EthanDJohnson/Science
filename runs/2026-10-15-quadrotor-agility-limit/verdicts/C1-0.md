# Verdict on C1 (refuter 0, angle: simulation, with one piece of evidence)
claim: On an unseen course the perceived volume ahead binds first: usable look-ahead range, plus the solid angle still visible at the attitude the manoeuvre demands.
status: final
verdict: weakened
basis: calculation
narrowed: On an unseen course the perceived volume ahead is the limit whose relaxation buys the most speed, but it becomes the *active* constraint only above about 10-13 m/s; at the 5-10 m/s where documented depth-camera hardware actually loses 90-per-cent success both of its legs have a factor 1.6-3.8 of slack, so something else binds there.

## Strongest attack

C1's two legs are bounds, and a bound only binds where it is **active**. I recomputed both
legs at the operating point C1 itself anchors on (the A-20 platform: 890 g, T/W 4.4,
D435i depth, ~90 deg HFoV / 58 deg VFoV, tilted 30 deg above 7 m/s; 100 per cent at 3 and
5 m/s, 80 per cent at 7 m/s, 60 per cent at 10 m/s), and neither is active there.

1. **The radial leg is slack by 1.6-3.8x at the measured ceiling.** With a_h = 42.02 m/s^2
   (T/W 4.4) and the mechanist's own tau_sense = 107 ms, the look-ahead a 7 m/s stop needs is
   **1.33 m** and a 7 m/s dodge needs **2.62 m**, against 6-10 m of usable depth (or 4.11 m,
   C1's own stereo-noise ceiling). Read as a speed bound: s = 4.11 m permits 14.6 m/s (stop)
   or 11.0 m/s (dodge); s = 6 m permits 18.4 / 16.0 m/s. The brief's 90-per-cent metric is
   already lost at 7 m/s, i.e. **2.1-2.6x below the range bound** [calc: .../calc/falsifier-C1-0_slack.py, section A].
   For the range leg to be active at 7 m/s the usable range would have to be 0.86-2.9 m
   (2.25-4.05 m even with the FoV-capped 16.33 m/s^2); only with *both* the level-mount cap
   (5.44 m/s^2) and a 107-150 ms chain does the requirement (5.25-5.55 m) reach the bottom of
   its own 6 m figure [section C]. The only input that would make it active outright is the
   "0.3-3 m usable D435i" figure, which C1's own evidence-against flags as a search summary
   [dialectician F3].

2. **The candidate's own source agrees that perception is not the active bound at 7 m/s.**
   A-21 is the *same paper and platform* as A-20: its perception bound from sensing range and
   latency, with rotational dynamics included (1.5 m pole at 6 m, 0.2 m sphere), is
   **12.0 / 13.2 / 13.5 m/s** — 1.71-1.93x above the 7 m/s where its own hardware already
   fails 20 per cent of the time, and 1.20-1.35x above the 10 m/s 60-per-cent point
   [section B]. C1 concedes this in its own words ("the budget closes in the 10-20 m/s band"),
   which is above the regime the brief's reading (a) selects.

3. **The headline elasticity is a structural identity of an inactive bound, not a sensitivity
   of the metric.** For the dodge bound v = s/(tau + 2 sqrt(d/a)), sympy gives
   d ln v/d ln s = **1 identically**, for every (s, a, tau) > 0 — including values where the
   bound sits far above the achieved speed; for the stop bound d ln v/d ln s + d ln v/d ln a = 1
   identically [section F; the same identities are what M-SIMULATOR-08 and M-EXAMINER-01
   verified]. The metric is the minimum over active bounds, so d ln v_metric/d ln s = 0 at any
   operating point where another bound is lower — which, by items 1 and 2, is the documented
   one. The +0.986/+1.082 figures were measured on an isolated single-obstacle dodge where
   range is by construction the *only* limit; the simulator's own attempt to measure a range
   elasticity on a course returned nan, every run failing at 5 m/s for an unrelated controller
   reason [simulator F13].

4. **The angular leg faces a sign dilemma, and on the real optics it is inactive either way.**
   A quadrotor decelerates by pitching **nose-up**, so a camera already tilted 30 deg up has
   phi = 29 - 30 < 0 deg of usable braking cone: read with the physical sign, C1's cap forbids
   braking at any speed, contradicting A-20's 100 per cent at 3 and 5 m/s. Read with C1's sign
   (tilt added), the coupled bound is 11.8-12.4 m/s, above the 7 m/s onset. And the FoV a
   lateral dodge uses is the *horizontal* one (~90 deg on A-20's own sensor), giving
   a_u = 9.81 m/s^2 level and 36.6 m/s^2 with the tilt, i.e. a bound of 9.9-17.4 m/s rather
   than 7.4-11.8 m/s [section D]. Finally, for the dodge manoeuvre the cap is inactive by
   construction: a roll is about the body-x axis, which stays on the velocity vector, and the
   velocity's own side-slip at manoeuvre completion is only 11-39 deg, inside a 45 deg
   half-HFoV in every case I computed [section E].

What survives the attack is the *cross-regime* claim, and one new piece of evidence supports
it: SUPER (Ren et al., Science Robotics 2025), a 1.5 kg onboard LiDAR MAV, flies "over
20 m/s in unknown environments" — roughly 3x the depth-camera unseen-course figures with
roughly 7-12x the sensed range, which is close to what C1's *stop-bound* elasticity (0.53)
predicts and far short of what its near-unity dodge elasticity predicts. It is a
between-apparatus comparison (T/W > 5, different planner, no success rate or trial count), so
it supports the mechanism without measuring the brief's metric.

## In principle vs. in practice

*In principle:* the perceived-volume bound is real and correctly derived (its two identities
are verified), and it must eventually bind, since v grows linearly in range at fixed latency
while every in-vehicle bound saturates. *In practice, on 2026 hardware:* the bound sits above
the demonstrated operating point by 1.6-3.8x, so no attainable increment of range or FoV can
be what moves the 5-10 m/s unseen-course ceiling — which is precisely the test the brief sets
("a candidate is refuted if relaxing it changes the metric little"). The one hardware point
where a large perceived-volume increment *was* taken (LiDAR, 7-12x the range) did move the
speed by about 3x, but with the airframe, planner and T/W all changed at the same time.

## Corrections to details

- "The coupled stop bound is then 7.4 m/s level-mounted and 11.8 m/s tilted — against a
  measured failure onset at 7 m/s": the 7.4 m/s number uses a **level** mount and the 58 deg
  **vertical** FoV, while the platform it is compared with ran a 30 deg tilt above 7 m/s and a
  ~90 deg horizontal FoV. On the platform's own geometry the bound is 9.9-17.4 m/s. The
  apparent coincidence with 7 m/s is a mismatch of configurations (and, per M-MECH-02, also
  convention-dependent at the 8 per cent level).
- "T/W_sat = sec(half-FoV + tilt) = 1.94 ... while every onboard-only platform carries T/W
  4.1-4.5" uses the 58 deg vertical FoV at level mount. On the geometry the leg's own platform
  flies (~90 deg HFoV, 30 deg tilt, phi = 75 deg) my log gives a_u = 36.60 m/s^2, i.e.
  T/W_sat = sqrt(1 + (a_u/g)^2) = 3.86 — so the contrast with a flown T/W of 4.4 is 1.14x, not
  2.3x, and the "thrust saturates against the camera cone" prediction nearly vanishes.
- The claim "+0.986 to +1.082 ... which is 1.9-7.6 times every in-vehicle rival" is already
  corrected from "2 to 10 times" by M-SIMULATOR-12; my point is separate — the comparison is
  between elasticities of *different manoeuvres*, one of which (the isolated dodge) has no
  rival limits in it at all.

## What survives and remaining doubt

Surviving core: perceived volume is the limit with the largest headroom-to-gain ratio on an
unseen course, and it becomes the active constraint in the 10-13 m/s band and above — C1's own
"budget closes in the 10-20 m/s band", and A-21's 12.0-13.5 m/s, say the same thing. The
cross-apparatus LiDAR point is consistent with it.

What does not survive: "binds first" at the operating point the brief's reading (a) selects.
At 5-10 m/s both legs are slack, the near-unity elasticity is an identity of an inactive
bound, and the one *hardware* attribution at 7 m/s names a different quantity: D-13 quotes the
A-20 authors as attributing the 7 m/s failures to "objects entered the field of view very late
because of the **high angular velocity** of the platform" — a body-*rate* effect that couples
blur and feature loss (C2) and reorientation time (C5b), not the static solid angle at a
commanded attitude that C1 computes. If the mechanism is late entry at high body rate, the
remedy is a stabilised or hemispherical view and lower rates, not more range, and C1's own
prediction (double the range, gain 1.44-2.0x) is not the one the data would test.

Remaining doubt, honestly stated. (i) I could not separate the LiDAR confound: if SUPER's 3x
is mostly range, C1's mechanism is stronger than my slack calculation suggests, and the
resolution is a within-apparatus depth-clip sweep — exactly C1's decisive test, which nobody
has run. (ii) My slack numbers use tau_sense = 107 ms and a single static obstacle; in real
clutter the vehicle must keep re-perceiving while manoeuvring, which raises the effective
required volume by a factor I did not compute, and which is the honest route by which C1 could
become active below 10 m/s. (iii) The 7 m/s failure attribution rests on 2 crash events,
Wilson [0.34, 1.00] [statistician F8], so neither C1's claim nor my counter-reading of it is
resting on much data.
