# Verdict on C6 (refuter 0, angle: simulation, with a hardware-anchored evidence leg)
claim: Unmodelled aerodynamics binds first as model error against gate or obstacle clearance, and only for model-based pipelines above about 13 m/s.
status: final
verdict: refuted
basis: calculation
narrowed: none

## Strongest attack

C6's own decisive test names the discriminator: "measure *closed-loop* tracking error at
15–20 m/s to see whether it follows the open-loop power law (about 0.4 m) or is rejected
(about 0.05 m)". I ran that test twice — once in the simulator lens's own verified 17-state
plant, once against the one published hardware experiment that isolates the aerodynamic model
term — and C6 loses both.

**1. The 13.1 m/s crossing conflates an open-loop replay error with a closed-loop one.**
e_model(v) = 0.0493 v^0.858 m is fitted to [A-18]'s *replayed-flight* positional RMSE, i.e.
the error of integrating a model open-loop against mocap data. C6 then subtracts it from a
0.449 m clearance budget as if the closed loop did not act on it. In the lens's plant, with
the controller modelling **no** aerodynamics at all (maximal model error: the plant's aero
force is 3.4 N at 13 m/s, 46 per cent of weight), the worst lateral/vertical gate error is
0.151 m at U = 13 m/s and 0.202 m at 15 m/s, against the power law's 0.445 m and 0.503 m —
ratios 0.34 and 0.40 [calc: runs/2026-10-15-quadrotor-agility-limit/calc/falsifier-C6-0_aero.py].
Both runs pass all six gates at the 0.50 m tolerance that defines the budget. The first
failure arrives at 20 m/s and its mode is `loss_of_control`, not `gate_miss` — an
attitude/actuator-authority failure, not a clearance failure.

**2. Removing *all* aerodynamic model error makes the vehicle slower, not 1.8–2.2x faster.**
C6 predicts "a NeuroBEM-grade model buys 1.8–2.2x the speed of a polynomial drag model on the
same airframe and track". I built the limiting case of any aero model — exact feedforward of
the plant's own aero force from the measured attitude and velocity, i.e. zero aerodynamic
model error — and the largest trackable reference rate *falls*: U* 14.961 -> 8.852 m/s
(x0.59) at the 0.20 m tolerance and 18.148 -> 14.031 m/s (x0.77) at 0.50 m. The lens's own
`know_drag=True` arm agrees (x0.65), and so does the independent configuration in which the
model error is removed by deleting the aerodynamics from the plant instead of adding it to the
controller (U* = 8.984 m/s at tol 0.20, within 1.5 per cent of the exact-feedforward arm).
Measured 0.59–0.77x against a predicted 1.8–2.2x is a sign inversion and a factor-of-three
miss. Mechanically, the Sun et al. aero force is velocity-proportional damping plus a
lift-producing h-force; it *stabilises* a finite-bandwidth cascade at high speed, which is why
M-SIMULATOR-07 (verified) records the elasticity of speed to unmodelled-drag magnitude as
**+0.381**, positive.

**3. On the only hardware experiment that separates the aero term from its rival, the aero
term is second by a factor 2.5 — in C6's own currency.** C6 cites [D-09] (Sun et al., T-RO
2022) as support, but that paper's Table VIII decomposes the 78 per cent error reduction:
position RMSE 0.102 m with INDI and the drag model, 0.249 m with INDI but **no drag model**
(+144 per cent), 0.473 m with the drag model but **no INDI** (+364 per cent), over references
peaking at 12.1–19.0 m/s and 48.5–54.5 m/s^2. So at exactly the speeds C6 claims the
aerodynamic budget closes, the aerodynamic model error is worth 0.249 - 0.102 = 0.147 m of
position RMSE while the inner-loop (actuator-lag inversion, C5's mechanism) is worth
0.473 - 0.102 = 0.371 m. Fed into C6's own budget, 0.147 m is 33 per cent of the 0.449 m
clearance at 19 m/s, not 100 per cent of it at 13.1 m/s; and the *total* closed-loop RMSE with
both terms modelled is 0.102 m, a fifth of the budget at 19 m/s. The rival term inside C6's own
citation is 2.5x larger, which defeats "binds first" rather than merely narrowing it.

**4. Regime.** C6 self-restricts to "above about 13 m/s" and to model-based pipelines, while
the operating point the brief's metric selects (onboard-only, unseen course, 2026 hardware) is
5–7 m/s with 60 per cent success at 10 m/s [A-20]. Even on its own terms C6 names a limit that
first becomes active above every onboard-only unseen-course speed ever flown, so it cannot be
the limit that binds first in the brief's reading (a).

## In principle vs. in practice

In principle, nothing forbids an aerodynamic model error from closing a clearance budget: push
the speed high enough, or the gate tight enough, and the power law must eventually dominate.
In practice the two measured routes to that budget — my closed-loop runs on the verified plant
and the Sun et al. hardware decomposition — put the aerodynamic share at a third or less of the
budget at 15–19 m/s, with a rival term 2.5x larger, and both put the *total* closed-loop error
at 0.10–0.20 m where C6 needs 0.449 m. C6's crossing speed is therefore not a prediction of
the mechanism it names; it is an artefact of using an open-loop replay residual as a
closed-loop error.

## Corrections to details

- "the only single perturbation in the constraints analysis that moves a speed ceiling by more
  than 50 per cent": true of the *algebra* v* = (B/c)^(1/k) with k < 1, which is why the
  elasticity -1/k exceeds unity by construction. It is a property of the exponent of a fit over
  1.67–6.92 m/s data, not a measured sensitivity.
- The two fits disagree by 1.8x on the crossing speed (13.1 vs 7.3 m/s) from the same four data
  points; the headline "about 13 m/s" quietly selects the more favourable one.
- C6 reads [D-09] as aerodynamic evidence. [D-09]'s abstract attributes the 78 per cent jointly
  to "an inner-loop controller **and** aerodynamic drag model"; its Table VIII, quoted above,
  assigns the larger share to the inner loop.

## What survives and remaining doubt

What survives is not C6's core but a second-order statement nobody disputes: for a model-based
pipeline at 12–19 m/s an aerodynamic residual model is worth about 0.15 m of position RMSE
(0.249 -> 0.102 m on hardware), i.e. a real but non-binding term, ranked behind actuator-lag
inversion in the same experiment. That is C5/C4 territory, not a first-binding aerodynamic
limit, so I record the verdict as refuted rather than weakened.

Remaining doubt, stated honestly. (i) My sign result is architecture-dependent: the lens's
cascade has no aero-aware damping and a 60 rad/s rate-loop cap, so the drag it does not model
happens to supply damping it lacks; a controller that already compensates drag in its velocity
loop would not show the -23 to -41 per cent penalty, though it also would not show C6's
+1.8-2.2x. (ii) The plant's aero model is Sun et al. eq. 9 — linear rotor drag plus the h-force
— with **no** aerodynamic torque, blade flapping, prop-wash or battery sag, and the lens's own
model card says those omissions "would make high speed HARDER (aero candidate up)". So the
magnitude of the aerodynamic penalty in my runs is a lower bound. Neither doubt touches leg 3,
which is hardware and measures the aero term against its rival directly, nor leg 1's ratio,
which is C6's own discriminator run in C6's own currency. (iii) The hardware legs are
motion-capture results, hence controller evidence by the brief's rule — but that is the class
of *all* of C6's own aerodynamic evidence [A-18, D-09], so the comparison is like for like.
