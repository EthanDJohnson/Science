YES, in the generic decoherence exponent: PRL eq (6) -3/2 t_P^{4/3} T^{2/3} omega^2; 2005 (gr-qc/0501027) sigma = t_P/36 (...)^{1/3} and exponent -1/24 (footnote: PRL's sigma was "an estimated" one); 2007 review eq (18) coefficient 1 with sigma = t_P(...)^{1/3} again unnormalised. For the black-hole-specific coefficient (8 pi)^-2 of PRL eq (5): never repeated or corrected anywhere in their later papers; the omega12 prefactor (8 pi)^2 of eq (4) is repeated unchanged in 2005 eq (12) and 2007 eq (19). None of the later papers mentions the (4)/(5) mismatch.
2. Did they restate the claim more weakly? YES. 2005 Braz.J.Phys: PRL "erased completely the information" but used "only the first order expansion" which "is not a valid expansion for t = Tmax"; new full-integration result "large level of decoherence", |rho12(Tmax)| ~ (M_P/M_Sun)^{2/3} ~ 1e-28 |rho12(0)|, "a very crude model... more detailed calculation is needed before one can completely write off the black hole information puzzle". 2005 reviews and 2007 review repeat 'de facto mixed', 'unobservable in practice', 'effectively eliminated for large black holes and we cannot say for sure for smaller ones'. They never say 'complete only at the very end'; they switch to a finite residual from a different (window-average) calculation. By my own arithmetic (M_P/M_sun)^{2/3} = 10^-25.3 not 10^-28.
3. Did they answer a criticism? None found: no Reply/Erratum/Comment for the PRL in INSPIRE/Crossref/WebSearch; the only Gambini-Pullin replies are on unrelated papers. Their own later black-hole work (2014-2020, LQG quantum-geometry papers) starts from unitary evolution/complementarity and does not cite the clock mechanism (greps: no 'clock', 'Porto', 'relational').
4. NJP 6,45 caveat: resolved. NJP has no black-hole evaporation calculation and defers it to ref [24] 'in preparation'; its sigma(t) is the general moment formula (44) and a cosmological toy estimate (51), not sigma(T)=(t_P/(Tmax-T))^{1/3}t_P.

Inside the validity region the authors themselves state in 2005 (Tmax - T >> tau(Tmax) = t_P^{2/3} Tmax^{1/3}), PRL eq (5) leaves 0.75-0.87 (printed k) or >0.9995 (k=(8 pi)^-4) of the coherence at the edge, so PRL's 'vanishes at Tmax' lives entirely in the region the authors later call outside validity (my calc).
VERDICT: partly-anticipated. Our point (2) is partly anticipated by the authors' own 2005 paper (and softened in later reviews), through a different calculation and without our numbers; our point (1) (eq (4) vs (5) coefficient mismatch) is not anticipated anywhere in the own-later corpus (and the predecessor paper's omega = 1/(8 pi M) suggests eq (4)'s extra (8 pi) factor may be the misprint, so wording should be 'inconsistent as printed').
