The basin is tens of degrees wide, and it is NOT a level set of the LQR cost: the cost-to-go at
its edge spans 405 to 1200, a factor of 3.0, because the velocity directions reach
further than the cost weights say. A cost sublevel set is still the convenient handover test, but
it has to be set from the SMALLEST edge cost (405), not an average, and with margin below that.

Does the basin move when the weights change?
   R =        1: |K|max   265.5, shoulder basin 0.2770 rad (15.87 deg)
   R =      100: |K|max   231.3, shoulder basin 0.2293 rad (13.14 deg)
   R =    10000: |K|max   231.0, shoulder basin 0.2445 rad (14.01 deg)
   Hardly at all: raising R by four orders of magnitude leaves the basin within 20 per cent and
   the gain near the same floor. The plant's unstable pole at 6.09 rad/s forces a minimum gain, so the LQR cannot be
   made gentle enough to widen its basin. A swing-up controller has to arrive accurately.

5. FAILURE BOUNDARY
==============================================================================
a. Torque limit. The nominal run peaks at 18.93 N m.
   swing-up still reaches the basin down to about 8.04 N m (43% of the nominal peak);
   below about 7.81 N m it never gets there. The peak torque is a requirement, not a preference.

b. Model error. The controller keeps its nominal model while the plant changes.
   +-5% on every parameter: 12/12 reach the basin, rate 1.00, 95% interval 0.76 to 1.00
   +-10% on every parameter: 10/12 reach the basin, rate 0.83, 95% interval 0.55 to 0.95
   +-20% on every parameter: 9/12 reach the basin, rate 0.75, 95% interval 0.47 to 0.91
   The interval is what the trial count supports; the rate alone is not a result.

c. Sensor noise on the measured state (the plant still integrates the truth).
   sigma_q = 1e-04 rad, sigma_qdot = 1e-03 rad/s: handover 49.272, final error 3.283e-03
   sigma_q = 1e-03 rad, sigma_qdot = 1e-02 rad/s: handover 46.032000000000004, final error 3.284e-02
   sigma_q = 1e-02 rad, sigma_qdot = 1e-01 rad/s: handover 38.072, final error 3.365e-01
   The balance controller's gains multiply velocity noise directly, so the noise floor it
   tolerates is set by the same minimum gain that makes the basin narrow.

==============================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SHOW
==============================================================================
Shown: in this model, with these parameters, Spong's energy swing-up reaches the
LQR's basin and the LQR then balances the Acrobot; the basin is a few degrees wide and
that is forced by the plant, not by the weights; the peak torque, the model error and the
noise at which it stops working are measured, not asserted.

Not shown: that this model matches any physical Acrobot. There is no joint friction beyond
viscous damping, no actuator dynamics or torque-speed envelope, no backlash, no flexibility,
no state estimator and no computation delay. Each of those costs margin, and the delay
margin above says how little there is to spend. Nothing here is evidence about hardware;
for that, the only evidence is hardware.
