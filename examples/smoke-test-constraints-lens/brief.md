# Brief: Negative-energy options for an Alcubierre drive
slug: smoke-constraints | depth: standard | type: feasibility | date: 2026-09-29

## Question as asked
"Considering Alcubierre drives, what are viable engineering option(s) for the negative energy required to travel at superluminal effective speeds?"

## Question made precise
- "Alcubierre drive": the warp-bubble metric of Alcubierre (1994) and its close variants (Natário, Van Den Broeck).
- "Negative energy": negative energy density as measured by Eulerian observers (normal to t = const slices), which is what the Einstein equations require for these metrics.
- "Superluminal effective speed": bubble speed v > c relative to distant observers in asymptotically flat space.
- Admissible physics: GR plus QFT on curved spacetime (semiclassical gravity). Speculative extensions allowed only if labelled.
- "Viable": (a) in principle, consistent with admissible physics; (b) in practice within ~100 years.

## Hidden premises to test
- That negative energy is required (vs. positive-energy warp shells).
- That any known source of negative energy can be concentrated at the required magnitude and duration.
- That a superluminal bubble can be created and controlled from inside.

## What counts as an answer
A ranked list of candidate negative-energy sources with, for each: in-principle status, the orders-of-magnitude gap to the requirement, and the decisive obstacle.
